BurgerFuel Wellington: Locations, Menu, Prices and Opening Hours

All 9 BurgerFuel stores in the Wellington region: addresses, hours (Courtenay Place till 3am Fri–Sat), NZ$ menu prices and whether it's worth it.

By AlexPublished October 7, 2026

BurgerFuel has five restaurants in Wellington city and nine across the wider Wellington region. If you only need one, make it BurgerFuel Courtenay Place, at 101 Courtenay Place in Te Aro: it stays open until 3am on Friday and Saturday nights and has by far the most reviews. BurgerFuel Cuba Street, at 191 Cuba St, is the calmer neighbourhood option on the city’s creative strip. On a price list dated July 2026, signature burgers ran from NZ$13.90 to NZ$22.50, and kūmara fries cost NZ$7.90. My verdict? A dependable, late-opening Kiwi chain. Not the best burger in Wellington, and below I explain where it fits.

How many BurgerFuel restaurants are in Wellington?

There are five BurgerFuel restaurants in Wellington city and nine in the Wellington region, according to the official BurgerFuel store locator (checked 7 October 2026). The five city stores are Courtenay Place, Cuba Street, Adelaide Road (Newtown), Hataitai and Johnsonville. The other four are in Lower Hutt, Upper Hutt, Porirua and on the Kāpiti Coast (Paraparaumu). An older BurgerFuel press release mentions “seven restaurants”. Ignore it: that count is out of date.

BurgerFuel Courtenay Place: the late-night pick

BurgerFuel Courtenay Place is the Wellington store that stays open latest: 10am to 3am on Friday and Saturday. It sits at 101 Courtenay Place in Te Aro, in the middle of the city’s main nightlife strip, and has traded there since May 2006, according to the official store page. After a show or a night out, it’s the obvious stop. The rest of the week it closes at 10pm, Monday to Wednesday and on Sunday.

Courtenay Place at night seen from Mount Victoria: Wellington’s nightlife strip, where BurgerFuel stays open until 3am on Fridays and Saturdays. (Photo: Clilly4, CC0 1.0, via Wikimedia Commons)

It also has the largest pool of reviews of any Wellington store: Wanderlog shows a Google rating of 4.6/5 from 2,505 reviews. Diners praise the beef patties, the friendly staff, the kūmara fries, the shakes and the value for money. The complaints repeat too: uneven cleanliness, loud music, not enough hands on deck at peak times. None of that surprises me in a store that serves the 2am crowd. Wanderlog doesn’t say when it collected these figures, so treat them as a snapshot and check Google Maps for the current score.

BurgerFuel Cuba Street: the neighbourhood option

BurgerFuel Cuba Street, at 191 Cuba St in Te Aro, is the one for daytime visits and early evenings in Wellington’s creative quarter. It opened on 2 April 2012. BurgerFuel’s newsroom says the site was chosen to sit between Courtenay Place and Lambton Quay, within walking distance of both the nightlife district and the CBD. It closes at midnight on Friday, 11pm on Saturday and 10pm on other nights.

Cuba Street, Wellington’s creative quarter in Te Aro, home to BurgerFuel Cuba Street at no. 191 (January 2026). (Photo: Panamitsu, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)

Wanderlog shows a Google rating of 4.4/5 from 717 reviews. Reviewers like the inventive burgers, the fresh ingredients, the vegetarian choice, the Bacon BBQ Roadster, the aioli and, again, the kūmara fries. The one complaint that keeps coming back is price: several reviewers find it dearer than nearby competitors. Funny, given that Courtenay Place reviewers praise the value of the same national menu. Of the official Wellington store pages, only Cuba Street lists catering.

Which BurgerFuel in Wellington should you choose?

For a late night, pick Courtenay Place. For a relaxed meal in town, pick Cuba Street. Outside the centre, go to whichever suburban store is closest: the menu doesn’t change.

BurgerFuel Wellington menu: what to order

The menu is the same across New Zealand; I found no burger exclusive to Wellington. BurgerFuel says it uses 100% grass-fed New Zealand beef and free-range chicken, and it offers vegetarian and vegan burgers, sides and thickshakes. Its best-known burger is the Bastard: grass-fed beef, melted cheddar, bacon, raw grated beetroot with chia seeds, mango, smashed avocado, lettuce, tomato relish and aioli. Beetroot on a burger is a Kiwi habit, and the Bastard is the one I’d point a first-timer to. The other regulars include the Ring Burner, American Muscle, V8 Vegan, Ford Freakout, Peanut Piston, Combustion Tofu and Vege Pinto, the last one built on Motobites (pumpkin, carrot, chickpea and ginger patties). There’s also a Minis range: Bambina, Hamburgini and Chook Royale.

On 7 October 2026 the official menu page at burgerfuel.com/nz/menu returned an error. Burger names and recipes change, so check the current line-up in the app or on the in-store menu board.

BurgerFuel kūmara fries

BurgerFuel’s kūmara fries are cut from kūmara, New Zealand’s sweet potato, and served with aioli for NZ$7.90. They’re the side mentioned most often in reviews of both central Wellington stores. Regular potato fries with aioli cost NZ$6.90. For one dollar more, the kūmara is the upgrade worth paying for.

Vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free and halal options

BurgerFuel’s standard menu includes vegan and vegetarian burgers: the V8 Vegan, Combustion Tofu, Vege Pinto and the plant-based VTEC. That’s a wider plant-based choice than most chains offer. Nationally, the brand also advertises gluten-free, low-carb, halal and dairy-free options, but I couldn’t confirm that every Wellington store offers all of them. If you have a strict dietary need, call ahead, especially for halal.

The BurgerFuel V8 Vegan burger, one of the plant-based options on the national menu served in Wellington (photographed at an Auckland store). (Photo: Mx. Granger, CC0 1.0, via Wikimedia Commons)

Ordering, delivery and bookings

BurgerFuel Wellington offers dine-in, takeaway, delivery and online ordering through eat.burgerfuel.com. It’s fast-casual, so there are no reservations. Delivery also runs through Uber Eats, DoorDash and Delivereasy, where prices can differ from the store. BurgerFuel’s official pages don’t list payment methods, wheelchair access, parking or the nearest bus stops. Check Google Maps or ask the store before relying on any of them.

BurgerFuel history: New Zealand’s home-grown burger chain

BurgerFuel is a New Zealand chain, founded by Chris Mason in October 1995 in Ponsonby, Auckland. Josef Roberts, founder of Red Bull Australasia, invested in 2003. Burger Fuel Group Limited listed on the NZX in 2007 under the code BFG. At 31 March 2026, it had 62 restaurants in New Zealand and three still open in the Middle East.

A BurgerFuel restaurant in Auckland, the city where Chris Mason founded the New Zealand burger chain in 1995. (Photo: Mx. Granger, CC0 1.0, via Wikimedia Commons)

The numbers are healthy. For the year to 31 March 2026, the group reported a record net profit of NZ$1,968,937, up from NZ$1,026,779 the year before, on network sales up 2.93% to NZ$111.4 million. In December 2025 it sold its company-owned Ponsonby store, where the brand began, for a one-off gain of NZ$288,000. Sources: Wikipedia, Burger Fuel Group history, NZX and FilingReader, 30 June 2026. Is BurgerFuel worth it in Wellington?

Yes, if you want a dependable chain burger, especially late at night. No, if you have one burger meal in Wellington and want the city’s best. The official Wellington tourism burger guide doesn’t mention BurgerFuel at all. It sends visitors to independents such as The Ram, Burger Liquor, Soul Shack, Lucky, Little Beer Quarter, Parrotdog and The Botanist (WellingtonNZ, checked 7 October 2026). I agree with that list’s logic. A city that runs Burger Wellington, the burger contest of the Visa Wellington On a Plate festival (244 burgers from 3 to 23 August 2026), deserves a local burger first. BurgerFuel earns its place on hours and consistency. Its vegan range and its kūmara fries are the bonus. For the festival and the neighbourhoods worth exploring, see our Wellington burger hub.

BurgerFuel Wellington FAQ

Which BurgerFuel in Wellington is open latest? BurgerFuel Courtenay Place, at 101 Courtenay Place in Te Aro. It’s open until 3am on Friday and Saturday nights.

What is BurgerFuel’s most popular burger? The Bastard is BurgerFuel’s best-known signature burger: grass-fed beef, cheddar, bacon, beetroot with chia seeds, mango, avocado, tomato relish and aioli. In Wellington reviews, though, the kūmara fries and the shakes get as many mentions as any burger.

How much does a burger cost at BurgerFuel? In July 2026, signature burgers ranged from NZ$13.90 (Combustion Tofu) to NZ$22.50 (Parmageddon). An American Muscle single was NZ$15.00. These figures come from an unofficial price list, so confirm them in the app.

Does BurgerFuel deliver in Wellington? Yes. Order online at eat.burgerfuel.com for takeaway or delivery, or use Uber Eats, DoorDash or Delivereasy.

Do I need to book at BurgerFuel? No. BurgerFuel is fast-casual and doesn’t take reservations. You order at the counter, online or through a delivery app.

Is BurgerFuel a New Zealand company? Yes. Chris Mason founded BurgerFuel in Ponsonby, Auckland, in October 1995. It has been listed on the NZX since 2007 (code BFG).
