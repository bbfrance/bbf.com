Holy Cow Burger Lausanne: Founder Story, 3 Addresses and Swiss Beef

Richard Williams founded Holy Cow! in Lausanne in 2009. Its three Lausanne addresses, who owns the chain now, and how Swiss its beef really is.

By AlexPublished October 7, 2026

If you want a Holy Cow! burger in Lausanne, there are three branches: Rue des Terreaux 10 and Cheneau-de-Bourg 17 in the city centre, and Les Arcades on the EPFL campus in Ecublens. This is where the chain began. Richard Williams founded Holy Cow! Gourmet Burger Co. in Lausanne in 2009 on a simple pitch: a fresh gourmet burger made from Swiss ingredients, served in 5 to 8 minutes. Since August 2023 the company has been majority-owned by Better Burger Society, set up by the Finnish restaurant group NoHo Partners and Intera Partners.

No, Anne-Sophie Pic does not serve burgers in Lausanne. Her name comes up in searches for a burger here, so let me settle it. PIC au Beau-Rivage Palace (Chemin de Beau-Rivage 21, 1006 Lausanne) is fine dining: 2 Michelin stars, 18/20 from Gault&Millau, Jordan Theurillat as chef de cuisine cooking to her vision, and no burger on the menu. Lunch, served Wednesday to Friday, starts at CHF 120. Sources: Beau-Rivage Palace and Lausanne Tourisme, checked 7 October 2026. Lausanne’s burger story belongs to someone else: Richard Williams.

The Beau-Rivage Palace in Lausanne-Ouchy, home of Anne-Sophie Pic’s 2-Michelin-star PIC restaurant: fine dining, no burger on the menu (Photo: Gregor Julien Straube, CC BY-SA 4.0 (https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=42880906), 2502×1815)

Who founded Holy Cow! burgers in Lausanne?

Richard Williams founded Holy Cow! Gourmet Burger Co. in Lausanne in 2009. He drew the concept and the recipes from his work and travels in food businesses around the world. He is a beef farmer’s son, and the brand says he is still with the company as head of quality assurance. Sources: Holy Cow! ‘Our values’ (first name only) and L’Hôtellerie Restauration (full name), both checked 7 October 2026.

That last detail is why I take the Swiss-beef claim more seriously than most chain slogans. The person who grew up around cattle is the one who signs off on quality. The branding, on the other hand, never tried to look serious. Holy Cow! lists its inspirations as Anglo-Saxon vibes, South Park and Ben & Jerry’s (Holy Cow! 15th anniversary news). Cartoon tone, strict sourcing: that pairing is the brand, and it has outlasted two changes of owner.

The Holy Cow! timeline: from Lausanne start-up to Finnish ownership

Three dates shaped Holy Cow!: 2009 in Lausanne, the 2017 takeover that made it a national brand, and the 2023 sale to majority Finnish ownership. The table lists every milestone I could confirm in a primary or reputable source.

Who owns Holy Cow! today?

Better Burger Society, the company formed by NoHo Partners and Intera Partners, has owned 76% of Holy Cow! since the deal closed on 14 August 2023. When the deal was announced, the chain had 16 restaurants and expected about EUR 40 million in sales for 2023 (NoHo Partners, 6 July 2023). The official site names Adrian as CEO. I have left out his surname because no primary source I found gives it.

Is Holy Cow! beef Swiss?

Yes. Holy Cow! uses only Swiss meat, and its fries carry the Suisse Garantie label. The brand says its meat, bread, potatoes and salad are all sourced and produced in Switzerland (Holy Cow! ‘Our values’, checked 7 October 2026).

Thick-cut fries next to a sesame and poppy seed bun at Holy Cow! Lausanne; the chain says its potatoes are grown in Switzerland and its fries carry the Suisse Garantie label (Photo: Guilhem Vellut, CC BY 2.0 (https://www.flickr.com/photos/22539273@N00/36079284164), 1024×683)

That is the whole pitch: Swiss ingredients, cooked to order, served in 5 to 8 minutes. It places Holy Cow! between fast food and a sit-down burger restaurant, and that’s the right yardstick for it. Next to other quick burgers, fully Swiss sourcing is a real edge. Next to a place that bakes its own buns, you are choosing speed over craft.

My take: Holy Cow! is the pick when time is short and you want fully Swiss sourcing. With three branches, one is rarely far away. If homemade bread and named local suppliers matter more to you, go to Zooburger. Inglewood is for regulars who like a menu that moves, because its burger changes every month. Holy Cow burger Lausanne FAQ

Does Anne-Sophie Pic have a burger restaurant in Lausanne? No. Her Lausanne restaurant is PIC au Beau-Rivage Palace, a 2-Michelin-star fine-dining restaurant with no burger on the menu. She opened it in 2009 (Wikipedia).

Who founded Holy Cow! burgers, and is it from Lausanne? Yes, it is from Lausanne. Richard Williams founded Holy Cow! Gourmet Burger Co. there in 2009.

Who owns Holy Cow! today? Better Burger Society, a company created by NoHo Partners and Intera Partners, has owned 76% of Holy Cow! since 14 August 2023. It paid about EUR 24 million for that stake.

Where are the Holy Cow! restaurants in Lausanne? At Rue des Terreaux 10 and Cheneau-de-Bourg 17 in 1003 Lausanne, and at Les Arcades, Route Louis-Favre 8d, on the EPFL campus in Ecublens. Check opening hours on holycow.ch, as I could not confirm them.

Is the beef at Holy Cow! Swiss? Yes. Holy Cow! uses only Swiss meat, and says its bread, potatoes and salad are Swiss too. Its fries are Suisse Garantie certified.

Where is the best burger in Lausanne? Holy Cow!, Zooburger and Inglewood are the three names most often cited. Holy Cow! stands out for fully Swiss sourcing, Zooburger for in-house bread and named local suppliers, and Inglewood for its monthly burger.

How much does a burger cost in Lausanne? I could not verify current burger prices in CHF on an official menu, so I don’t quote any. For reference, Holy Cow! ran a CHF 15.00 menu on the 15th of each month in 2024 for its 15th anniversary.
