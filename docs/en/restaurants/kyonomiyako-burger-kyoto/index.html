KYONOMIYAKO BURGER Kyoto: Owner, Menu and Prices

Masahiro Komori's samurai-themed burger shop in Kyoto's Higashiyama: dashi omelet and tofu burgers, domestic beef, yen prices checked Nov 2026.

By Hana, Restaurants writer, Japan and IndonesiaPublished November 20, 2026

KYONOMIYAKO BURGER (京の都バーガー) is a Japanese-style burger shop at 690 Higashikawara-chō in Kyoto’s Higashiyama Ward, about 9 minutes on foot from Keihan Shichijō Station. Masahiro Komori opened it on April 27, 2024. He grew up near Kinkaku-ji, used to work in transport and now calls himself a “hamburger artist”. Savory burgers cost ¥1,650 to ¥3,080 (checked November 11, 2026). Start with the house Kyonomiyako Burger: extra-thick bacon, a soft-boiled egg and a house cheese blend, ¥1,650 on the official menu and ¥1,705 on Tabelog. Then try the ankake dashimaki burger, a rolled dashi omelet in a bun. My take: if you want a burger that tastes of Kyoto rather than of Brooklyn, go here.

Who owns KYONOMIYAKO BURGER? Masahiro Komori, the “hamburger artist”

KYONOMIYAKO BURGER was founded and is owned by Masahiro Komori (小森雅弘), who grew up in Kyoto and bills himself as a ハンバーガー・アーティスト, a “hamburger artist”. He told his story on KBS Kyoto’s talk show Taniguchi Ryūryū (谷口流々), episode #354, which aired on Saturday, September 20, 2025, with host Kiyoko Taniguchi. KBS’s Kyoto Kurasu site published a write-up of the interview on November 29, 2025.

Searching for “Kyo no Hamburger”? I found no Kyoto burger shop listed under that name. The Japanese name on this menu, 京の都バーガー, reads “Kyo no Miyako Burger”, and every source I checked names Komori as its founder.

From transport worker to burger maker

Komori did not come up through a kitchen. According to KBS Kyoto, he grew up near Kinkaku-ji, boxed in middle school and changed jobs several times. Then overwork in the transport industry put him in hospital with a lumbar disc herniation.

The injury pushed him to work for himself. A chance visit to a burger shop gave him the idea, and he researched and cooked test batches before opening his own place. KBS gives neither his age, nor the year he was in hospital, nor the name of the shop that inspired him, so I don’t either.

Komori’s mission: make the world love Kyoto more

Komori’s stated goal is to make “people around the world love Kyoto more” (世界中の人に京都をもっと好きになって欲しい) through burgers that bring smiles. Kyoto Kurasu quotes the line, and it explains the shop better than any sign on the wall. The menu borrows Kyoto dishes and the dining room borrows samurai history. Even the multilingual service follows from it: the visitors he wants to win over are tourists.

Where is KYONOMIYAKO BURGER? Address, hours and how to get there

KYONOMIYAKO BURGER is at 690 Higashikawara-chō, Higashiyama-ku, Kyoto 605-0951 (〒605-0951 京都府京都市東山区東瓦町690), about 9 minutes on foot from Keihan Main Line Shichijō Station. Higashiyama is the temple ward that also holds Kiyomizu-dera, which makes the shop an easy lunch stop on a sightseeing day.

  • Hours: 11:30 to 21:00, last order 20:30.
  • Closing days: irregular (不定休), so check before you go.
  • Phone: 075-275-9662, or +81 75-275-9662 from outside Japan.
  • Parking: none on site. Coin parking is nearby.
  • Takeout: available.

What is a Japanese-style burger at KYONOMIYAKO?

KYONOMIYAKO sells what it calls “5-star burgers pursuing Japanese style” (和を追求した五つ星ハンバーガー). In practice that means Kyoto dishes in burger form: a rolled dashi omelet under thickened sauce, tofu and okara patties with yuzu sauces, and red-bean mini burgers for dessert. Tabiiro reports that Komori toured many Kyoto burger shops before he designed his own lineup.

A samurai dining room

The interior is built for photos. Tabiiro describes swords and helmets on display for a “samurai experience”, and chopstick rests shaped like cranes. Several burgers carry names from Japanese history: Bakumatsu, the last years of the shogunate, and Sengoku, the Warring States period.

Buns from a Kyoto bakery

The buns are made specially for the shop by Ame no Hi mo Kaze no Hi mo (雨の日も風の日も), a Kyoto bakery near Kitaōji Station. Both the official site and an opening-week visit report on osumituki confirm the partnership. For me it is the most telling detail of the whole operation: a shop that wants to taste of Kyoto starts with Kyoto bread.

What should you order at KYONOMIYAKO BURGER?

Order the house Kyonomiyako Burger on a first visit. Come back for the ankake dashimaki burger, the most Kyoto dish on the menu.

The house Kyonomiyako Burger

This is the burger the shop named after itself. It stacks extra-thick bacon, a soft-boiled egg and a house cheese blend on a patty that the menu describes as 100% domestic wagyu with a hidden ingredient. The menu does not say what the ingredient is.

The ankake dashimaki burger

This is the burger I would cross Higashiyama for. Dashimaki is a Japanese rolled omelet made with dashi broth, and ankake is a glossy sauce thickened with starch. I found neither 1 in a bun on any other Kyoto burger menu I checked.

The Yakiniku Rice Samurai and the premium burgers

The Yakiniku Rice Samurai uses a proprietary sauce from a well-known Osaka yakiniku restaurant, as part of a collaboration reported by osumituki in April 2024. The restaurant’s name has not been published. For a bigger meal, Tabelog lists the Bakumatsu, with Kyoto beef and kimchi, at ¥2,530, and the Sengoku double at ¥3,080, the most expensive burger on either menu.

Tofu and okara burgers: Kyoto’s soybean tradition

The tofu burger and the okara burger with sesame sauce both use yuzu-based sauces. Okara is the soybean pulp left over from making tofu. If you avoid beef, these are the obvious picks. The menus do not say whether they are fully vegetarian, so ask the staff before you order.

Dessert: anko macaron mini burgers

For dessert, the shop serves a set of 4 macaron-style mini burgers filled with anko, the sweet red-bean paste of Japanese confectionery (¥990 on the official menu). Tabelog also lists a French toast burger at ¥1,320.

Is KYONOMIYAKO BURGER a wagyu burger?

KYONOMIYAKO BURGER uses Japanese beef, but the shop does not say which wagyu. The home page says the patties are 100% domestic beef (国産牛100%). The menu copy for the house burger goes further and says 100% domestic wagyu (国産和牛). The shop publishes no breed, no brand such as Ōmi beef and no patty weight. If you searched for “wagyu burger Kyoto”, treat this as Japanese beef in a Kyoto setting, not a named-brand wagyu.

How does KYONOMIYAKO compare with other Kyoto burger shops?

KYONOMIYAKO BURGER stands out for its Kyoto dishes, not its beef. Burger Revolution Kyoto, Shogun Burger and Dragon Burger are the shops that come up next to it in searches for a wagyu burger in Kyoto, and they compete on the patty.

As far as their listings show, none of them serves a dashi omelet burger, tofu and okara burgers with yuzu sauces, or red-bean dessert burgers on a bun from a Kyoto bakery. My advice: if the patty is what you travel for, compare those shops. If you want a burger that could only exist in Kyoto, start here. Every Kyoto address we cover is on our Kyoto burger hub.

Verdict: is KYONOMIYAKO BURGER worth it?

Yes, if you are in Higashiyama and want a Kyoto burger rather than a straight American smash. The idea holds together from the bun to the dessert: bread from a Kyoto bakery, a dashi omelet under ankake, anko in a macaron. Komori’s story gives the shop a point of view, and you can read it in every line of the menu. Most burgers cost ¥1,650 to ¥2,090. Only the premiums listed on Tabelog go past ¥2,500.

There are 2 caveats. The beef is sold as domestic wagyu with no brand, so if wagyu is your reason to go, look elsewhere. And the review base is thin: Tabelog shows a score of 3.04 from only 6 reviews. The shop’s own site claims more than 1,500 Google reviews at 4.9 stars and a Restaurant Guru 2025 “Recommended” award. I could not verify either.

Frequently asked questions about KYONOMIYAKO BURGER

Who owns KYONOMIYAKO BURGER in Kyoto?

Masahiro Komori (小森雅弘) founded and owns KYONOMIYAKO BURGER. He grew up near Kinkaku-ji in Kyoto and calls himself a “hamburger artist”. He opened the shop on April 27, 2024, after a back injury forced him out of the transport industry.

Where is KYONOMIYAKO BURGER and how do I get there?

The shop is at 690 Higashikawara-chō, Higashiyama-ku, Kyoto 605-0951. The nearest station is Keihan Main Line Shichijō Station, about 9 minutes away on foot. There is no parking, but coin parking is nearby.

How much does a burger cost at KYONOMIYAKO BURGER?

Savory burgers cost ¥1,650 to ¥3,080 (checked 2026-11-11). The house burger is ¥1,650 on the official menu and ¥1,705 on Tabelog. The 4 anko mini burgers cost ¥990. With sides and a drink, expect ¥2,000 to ¥3,000 per person.

Is the beef at KYONOMIYAKO BURGER wagyu?

The menu describes the house burger as 100% domestic wagyu, and the home page says 100% domestic beef. The shop does not name the breed, the brand or the patty weight.

Does KYONOMIYAKO BURGER have vegetarian options?

It has a tofu burger and an okara burger with yuzu-based sauces, both made without a beef patty. The shop does not say whether they are fully vegetarian, so confirm with the staff.

Do I need a reservation at KYONOMIYAKO BURGER?

No source I checked mentions reservations. The shop is open from 11:30 to 21:00 (last order 20:30) with irregular closing days, so call 075-275-9662 (+81 75-275-9662 from abroad) or check its Instagram before a special trip.

Does KYONOMIYAKO BURGER have an English menu?

Yes. It has an English menu, multilingual service and takeout, and it accepts major credit cards, IC transit cards and QR payments.
