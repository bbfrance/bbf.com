Portillo's Chicago: Locations, Menu and Hours

Portillo's has 3 Chicago restaurants and a fourth coming to Michigan Avenue. Addresses, hours, what to order and where the burger fits.

By Daniel, Restaurants writer, United StatesPublished November 18, 2026

Portillo’s has 3 restaurants in Chicago. If you only have time for one, make it Portillo’s & Barnelli’s at 100 W. Ontario St. in River North, open daily from 10 a.m. to 1 a.m. Order the Chicago-style hot dog or the Italian beef. That’s what built the chain. The char-broiled burger is on the menu, but it’s a supporting act. The other 2 city locations are Canal & Taylor in the South Loop and Addison & Kimball in Avondale. A fourth, at 304 N. Michigan Ave., was announced in June 2026 and hasn’t opened yet.

I checked every address, phone number and opening hour below against Portillo’s official location pages on November 13, 2026. When a fact comes from a third party, I name it. Where I couldn’t confirm something, I say so instead of guessing. 1 more thing, since this is a burger site: nobody goes to Portillo’s for the burger, and I won’t pretend otherwise. If a burger is the point of your trip, start with our Chicago burger guide.

Portillo’s Chicago locations: which one to choose

Portillo’s runs 3 restaurants inside Chicago city limits: River North (downtown), Canal & Taylor (South Loop) and Addison & Kimball (Avondale). For a first visit I’d pick River North, which is the most central and the one tourists review most. Canal & Taylor suits the West Loop and the UIC area. Addison & Kimball is the one to drive to, with a parking lot and a double drive-thru.

Portillo’s River North (100 W. Ontario St.): the downtown pick

Portillo’s & Barnelli’s Chicago, at 100 W. Ontario St. in River North, is the downtown Portillo’s and the best choice for a first visit. According to Time Out Chicago, it sits between Clark St. and LaSalle St., a short walk from the Chicago and Grand Red Line stops. It opened in 1994, per Wikipedia and company histories. Inside, the room is dressed as a 1930s–40s Prohibition-era Chicago street.

Inside Portillo’s River North: the Prohibition-era street-scene decor, red-checked tables and the Barnelli’s Pasta Bowl counter under the facades (photo taken in November 2013, with holiday decorations). (Photo: Ken Lund, CC BY-SA 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Portillo%27s\_Hot\_Dogs,\_Chicago,\_Illinois\_(11004202155).jpg (4000×3000))

The official page lists a drive-thru, delivery, kiosk ordering and catering. The same building houses Barnelli’s, Portillo’s Italian concept, which dates back to 1993. The dining room and the drive-thru both run from 10 a.m. to 1 a.m. every day. Holiday hours vary.

Watch out for older listings. Time Out Chicago still shows an 11 p.m. or midnight close and the ZIP code 60654. The official page says 1 a.m. and 60610. Trust the official page.

Portillo’s Canal & Taylor: the South Loop location

Portillo’s Canal and Taylor, at 520 W. Taylor St., is the South Loop location. It was the city’s second Portillo’s and opened on May 31, 2016. Time Out Chicago and Rejournals described it at the opening as a 2-story building with 2 patios and an Americana theme. From 311 S. Wacker Dr. in the Loop it’s about an 11-minute walk. The nearest train is the Clinton Blue Line stop.

The official page lists Grab & Go pickup, a drive-thru, delivery and kiosk ordering. The dining room and the drive-thru are open daily from 10 a.m. to 1 a.m.

Portillo’s Addison & Kimball: the Avondale location with parking

Portillo’s Addison & Kimball, at 3343 W. Addison St. in Avondale, is the largest of the 3 and the easiest to reach by car. According to Portillo’s own press release, it opened on November 2, 2020. The building covers about 9,000 square feet and seats more than 280 people. It has a double drive-thru, an adjacent parking lot, a seasonal patio and 1920s decor. ABC7 Chicago places it near the Addison stop on the CTA Blue Line.

I couldn’t find an official location page for this restaurant. Portillo’s page for “Addison” covers the suburb of Addison, not this address. The phone number and hours circulating online come only from aggregators, so I left them out. Check the Portillo’s app or Google Maps before you go.

Portillo’s Michigan Avenue: announced, not yet open

Portillo’s plans a fourth Chicago restaurant at 304 N. Michigan Ave., in the Loop just south of the river near East Wacker Place. The Chicago Sun-Times and FOX 32 reported the project in June 2026. The plans call for about 5,500 square feet, close to 100 seats, dine-in and carryout, and no drive-thru. It will be the 45th Portillo’s in the Chicago area. As of November 13, 2026, no opening date has been announced.

Portillo’s Chicago menu: what to order

At Portillo’s, order a Chicago-style hot dog or an Italian beef first, then finish with the chocolate cake or the chocolate cake shake. The official location pages list char-broiled burgers, Italian beef sandwiches, Chicago-style hot dogs, chopped salads and the chocolate cake shake. In reviews, the dishes people name again and again are the hot dog, the Maxwell Street Polish, the Italian beef and the cake.

The Chicago-style hot dog: never with ketchup

A Portillo’s Chicago-style hot dog is an all-beef Vienna Beef natural-casing frank in a steamed poppy seed bun. According to Portillo’s and Tasting Table, it comes topped with mustard, relish, onion, tomato, a kosher pickle, sport peppers and celery salt. Ketchup is not part of the build, and asking for it is the fastest way to out yourself as a visitor. Of everything on the menu, this is the order I’d defend hardest: it’s the dish the city is known for, done by the chain that sells more of them than anyone else in town.

A Portillo’s Chicago-style hot dog in Chicago: poppy seed bun, tomato slices, a kosher pickle spear and sport peppers, and no ketchup (photo taken in November 2016). (Photo: FrugalGlutton.com, CC BY 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/35815427@N02/31121290956 (1024×768))

Italian beef: wet, dry or dipped, sweet or hot

An Italian beef is a Chicago sandwich of thinly sliced roast beef on French bread, soaked in a seasoned jus. According to The Takeout and TripAdvisor listings, Portillo’s roasts its beef for about 4 hours before slicing it. You choose how much jus goes on and which peppers:

  • Dry: the beef goes on the bread with little jus.
  • Wet: extra jus is spooned over the sandwich.
  • Dipped: the whole sandwich is dunked in the jus.
  • Sweet: topped with sweet peppers.
  • Hot: topped with giardiniera, the spicy pickled vegetable relish.

My advice for a first order is hot and dipped. The bread goes soft, the giardiniera cuts through the richness, and you’ll understand why locals eat it leaning over the counter. In May 2025 Portillo’s launched “The Leo”, an Italian beef named after Pope Leo XIV, a Chicago native, as Food Republic reported.

The Portillo’s burger: good to know, not the reason to go

The Portillo’s burger is a char-broiled burger, and on a menu built around hot dogs and Italian beef it’s a side act. I couldn’t confirm the exact lineup (single, double, bacon, patty weights) or any Chicago-only burger from an official source, so I’m not listing one. Here’s my position: if you’re in the Midwest for burgers, Milwaukee is the better trip, with the butter burger at Solly’s Grille or Culver’s in Milwaukee. At Portillo’s, add a burger for the table if you must, and let the beef and the hot dog do the heavy lifting.

Chocolate cake and the chocolate cake shake

Portillo’s chocolate cake is the chain’s signature dessert. The chocolate cake shake turns it into a milkshake. The cake even made headlines in 2025, when Pope Leo XIV received one for his 70th birthday, as ABC7 Chicago reported.

Breakfast at Portillo’s Chicago

Portillo’s started testing a breakfast menu on April 15, 2025, served from 6:30 a.m. to 10:30 a.m., and later extended it to 10 restaurants. According to ABC7 Chicago and Portillo’s, those included the South Loop (Taylor) and Addison locations in Chicago. In 2025 a breakfast combo of a sandwich, a small coffee and Hash Brown Bites cost $7.99. I couldn’t confirm that breakfast is still served in November 2026: the official Canal & Taylor page now shows a 10 a.m. opening.

How much does Portillo’s cost in Chicago?

Plan on roughly $10 to $20 per person at Portillo’s River North, according to the third-party guide Pearl. Illinois and Chicago sales tax is added at the register. I couldn’t read official menu prices because Portillo’s website returned an error, and price aggregators disagree by up to a few dollars on the same item. Rather than print an undated number, I’d check the price in the Portillo’s app for the location you plan to visit.

Portillo’s Chicago reviews: is it worth the hype?

Yes, for the hot dog and the Italian beef. Portillo’s River North holds a 4.4 out of 5 on Google across more than 23,000 reviews, as relayed by Pearl on November 13, 2026. The same source notes that it ranked No. 189 on Opinionated About Dining’s Cheap Eats North America 2025 list, its second year on the list. Ratings on Yelp, on TripAdvisor and for the other 2 Chicago locations weren’t readable when I checked, so I don’t report them.

Reviewers on TripAdvisor and Pearl keep praising the same things:

  • Fresh, flavorful food, with the hot dog, the Maxwell Street Polish, the Italian beef and the chocolate cake named most often.
  • Fast service despite the volume.
  • A loud, lively room that works for families and for late nights.

The main complaint is predictable: tourist crowds and lines at peak times.

The Portillo’s Trustpilot page covers the whole company rather than 1 restaurant, and it shows 2.9 out of 5. Those reviews mix delivery, shipping and customer-service complaints from every state, so they tell you little about a meal at River North.

When to go: crowds and reservations

Portillo’s doesn’t take reservations, so timing is your best tool. According to Pearl, the rush at River North runs from noon to 1 p.m. and from 6 p.m. to 8 p.m. Go around 5 p.m. or after 9 p.m. for shorter lines. River North and Canal & Taylor stay open until 1 a.m., which makes them 2 of the easiest late-night meals in central Chicago.

A short history of Portillo’s

Portillo’s began on April 9, 1963, when Dick Portillo opened The Dog House in Villa Park, Illinois: a 12-foot trailer with no bathroom or running water. According to Wikipedia and The Daily Meal, he bought it with $1,100 in savings and help from his brother Frank. The stand became Portillo’s in 1967.

Today the company is based in Oak Brook, Illinois, and runs more than 100 restaurants in 11 states. Berkshire Partners became majority owner in 2014, and Portillo’s listed on the Nasdaq under PTLO on October 21, 2021. Brett Patterson has been CEO since February 2026. He succeeded Michael Osanloo, who left in September 2025. Per the Chicago Sun-Times, the company reported $732.1 million in revenue for 2025, up 3%, with same-restaurant sales down 0.5% and net income of $21.1 million, down from $35 million in 2024.

Portillo’s is Chicago’s version of what Whataburger in Houston is to Texans: a regional chain locals defend fiercely. Chicago is also home to the world’s biggest burger chain, since McDonald’s has its headquarters in the West Loop.

Portillo’s Chicago FAQ

How many Portillo’s are there in Chicago?

3 Portillo’s are open in Chicago: River North (100 W. Ontario St.), South Loop (520 W. Taylor St.) and Avondale (3343 W. Addison St.). A fourth, at 304 N. Michigan Ave., was announced in June 2026 and has no opening date yet.

What is the address of the downtown Portillo’s?

The downtown Portillo’s is Portillo’s & Barnelli’s Chicago, 100 W. Ontario St., Chicago, IL 60610, in River North. Its phone number is (312) 587-8910.

What are Portillo’s Chicago opening hours? Is it open late?

According to Portillo’s official pages, checked on November 13, 2026, River North and Canal & Taylor are open every day from 10 a.m. to 1 a.m., for both the dining room and the drive-thru. Holiday hours vary. Hours for Addison & Kimball were not confirmed on an official page.

What should I order at Portillo’s: hot dog, Italian beef or burger?

Start with the Chicago-style hot dog or the Italian beef, which are Portillo’s specialties. The char-broiled burger is a secondary item. Finish with the chocolate cake or the chocolate cake shake.

What do wet, dipped, sweet and hot mean for an Italian beef?

Wet means extra jus spooned on top, dipped means the whole sandwich is dunked in jus, and dry means little jus. Sweet adds sweet peppers, and hot adds spicy giardiniera.

Why no ketchup on a Chicago hot dog?

The Chicago-style hot dog is defined by mustard, relish, onion, tomato, pickle, sport peppers and celery salt, and Portillo’s builds it without ketchup. Those toppings are the whole recipe.

Does Portillo’s have vegetarian options?

Portillo’s official location pages list chopped salads, but I couldn’t confirm a vegetarian hot dog or burger from an official source. Check the ingredients in the Portillo’s app before ordering.

Do I need a reservation at Portillo’s?

No, Portillo’s doesn’t take reservations. To avoid the lines at River North, skip noon to 1 p.m. and 6 p.m. to 8 p.m., and go around 5 p.m. or after 9 p.m.

Does Portillo’s serve breakfast in Chicago?

Portillo’s tested breakfast from April 2025 and extended it to several restaurants, including the South Loop and Addison locations. Whether it is still served in November 2026 is not confirmed.
