Best Burgers in Fukuoka: Tenjin to Hakata

The best burgers in Fukuoka are concentrated in Tenjin and the side streets of Daimyō and Imaizumi, the city’s shopping and nightlife core, with a second cluster around Hakata Station. Small independent shops in Daimyō are where to look for made-to-order gourmet burgers; the station areas lean towards quick chain burgers.

Fukuoka eats out, and it eats late. Its signature is the yatai, open-air food stalls that set up each evening along the Naka River in Nakasu and in Tenjin, better known for Hakata tonkotsu ramen than for anything on a bun. The city’s other pride is mentaiko, spicy marinated pollock roe, which Japanese kitchens like to fold into mayonnaise. For burger history, Kyushu has its own reference point: the Sasebo burger, a large made-to-order burger that grew up around the US Navy base in Sasebo, Nagasaki Prefecture, from the 1950s.

Burgers in Fukuoka: key facts

  • Neighbourhoods to explore: Tenjin, Daimyō, Imaizumi, Nakasu and the Hakata Station area.
  • Regional style: the Sasebo burger, from neighbouring Nagasaki Prefecture.
  • Local flavour to watch for: mentaiko, Fukuoka’s best-known food souvenir, often turned into a creamy sauce.
  • When locals eat: dinner runs late, and the yatai stalls only open in the evening.
  • Getting around: the Kūkō subway line links Fukuoka Airport to Hakata and Tenjin in a few stops.
  • Prices: quoted in yen (JPY), and only from a dated source.

Every guide we publish on Fukuoka is dated and lists only venues, addresses, prices and ratings we could verify. Other Japanese and Asian cities are in our burger restaurant guides by city.
