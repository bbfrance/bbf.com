Holeman and Finch Burger: Linton Hopkins' Atlanta Double Stack

Holeman and Finch's double cheeseburger in 2026: address, hours, the build, and how Linton Hopkins' 24-a-night special became America's No. 1.

By AlexPublished October 15, 2026

The Holeman and Finch burger is a double-stack cheeseburger that Atlanta chef Linton Hopkins created in 2008. You order it today at Holeman and Finch Public House, 1201 Peachtree Street NE, Building 400, Colony Square, in Midtown Atlanta. Forget the 10 p.m. scramble: the restaurant’s own site now reads “Burger Time, All The Time”, so the burger is on whenever the kitchen is. Want it without a sit-down meal? H&F Burger, the group’s fast-casual counter at Ponce City Market, serves the same double cheeseburger.

A word on method first. I have not eaten this burger at Colony Square yet, so this is a sourced profile, not a review. I built it from the restaurant’s site, the Hopkins and Company group site, and dated reporting in Atlanta Magazine, Resy, Rough Draft Atlanta, The Daily Meal and Food Republic. Every current fact was rechecked on October 10, 2026. Where something is history, or where I could not confirm it, I say so.

What is on the Holeman and Finch burger

The Holeman and Finch burger is two 4-ounce griddled patties of chuck and brisket with American cheese, bread-and-butter pickles and red onion on a toasted house-baked bun.

The build below is the one documented by The Daily Meal in 2019 and Food Republic in 2011. No 2026 primary source confirms every detail at Colony Square, so read it as the documented recipe, not a guarantee of tonight’s plate.

Component Documented build (2011–2019 sources)
Patties Two 4 oz patties, freshly ground
Beef Pasture-raised chuck and brisket
Seasoning and cooking Salt only, griddled until crusty
Cheese American
Toppings Bread-and-butter pickles, red onion
Bun House-baked pain de mie, buttered and toasted
Sides Fresh-cut fries
Condiments House-made ketchup and mustard

Close-up of the Holeman and Finch double cheeseburger: two griddled 4 oz chuck-and-brisket patties, melted American cheese and bread-and-butter pickles on a toasted house-baked bun (2013; 1024×768) (Photo: T.Tseng (Flickr, https://www.flickr.com/photos/68147320@N02/11247709575), CC BY 2.0)

Read the spec sheet and the restraint jumps out. No sauce. No lettuce, no tomato. Two thin griddled patties, melted American and sharp pickles carry the whole thing, and I like that bet: it leaves the beef and the crust nowhere to hide, which most gastropub burgers avoid by piling on toppings. The pain de mie is a soft, tight-crumbed sandwich loaf, nearer a bakery bun than a supermarket one. To try that style at home, our homemade burger buns recipe walks through an enriched dough.

Is the Holeman and Finch burger still limited to 24 a night at 10 p.m.?

No. The 24-burgers-at-10-p.m. ritual is historical; the official site now promotes “Burger Time, All The Time”, meaning the burger is available throughout service.

The bullhorn story is still the first thing people tell about this burger, so the dates matter. Here is how the rule changed, according to Atlanta Magazine and Food Republic:

  • 2008: Hopkins launches the double stack as a late-night item. Only 24 are made, released at 10 p.m., and announced with a bullhorn: “It’s burger time!”
  • Later: the burger moves to the regular dinner menu with no limit.
  • September 10, 2016: chef de cuisine Spencer Gomez (formerly of Gunshow) and the Hopkinses bring back a cap of 24 burgers each at lunch, brunch and dinner, with no 10 p.m. wait. The reason given was limited kitchen space.
  • 2026: the official site says “Burger Time, All The Time”.

The bar at the original Holeman and Finch Public House in Buckhead in 2013, the room where 24 burgers were once announced by bullhorn at 10 p.m. (1024×768) (Photo: T.Tseng (Flickr, https://www.flickr.com/photos/68147320@N02/11247750456), CC BY 2.0)

One question I could not settle: does a per-service cap still apply at Colony Square? All-day availability doesn’t prove the cap is gone. If the burger is the only reason you’re going, call 404-948-1175 first.

Who is Linton Hopkins? The chef behind the Holeman and Finch burger

Linton Hopkins is an Atlanta-born chef and restaurateur who co-founded Holeman and Finch with his wife Gina Hopkins and won the 2012 James Beard Award for Best Chef: Southeast.

Hopkins graduated from Emory University in anthropology in 1992 and from the Culinary Institute of America in 1993, according to Emory Magazine. He trained in New Orleans, with an externship at Mr. B’s Bistro and time at The Grill Room at the Windsor Court Hotel. He then became chef de cuisine at D.C. Coast in Washington, per a Food GPS interview.

Chef Linton Hopkins, James Beard Best Chef: Southeast 2012 and co-founder of Holeman and Finch, at the opening of Delta’s Sky Club at Atlanta’s Hartsfield-Jackson airport, September 2016 (3600×2400) (Photo: Chris Rank, Rank Studios, for Delta News Hub (Wikimedia Commons, https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=51627374), CC BY 2.0)

Back in Atlanta, he and Gina opened Restaurant Eugene in spring 2004. Holeman and Finch followed in 2008 as its casual counterpart. The James Beard Foundation recognized him in 2012, in a rare tie with Hugh Acheson.

Restaurant Eugene in Atlanta in 2010, the fine-dining restaurant Linton and Gina Hopkins opened in 2004 and closed in August 2019, named after Linton’s grandfather Eugene Holeman (612×1024) (Photo: AVID Vines (Flickr, https://www.flickr.com/photos/75921150@N00/5095030073), CC BY 2.0)

Gina Hopkins, co-founder and co-owner

Gina Hopkins co-founded Holeman and Finch and Restaurant Eugene with Linton, and she remains co-proprietor through Hopkins and Company. Every source I checked credits the restaurants to the couple, not to the chef alone. She belongs at the top of this story, not in a footnote.

The Hopkins and Company restaurants in 2026

Hopkins and Company runs four restaurants in 2026, per hopkins-co.com:

  • Holeman and Finch Public House – Colony Square, Midtown
  • C. Ellet’s Steakhouse – The Battery Atlanta, opened 2017
  • Hop’s Chicken – Ponce City Market, opened 2015
  • H&F Burger – Ponce City Market, opened 2015

On September 17, 2026, Rough Draft Atlanta reported that the couple plans Linton’s Bar & Grill and a reopened The Buttery for spring 2027 at 2970 Peachtree Rd in Buckhead, the former Brio Tuscan Grille space. The Buttery, their bakery-cafe, closed in 2024 after four years in Morningside-Lenox Park. Until the doors open, treat 2027 as an announcement, nothing more.

Hopkins and Company says H&F Burger grew out of the “24 cheeseburgers served every night at 10 p.m.” special. The space was about 1,543 sq ft at opening, per UrbanDaddy. My pick: book Holeman and Finch when you want the burger with oysters, a drink and the room around it; go to Ponce City Market when the burger is the whole plan. Older articles also place H&F Burger at Truist Park, Turner Field or the airport. The group lists none of those in 2026, so I name only Ponce City Market as current.

Why the Holeman and Finch burger is one of the best burgers in Atlanta

The Holeman and Finch burger earned its reputation through scarcity, a strict build and a national ranking: The Daily Meal named it No. 1 of its 101 Best Burgers in America in December 2019.

Scarcity made the legend. The kitchen and the ranking kept it alive:

  1. The legend. Twenty-four burgers, a bullhorn and a 10 p.m. release turned it into a local ritual from 2008, documented by Atlanta Magazine and Food Republic.
  2. The kitchen behind it. It comes from a James Beard–winning chef whose gastropub works on a “whole animal, whole vegetable” philosophy, with house-ground beef, house bread and house condiments.
  3. The national award. The Daily Meal ranked it the best burger in America in 2019, at $17 at the time.

My verdict and how I reached it

My verdict: if you eat one chef’s burger in Atlanta, make it this one. A James Beard–winning kitchen, a No. 1 national ranking and an unchanged, sauce-free build carried across two locations add up to a record few burgers anywhere can match. I am not giving it a score, and that’s deliberate: a number belongs to a tasting, and I haven’t sat down at Colony Square yet. Holeman and Finch burger FAQ

Who owns Holeman and Finch in Atlanta? Gina and Linton Hopkins own Holeman and Finch Public House through their restaurant group, Hopkins and Company. They co-founded it in 2008.

Where is Holeman and Finch now, and did it close? The original Buckhead location at 2277 Peachtree Rd NE closed in 2020. Holeman and Finch reopened on February 9, 2023, at Colony Square, 1201 Peachtree Street NE, Building 400, Midtown Atlanta.

Is the Holeman and Finch burger still limited to 24 a night at 10 p.m.? No. The 10 p.m. release had ended by 2016 at the latest, and the official site now says “Burger Time, All The Time”. It is not confirmed whether a per-service cap, last documented in 2016, still applies.

What is on the Holeman and Finch burger? Two 4 oz griddled patties of pasture-raised chuck and brisket, American cheese, bread-and-butter pickles and red onion on a buttered, toasted house-baked pain de mie bun, with fries and house ketchup and mustard. That is the build documented through 2019.

How much does the Holeman and Finch burger cost? I could not confirm a 2026 price. The Daily Meal listed it at $17 in 2019, and older sources gave $12 to $14. Check the menu on holeman-finch.com before you go.

What is the difference between Holeman and Finch and H&F Burger? Holeman and Finch is a full-service gastropub at Colony Square. H&F Burger is the fast-casual spinoff at Ponce City Market, opened in fall 2015, serving the double cheeseburger and twice-fried fries.

Was the Holeman and Finch burger really named the best burger in America? Yes. The Daily Meal ranked the H&F cheeseburger No. 1 in its “101 Best Burgers in America”, published December 10, 2019.

Who is Linton Hopkins and what awards has he won? Linton Hopkins is an Atlanta-born, CIA-trained chef who co-founded Restaurant Eugene and Holeman and Finch. He won the James Beard Foundation Best Chef: Southeast award in 2012, tied with Hugh Acheson.

Where does the name Holeman and Finch come from? “Holeman” honors Linton’s maternal grandfather, Eugene Holeman, who also gave his name to Restaurant Eugene. I found no documented source for “Finch”.

Do you need a reservation at Holeman and Finch? I could not confirm the current reservation policy. Call 404-948-1175 or email info@holeman-finch.com, especially for Thursday to Saturday evenings.
