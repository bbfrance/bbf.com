De Drie Gezusters Groningen: Burgers and History

De Drie Gezusters Groningen: who has run the Grote Markt grand café since 1972, what its burgers cost (€16.90–€17.90) and where to find a better burger nearby.

By Sofia, Restaurants writer, Italy, Switzerland and the BeneluxPublished November 24, 2026

De Drie Gezusters is a historic grand café at Grote Markt 36-39, on the south side of Groningen’s main square. Go for the building and a drink. The burgers are a short, conventional café menu, not a reason to cross town. On the menu I checked on 2 November 2026, the cheeseburger costs €17.50, the Spicy Chicken Burger €16.90 and the FLFL falafel burger €17.90, all with fries. The café opened in 1972 as an initiative of Koos Huizenga, and Meyer Beheer, the company of Laurens Meyer, has run it since 1 March 2017.

History of De Drie Gezusters: an ownership timeline from 1972 to 2017

De Drie Gezusters opened on 18 November 1972, passed to bar magnate Sjoerd Kooistra in the 1980s, and has been run by Meyer Beheer since 2017. The dates below come from Dutch Wikipedia, the city’s historical marker (HMdb), DutchNews, Faillissementsdossier, Misset Horeca and De CaféKrant.

  • 18 November 1972: the grand café opens, inaugurated by the Groningen writer Belcampo. It is an initiative of Koos Huizenga and his BV Pieter Brueghel Group, according to the historical marker. Dutch Wikipedia also names Wouter Hoogland as co-founder.
  • 1980s (1983 according to the city’s historical marker): hospitality entrepreneur Sjoerd Kooistra takes over, via his BV Plassania per the HMdb marker. He links the neighbouring cafés at the back and uses the name “De Drie Gezusters” for venues in other Dutch cities.
  • June 2010: Kooistra is found dead at his home near Nijmegen. He owned about 85 cafés and bars, mainly in Groningen and Amsterdam (DutchNews).
  • 7 March 2011: Vastgoed Horeca Groningen (VHG) agrees with curator Seerp Gratama (Certa Legal) and Heineken to buy the Groningen properties of the bankrupt Plassania Beheer, for about €40 million (Faillissementsdossier). No source I found confirms whether this exact building was part of that deal.
  • 1 February 2017: the sale of De Drie Gezusters to Meyer Beheer is announced. The sellers and operators are Grietje van der Veen and Henk Wustenveld of Horeca Combinatie Groningen (Misset Horeca, De CaféKrant).
  • 1 March 2017: Meyer Beheer, led by Laurens Meyer, takes over.

Who owns De Drie Gezusters today

Meyer Beheer, the company of Laurens Meyer, has run De Drie Gezusters since 1 March 2017. The deal covered a whole complex of connected venues on the Grote Markt: De Drie Gezusters (Zusjes and Pub), Hoppe, Groote Griet, De Kast, Hotel de Doelen and Upstairs. At the time, Meyer Beheer reported about 1,000 employees and more than 30 years in hospitality (Meyer Horeca Groep). The group’s head office is at Pottenbakkerstraat 30, 4871 EP Etten-Leur.

The south side of the Grote Markt in daylight (September 2015), showing the connected venues Meyer Beheer bought in 2017: Hotel de Doelen, De Drie Gezusters at number 37, the Groote Griet and the 18th-century corner façade of De Drie Gezusters at number 39. (Photo: JoachimKohler-HB, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Grote\_Markt\_Groningen.jpg)

Laurens Meyer called the venue “een iconisch Nederlands merk”, an iconic Dutch brand. Some headlines spell his surname “Meijer”. I use “Meyer”, as Misset Horeca and the company itself do.

Who owns the building itself, as opposed to the business, is not confirmed by any source I found, so I leave it open.

The building: a 13th-century core behind an 18th-century façade

The De Drie Gezusters building has an 18th-century façade on the Grote Markt, and its oldest part, the voorhuis, dates from the 13th century, according to Dutch Wikipedia. VisitGroningen gives the exact year 1225 for the stone building. I have not found a monument record confirming that year, so “13th century” is the safer date.

Myths about the Three Sisters in Groningen

Two stories follow De Drie Gezusters around: that it is the largest pub in Europe, and that it is named after Peter Stuyvesant’s three daughters. Neither holds up.

Is it the largest pub in Europe?

The “largest pub in Europe” title is a reputation, not a measured fact. It comes from tourist and review aggregator sites, and none of them gives a floor area, a capacity or a comparison with other venues. The complex is big, a run of linked bars and venues along the south side of the square. Big is not the same as biggest, and I found no measurement behind the superlative.

Why is it called De Drie Gezusters (The Three Sisters)?

No verified source explains the name De Drie Gezusters, which means “The Three Sisters”. VisitGroningen tells a story linking it to three daughters of Peter Stuyvesant, and presents it as a legend. Historical records give Stuyvesant only sons. Treat it as local folklore.

The Drie Gezusters burger: what’s on the menu and what it costs

De Drie Gezusters serves three burgers across its lunch and dinner menus: a beef cheeseburger at €17.50, a Spicy Chicken Burger at €16.90 and a Falafel Burger from FLFL at €17.90, each with fries. The cheeseburger: Gouda and cheddar with onion compote

The cheeseburger is the one to order at De Drie Gezusters. It is the only beef burger on the menu and the only burger on both the lunch and dinner cards. Gouda next to the cheddar is the one Dutch touch on the whole burger list, and onion compote is a sweeter choice than raw or fried onion. What the menu leaves out matters more: no patty weight, no beef origin, no word on whether it is grilled or smashed. The venue does not name its head chef publicly either.

The FLFL falafel burger: vegetarian, and a Groningen collaboration

The Falafel Burger comes from FLFL, a Groningen street-food brand with shops at Carolieweg 30 and Oude Kijk in ’t Jatstraat 44. It is the meat-free option. The menu labels it vegan, yet it lists feta and yoghurt mayonnaise, which are dairy. Until the venue clears that up, I’d call it vegetarian. Vegans should ask staff to leave off the feta and check the mayo.

How the price compares with the rest of the menu

At €16.90 to €17.90, the burgers are among the cheaper mains at De Drie Gezusters. The biefstuk (steak) costs €27.90, the spareribs and the schnitzel €23.50 each, and the kipsaté (chicken satay) €20.50. Only the house sandwich, the “Ciabatta de drie gezusters” at €16.50 with pan-fried beef tips, gambas, bell pepper and mushrooms, comes in lower. In other words, a burger is the budget way to eat here, not the splurge.

For bigger appetites, the all-you-can-eat “Onbeperkt Tafelen” runs from 17:00 to 21:00. It costs €39.50 per person Sunday to Wednesday and €42.50 Thursday to Saturday.

Beyond the burger: house gin, karaoke and sister venues

De Drie Gezusters has its own gin, made with Mr. Mofongo’s Distillery from juniper, mustard seed and clove, a nod to Groningen’s mustard tradition. The complex also has five private karaoke booths, La Trappe beer tastings, pub quizzes and live sport. The official site lists Sports Bar De Groote Griet, Café Hoppe, De Draaibar, Onbeperkt Tafelen and Boutique Hotel De Doelen as sister venues.

Verdict: is De Drie Gezusters worth it for a burger?

De Drie Gezusters is worth it for a burger if you want to eat in a historic grand café on the Grote Markt, but not if the burger is the point of the trip. The menu is short and clearly priced, and the Gouda-and-cheddar cheeseburger is the best bet on it. Still, a kitchen that publishes nothing about its patty is not competing with Groningen’s burger bars, and VisitGroningen leaves it out of its list of 10 burgers to eat in Groningen.

How I built this profile: from the official menus, opening hours and the sources cited above, all checked on 2 November 2026. It is not a scored tasting, so there is no star rating. My advice: come for the setting, a La Trappe tasting or the house gin, and order the cheeseburger if you get hungry. If the burger is the goal, walk across the square to Smoke.

Burger on the Grote Markt in Groningen: where else to go

For a dedicated burger near the Martinitoren, Smoke at Grote Markt 21A is on the same square. It is one of the ten spots in VisitGroningen’s 10x burgers list. The other nine are Wereldburgers, Dogs Bollocks, Le Smash, FLFL, Pacific, Folkingebrew, O’Malley’s, Lust and Block & Barrels. For the falafel burger without the café markup, go straight to FLFL’s own shops on the Carolieweg or the Oude Kijk in ’t Jatstraat.

FAQ about De Drie Gezusters Groningen

Who owns De Drie Gezusters in Groningen? Meyer Beheer, the company of Laurens Meyer, has run De Drie Gezusters since 1 March 2017. It bought the business from Grietje van der Veen and Henk Wustenveld of Horeca Combinatie Groningen.

When did De Drie Gezusters open and who founded it? It opened on Saturday 18 November 1972, as an initiative of Koos Huizenga and his BV Pieter Brueghel Group. Dutch Wikipedia also names Wouter Hoogland as co-founder.

Is De Drie Gezusters really the largest pub in Europe? There is no evidence for it. The claim is repeated by tourist and aggregator sites, but none gives a measurement or comparison.

Why is it called De Drie Gezusters (The Three Sisters)? No verified source explains the name. The story about Peter Stuyvesant’s three daughters is a local legend: the records give Stuyvesant only sons.

What burgers does De Drie Gezusters serve and how much do they cost? On the menu checked on 2 November 2026: a cheeseburger at €17.50, a Spicy Chicken Burger at €16.90 and the FLFL Falafel Burger at €17.90, all served with fries.

Does De Drie Gezusters have vegan or vegetarian burgers? Yes, the FLFL Falafel Burger is vegetarian. The menu calls it vegan, but it lists feta and yoghurt mayonnaise, so check with staff if you eat vegan.

What are the opening hours of De Drie Gezusters? According to the official site (checked 2 November 2026): Sunday to Wednesday 11:00–01:00, Thursday and Friday 11:00–03:00, Saturday 10:00–03:00.

Can you book a table or group at De Drie Gezusters? The official site does not publish a booking policy that I could confirm. Contact the venue on +31 85 084 1538 or at info@dedriegezusters.nl for tables, groups, karaoke booths or tastings.

Where else can you eat a burger near the Grote Markt in Groningen? Smoke, at Grote Markt 21A, is a dedicated burger venue on the same square. FLFL, Le Smash, Wereldburgers and the other spots in VisitGroningen’s burger list are all in the city.
