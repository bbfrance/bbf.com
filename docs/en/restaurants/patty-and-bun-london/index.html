Patty and Bun London: Where to Eat, Menu, Founder

Patty and Bun London after the 2026 closures: Canary Wharf address and hours, five partner venues, the Ari Gold menu and founder Joe Grossman.

By Emma, Restaurants writer, United KingdomPublished November 24, 2026

Yes, Patty and Bun is still open in London in November 2026, but only just. One standalone restaurant is left, Patty & Bun Canary Wharf at 15 Park Drive, London E14 9GG, and five partner venues across the city serve its burgers. Joe Grossman founded the brand as a pop-up in 2011; Tom Byng, often credited by mistake, founded Byron. On 30 March 2026 Patty & Bun shut four London restaurants, including the original on James Street in Marylebone.

Patty & Bun now runs one restaurant of its own, in Canary Wharf, and its burgers are on the menu at five partner pubs and venues. I checked every address and opening time below against the official Patty & Bun website on 14 November 2026.

Patty and Bun Canary Wharf: address, hours and seating

Patty & Bun Canary Wharf, at 15 Park Drive in Wood Wharf (London E14 9GG), is the brand’s last standalone restaurant. It opened in August 2022, billed at the time as the brand’s 11th site and its biggest (Canary Wharf Group, Hot Dinners). It seats 55 inside and 35 on the terrace.

Who founded Patty and Bun? Joe Grossman, not Tom Byng

Joe Grossman founded Patty & Bun, starting it as a pop-up in 2011 (Restaurant Online). He came to burgers from insurance, a job he told Restaurant Online he “absolutely hated” (Restaurant Online, 2019).

Tom Byng founded Byron, not Patty & Bun

Tom Byng has no link to Patty & Bun. He founded Byron Hamburgers in 2007 (Wikipedia, Hot Dinners). In December 2016 it was announced that he would step down as Byron’s chief executive in early 2017, by which time Byron was an “£80m-turnover business” (Restaurant Online, MCA Insight). The mix-up is easy to make: both men built London burger brands in the same boom, a few years apart.

From pub pop-up to Marylebone: how Patty & Bun started

Patty & Bun began as a pop-up in London pubs, The Endurance in Soho among them, long before it had a room of its own (Restaurant Online, 2019). The first bricks-and-mortar site opened in November 2012 on James Street, Marylebone, with about 30 covers (Time Out London).

A Patty and Bun burger cut open in April 2012, when the brand was still a pop-up cart at the Doodle Bar in Battersea: medium-rare beef patty, pickled red onions, tomato and lettuce in a toasted bun. 1024×683, source: https://www.flickr.com/photos/55935853@N00/7440738898 (Photo: Ewan Munro, CC BY-SA 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/55935853@N00/7440738898)

That small room punched well above its size. By 2019 James Street was described as the chain’s highest-grossing site. I could not pin down the place and date of the very first 2011 pop-up, so I have left them out.

The rise of Patty & Bun: 3 sites to 12 sites plus 5 concessions

Patty & Bun grew slowly, then very fast. Grossman built the first three sites organically. Two investments from New World Private Equity followed, and by July 2019 Patty & Bun ran 12 sites plus 5 concessions (Restaurant Online, 2019).

That same year Grossman launched two spin-offs inside Incipio Group venues: Jefferies for chicken and Smash Patty for stripped-back burgers. One line from that interview reads differently now: “Complacency is death in restaurants. You need to be constantly striving to improve.” Nobody could accuse the 2019 Patty & Bun of complacency. Expansion turned out to be the bigger risk.

Patty and Bun closures: what shut in 2026

Patty & Bun closed four London restaurants on 30 March 2026, then its Brighton site a few months later. The four London sites were (Time Out London, The Caterer):

  • James Street, Marylebone (the original, opened in 2012)
  • Kingly Street, Soho
  • Liverpool Street, City of London
  • Northcote Road, Clapham/Battersea

Patty and Bun closures in 2026: James Street (Marylebone), Kingly Street (Soho), Liverpool Street (City) and Northcote Road (Clapham/Battersea) closed on 30 March 2026, then Ship Street in Brighton around June 2026 (Time Out, The Caterer, FoodBible) (Table: Burger Breton, data from Time Out London, The Caterer and FoodBible (facts, no license required))

The brand’s statement was short: “This isn’t goodbye to Patty&Bun. It’s just the end of this chapter.” The Brighton restaurant on Ship Street followed in June 2026, according to FoodBible; only one source reports it, so I have not given a day. With Brighton gone, Canary Wharf is the last standalone Patty & Bun.

The decline before 2026: a CVA and a restructure

The 2026 closures did not come out of nowhere. About two years earlier, a CVA (company voluntary arrangement) closed two sites. Around March 2025 Grossman completed a restructure in which a new company, led by the existing management team, took over the group’s assets (Restaurant Online). I could not confirm the date of the CVA, which sites it closed, or Grossman’s current job title. The sources show only that he founded the brand and led the 2025 restructure.

The Ari Gold burger and the Patty and Bun menu

The Ari Gold cheeseburger is Patty & Bun’s signature, named after the fast-talking agent in the TV series Entourage. The official menu lists a dry-aged beef patty with cheese, lettuce, pickled onions, ketchup and Smokey P&B Mayo in a bun (Patty & Bun menu).

The names do half the marketing. Soul singers, film titles and TV characters have been punned on from the start, and the Lambshank Redemption is still the best of them. If I had one order at Canary Wharf, it would be the Ari Gold. The ingredient list is short, and the pickled onions and smoky mayo are there to cut through the dry-aged beef. That is a reading of the menu, not a tasting note.

Where Patty & Bun gets its beef

Patty & Bun has worked with the London butcher HG Walter since its early days. Their St Patrick’s collaboration, the ‘Patty O’ Bun’, used aged grass-fed Irish beef (Meat Management). Today’s menu calls the Ari Gold patty dry-aged, and that is as far as the public record goes. I could not confirm whether HG Walter still supplies the beef, where it comes from, how long it is aged or how the patties are cooked.

Patty & Bun in London’s burger scene

Patty & Bun and Byron were two of the brands that defined London’s gourmet burger era. Patty & Bun went from a pub pop-up in 2011 to a dozen sites by 2019, then back down to one restaurant by mid-2026. What survives is telling: one restaurant and five partner venues, most of them pubs. That is close to where Grossman started, cooking his burgers in someone else’s pub. Patty and Bun London: FAQ

Who founded Patty & Bun? Joe Grossman founded Patty & Bun in 2011 as a pop-up, after leaving a job in insurance. Tom Byng is often named by mistake: he founded Byron Hamburgers in 2007.

Is Patty & Bun still open in London in 2026? Yes. Its only standalone restaurant is at 15 Park Drive, Canary Wharf, E14 9GG. Its burgers are also served at Star of Kings, Leyton Star, Star of Liverpool St, Crown and Castle and Swingers (official locations page, checked 14 November 2026).

Which Patty & Bun restaurants have closed? On 30 March 2026 it closed James Street (Marylebone), Kingly Street (Soho), Liverpool Street (City) and Northcote Road (Clapham/Battersea). The Brighton restaurant on Ship Street was reported closed in June 2026.

What are the Patty & Bun Canary Wharf opening hours? Monday to Wednesday 12pm–10pm, Thursday to Saturday 12pm–10:30pm and Sunday 12pm–9:30pm, according to the official page checked on 14 November 2026. The phone number is 020 3951 9715.

What is in the Ari Gold burger, and why is it called that? The Ari Gold is a dry-aged beef patty with cheese, lettuce, pickled onions, ketchup and Smokey P&B Mayo. It is named after Ari Gold, the agent in Entourage.

Does Patty & Bun have vegan burgers? Yes. The menu lists vegan versions of the Ari Gold and the Smokey Robinson, made with Redefine Meat patties.

Is Patty & Bun halal? The official website does not say that Patty & Bun is halal, and I could not confirm it. Ask the restaurant directly before you go.

What is the difference between Patty & Bun and Byron? They are two separate London burger brands with different founders. Tom Byng founded Byron in 2007; Joe Grossman founded Patty & Bun in 2011.
