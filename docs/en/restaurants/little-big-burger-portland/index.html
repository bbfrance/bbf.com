Little Big Burger Portland: History and Locations

Who built Portland's truffle-fry burger chain in 2010, who owns it after the 2024 bankruptcy, and its eight Oregon locations, menu and prices.

By Daniel, Restaurants writer, United StatesPublished November 1, 2026

Little Big Burger is a Portland fast-casual chain founded in the Pearl District in 2010 by Micah Camden and Katie Poppe, known for quarter-pound burgers on brioche, truffle fries and a house ketchup named after Camden. Camden sold it to Chanticleer Holdings for $6.1 million in July 2015 and has had nothing to do with it since. On October 16, 2026, the official site listed eight locations, all in Oregon. The original Pearl District shop is closed.

Still worth knowing about? Yes, with caveats. The truffle fries and the short menu survive. The founder’s restaurant does not: this is now a corporate brand that has been through a bankruptcy, and at around $8.75 for a burger plus $4 for fries it is no longer the cheap counter it was built to be. Below, the man who made it, then the chain as it stands today.

Who is Micah Camden?

Micah Camden is a Portland restaurateur who started in fine dining, then founded Little Big Burger, Blue Star Donuts, Boxer Ramen and the SuperDeluxe drive-thru.

His path there was not a culinary one. According to a 2018 Willamette Week profile, Camden grew up in Gary, Indiana, moved to Salt Lake City at 7 and dropped out of school in ninth grade. Makeup artist, bartender. Then Portland, in 2005.

From fine dining to fast casual

Camden made his name in Portland with ambitious restaurants before he turned to cheap, fast food. Yakuza came first, in 2006, at NE 30th Ave and Killingsworth St. Beast followed in 2007 with chef Naomi Pomeroy, then DOC in 2008.

Then he switched. “Fame versus fortune,” he told Willamette Week. “That’s when I came up with Little Big Burger.” Most restaurateurs dress that choice up. Camden didn’t, and I respect him for it: everything he opened afterward was built for volume, not for critics.

How Little Big Burger started in 2010

Little Big Burger opened in 2010 at 122 NW 10th Ave in the Pearl District as a small, cheap burger counter built on Camden’s Yakuza sliders.

In August 2010, PDX Monthly reported the shop would open in about three weeks, with $3 burgers billed as downsized Yakuza sliders, plus fries, salads, sodas, beer and floats. Camden’s pitch was blunt: “There’s no place around here to get something cheap and fast. It’s just gonna be burgers, fries, salads, and sodas. And beer.”

The idea was burgers “just a bit bigger than a slider” and fries splashed with truffle oil, priced low. Karen Brooks wasn’t sold, writing that Camden was “playing it a little too safe”. Diners were. Branches soon opened on NE Alberta St and SE Division St, and both neighborhoods still have one.

Camden’s catsup

Camden’s catsup is Little Big Burger’s house ketchup, made with tomatoes, honey, onions and chile peppers, and it still carries the founder’s name on the menu.

It is the most personal trace Camden left on the brand. Literally: PDX Monthly reported in 2015 that he has it tattooed on his arm.

Who owns Little Big Burger?

Micah Camden no longer owns Little Big Burger: he and Katie Poppe sold it to Chanticleer Holdings in 2015, and the brand later sat under Amergent Hospitality Group, which filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in July 2024.

Chanticleer, which also owned Hooters franchises, announced the $6.1 million purchase on July 31, 2015. At the time Little Big Burger had eight Oregon locations and about $6 million in annual revenue. Camden to PDX Monthly: “It’s surreal. I’m a bit dazed. I may need therapy.” He and Poppe sold to focus on Blue Star Donuts and their other concepts.

Chanticleer formed Amergent Hospitality Group in 2020, and Amergent is listed as Little Big Burger’s parent. It filed for Chapter 11 in July 2024. I could not find a confirmed buyer or new operator after that filing, so I won’t name one.

Expansion, the Seattle exit and the tip settlement

Under corporate ownership, Little Big Burger grew beyond Oregon, then pulled back, and in 2024 it was ordered to repay withheld tips.

  • Seattle: the chain arrived in 2016. Its Capitol Hill location at 1200 E Pike St closed in November 2022, according to Capitol Hill Seattle Blog.
  • Labor: workers began a union drive in 2019.
  • Tips: in May 2024, the US Department of Labor ordered the company to pay $316,500 after finding that 14 Oregon and Washington locations had withheld tips from workers.

Is the original Pearl District Little Big Burger still open?

No. The original Little Big Burger at 122 NW 10th Ave in the Pearl District is marked closed on Yelp, and it is not on the official list of locations.

The NW 23rd Ave branch (930 NW 23rd Ave) is marked closed too. No news report I found gives the exact closing date of either.

Little Big Burger menu and prices

The Little Big Burger menu is short: quarter-pound beef burgers, a chicken burger, a Beyond Burger, plain or truffle fries, with burgers at about $8.00–$8.75 and fries at about $4.00.

The classic is the Cheeseburger: a 1/4 lb Cascade Natural beef patty with shredded lettuce, red onion, pickles and Camden’s catsup on a fresh-baked brioche bun. In 2010 the promise was a $3 burger. In 2013 the Division menu board still read $3.75 for a cheeseburger. Today you pay more than double that.

Little Big Burger truffle fries

Little Big Burger’s truffle fries are fries tossed in white truffle oil, according to the online menu, and they have been the brand’s signature side since 2010.

Truffle oil, not shaved truffle: the menu is clear about it. In 2010 that was the hook, a fine-dining flourish at a cheap counter. It still leads the About page, next to cooked-to-order burgers and root beer floats.

What Micah Camden did after Little Big Burger

After selling Little Big Burger, Camden kept opening fast-casual concepts in Portland, with mixed results: Blue Star Donuts, Boxer Ramen and SuperDeluxe are the best known.

  • Blue Star Donuts (2012): co-founded with Katie Poppe. Poppe kept it when the two split their portfolio.
  • Boxer Ramen (November 2013, Union Way): filed for Chapter 11 in February 2024. Camden closed all locations after service on April 28, 2024.
  • SuperDeluxe (July 2018): a burger drive-thru in SE Portland opened with Matt Lynch. Wikipedia reports a Chapter 11 filing in February 2024 and that, by 2026, only the original Portland location and Sherwood remained, after Bend closed in March 2026.
  • Closed concepts: Son of a Biscuit, Hop Dog, Fats and Boxer Sushi.

SuperDeluxe is the closest thing Camden has had to a second act in burgers. Whether he still holds a stake in it after the bankruptcy filing, I could not confirm.

Is Little Big Burger worth visiting today?

Little Big Burger is worth a stop if you want a fast, simple quarter-pound burger with truffle fries in Portland, but it is no longer the cheap, founder-run counter that made its name in the Pearl District.

My take: the formula Camden designed is intact. Short menu, brioche bun, a ketchup with his name on it, truffle fries. That is still the reason to go. The value argument is not. A burger and fries come to roughly $12.75 before tax and tip, which puts it in the same bracket as places that grind their own beef. And the context has changed too: the original shop is gone, the parent company went through Chapter 11, and the 2024 tip ruling is now part of the record.

FAQ about Little Big Burger Portland

Who founded Little Big Burger? Micah Camden and Katie Poppe founded Little Big Burger in Portland’s Pearl District in 2010.

Does Micah Camden still own Little Big Burger? No. Camden and Poppe sold Little Big Burger to Chanticleer Holdings for $6.1 million in July 2015, and Camden has not owned or run it since.

Who owns Little Big Burger now? The brand was owned by Chanticleer Holdings from 2015, then by Amergent Hospitality Group, which Chanticleer formed in 2020. Amergent filed for Chapter 11 in July 2024. No new owner or operator has been confirmed.

Is Little Big Burger closing after the Amergent bankruptcy? Not as of October 16, 2026: the official site still lists eight locations in Oregon. Several older locations have closed, including the Pearl District original, NW 23rd Ave and Seattle’s Capitol Hill, so check before you go.

Is the original Pearl District Little Big Burger still open? No. The first location at 122 NW 10th Ave is marked closed on Yelp. Its exact closing date has not been reported.

How much does a burger cost at Little Big Burger? About $8.00 to $8.75 for a burger and $4.00 for fries, based on the online ordering pages in October 2026.

Are Little Big Burger’s truffle fries made with real truffle? The menu says the fries are tossed in white truffle oil. It does not mention fresh or shaved truffle.

What is Camden’s catsup? It is Little Big Burger’s house ketchup, made with tomatoes, honey, onions and chile peppers and named after founder Micah Camden. It comes on the Cheeseburger.

What other restaurants did Micah Camden open? Yakuza, Beast (with Naomi Pomeroy) and DOC in fine dining, then Blue Star Donuts, Boxer Ramen and SuperDeluxe, among others. Boxer Ramen closed in April 2024.
