Hamburgueria da Parada Lisbon: Kiosk Burger Guide

A park-kiosk burger in Campo de Ourique since 2012: the menu, its 2020 prices, the partner behind it and his fried-cuttlefish bar, Choco Frrito.

By Valentina, Restaurants writer, Spain, Portugal and ArgentinaPublished November 25, 2026

Hamburgueria da Parada is a burger kiosk with a terrace in Jardim da Parada, a park in Lisbon’s Campo de Ourique district. It opened in October 2012. In March 2020, the last dated menu I could find, its burgers cost €6.75 to €8.05. In 2017 Time Out Lisboa named Nuno Mourão as one of its partners. He also co-founded Choco Frrito, a bar that fries Setúbal-style cuttlefish and doesn’t sell burgers. Chef Nuno Mendes shares the first name and nothing else: no source links him to either place.

Two Nunos, two different stories. The Nuno behind Hamburgueria da Parada and Choco Frrito is Nuno Mourão, who started Choco Frrito with Francisco Poças. Chef Nuno Mendes, the Lisbon-born chef who made his name in London, runs neither, and I found no source that says he does. If he’s the one you came for, skip to where Nuno Mendes cooks in Lisbon today.

Hamburgueria da Parada in Lisbon at a glance

Hamburgueria da Parada is a neighbourhood burger kiosk in a Campo de Ourique park. It has been open since October 2012 and serves burgers, gin, cocktails and draught beer on an outdoor terrace. Several of the facts below are old, so each one comes with its source and date.

Where to eat a burger in Campo de Ourique: the Parada kiosk

In Campo de Ourique, Hamburgueria da Parada is where you eat a burger outdoors: a kiosk in Jardim da Parada with tables under the trees. NiT dates the opening to October 2012, when it took over the garden’s kiosk, opposite no. 26 Rua 4 de Infantaria.

Time Out Lisboa describes it as a kiosk with a terrace, and the bar matters as much as the grill: gin, cocktails and imperial, the Lisbon word for a small draught beer. In winter the terrace gets heaters and blankets. To me that says it is run as a year-round neighbourhood hangout, not a summer pop-up.

The burger menu: cheeseburger, requeijão and pesto, island cheese

Hamburgueria da Parada keeps a short list of beef burgers that differ mainly in their cheese and sauce. Time Out’s listing names a plain cheeseburger, a requeijão (a soft fresh cheese) with pesto, a version with queijo da ilha from the Azores, and bacon with Dijon mustard.

NiT’s March 2020 menu overlaps with that list and adds a few options, including a falafel wrap for anyone who doesn’t eat meat. On that menu, the bacon burger came with sweet-and-sour sauce instead of Dijon. No source names a signature burger, and none gives the patty weight, the origin of the beef, the bakery or the cooking method. I won’t guess at any of them.

What is queijo da ilha?

Queijo da ilha, or island cheese, is cheese from the Azores, Portugal’s archipelago in the Atlantic. On the 2020 menu it came on a burger with red onion and curry sauce. If I ordered one burger here, it would be this one: it’s the only item on the list you won’t find at a burger bar outside Portugal.

Who is behind Hamburgueria da Parada? Nuno Mourão

The only published name tied to Hamburgueria da Parada is Nuno Mourão, whom Time Out Lisboa called one of its partners in August 2017. That’s one source, and it is more than nine years old. Nothing I found names the founders or confirms Mourão’s role today, so I describe him as a partner as of 2017, not as the founder or the owner.

His other project is better documented. With his childhood friend Francisco Poças, then a surf instructor at the Fy surf school, he started Choco Frrito, a stand built around Setúbal’s best-known dish.

Choco Frrito in Lisbon: fried cuttlefish, not burgers

Choco Frrito is a Lisbon petiscos bar built around choco frito, the fried cuttlefish of Setúbal, and it doesn’t serve burgers. On the 2022 menu at its Alvalade restaurant, the only beef in bread was a prego, Portugal’s steak sandwich.

What is choco frito, and why ‘Frrito’ with two r’s?

Choco frito is strips of battered, fried cuttlefish, the signature dish of Setúbal, a port south of Lisbon. ‘Choco’ means cuttlefish in Portuguese, not chocolate, although partner Miguel Santos told Time Out that plenty of first-time customers expected chocolate. The double r mimics the ‘charroco’ accent of Setúbal, which you still hear in the Troino neighbourhood.

From a beach stand to two Lisbon restaurants

Choco Frrito went from a summer beach stand in 2017 to a 70-seat restaurant in Alvalade by 2022. The dates below come from Time Out Lisboa, NiT and Lisboa Secreta.

  • July 2017: Mourão and Poças open a seasonal stand at Praia da Saúde, Costa da Caparica. They had the idea four years earlier but lacked the time and the money. Fried cuttlefish costs €5.50, or €9.00 for a large portion, plus €3 for a drink and fries.
  • Summer 2019: after two summers on the beach, Poças, Mourão and Miguel Santos open the first permanent venue at Rua da Penha de França 116C.
  • End of 2021: a second, larger Choco Frrito opens at Rua Frei Amador Arrais 16, 1700-220 Lisboa, in Alvalade near Avenida de Roma. Chef Afonso Alves, then 23, writes a longer petiscos menu.
  • January 2022: Lisboa Secreta reports that the Penha de França venue is temporarily closed.

Is Choco Frrito open in 2026?

I could not confirm in November 2026 that Choco Frrito is open, or who owns it. In January 2022 the Alvalade venue opened from 11:00 to 01:00, closed at 15:00 on Sunday and all day Monday (+351 938 069 004). I found no dated 2026 press coverage, and the official site, chocofrrito.com, did not load when I checked on 24 November 2026. Nothing shows that Penha de França reopened after 2022. Poças, Mourão and Santos were the partners from 2019 to 2022, and no recent source says they still are.

Where chef Nuno Mendes cooks in Lisbon today

Chef Nuno Mendes leads the kitchen at Santa Joana in Lisbon, which opened in October 2024 at Rua de Santa Marta 61, inside the Locke de Santa Joana hotel, a 17th-century former convent. At the opening, Time Out Lisboa named Maurício Varela as head chef and quoted Mendes calling the cooking ‘free’ rather than fusion. The 2024 menu had no burger.

Mendes was born in Lisbon in 1973 and made his name in London. Viajante, which he opened there in 2010, earned a Michelin star within its first year, and he later ran Chiltern Firehouse as executive chef. He opened Lisboeta in London in March 2022. In August 2025 Restaurant Online reported that he was leaving it to focus on his projects in Portugal, with chef Leo Carreira taking over the site. In May 2026 he signed a new Santa Joana menu, and the restaurant announced an outdoor space, the Yard, opening on 2 June. No burger there either.

His cooking is worth a trip in its own right. It just has nothing to do with Choco Frrito or Hamburgueria da Parada.

Is Hamburgueria da Parada worth a visit for a burger in Lisbon?

Yes, if you’re already in Campo de Ourique and want a simple burger and a drink on a park terrace. No, if you’re crossing Lisbon for a chef-driven burger. What makes it worth the stop is the setting and the cheese: requeijão and queijo da ilha give a plain kiosk menu a Portuguese accent. At €6.75 to €8.05 in 2020, it was priced as a neighbourhood place, not a destination.

I haven’t rated it. Burger Breton rates only restaurants we have visited and checked against current sources, and the newest dated information on this kiosk is from 2020. For more places to eat burgers in the city, start from our guide to burgers in Lisbon.

FAQ

Where is Hamburgueria da Parada, and what are its opening hours?

It’s in the kiosk of Jardim Teófilo Braga (Jardim da Parada) in Campo de Ourique, opposite no. 26 Rua 4 de Infantaria, 1350-266 Lisboa. Time Out lists it as open daily from 09:00 to 02:00, but the listing is undated. Call +351 960 055 535 before you go.

How much does a burger cost at Hamburgueria da Parada?

In March 2020 burgers cost €6.75 to €8.05 at the Campo de Ourique kiosk, and fries cost €2.50. I couldn’t confirm current prices.

Who owns Choco Frrito in Lisbon, and is it linked to chef Nuno Mendes?

Choco Frrito was founded in 2017 by childhood friends Nuno Mourão and Francisco Poças, and Miguel Santos was a partner by 2019. No source links it to chef Nuno Mendes, and no 2025 or 2026 source confirms who owns it today.

Does Choco Frrito serve burgers?

No. Its menu is built around fried cuttlefish and petiscos. In 2022 the only beef sandwich on it was a prego.

What does ‘choco frito’ mean?

It means fried cuttlefish in Portuguese. It’s the signature dish of Setúbal, and it has nothing to do with chocolate.
