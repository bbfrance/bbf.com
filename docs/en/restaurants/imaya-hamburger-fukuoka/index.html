Imaya Hamburger Fukuoka: The Alien's Burger Truck

Who Katsumi Imazaki is, how his long burger is built, and where, when and for how much you can eat it in 2026, at the truck and at the branches.

By Hana, Restaurants writer, Japan and IndonesiaPublished November 12, 2026

Imaya Hamburger (今屋のハンバーガー) is a food truck in Fukuoka’s Nishi Park run by its founder, Katsumi Imazaki (今崎勝美), the man everyone calls “the alien” (宇宙人, uchūjin). As of 4 November 2026 the truck stands in the park’s East Parking Lot, 3-9-5 Minato, Chuo-ku. It opens at 11:00, shuts when the food runs out and takes days off without warning. What you queue for is a koppepan burger: a handmade beef-pork patty, garlic-fried cabbage, caramelized onions, burger sauce and yellow mustard in a soft oval roll, baked a second time until the crust turns crisp.

The truck reopened on 20 September 2026 after a closure caused by the heat, according to the official site. Imazaki, reported to be 81 this year, still cooks every burger alone, and reported queues run from one to two and a half hours. Can’t get to Nishi Park? Imaya now has branches in Ropponmatsu (Fukuoka), Ebisu (Tokyo), Osaka, Mukonoso (Amagasaki) and Taipei.

My advice: go to the truck if you can. The branches are easier to reach and to plan around. Only Nishi Park has Imazaki himself at the griddle, though, and he is the reason this burger has outlasted almost five decades of chains.

Who founded Imaya Hamburger? Katsumi Imazaki, “the alien”

Katsumi Imazaki founded Imaya Hamburger in the late 1970s, and he still runs it and cooks at the original truck himself. According to the official site, he started out selling hot dogs from a mobile van a friend gave him.

Then McDonald’s and the other chains spread across Japan, and his hot-dog sales fell. Imazaki taught himself to make burgers. Rather than copy the newcomers, he put a hamburger patty in the koppepan he already used for his hot dogs. That improvised answer became the house style.

The official site says the chains arrived “around the same time”. That needs a caveat: McDonald’s Japan opened its first restaurant in Ginza in 1971, so Imazaki’s slump more likely tracks fast food spreading through the decade than a single opening. For how the burger reached Japan in the first place, see our history of the hamburger.

The nickname is part of the show. Taiwanese media report that Imazaki jokingly tells children he is an alien and the truck is his spaceship. Fukuoka press now calls the truck “the spaceship” too. LOG FUKUOKA, Taiwan News and Marie Claire Taiwan spell his name Imazaki; NOWnews and VOIX write Imasaki. I use Imazaki.

Imaya Hamburger timeline

  • Late 1970s: Imazaki starts selling hot dogs from a van a friend gave him, then switches to burgers in koppepan rolls. Sources disagree on the exact year, and the official site only says “late 1970s”.
  • 2 August 2007: Imaya places 3rd nationwide in a selection by the New Hamburger Lovers’ Association (新・ハンバーガー愛好会), published in CarSensor EDGE vol. 46.
  • 2020: Imazaki, reported to be 74, still charges ¥350–500 at the truck. Those prices were reported as unchanged for over 40 years.

  • December 2024: Nishi Park redevelopment moves the truck to the East Parking Lot, for about two years.
  • 1 January 2025: Prices are revised (official news).
  • 17 November 2025: Delivery is added on Rocket Now.
  • 19 May 2026: The first Osaka store opens in Nipponbashi.

What is a koppepan burger, and why is Imaya’s different?

A koppepan burger puts a hamburger patty in a koppepan, a long, soft Japanese roll, instead of a round bun. That is why the official site calls Imaya’s product a “hot dog-style hamburger”. The koppepan dates from Japan’s Taishō era (1912–1926), and the press describes it as a cross between the French coupé loaf and the American hot-dog bun.

What a koppepan is: two soft, oval Japanese koppepan rolls, one cut open to show a thin layer of jam and margarine. These are supermarket rolls by Yamazaki Baking (illustrative photo, not taken at Imaya). Imaya fills the same kind of long roll from Tojin Bakery with garlic-fried cabbage, a beef-pork patty, caramelized onions, sauce and yellow mustard, then bakes it until the crust is crisp. (Photo: Ocdp, CC0 1.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Koppe\_sandwiches\_001.jpg)

Imaya’s version breaks from the standard burger in three places. The roll is long, not round. The greens are cabbage fried with garlic, not raw lettuce. And the assembled burger goes back into the oven, so the crust crisps up. Yellow mustard, and a frankfurter on some items, give away its hot-dog past.

How Imaya builds its burger, step by step

Imaya’s burger is cooked slowly on a griddle and finished in the oven. This is the method as press reports from 2020 to 2026 describe it:

  1. Cook the patty slowly. A thick, handmade beef-pork patty cooks on an iron griddle over low heat.
  2. Split and sear. The patty is split and seared on both sides.
  3. Warm the bread. A koppepan roll is warmed in the oven. A 2020 report names the supplier as the nearby Tojin Bakery (唐人ベーカリー).
  4. Fill it. Garlic-fried cabbage goes in first, then the patty, caramelized onions, burger sauce and yellow mustard. Frankfurter items add a sausage.
  5. Bake to crisp. The filled burger goes back into the oven until the crust is crisp.

Where is Imaya Hamburger in Nishi Park now?

As of 4 November 2026, Imaya’s truck is in the East Parking Lot of Nishi Park, at 3-9-5 Minato, Chuo-ku, Fukuoka 810-0061 (福岡市中央区港3-9-5 西公園 東側駐車場). Redevelopment works in the park pushed it there. When the move was reported in December 2024, it was expected to last about two years.

For decades the truck stood on the Central Observation Plaza (中央展望広場) at the top of the park, listed as 13-13 Nishikoen. Many travel blogs, and the Tabelog and Retty listings, still give that old address; only Retty marks it as relocated. No date has been announced for a return to the plaza, so check the official Instagram, @imayahamburger, before you go.

Older walking directions from Ohorikoen Station lead uphill to the observatory, which is now the wrong place. Put the East Parking Lot address into a map app instead.

Imaya Hamburger opening hours, closing days and queue

The Nishi Park truck opens at 11:00 and closes when it sells out. It also shuts on irregular days, which the official site puts down to “the alien’s whim” (宇宙人の気まぐれ). Closures and opening days are posted on the official Instagram.

Plan on a long wait. One visitor queued about an hour in May 2025, and other reports go up to two and a half hours. The reason is simple: there is one cook, and every burger goes through both the griddle and the oven. Arrive for the 11:00 opening and check Instagram that morning, because a sold-out truck doesn’t wait for latecomers.

Some sources mention a daily cap on the number of burgers. I could not confirm it, so treat “until sold out” as the rule.

Imaya Hamburger menu and prices in 2026

The official site publishes current prices for the Ropponmatsu branch, not for the Nishi Park truck. Prices were revised on 1 January 2025. The only post-revision price reported at the truck is the Mixed Egg at ¥450, from a blogger’s visit in February 2025.

In 2020 the truck charged ¥350 for a Hamburger, ¥400 for a Mixed Burger and ¥500 for a Frankfurter Egg Cheese. Those prices are history. The ¥350 figure still circulates online; don’t budget on it.

What to order: Mixed Egg or Frank Egg Cheese?

The Mixed Egg and the Frank Egg Cheese come up again and again in a decade of visit reports. The Mixed Egg is the only item with a confirmed truck price after the 2025 revision (¥450); at Ropponmatsu it costs ¥650. The Frank Egg Cheese brings back the frankfurter from Imazaki’s hot-dog days. It cost ¥500 at the truck in 2020 and is among the two most expensive items at Ropponmatsu (¥700).

On a first visit, though, I’d order the plain Hamburger. Nothing gets between you and the patty, the garlic cabbage and the mustard, and that combination is what Imazaki built his name on. Save the egg and the frankfurter for the second trip.

Imaya branches: Ropponmatsu, Tokyo, Osaka, Hyogo and Taipei

Imaya’s burger is also sold at five other addresses: a second Fukuoka shop in Ropponmatsu, stores in Tokyo Ebisu, Osaka and Mukonoso (Amagasaki, Hyogo), and an overseas store in Taipei. The official sources don’t say who owns or runs the Japanese branches outside Fukuoka. Taipei is the only one with a named operator.

Ropponmatsu is the official second Fukuoka shop. It has a phone number, fixed hours, an evening service and the only official price list. Go there if the truck is closed or the queue is too long.

Tokyo Ebisu opened with 13 burgers on the menu, according to Time Out Tokyo in May 2023. Its hours date from that year, so confirm it is open before you cross the city. For another Japanese owner-chef story, read our MUNCH’S Burger Shack Tokyo profile. The rest of the city is in our guide to the best burgers in Tokyo.

Osaka uses a bun developed for the city and ground meat delivered daily, and it closes when either runs out. Our Osaka burger guide covers the rest of the city.

Mukonoso is listed on Uber Eats and Autoreserve in Amagasaki. Our nearest city guide is Kobe.

Taipei is run by Eric Tseng (曾裕博), described as Imazaki’s first overseas apprentice. Its 11 items cost NT$129–199, and the signature burger is NT$189.

Imaya and the best burgers in Fukuoka

In my view Imaya is Fukuoka’s most storied independent burger. It started as a one-man truck, placed 3rd nationwide in 2007 and grew into a small network of shops without turning into a chain. Rankings from hamoni, Autoreserve and Fukuoka Now list other names too, and our Fukuoka burger guide gathers them city-wide.

For contrast, look at Japan’s big home-grown chain: the MOS Burger brand profile tells the story of standardized burgers sold nationwide. If you’re taking the Shinkansen from Hakata along western Honshu, MOS Burger in Hiroshima is on the same line. More cities are in the Restaurants section.

Delivery and merchandise

Imaya Hamburger delivers through Uber Eats, Demae-can, Wolt, Menu and, since 17 November 2025, Rocket Now, according to the official site. It also sells official merchandise. Which branch delivers to you depends on your address, so check each app.

FAQ: Imaya Hamburger Fukuoka

Who founded Imaya Hamburger in Fukuoka? Katsumi Imazaki (今崎勝美) founded it in the late 1970s, first as a hot-dog van. He still runs the Nishi Park truck and cooks there alone.

Why is Imazaki called “the alien”? Taiwanese media report that he jokingly tells children he is an alien (宇宙人) and that his truck is his spaceship. The nickname stuck; even the official site uses it.

What are Imaya Hamburger’s opening hours? The Nishi Park truck opens at 11:00 and closes when it sells out, with irregular days off posted on Instagram @imayahamburger. Ropponmatsu is open 11:00–15:00 and 17:00–23:00 and closes on Tuesdays.

How long is the queue at Imaya Hamburger? Reported waits run from about one hour (May 2025) to two and a half hours. Arrive at the 11:00 opening for the best chance before it sells out.

How much does a burger cost at Imaya Hamburger in 2026? At Ropponmatsu, items cost ¥500–700 including tax. The truck’s current prices are not published officially; a blogger reported the Mixed Egg at ¥450 there in February 2025, after the 1 January 2025 price revision.

What is a koppepan burger? It is a hamburger patty served in a koppepan, a soft oval Japanese roll from the Taishō era. Imaya fills it with garlic-fried cabbage, caramelized onions, sauce and yellow mustard, then bakes it until crisp.

Is there an Imaya Hamburger in Tokyo, Osaka or outside Japan? Yes. There are stores in Tokyo Ebisu (opened March 2023), Osaka Nipponbashi (May 2026), Mukonoso in Amagasaki, and Taipei (September 2026), the first overseas store.

Is the Ropponmatsu branch as good as the Nishi Park truck? I haven’t tasted them side by side, so I won’t rank them. What I can say for certain: Imazaki himself cooks at the truck, while Ropponmatsu offers fixed hours, an evening service and an official price list.

Can you pay by card at Imaya Hamburger? The official sources don’t say whether the truck takes cards. Bring cash, or order through a delivery app.
