Best Burgers in Oklahoma City: 10 OKC Spots

The best burgers in Oklahoma City, ranked: thick onion burgers, El Reno fried-onion smashes and modern doubles, with full addresses and sources.

By Daniel, Restaurants writer, United StatesPublished November 20, 2026

The best burgers in Oklahoma City are at Nic’s Grill for the thick half-pound onion burger, Sun Cattle Co. for the El Reno-style fried-onion smash, and Bar Arbolada for the double cheeseburger Alton Brown called “the best double cheeseburger in America.” Tucker’s Onion Burgers, New State Burgers & Spirits and The Garage, the readers’ pick in Best of the 405 2026, complete my top 6. All 10 picks below come with full addresses, the burger to order and the source behind each fact, checked on November 16, 2026.

My bias, up front: in this city the onion is the point. A burger without one can still make my list, but it has to be exceptional at something else. That’s why only 2 onion-free doubles made my top 5.

What is an Oklahoma onion burger, and how is it different from a smash burger?

An Oklahoma onion burger is a beef patty cooked on a flat-top under a heap of thinly sliced onions, so the onions fry in the beef fat and fuse into the meat. A smash burger skips the onions and is pressed thin for crisp, caramelized edges. OKC has both, plus a hybrid: the El Reno-style burger, smashed with the onions.

The style was born in El Reno, about 30 miles (50 km) west of Oklahoma City on Route 66, in the 1920s and 1930s, at Homer and Ross Davis’s Hamburger Inn. As the story goes, frying onions into the beef stretched the meat in hard times. For the longer story, read our history of the hamburger.

If you’ve never had one, try both sides of the family: the thick version at Nic’s, then the smashed 1 at Sun Cattle or Tucker’s. Same beef and onions, 2 very different textures.

How I ranked the best burgers in Oklahoma City

I ranked these 10 from the city’s recognized burger lists, local food press and official listings, then cut every place that has closed. The guide is built from dated sources, not from review visits, so no restaurant here gets a Burger Breton star rating.

The sources are VisitOKC’s “Best Burgers in OKC” (August 4, 2025), The Infatuation’s Sun Cattle Co. review (July 16, 2026), the Best of the 405 2026 readers’ awards, Dave Cathey’s top 10 burgers in the 405 (June 4, 2023), Oklahoma Magazine, News 9, the Oklahoma City Free Press and the official TravelOK listings. I weighed what the critics who ate the burgers said, how deep each spot’s local reputation runs, and how well it represents an OKC style. When those pulled apart, style won. That’s why the onion-burger counters outrank the bigger burger bars.

Sun Cattle Co. (The Infatuation, July 2026) and The Garage (Best of the 405 2026) are confirmed open by 2026 sources, and Nic’s Grill by our own check on November 2, 2026. For the other 7, I rely on their official listings as of November 16, 2026, so call ahead before a special trip. You won’t find prices here: I couldn’t confirm current figures on the official menus, and I’d rather print nothing than a stale number. Charcoal Oven (3604 N May Ave), an institution since 1958, closed on August 31, 2025, according to The Oklahoman via Yahoo News.

The 10 best burgers in Oklahoma City, ranked

1. Nic’s Grill: the most famous onion burger in OKC

Nic’s Grill is the most famous burger restaurant in Oklahoma City. It tops my list because it is the purest example of the thick onion burger: half a pound (about 225 g) of beef with the onions griddled in. Owner Justin “Nic” Nicholas still works the flat-top himself, and the counter appeared on Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives.

Dave Cathey ranked it No. 1 in his 2023 top 10. He called it a “beastly hunk” of beef covered in fried onions, plus jalapeños on request, under a layer of cheese. The room is tiny. Cathey counts 17 seats, while TravelOK and VisitOKC list 9 stools and 3 2-person tables. Expect a line at lunch.

  • Address: 1201 N Pennsylvania Ave, Oklahoma City, OK 73107
  • Phone: +1 405-524-0999
  • Payment: cash only
  • Good to know: Nic’s has no official website or social account, so use its Google Business Profile for directions.
  • Sources: TravelOK, VisitOKC

I covered this counter in depth earlier this month: read my full Nic’s Grill onion burger profile.

2. Sun Cattle Co.: the best onion burger in OKC right now

Sun Cattle Co. serves the best El Reno-style fried-onion burger in Oklahoma City, with beef from its own ranch. The Infatuation called it “Oklahoma City’s best new purveyor of fried-onion burgers” in July 2026, and the only one with a bar. If I had 1 evening in OKC, this is where I’d spend it.

The beef comes from the Sun Cattle ranch in Arcadia, Oklahoma, roughly 25 miles away. The kitchen goes through 6 to 12 50-pound sacks of onions a week, News 9 reported. Mike Rowe called it the “best onion burger I’ve ever had.”

Start with the classic fried onion cheeseburger. The Big Jack swaps the middle bun for potato cakes between 2 onion steaks. The #6 Theta crosses the El Reno onion burger with Norman’s Theta burger and its Split-T sauce, per the Oklahoma City Free Press.

  • Address: 800 W Sheridan Ave, Oklahoma City, OK 73106 (Film Row)
  • Team: owner Russ Johnson (Free Press); chef and COO Brad Ackerman (News 9)
  • Website: suncattle.com
  • Source: The Infatuation

3. Bar Arbolada: the best smash burger in Oklahoma City

For a smash burger without the onion pile, go to Bar Arbolada: a single or double with crisp, caramelized edges. Alton Brown called its double cheeseburger “the best double cheeseburger in America.” That’s a big claim, and it’s the reason this bar outranks 2 onion-burger specialists.

Los Angeles restaurateur Dustin Lancaster runs it. It sits downtown on the edge of Film Row, a short walk from Sun Cattle Co., so you can compare the two styles in 1 evening.

  • Address: 637 W Main St, Oklahoma City, OK 73102
  • Instagram: @bararbolada
  • Sources: TravelOK, VisitOKC

4. Tucker’s Onion Burgers: the local onion burger chain

With 3 addresses in the city, Tucker’s Onion Burgers is the easiest place in Oklahoma City to get a smashed onion burger. It calls itself “Oklahoma’s first premium onion burger restaurant” and uses fresh-ground, natural, hormone-free beef from regional ranchers.

Sean Ray, Heather Paul and Keith Paul (A Good Egg Dining Group) opened the first 1 in Uptown 23rd in 2011, and named it after the Pauls’ English mastiff. VisitOKC notes vegan and vegetarian options, which are rare on this list.

  • Addresses: 324 NW 23rd St (Uptown 23rd); 5740 N Classen Blvd, Suite 3; 15001 N May Ave; plus 1 location in Norman
  • Website: tuckersonionburgers.com
  • Sources: Oklahoma Magazine, TravelOK

5. New State Burgers & Spirits: 5 burgers and a speakeasy

New State Burgers & Spirits in the Plaza District keeps its menu to 5 burgers, and I trust a kitchen that knows when to stop. The signature is 2 thin patties with cheese, dijonnaise and pickles.

It opened in 2019, with a speakeasy next door called Good For A Few. Dave Cathey ranked its cheeseburger, topped with diced pickles, No. 4 in 2023. The Hot Hamburger is a weekend-only special, and there’s a black bean burger for vegetarians.

  • Address: 1705 NW 16th St, Suite A, Oklahoma City, OK 73106 (Plaza District)
  • Phone: 405-724-7524
  • Source: TravelOK

6. The Garage Burgers & Beer: the readers’ pick

The Garage Burgers & Beer won both the Burger and the French Fries categories in the Best of the 405 2026 readers’ vote. 405 Magazine summed it up in 3 words: “Simple, delicious, affordable.”

With 4 Oklahoma City locations, it’s the easy choice for a group or a family meal. It sits sixth, not higher, because a readers’ vote measures popularity more than the burger itself.

  • Addresses: 1117 N Robinson Ave, Oklahoma City, OK 73103; 6900 NW 122nd St; 1024 W I-240 Service Rd; 2900 W Britton Rd
  • Website: eatatthegarage.com
  • Source: 405 Magazine

7. Patty Wagon: from food truck to a former Whataburger

Patty Wagon serves griddled Angus beef burgers and hand-cut fries from a former Whataburger building on N May Ave. Order the Red Dirt Burger: thick bacon, cheddar and Oklahoma barbecue sauce.

Founder Bryce Musick started it as a food truck in 2011 and moved into the building a year later. Musick has since died of brain cancer, News 9 reported.

  • Address: 3600 N May Ave, Oklahoma City, OK 73112 (near NW 36th St)
  • Phone: 405-917-1711
  • Website: thepattywagonok.com
  • Source: Oklahoma Magazine

8. Burger Punk: Doritos on a burger, near the Paseo

Burger Punk’s best seller, The Clash, comes with nacho Doritos. That tells you the tone. It comes from the team behind Empire Slice, Revolución and Gōrō Ramen (84 Hospitality).

It started as a food truck in 2018 and opened its first restaurant in 2020. Sources disagree on what else goes on The Clash, so check the menu board before you order.

  • Addresses: 3012 N Walker Ave, Oklahoma City, OK 73103 (near the Paseo); 915 W Britton Rd, OK 73145
  • Sources: Oklahoma Magazine, TravelOK

9. S&B’s Burger Joint: the Smokin’ Okie and fried pickles

S&B’s Burger Joint is a local mini-chain with 7 locations across the OKC metro, per VisitOKC in 2025. Order the Smokin’ Okie, and add the fried pickles VisitOKC singles out.

  • Locations include: NW 9th St, S Walker Ave, N May Ave and NW Expressway
  • Sources: VisitOKC, TravelOK

10. Paseo Grill: a steakhouse burger in the Paseo Arts District

Paseo Grill makes a steakhouse cheeseburger: a grilled tenderloin patty on a buttered, toasted brioche with cheddar. According to VisitOKC, the patty is ground from filet and ribeye trimmings. It closes my list because it’s a steakhouse burger more than an OKC style, but it’s the one I’d pick for a sit-down dinner in the arts district.

  • Address: 2909 Paseo, Oklahoma City, OK 73103 (Paseo Arts District)
  • Phone: 405-601-1079
  • Sources: VisitOKC, Toast menu

Also worth a stop: R&J Lounge and Spark

2 more places just missed my top 10: The R&J Lounge & Supper Club in Midtown and Spark in Scissortail Park.

  • The R&J Lounge & Supper Club, 320 NW 10th St (Midtown), 405-602-5066, rjsupperclub.com. A 1950s-style supper club whose South West burger comes with fried jalapeños, pepper jack, chili-lime aioli and pico de gallo. It also serves an Umami burger and a lamb burger (405 Magazine).
  • Spark, 300 Oklahoma City Blvd, inside Scissortail Park downtown. It opened October 26, 2021, from The Social Order Dining Collective with chef Kevin Lee. Get the BLC: pimento cheese, bacon, cheddar chips and Prairie Ranch (405 Magazine).

Route 66 side trip: the onion burger in El Reno

If you’re driving Route 66, El Reno, the onion burger’s hometown, is about 30 miles (50 km) west of Oklahoma City. Go for the history. You don’t need the detour for a great onion burger: Nic’s Grill, Sun Cattle Co. and Tucker’s all serve 1 in the city.

Route 66 side trip map: El Reno, birthplace of the onion burger, is about 30 miles west of downtown Oklahoma City along I-40 and old Route 66. (Map: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL, https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)

Sid’s Diner, the El Reno name that turns up on most onion burger lists, is outside Oklahoma City, which is why it isn’t ranked here. See TravelOK’s Oklahoma burger guide and 405 Magazine on the evolution of the onion burger for the road-trip version.

FAQ: best burgers in Oklahoma City

Where can I get the best burger in Oklahoma City?

For the classic local onion burger, go to Nic’s Grill at 1201 N Pennsylvania Ave. For an El Reno-style fried-onion burger with a bar, go to Sun Cattle Co. on Film Row. For a smash burger, go to Bar Arbolada at 637 W Main St.

What is the most famous burger restaurant in Oklahoma City?

Nic’s Grill is the most famous. Its half-pound onion burger was featured on Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives, and Dave Cathey ranked it No. 1 in his 2023 list of the top 10 burgers in the 405.

Which OKC burger joints have been on TV or praised by celebrities?

Nic’s Grill appeared on Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives. Alton Brown called Bar Arbolada’s double “the best double cheeseburger in America,” and Mike Rowe called Sun Cattle Co.’s the “best onion burger I’ve ever had.”

Do I need to go to El Reno for a real onion burger?

No. El Reno, about 30 miles west on Route 66, is where the onion burger was born. But Nic’s Grill, Sun Cattle Co. and Tucker’s Onion Burgers all serve 1 in Oklahoma City.

Does Nic’s Grill take credit cards?

No. Nic’s Grill is cash only. Hours can change, so check its Google Business Profile or call +1 405-524-0999 before you go.

Where can I get a burger near Film Row, downtown or the Plaza District?

On Film Row and downtown, go to Sun Cattle Co. (800 W Sheridan Ave) or Bar Arbolada (637 W Main St). Spark is also downtown, inside Scissortail Park. In the Plaza District, go to New State Burgers & Spirits (1705 NW 16th St).

Are there vegetarian or vegan burgers in Oklahoma City?

Yes. Tucker’s Onion Burgers offers vegan and vegetarian options, according to VisitOKC, and New State Burgers & Spirits has a black bean burger.
