Artichoke Singapore: Bjorn Shen's Pizza, Burgers

Bjorn Shen turned his Singapore restaurant Artichoke from Middle Eastern to pizza in 2025. His burgers, from the M.E.C. Spicy to the Banjir, were one-offs.

By Léo, Restaurants writer, Oceania and Southeast AsiaPublished November 18, 2026

Bjorn Shen founded Artichoke in Singapore in 2010 and still runs it as chef-owner, but Artichoke is not a burger restaurant. For 15 years it was a self-styled ‘new school’ Middle Eastern restaurant. Since 15 August 2025 it has been Artichoke Pizza Parlor, at New Bahru, 46 Kim Yam Road, #01-02, Singapore 239351. A Bjorn Shen burger now belongs to the past: the lamb merguez M.E.C. Spicy on the 2024 menu, and the Triple Cheese Banjir Burger he made with Wildfire Burgers in 2022. Both are below, with what they cost and who reported them.

I have not eaten at Artichoke yet, so this portrait carries no rating. It is built from Artichoke’s and Bjorn Shen’s official sites and from Singapore’s food press: HungryGoWhere, Eatbook, SG Food on Foot, City Nomads, Honeycombers, AsiaOne, Spirited Asia and The Straits Times. Every price comes with the date the outlet printed it. Singapore menus move fast, and Artichoke’s has moved faster than most.

Who is Bjorn Shen?

Bjorn Shen is a Singaporean chef-owner, a judge on MasterChef Singapore and the founder of Artichoke. Local media like to call him ‘Singapore’s most rebellious chef’ and the pioneer of ‘dudestronomy’.

He started at the sink. After national service he washed dishes at The Outsider Cafe at Tanglin Mall in 2004, according to Honeycombers. He left culinary school with only a Certificate 1, then earned a Bachelor’s in Tourism and a Master’s in Business, per Honeycombers and his official site.

Dishwasher first, business graduate second: you can see both in how he runs Artichoke. He opened it in 2010 as a first-time owner. 15 years later he has moved it once and torn up its concept once, rather than let it age politely.

Bjorn Shen’s restaurants today

Bjorn Shen’s official site lists 4 current concepts: Artichoke, Small’s, Nep! in Penang and Baba G’s Pizza Place in Bali.

  • Artichoke (Singapore): billed as a ‘new school pizza parlor’.
  • Small’s (Singapore): a chef’s counter omakase with a handful of seats. His site says 7, Robb Report says 8, and I could not find where it runs today.
  • Nep! (Penang, Malaysia): small plates, cendol and natural wine.
  • Baba G’s Pizza Place (Seminyak, Bali): pizza.

Past ventures listed by Honeycombers include Bird Bird (since closed), Overdoughs and Neh Neh Pop.

The Middle Eastern years: ‘the least-Middle-Eastern Middle Eastern restaurant’

From 2010 to August 2025, Artichoke was a Middle Eastern restaurant that never pretended to be traditional, and Shen himself called it ‘the city’s least-Middle-Eastern Middle Eastern restaurant’.

He meant it. The dishes that made its name were twists, not classics: shawarma made with cuttlefish, lamb turned into crispy floss for weekend brunch, baklava folded into a cheesecake.

If you came here for burgers, that is the part to remember. The same instinct later put a lamb merguez patty, scrambled egg and guava barbecue sauce in 1 bun.

Artichoke Pizza Parlor: the 2025 relaunch and its menu

Artichoke Pizza Parlor is styled on the American-Italian pizzerias of the 1980s and 1990s, and serves 3 formats: ‘Undercrunch’ slabs, stone-baked Rounds and Stacks.

The slabs are the signature. Their ‘Undercrunch’ base is a layer of crispy cheddar under the dough, according to Eatbook and HungryGoWhere. Shen explained the switch to HungryGoWhere this way: ‘It’s like we’ve been playing the same video game for 15 years… now we’re switching things up — same game, new weapon.’

June 2026: the Middle Eastern dishes come back

Since June 2026, Artichoke serves Middle Eastern favourites again alongside its pizzas, so it is now a pizza parlour with a hummus habit.

HungryGoWhere (11 June 2026) and SG Food on Foot (23 June 2026) reported Hummus at S$16, Garlic Bread at S$10 and the OG Date Pudding at S$16. New plates arrived too: Baked Camembert at S$28, an Arti-Caesar at S$24 and a Crab-Onara at S$34. Neither report lists a burger.

Does Artichoke serve a burger? The Bjorn Shen burger story

No current source lists a burger on Artichoke’s menu: Bjorn Shen’s burgers are a 2024 dish and a 2022 collaboration, not a standing offer.

I’ll say it plainly, because the reputation has outlived the menu. Search ‘bjorn shen burger’ and you find 2 burgers worth knowing. You also find a few claims I could not confirm.

The M.E.C. Spicy: a Middle Eastern burger in 2024

The M.E.C. Spicy was a S$28 lamb merguez burger on Artichoke’s New Bahru menu in 2024, and its name was a nod to McDonald’s.

HungryGoWhere described it in July 2024. The build was dense and very Artichoke:

  • Lamb merguez patty
  • Scrambled egg
  • Cheddar
  • Cucumber pickles
  • Toum (Lebanese garlic sauce)
  • Guava barbecue sauce
  • Sesame and sunflower-seed bun

It reads like a breakfast sandwich run through the Levant. If you are looking for a Middle Eastern burger in Singapore, it is still the reference point. But no source confirms it survived the August 2025 pizza relaunch or the June 2026 refresh. Treat it as a 2024 dish until Artichoke says otherwise. My guess: if it ever comes back, it will be as a special, not a fixture.

The Triple Cheese Banjir Burger with Wildfire Burgers (2022)

The Triple Cheese Banjir Burger was a S$18 collaboration between Bjorn Shen and Wildfire Burgers, sold at the Keppel and Bencoolen outlets from November to 18 December 2022.

It took its idea from 3 dishes: prata banjir (prata ‘flooded’ with curry), the Turkish ıslak burger (the ‘wet burger’) and the American cheeseburger. According to AsiaOne and Spirited Asia, it was built like this:

  • Charcoal-grilled USDA Black Angus patty
  • Halloumi and American cheese
  • Sumac onions, tomato and caramelised onion
  • Butter-toasted bun
  • Finished at the table with Artichoke’s red pepper sauce and a cheese sauce

The flood of sauce was the whole point. Shen made a game of it: an Instagram ‘hands-free’ eating challenge, with a bucket hat from Small’s as the prize. Wildfire has not forgotten it either. A Spirited Asia piece of 11 November 2026 on the brand’s 12 years lists the Banjir among its milestones.

Burger claims I left out

Several Bjorn Shen burger stories circulate online, but I could not confirm them with 2 sources, so they are not in this portrait.

A lamb burger at the old Middle Road address shows up only in a user post I could not access. A chicken burger ‘takeover’ at another burger chain appears only in a search snippet. And while 1 profile says Shen opened B Burger at Cathay Cineleisure in 2017, other coverage of that opening does not mention him. If you have a dated menu or press release for any of these, I would like to see it.

How to get to Artichoke at New Bahru

Artichoke is in New Bahru, a creative and food enclave run by The Lo & Behold Group, about a 13–14 minute walk from Fort Canning MRT, Exit A.

New Bahru sits at 46 Kim Yam Road, between the Fort Canning and River Valley side of town and the Tiong Bahru area. Watch the postcode: it is 239351. Eatbook printed 239531, which looks like a typo, so type the official 1 into your map app.

Is Artichoke worth a visit for burger lovers?

Go to Artichoke for Bjorn Shen’s cooking and his crispy-cheddar pizza slabs, not for a burger.

My take, from the record rather than a meal: Shen treats the burger as a playground. Merguez with toum and guava barbecue sauce, halloumi drowned in red pepper sauce: those are a chef’s ideas, not a burger joint’s. But he plays in bursts, and right now the burst is pizza. If you are building a burger itinerary, start with our guide to the best burgers in Singapore and the Honbo smash burger profile, which covers Honbo’s 2023 Singapore opening at CHIJMES. Then book Artichoke for an evening of slabs, hummus and date pudding.

FAQ: Bjorn Shen and Artichoke

Is Artichoke in Singapore still open, and where is it now?

Yes. Artichoke is open as Artichoke Pizza Parlor at New Bahru, 46 Kim Yam Road, #01-02, Singapore 239351. It moved there from Sculpture Square on Middle Road in May 2024.

Is Artichoke still a Middle Eastern restaurant, or only pizza?

It is a pizza parlour first. Artichoke stopped its Middle Eastern menu on 10 August 2025 and reopened as a pizza parlour on 15 August 2025, according to The Straits Times. Since June 2026, Middle Eastern favourites such as hummus (S$16) and the OG Date Pudding (S$16) are back on the menu.

Who is Bjorn Shen and what restaurants does he own?

Bjorn Shen is the Singaporean chef who founded Artichoke in 2010 and a judge on MasterChef Singapore. His official site lists Artichoke, the chef’s counter Small’s, Nep! in Penang and Baba G’s Pizza Place in Seminyak, Bali.

Does Artichoke serve a burger?

Not according to any 2025 or 2026 menu coverage. The M.E.C. Spicy lamb merguez burger (S$28) was on the menu in 2024, and nothing confirms it after the pizza relaunch.

What was the Triple Cheese Banjir Burger?

It was a Bjorn Shen and Wildfire Burgers collaboration sold for S$18 from November to 18 December 2022: a charcoal-grilled Black Angus patty with halloumi and American cheese, flooded at the table with Artichoke’s red pepper sauce and a cheese sauce.

What is ‘dudestronomy’?

‘Dudestronomy’ is the nickname local media give to Bjorn Shen’s style, and he is often called its pioneer. The sources I used do not define it formally, but it fits a chef who calls his own place ‘the least-Middle-Eastern Middle Eastern restaurant’.

How do I get to New Bahru by MRT, and do I need a reservation?

Take the MRT to Fort Canning, leave by Exit A and walk about 13–14 minutes to 46 Kim Yam Road. None of my sources states a reservation policy, so check Artichoke’s official page or Instagram before you go.

How much does a meal at Artichoke cost?

At the September 2025 launch, slabs cost S$22 to S$32 and the fried chicken S$24. The June 2026 additions run from S$10 for garlic bread to S$34 for the Crab-Onara. Prices are ++, so service charge and GST are added.

What is Small’s by Bjorn Shen?

Small’s is Bjorn Shen’s chef’s counter omakase, with only a few seats: 7 according to his site, 8 according to Robb Report. His site lists it as current, but I could not find where it runs today.
