Grill'd Melbourne: Branches, Menu, Prices, Hours

I compared the Grill'd branches in the city where the chain began: addresses, Saturday hours, menu prices in A$, diner ratings and the one I would pick.

By Léo, Restaurants writer, Oceania and Southeast AsiaPublished November 19, 2026

Grill’d has 12 restaurants in central Melbourne and the inner suburbs, according to the chain’s official list, checked on 6 November 2026. If you only try one, make it Grill’d Swanston Street, at 107-109 Swanston St. It opened on 5 February 2026 and is the brand’s biggest city restaurant, with 170 seats in a heritage-listed WWII basement. Grill’d Melbourne Central is the only central branch with a rating I could verify: 4.2/5 on Google from 1,315 reviews. Budget about A$14.50 to A$18.90 for a burger, based on national average prices.

This guide sits in our Melbourne burger city guide and the Burger Breton restaurants section. Melbourne is the chain’s home town, and this guide joins the rest of my Grill’d series: Grill’d Sydney: 25 restaurants, menu and prices, Grill’d Canberra, Grill’d Brisbane, Grill’d Perth and Grill’d Hobart. The chain’s history and key figures are on the Grill’d brand profile.

How I checked this guide

Every address, phone number and service below comes from the official Grill’d restaurant pages and the City of Melbourne’s What’s On listings, checked on 6 November 2026. Ratings come from Wanderlog, which aggregates Google and TripAdvisor scores, because I could not open Google Maps or TripAdvisor directly. Prices come from menuprices.com.au, last updated 9 August 2026. They are national averages, not prices I recorded in Melbourne. This is a desk guide, not a review: I have not eaten at a Melbourne branch for it, so it carries no rating of ours. When 2 sources disagreed, I say so instead of picking 1.

Grill’d Swanston Street: the biggest Grill’d in the city

Grill’d Swanston Street, at 107-109 Swanston St, Melbourne VIC 3000, is the largest city restaurant the brand has ever opened. It opened on Thursday 5 February 2026 and spreads over 2 levels. Upstairs: ordering kiosks, takeaway and outdoor seating. The basement holds 170 seats and the brand’s first private dining room, which takes up to 70 people (Inside Retail, QSR Media).

You can book a table online through Obee. The official page also lists Pick Up, delivery and catering. Phone: +61 3 7071 9090 (official page).

The Dug Out: a WWII club under Swanston Street

The basement of Grill’d Swanston Street, nicknamed The Dug Out, was an Allied Services Club run by the Myer department store during the Second World War. The building is heritage-listed (realcommercial.com.au / Herald Sun, Franchise Executives). That history is the reason I would eat downstairs rather than grab a bag at the kiosk.

Founder Simon Crowe put it this way the day before the opening: “Melbourne is our home and my passion, so opening our biggest restaurant in such an iconic, heritage-listed building is very exciting” (Inside Retail). The site makes commercial sense too. About 12 million pedestrians walk Swanston Street every year (realcommercial.com.au).

The opening came with a nod to the chain’s roots. The first 754 customers got a free burger, a reference to the first restaurant at 754 Glenferrie Rd. Burgers were A$10 on 5 and 6 February 2026.

Swanston Street opening hours: call before a late visit

The weekend closing time at Grill’d Swanston Street is unconfirmed. The City of Melbourne lists Monday to Thursday 10:30 am – 10:30 pm, Friday and Saturday 10:30 am – 11 pm, and Sunday 10:30 am – 10:30 pm. The official Grill’d page showed 10 am – 2 am on Saturday. I would not count on a 2 am burger without phoning +61 3 7071 9090 first (City of Melbourne).

Grill’d Melbourne Central: ratings, hours and how to get there

Grill’d Melbourne Central is on Level 3 of the Melbourne Central centre, at the corner of La Trobe St and Swanston St. It is the only central branch whose ratings I could verify: 4.2/5 on Google from 1,315 reviews and 4.0/5 on TripAdvisor from 118. Wanderlog also ranks it 6th of the 44 best fast food restaurants in Greater Melbourne (Wanderlog).

The official page lists dine-in, Pick Up, delivery, catering and group bookings. It recommends coming by tram or by train to Melbourne Central station rather than by car. Phone: +61 3 9650 0085 (official page).

Wanderlog gives the full week: Sunday 10:30 am – 9:30 pm, Monday to Thursday 10:30 am – 10 pm, and Friday and Saturday 10:30 am – 10:30 pm. Its Saturday hours match the official page.

Which Grill’d to choose in Melbourne

My pick depends on why you are in town. For a first visit or a group, go to Swanston Street. For the laneways, try Degraves Street. For a late Saturday dinner, choose Southern Cross or Carlton.

  • First visit to Melbourne: Swanston Street, for the WWII basement and the bookable tables.
  • Laneway wander: Degraves Street, at 15 Degraves St, sits in the city’s best-known laneway. It offers dine-in, Pick Up, delivery, catering and bookings (official page).
  • A booked table in the CBD: Flinders Lane, at 83 Flinders Ln, takes bookings through Obee and is open 10:30 am – 10 pm on Saturdays (official page).
  • Late dinner: Southern Cross and Carlton both serve until 11 pm on Saturdays. Carlton sits at 350 Lygon St, near the Cinema Nova (official page).
  • Catching a train: Southern Cross is inside the station. Enter from Collins St, ground floor, next to the V/Line ticket office. It lists Pick Up, delivery and catering (official page).
  • Group of up to 70: Swanston Street’s private room, the first in the chain.
  • Shopping break: Melbourne Emporium follows the centre’s hours and closes at 7 pm on Saturdays. Its page lists Pick Up and catering only (official page).

Grill’d Southgate: check before you go

Grill’d Southgate is at Shop G32, Southgate Restaurant Precinct, Southgate Ave, Southbank VIC 3006, on the Yarra next to Hamer Hall. On 6 November 2026 its official page showed “Saturday: Closed”. I could not tell whether that is a temporary closure, a permanent one or a display error. Phone +61 3 9690 1033 before walking over (official page).

Grill’d Hawthorn: where the chain began in 2004

The first Grill’d opened in Hawthorn on 2 March 2004, at 754 Glenferrie Rd, Hawthorn VIC 3122, and it is still trading. The chain’s page calls it “where it all began for us in 2004”. It offers dine-in, takeaway, online ordering, delivery, catering and group bookings, and opens 10 am – 10:30 pm on Saturdays. Phone: +61 3 9819 0004 (official page).

Grill’d Melbourne opening hours

On Saturdays, most central Grill’d restaurants open at 10:30 am and close between 10 pm and 11 pm. Southern Cross and Carlton stay open latest, until 11 pm. Emporium shuts first, at 7 pm. The official pages only display the current day, so Saturday is the only day I could confirm for most branches. Check the restaurant’s page on grilld.com.au for weekday hours, and note that wraps are only served until 5 pm.

The Mighty Melbourne: the burger named after the city

The Mighty Melbourne is the Victorian name of Grill’d’s national burger The Mighty, not a Melbourne-only recipe. It stacks a grass-fed, free-range beef patty, crispy bacon, a free-range egg, tasty cheese, beetroot, cos lettuce, tomato, red onion, relish and herb mayo. Other states sell it under other names, such as the Big Queenslander and the Mighty Crow-Eater (grilld.com.au). Still, if you order one burger in the chain’s home state, I would make it this one. Beetroot and egg make it the most Australian burger on the board, and Melbourne Central reviewers list it among their favourites.

What else is on the Grill’d menu

The Grill’d menu is built around Hi Fibre Lo GI buns, specialty burgers made with 100% Australian lamb and wagyu, and chicken tenders. The official menu also lists the High Performance double-patty range, whose Protein Pro claims 81 g of protein, plus sliders, salads and the Mini Me Pack for kids (grilld.com.au/menu).

  • Healthy Fried Chicken Tenders: 100% RSPCA Certified chicken cooked in extra virgin olive oil, in packs of 3 or 5.
  • Wraps: served until 5 pm only.
  • Lamb burgers: Melbourne Central reviewers single out the Baa Baa Burger and the Lamb Bam Basil.
  • Sides: Famous Grill’d Chips and sweet potato chips, in 2 sizes each.

Gluten-free, vegetarian and vegan burgers in Melbourne

Yes, Grill’d caters for gluten-free, vegetarian and vegan diets in Melbourne. The City of Melbourne lists gluten-free, dairy-free, vegetarian and vegan options at its Grill’d branches (City of Melbourne). Vegetarians can order the Garden Goodness, at about A$14.90, or the plant-based Beyond Garden Goodness, at about A$17.90.

Grill’d Melbourne reviews: what diners say

Grill’d Melbourne Central rates 4.2/5 on Google (1,315 reviews) and 4.0/5 on TripAdvisor (118 reviews), via Wanderlog. Diners praise fresh, tasty burgers, friendly and professional staff and a spacious dining room. The complaints come up again and again: uneven quality, with burnt bacon and burgers that fall apart, tables not always clean, patchy service at peak times, a few claims of overcharging, and chips and onion rings that some find overpriced (Wanderlog). My reading: a branch that does well off-peak and struggles at the lunch rush, so go before noon or after 2 pm.

Grill’d history: the Australian burger chain born in Melbourne

Grill’d is an Australian burger chain founded by Simon Crowe in Hawthorn, Melbourne, on 2 March 2004. It runs 181 restaurants in 2 countries: 180 in Australia and 1 in Bali. Victoria is its home state, with around 74 restaurants across Melbourne, Geelong, Ballarat and Bendigo on the official list (Wikipedia, grilld.com.au).

  • 2004: first restaurant at 754 Glenferrie Rd, Hawthorn.
  • 2010: grass-fed, free-range beef and lamb.
  • 2011: launch of the Local Matters programme.
  • 2016: RSPCA-approved chicken.
  • 2019: first restaurant outside Australia, in Seminyak, Bali.
  • 2021: plant-based Impossible burgers.
  • 2023: first halal-certified restaurant, in Blacktown, Sydney.
  • 2024: first drive-through, at Chadstone, Victoria.
  • 2026: Swanston Street, the biggest city restaurant, opens on 5 February.

Local Matters is the chain’s community programme. Every month, each restaurant gives money to local groups that customers vote for with a token handed out with their order. The group with the most tokens gets A$300 and the others get A$100 (City of Melbourne).

Grill’d Melbourne FAQ

How many Grill’d restaurants are there in Melbourne?

The official list shows 12 Grill’d restaurants in central Melbourne and the inner suburbs, out of around 74 in Victoria, checked on 6 November 2026. 6 of them are in the CBD: Swanston Street, Melbourne Central, Degraves Street, Flinders Lane, Emporium and Southern Cross.

Where was the first Grill’d?

The first Grill’d opened on 2 March 2004 at 754 Glenferrie Rd, Hawthorn VIC 3122, in Melbourne’s east. It is still open, with dine-in, takeaway, delivery and group bookings.

What is the biggest Grill’d in Melbourne, and can you book?

Grill’d Swanston Street, at 107-109 Swanston St, is the brand’s biggest city restaurant, with 170 seats in its basement. Tables can be booked through Obee, and its private room takes up to 70 people.

What is the Mighty Melbourne and how much does it cost?

The Mighty Melbourne is Grill’d’s Victorian name for The Mighty: beef, bacon, egg, tasty cheese, beetroot, cos lettuce, tomato, red onion, relish and herb mayo. It costs about A$17.90, a national average price from August 2026.

How much does a burger cost at Grill’d in Melbourne?

A Grill’d burger costs between about A$14.50 (Simply Grill’d) and A$18.90 (Wagyu Wunder), based on national averages from August 2026. Regular chips add about A$7.50, and delivery prices are higher.

Is Grill’d open late in Melbourne?

On Saturdays, Southern Cross and Carlton serve until 11 pm. The City of Melbourne lists Swanston Street until 11 pm on Fridays and Saturdays. The official page showed 2 am on Saturday, so phone before a late visit.

Does Grill’d have gluten-free, vegetarian or vegan burgers?

Yes. The City of Melbourne lists gluten-free, dairy-free, vegetarian and vegan options at Grill’d. Vegetarian picks include the Garden Goodness and the plant-based Beyond Garden Goodness.

Is Grill’d halal in Melbourne?

No Melbourne branch is documented as halal-certified. The only Grill’d documented as halal-certified is Blacktown, in Sydney, since 2023. The menu also includes pork, RSPCA-approved like its chicken, so ask the restaurant before ordering if halal matters to you.

Does Grill’d deliver in Melbourne?

Yes. Swanston Street, Melbourne Central, Degraves Street, Flinders Lane, Southern Cross and Carlton list delivery on their official pages, and Hawthorn also lists online ordering. Emporium lists Pick Up and catering only.

Is Grill’d actually healthy?

Grill’d builds its healthy-burger claim on Hi Fibre Lo GI buns, grass-fed free-range beef and lamb, RSPCA-approved chicken and tenders cooked in extra virgin olive oil. My take: that makes it better-sourced fast food, not health food. A Mighty Melbourne with bacon and egg, regular chips and a shake is a heavy meal whatever the bun. For a lighter order, look at the salads or the tenders.
