Burger Crush Victoria: Menu, Prices, Locations

How a Cook Street food truck grew into three no-frills smash burger shops in Victoria, Colwood and Vancouver, and what each burger costs in October 2026.

By Olivia, Restaurants writer, Canada and the US NortheastPublished October 23, 2026

Burger Crush Victoria is a smash burger shop at 787 Fort Street, downtown. It serves smashed Canadian Angus beef patties on brioche buns, and a single cheeseburger costs $12.50 CAD before 5% GST, according to burgercrush.ca in October 2026. It started as a food truck on Cook Street. Today there are three shops: downtown Victoria, Colwood on the West Shore and downtown Vancouver. The menu still fits on one board.

Who owns Burger Crush in Victoria?

Burger Crush is co-owned by Scott Renton, who founded the brand with a business partner. Daily Hive named him as co-owner on 27 December 2022. That is close to everything the public record says. No source says who cooks and who runs the business, so I won’t call either founder a chef.

One correction, because it keeps turning up: some online summaries tie a chef named Dustin Alston to Burger Crush. The official site, Daily Hive’s coverage from 2022 to 2024, Sherman’s Food Adventures and Yam Magazine never mention him. I found no source that does.

Renton’s pitch is nostalgia, and he put it plainly. “The current pace of life, with all its endless decisions and choices, had us feeling nostalgic for those times,” he told Daily Hive in December 2022.

The Burger Crush concept: a 1980s no-frills burger shop

Burger Crush sells burgers, fries and shakes, and very little else. On burgercrush.ca, the founders call themselves “kids of the 80s” who want to bring back the neighbourhood burger shop for “stress-free, top-notch fast food”. The motto is three words: “Simple. Honest. Delicious.”

Daily Hive heard an older echo. It compared the shop to “60s burger joints” and called it a “mom-and-pop burger experience”. Wrong decade, same idea. What I like is the restraint: three burgers, one house sauce and two kinds of fries. Plenty of burger menus now run to a dozen builds plus a rotating special, so keeping it this short is a deliberate choice.

From Cook Street food truck to three shops: the Burger Crush timeline

Burger Crush began as a food truck on Cook Street in Victoria in the late 2010s, then opened three storefronts between 2022 and 2024. Daily Hive dates the truck to 2018, other summaries say 2019, and I could not settle the year with a primary source.

When Daily Hive profiled the Fort Street shop on 29 June 2022, the truck was still parked on Cook Street. The piece described Memphis design-inspired interiors, hand-pressed patties and local beers on tap. That was four years ago. The room may have changed since.

Is the Burger Crush food truck still running on Cook Street?

Probably not. HappyCow lists the truck as closed, while the Fort Street storefront has its own active listing. I could not confirm when the truck stopped. For Burger Crush in Victoria, go to Fort Street.

Burger Crush Colwood

Burger Crush Colwood is the West Shore branch, at Unit 116, 1901 Sooke Road, in the Colwood Corner development. It opened in early May 2024 with 50 seats, about 10 km from downtown Victoria. If you live in Langford, View Royal or Metchosin, it saves you the drive into town.

Burger Crush in Vancouver

The Vancouver Burger Crush is at 755 Nelson Street, downtown, in the former Johnny Rockets space, with 40 seats. It was the brand’s first shop off Vancouver Island. For other burgers in the city, see our Vancouver burger guide.

What is the burger like? A smash burger on brioche

Burger Crush serves smash burgers: the official menu describes “smashed” Canadian Angus beef patties on brioche buns. The cheeseburger comes with lettuce, tomato, onion, pickles and Secret Crush Sauce. Sherman’s Food Adventures reports 4 oz patties at the Vancouver shop.

In its June 2025 review of the Vancouver shop, Sherman’s Food Adventures found an “In-N-Out quality” in the burgers, with patties twice the size and a softer bun. That’s the most useful comparison I’ve found: the In-N-Out template, made heavier. What the brand leaves out says just as much. It names no farm, no grind and no bakery for the bun, so “Canadian Angus” is all I can tell you about the beef. For a shop with “Honest” in its motto, I’d like to know more.

Do the maths on a full lunch: a double, Crush Fries and a milkshake come to $34.50 CAD before tax. That is not cheap fast food. Add-ons include fried mushrooms, fried onions, pepperoncini and Canadian cheddar, which Sherman’s Food Adventures listed at $2.00 CAD each at the Vancouver shop in June 2025.

What are Crush Fries?

Crush Fries are French fries topped with fried onions, cheese and Crush Sauce, for $10 CAD. At the Vancouver opening, Daily Hive reported that the fries were cut from Kennebec potatoes and cooked in vegetable oil.

Sauces, dips and shakes

Burger Crush has one house sauce, Secret Crush Sauce, which also comes in a vegan version. The dips are Original, Hot, Sweet and Herb Crush, each available vegan. The sauce recipe is not published.

Shakes come in chocolate, strawberry, vanilla, orange and root beer. The dairy shakes use 100% Canadian dairy; the vegan ones use Nora’s cashew and coconut milk ice cream. When the Vancouver shop opened, the drinks list also had a house Crush Lager, local beer, cider and BC wine.

Vegan burgers at Burger Crush

Burger Crush is a safe bet for vegans in Victoria, BC: the vegan dishes sit on the main menu, not on a separate card. The kitchen makes its own vegan patty and tofu nuggets, and every dip has a vegan version.

  • Vegan Cheeseburger ($13 CAD single, $17 CAD double): the house-made vegan patty with vegan cheese and vegan Crush Sauce.
  • Veggie Style Cheeseburger ($13 CAD single, $17 CAD double): the same vegan patty, but with dairy cheese.
  • Vegan Nuggets ($9.50 CAD): house-made from tofu.
  • Vegan shake ($10 CAD): Nora’s cashew and coconut milk ice cream.

The Veggie Style option is the clever one. It’s for vegetarians who want real melted cheddar, and it’s the kind of detail you only add after listening to customers.

Is Burger Crush the best burger in Victoria?

Burger Crush is a good pick for a fast smash burger in Victoria, but it is not the consensus best. BC Living’s 2026 list of the province’s best burgers names The Pink Bicycle, Bin 4 Burger Lounge, Big Wheel Burger, Ferris’ Burger & Oyster Bar and Bard & Banker, and leaves Burger Crush off.

Big Wheel Burger in Cook Street Village, Victoria, at dusk in September 2026: one of the burger spots on BC Living’s 2026 best burgers list, and a short walk from where the Burger Crush food truck once parked on Cook Street. (Photo: NevinThompson, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Big\_Wheel\_Burger\_in\_Cook\_Street\_Village,\_Victoria,\_BC.jpg)

I think that’s fair, because the two kinds of place are trying to do different things. The Pink Bicycle builds its burgers on Springford Farm Hereford beef and names the farm. Burger Crush sells speed, a short menu and a full vegan range.

My take: go to Burger Crush when you want a burger and a shake without a reservation, or when someone in your group eats vegan. Book The Pink Bicycle when you want a longer meal and beef with a farm’s name on it. Every Victoria spot we cover is on our Victoria, BC burger hub.

How I put this profile together

I built this profile from the Burger Crush website, Daily Hive’s reports from 2022 to 2024, reviews by Sherman’s Food Adventures (2023 and 2025), Yam Magazine (2023) and BC Living’s 2026 list. I checked every address, phone number and price on 16 October 2026. I have not rated Burger Crush, because a rating on Burger Breton needs a visit with tasting notes. Anything reported at an opening, such as the decor or the Crush Lager, is described as historical.

Burger Crush Victoria FAQ

Who owns Burger Crush in Victoria?

Burger Crush is co-owned by Scott Renton, one of the two founders, according to Daily Hive (27 December 2022).

When did Burger Crush start, and was it a food truck?

Yes. Burger Crush started as a food truck on Cook Street in Victoria in the late 2010s. Daily Hive dates it to 2018, while other summaries say 2019. The first storefront opened at 787 Fort Street in 2022.

Where are the Burger Crush locations?

There are three: 787 Fort Street, Victoria; Unit 116, 1901 Sooke Road, Colwood; and 755 Nelson Street, Vancouver.

How much does a burger cost at Burger Crush?

A single cheeseburger costs $12.50 CAD and a double costs $17 CAD, before 5% GST, as listed on burgercrush.ca in October 2026. The vegan and Veggie Style burgers cost $13 CAD single and $17 CAD double.

Is Burger Crush a smash burger?

Yes. The official menu describes smashed Canadian Angus beef patties on brioche buns. Sherman’s Food Adventures reports 4 oz patties.

What is in the Secret Crush Sauce, and is it vegan?

Burger Crush does not publish the recipe for Secret Crush Sauce. The standard sauce is not listed as vegan, but there is a vegan version, and the Original, Hot, Sweet and Herb Crush dips all come in vegan versions too.

Does Burger Crush have vegan or vegetarian options?

Yes. The menu has a Vegan Cheeseburger with a house-made patty, a Veggie Style Cheeseburger (the vegan patty with dairy cheese), house-made tofu nuggets and vegan shakes made with Nora’s cashew and coconut milk ice cream.

What are Crush Fries?

Crush Fries are fries topped with fried onions, cheese and Crush Sauce, for $10 CAD.

Is the Burger Crush food truck still running on Cook Street?

Probably not. HappyCow lists the food truck as closed, though the closing date is not confirmed. In Victoria, go to the Fort Street shop.

What are Burger Crush’s opening hours?

The official contact page does not list hours. Call Fort Street at (250) 882-4598, Colwood at (250) 590-3919 or Vancouver at (604) 416-5169 before you go.
