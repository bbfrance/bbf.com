Cédric Moulot in Strasbourg: From 231 East Street Burgers to 1741

How a hotel-school kid from Lorraine started one of France's first gourmet burger chains, built a Michelin-starred table, then left Strasbourg in 2023.

By AlexPublished October 9, 2026

Cédric Moulot founded 231 East Street, one of France’s early gourmet burger chains, in Strasbourg in July 2011. Eleven months later he opened 1741, a fine-dining restaurant on the quai des Bateliers that won a Michelin star in 2014. Only the first is a burger story. 1741 has never been a burger restaurant, and Moulot no longer has anything to do with it: his group merged into Salpa Restauration (the Burrus family group) in October 2021, and he left both the group and the city in April 2023.

Why 1741 is not a burger restaurant

1741, at 22 quai des Bateliers, is a one-Michelin-star fine-dining restaurant, not a burger joint. The two places get confused because they share a founder and opened less than a year apart: 231 East Street in July 2011, 1741 in June 2012. Otherwise they have little in common. One was a burger counter with meal deals at €7.50. At the other, the cheapest set menu cost €110 in 2024.

Who is Cédric Moulot?

Cédric Moulot is a self-made Strasbourg restaurateur from Lorraine who went from bartender to running a group of restaurants in the city in about 20 years. Wikipedia and his Who’s Who entry give his birth as Thionville, 1978. L’Hôtellerie Restauration’s 2018 profile describes a modest family, with Moulot the eldest of four children. He came to Strasbourg at 14 for hotel school. His first job behind a bar was at La Bourse.

In 2003 he took over Le Tire-Bouchon, a Strasbourg winstub (a traditional Alsatian wine tavern), first under a location-gérance, a French lease-management arrangement, and later as outright owner. That was his first restaurant of his own.

231 East Street: Cédric Moulot’s gourmet burger in Strasbourg

231 East Street was Moulot’s gourmet burger restaurant, opened in July 2011 and turned into a French franchise chain within a year. The idea came from a trip to New York, he told Toute la Franchise: the burgers there were better than anything he could find at home. His timing was good. The gourmet burger wave was just reaching France, and 231 East Street became one of the country’s early “French burger” chains.

The original 231 East Street at place Saint-Étienne

The first 231 East Street stood at 3 place Saint-Étienne, in central Strasbourg. When food critic Gilles Pudlowski reviewed it in June 2012, it opened daily from 11 a.m. to 1 a.m., wore 1960s American diner decor with pop-art touches, and sold meal deals at €7.50 and €9.90. Those details date from 2012. Don’t expect them today.

What made 231 East Street different from fast food

231 East Street cooked burgers fresh and to order instead of serving them pre-assembled. The 2012 write-ups in Pudlowski’s blog and L’Hôtellerie Restauration describe the concept:

  • Artisanal buns, fresh meat and fresh fries.
  • Patties shaped by hand and burgers assembled in front of the customer with onion, tomato, lettuce, ketchup or mayonnaise.
  • Meat cooked the way the customer asked.
  • Sauces and fries made in-house.
  • Just four burger recipes, with no frozen or pre-made products, according to Moulot in his Toute la Franchise interview.

None of the sources I trust name those four burgers, so you won’t find them here.

How fast 231 East Street grew

Within months of opening, 231 East Street was selling hundreds of burgers a day, though the sources disagree on how many. L’Hôtellerie Restauration reported about 300 a day in January 2012. Moulot himself told Toute la Franchise he had aimed for 50 a day, quickly hit 150 to 180, and made €500,000 in revenue in the first year. Oddly, the founder’s figure is the lower one. From one restaurant to a franchise

In early 2012, 231 East Street partnered with the Flam’s group, founded by Éric Senet and Franck Riehm, to franchise the concept, with the development company split 50/50, according to Le Journal des Entreprises. The 2012 terms: a €15,000 entry fee, €3,000 for training and a 5% royalty on revenue. By July 2018 the chain had more than 26 restaurants in France, according to the Observatoire de la Franchise, and that year it opened in Amiens, Biot-Sophia Antipolis and Marseille. One unit was in Bègles, just south of Bordeaux. For where to eat a burger in that area today, see our best burgers in Bordeaux guide.

Moulot later sold his shares in the chain. L’Hôtellerie Restauration reported the sale in April 2018 without giving a date.

Is 231 East Street in Strasbourg still open?

I could not confirm that 231 East Street still operates in Strasbourg in October 2026. PagesJaunes lists a 231 East Street at 19 rue d’Austerlitz, not the original place Saint-Étienne address, as “probably permanently closed”. An Uber Eats page titled “Place Saint-Étienne” still exists, but it shows no status I could rely on. Two listings, two addresses, no clear answer: call before you make the trip.

The wider chain is just as hard to pin down. The last verified count is more than 26 units in July 2018, and I found no reliable source on who owns the brand today.

1741 in Strasbourg: the Michelin-starred side of the story

1741 is a one-Michelin-star restaurant serving modern cuisine at 22 quai des Bateliers. Moulot created it in June 2012 in the former Fleur de Sel, and it won its star in 2014, two years after opening.

The front of 1741, the one-Michelin-star restaurant at 22 quai des Bateliers in Strasbourg, photographed in July 2025. It is fine dining, not a burger restaurant, and has been owned by Salpa Restauration since October 2021. Where the name 1741 comes from

The name is the year the Palais Rohan was completed, according to Gilles Pudlowski’s 2012 opening review. The palace faces the restaurant across the Ill and houses Strasbourg’s Musée des Beaux-Arts (fine arts museum).

Who owns 1741 and who is the chef today?

1741 has belonged to Salpa Restauration, the Burrus family’s group, since October 2021, and Jérémy Page has run the kitchen since at least January 2022. Before him came Thierry Schwartz, the opening chef, then Guillaume Scheer and Fabien Raux. Pudlowski describes Page as English-born and raised in Périgord, with nine years at L’Atelier de Joël Robuchon Saint-Germain in Paris and about five in London.

The Michelin Guide currently lists 1741 with one star in its €€€€ price band, and mentions intimate dining rooms and an open counter facing the kitchen. Gault&Millau rates it 15/20, a “Table Remarquable” (Remarkable Table).

The Michelin star plaque at 1741, 22 quai des Bateliers, photographed in May 2026. The restaurant has held one star since 2014 and is now run by chef Jérémy Page. (Photo: GFreihalter, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons (https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=194478670))

How much does a meal at 1741 cost?

In 2024, set menus at 1741 cost €110, €135 and €155. That was the year it reopened after eight months of renovation, with up to 30 seats and Michaël Wagner as director and sommelier, according to Pudlowski (September 2024). I couldn’t confirm 2026 prices or opening hours, and Michelin’s own pages disagree on the hours. Check the 1741 page on the Michelin Guide before you book.

What happened to Cédric Moulot after 2023?

On 27 April 2023, Moulot announced he was leaving Salpa Restauration and Strasbourg. He told Pokaa and Zepros he wanted to get back to innkeeping and live closer to his family in Lorraine. No source I found names a new restaurant or inn since then, and I won’t guess.

Why Cédric Moulot matters to burgers in Strasbourg

Moulot’s place in Strasbourg’s burger history comes from 231 East Street, not from his Michelin star. In July 2011 he opened a counter that shaped patties by hand and cooked them to order, just as the gourmet burger was reaching France. Within a year he had a franchise partner and a fee schedule. The star at 1741 is the achievement people remember. I think the burger chain was the bolder bet: in a city that eats tarte flambée and choucroute first, he turned a New York idea into a franchise model in under a year. Few restaurateurs have built both a burger chain and a starred restaurant. The closest parallel I know is Ron Blaauw’s, told in our profile of Ron Gastrobar in Amsterdam. How I researched this profile

This profile is built from published, dated sources. I did not eat at either restaurant for this piece. I read French trade and local press and cross-checked each date against at least two sources wherever I could: L’Hôtellerie Restauration (2012, 2018), Le Journal des Entreprises (2012, 2015), Toute la Franchise, Franchise Magazine, the Observatoire de la Franchise (2018), Pokaa and Zepros (2023), Gilles Pudlowski’s blog (2012, 2020, 2022, 2024), the Michelin Guide and Gault&Millau. Anything resting on a single unverified source stayed out, including some awards credited to Moulot and Au Crocodile’s star history. All sources were checked on 9 October 2026.

FAQ

Is 1741 in Strasbourg a burger restaurant?

No. 1741 is a one-Michelin-star fine-dining restaurant at 22 quai des Bateliers. Its founder, Cédric Moulot, separately created the burger concept 231 East Street in 2011.

Who founded 231 East Street, and when?

Cédric Moulot founded 231 East Street in Strasbourg in July 2011, after a trip to New York. In early 2012 he partnered with the Flam’s group to franchise it.

Is 231 East Street in Strasbourg still open?

It’s unconfirmed. In October 2026, PagesJaunes lists a Strasbourg address (19 rue d’Austerlitz) as probably permanently closed, and no listing confirms the original place Saint-Étienne restaurant is open. Call ahead before you go.

Who owns 1741 today, and who is the chef?

Salpa Restauration, the Burrus family’s group, has owned 1741 since October 2021. The head chef is Jérémy Page, who previously worked at L’Atelier de Joël Robuchon in Paris and in London.

Where does the name 1741 come from?

1741 is the year the Palais Rohan was completed. The palace stands across from the restaurant and houses Strasbourg’s Musée des Beaux-Arts.

How much does a meal at 1741 cost?

In 2024, the set menus cost €110, €135 and €155. Check the Michelin Guide or the restaurant for current prices.

What did Cédric Moulot do after leaving Strasbourg in 2023?

In April 2023 he said he planned to return to innkeeping and move closer to his family in Lorraine. No published source names a new restaurant since then.
