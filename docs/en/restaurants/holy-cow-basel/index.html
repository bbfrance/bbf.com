Holy Cow Basel: Address, Opening Hours, Menu Prices and Reviews

Holy Cow Basel at Steinenvorstadt 30: daily hours, every burger price in CHF from the June 2026 menu, ratings and FAQ, checked on 7 October 2026.

By AlexPublished October 7, 2026

Holy Cow Basel is at Steinenvorstadt 30, 4051 Basel. It opens every day at 11:00 and closes at 22:00 from Sunday to Wednesday, 23:00 from Thursday to Saturday. A burger costs 12.40 to 22.40 CHF on its own, or 17.90 to 27.90 CHF as a meal with fries and a drink. Diners rate it 4.6/5 on TripAdvisor and 4.3/5 on Google, according to Wanderlog. I have not eaten at this branch yet, so this page is a fact sheet, not a verdict. I checked every figure in it against the chain’s official sources on 7 October 2026. My reading of those figures: Suisse Garantie beef, seven days a week, on Basel’s busiest burger street, at prices that are fair by Swiss standards.

Holy Cow Basel address and how to get there

Holy Cow Basel sits at Steinenvorstadt 30, 4051 Basel, the only Basel address on the chain’s official restaurant list. The Steinenvorstadt is the pedestrian street that runs from Barfüsserplatz towards Heuwaage, lined with cinemas, bars and, these days, a lot of burger joints.

Holy Cow Basel is on the Steinenvorstadt, Basel’s pedestrian street of cinemas, cafés and burger restaurants between Barfüsserplatz and Heuwaage. This photo is from 2009, before the restaurant opened at no. 30. (Photo: Basmus, CC BY-SA 3.0 (Wikimedia Commons, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Basel\_Steinenvorstadt\_2009-05-31.jpg))

Two tram stops serve the street: Barfüsserplatz and Heuwaage. I could not confirm which lines stop closest to number 30 or how long the walk is, so check the BVB journey planner (bvb.ch) or the SBB app before you set off.

The official Basel page lists no phone number. The +41 21 312 24 04 number you will find on search.ch and TripAdvisor carries a Lausanne area code and is most likely the head office, not the Basel restaurant.

Holy Cow Basel menu and prices in CHF

The Holy Cow Basel menu runs from the Big Beef at 12.40 CHF to two double-beef burgers at 22.40 CHF. Turning any burger into a meal with fries and a soft drink adds 5.50 CHF. All prices below come from the official national menu PDF dated 15 June 2026. The PDF calls them indicative and says they can vary by restaurant and delivery platform. I could not open the Basel listings on Uber Eats or Just Eat to compare.

Cheapest burger per range at Holy Cow Basel (CHF, alone): beef 12.40 (Big Beef), veggie 13.90 (The Veggie), Swiss chicken 15.40 (The Chicken), double beef 22.40. Source: official menu, 15 June 2026. (Chart: Burger Breton, data from Holy Cow! Gourmet Burger Co. official menu PDF (15 June 2026))

The chain says its beef and fries are certified Suisse Garantie, and its buns are made in Switzerland and delivered fresh every morning.

Student and kids’ meals

Students and children get their own prices. With a student card, a Big Beef meal costs 15.90 CHF and a Big Cheese or Veggie meal costs 16.90 CHF, one meal per card. That is 2.00 CHF off the regular meal price in each case. Junior menus cost 7.90 to 12.90 CHF.

Holy Cow Basel reviews: what diners say

Diners rate Holy Cow Basel well: 4.6/5 from 14 TripAdvisor reviews and 4.3/5 from 808 Google reviews, as relayed by Wanderlog. Fourteen reviews is a thin base, so I give more weight to the Google figure. I read it on Wanderlog, not on Google Maps itself.

What reviewers keep praising, in the TripAdvisor and Wanderlog summaries:

  • Fresh burgers (singled out in a March 2025 TripAdvisor review)
  • Friendly, attentive staff and fast service
  • Local ingredients
  • Big portions at a reasonable price for Basel
  • Good, fresh fries
  • Solid vegetarian options

TripAdvisor ranks it no. 215 of 754 restaurants in Basel, in the $$-$$$ price band, under American and fast food. The summaries I read showed no recurring complaint. With only 14 TripAdvisor reviews, that proves little, and I would rather say so than invent a weakness.

Holy Cow history and its arrival in Basel

Holy Cow! is a Swiss gourmet burger chain founded in Lausanne in 2009 by Richard Williams, and Basel is one of its 23 Swiss restaurants. In 2017 a group of local entrepreneurs led by Adrian, the current CEO, bought the company. Since 14 August 2023, Better Burger Society, a vehicle of NoHo Partners and Intera Partners, has owned 76% of it. I tell the founding story in more depth in Holy Cow! in Lausanne, where the chain was born.

Holy Cow! in Lausanne, the city where Richard Williams founded the Swiss gourmet burger chain in 2009, more than ten years before it reached Basel’s Steinenvorstadt. (Photo: Guilhem Vellut, CC BY 2.0 (Wikimedia Commons, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Holy\_Cow!\_@\_Lausanne\_(36743121982).jpg))

The chain grew fast. When it opened its first German restaurant, in Cologne in February 2017, Tageskarte counted 12 Holy Cow restaurants in Switzerland. The official list now shows 23 Swiss locations, including three in Zurich, three in Lausanne, two in Geneva and one in Basel.

On 30 August 2019, bz Basel reported that Holy Cow would take over the former Cindy’s Diner at Steinenvorstadt 30, which had closed in 2011. The paper billed it as the chain’s 15th location and its first in Basel, with opening planned for the first quarter of 2020. I found no primary source for the actual opening date. The honest summary: announced in 2019, opened around 2020.

Holy Cow vs the Steinenvorstadt burger wave

Holy Cow is the only Swiss gourmet burger chain on the Steinenvorstadt, a street bz Basel described in 2019 as hit by a ‘burger wave’. As Holy Cow signed its lease, Hans im Glück was opening at Steinenvorstadt 1. Co-owner Andres Krattinger told the paper the chain looks for high-footfall spots with a good mix of customers, which made the Steinenvorstadt ‘ideal’. In the same piece, Union Diner manager Cyrill Lang said Holy Cow burgers cost about half as much as his. That was 2019, and I have not compared today’s Union Diner menu.

Delivery, Click & Collect and booking

You can get Holy Cow Basel delivered through Uber Eats or Just Eat (eat.ch), or order ahead with Click & Collect. The official page also lists dine-in and a loyalty app. The chain itself notes that delivery prices may differ from the menu prices above.

FAQ: Holy Cow Basel

Where is Holy Cow in Basel and how do I get there by tram?

Holy Cow Basel is at Steinenvorstadt 30, 4051 Basel. The Barfüsserplatz and Heuwaage tram stops serve the Steinenvorstadt. Check bvb.ch or the SBB app for the best line from where you are.

Is Holy Cow Basel open on Sunday?

Yes. It opens 11:00–22:00 on Sunday. Monday to Wednesday is also 11:00–22:00, and Thursday to Saturday is 11:00–23:00, according to the official page checked on 7 October 2026.

How much does a burger cost at Holy Cow Basel?

A burger alone costs 12.40 CHF (Big Beef) to 22.40 CHF (double-beef burgers). A meal with fries and a drink costs 17.90 to 27.90 CHF, based on the official menu of 15 June 2026.

Does Holy Cow Basel have vegetarian or vegan burgers?

It has four vegetarian burgers on a house-made veggie patty, from 13.90 CHF, plus the Veganator salad at 15.90 CHF. No burger is labelled vegan on the menu, so check the official allergen matrix if you eat vegan.

Can I get Holy Cow Basel delivered?

Yes, through Uber Eats and Just Eat (eat.ch). You can also order ahead with Click & Collect.

Is Holy Cow beef Swiss?

Yes. The chain says its beef and fries are certified Suisse Garantie, and the chicken is Swiss too.

Is there a student discount at Holy Cow?

Yes. With a student card, a Big Beef meal costs 15.90 CHF, and a Big Cheese or Veggie meal costs 16.90 CHF, limited to one meal per card.

Holy Cow or Hans im Glück on the Steinenvorstadt?

On TripAdvisor, Holy Cow scores 4.6/5 (14 reviews) against 3.9/5 (71 reviews) for Hans im Glück, but Holy Cow’s score rests on very few reviews. Pick Holy Cow if you want Suisse Garantie beef and Swiss twists such as the Röstigraben.

Do I need to book at Holy Cow Basel?

The official page mentions no table reservations, only dine-in, Click & Collect and delivery. To skip a queue at peak times, Click & Collect is the documented option.
