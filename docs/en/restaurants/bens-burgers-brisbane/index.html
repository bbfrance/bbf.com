Ben's Burgers Brisbane: Menu, Founders, Winn Lane

Who started Fortitude Valley's cult cheeseburger bar, what's on the menu, and what we can and can't confirm about it in November 2026.

By Léo, Restaurants writer, Oceania and Southeast AsiaPublished November 26, 2026

Ben’s Burgers is a small independent cheeseburger bar at 5B Winn Lane, Fortitude Valley, the laneway behind The Zoo in Brisbane. Brothers Ben and Nick Chiu opened it in 2014, and chef Ben Devlin, best known for Esquire, wrote the first menu. The order is the Cheesy, the plain cheeseburger the place built its name on. The Weekend Edition was still calling Ben’s a ‘bona fide star’ of Brisbane burgers in May 2024, and dated customer reviews show it trading through 2025. What I can’t give you is a current price list or reliable opening hours. Neither checked out on 18 November 2026, so ring before you make the trip.

Who founded Ben’s Burgers? The Chiu brothers and chef Ben Devlin

Ben’s Burgers was founded by brothers Ben Chiu and Nick Chiu, with a menu created by chef Ben Devlin. Time Out (2015) and The Weekend Edition both name the Chiu brothers as the founders.

Which Ben is Ben’s Burgers named after?

None of my sources says outright where the name comes from. The obvious candidate is co-founder Ben Chiu. It doesn’t help that the chef who wrote the first menu, Ben Devlin, is also a Ben.

Ben and Nick Chiu: from menswear and records to burgers

Before burgers, the Chiu brothers ran retail in Brisbane. They co-owned the menswear store Apartment and created the record shop A Love Supreme, according to Time Out’s 2015 review. You can see the shopkeeper’s instinct in the concept: a small, curated offer, nothing sprawling.

No recent primary source confirms who owns the business in 2026. So I say ‘founded by’ the Chiu brothers, not ‘owned by’.

Ben Devlin: the Esquire chef who wrote the menu

Ben Devlin created the original Ben’s Burgers menu and was a minor partner in the business, per The Weekend Edition and Broadsheet. He had been head chef at Esquire in Brisbane from its opening. Good Food reports that he helped Esquire reach three chef’s hats, a first for a Brisbane restaurant.

He later spent four years at Paper Daisy in Cabarita Beach. On 11 May 2019, he and his partner Yen Trinh opened Pipit in Pottsville, New South Wales (Good Food, Broadsheet). He was never the day-to-day chef at Ben’s Burgers, and I found nothing confirming whether he is still a partner. Read his role as part of the venue’s history, not its present.

The concept: one thing, done really well

Ben’s Burgers keeps its menu short on purpose. Broadsheet and The Weekend Edition describe it as inspired by ‘the simplicity of the Japanese approach to life – picking one thing and doing it really well’.

That line is why I wanted to write about this place. Brisbane has no shortage of burgers stacked until they topple. Ben’s never went that way: one tight cheeseburger, a couple of variations, chilli fries and a cherry cola made in-house. A decade of local press coverage, from Time Out in 2015 to The Weekend Edition in 2024, suggests the restraint was the right call.

Ben’s Burgers menu: what to order

The core of the menu is the Classic and the Cheesy, plus a vegetarian burger, according to Broadsheet’s listing updated on 26 May 2023. Sources from different years disagree on the details, so each item below carries its source and date.

The current menu as Broadsheet reports it (May 2023)

  • The Classic: grass-fed beef patty, onions, lettuce, tomato and cheese.
  • The Cheesy: the Classic without the salad.
  • The Vegetarian: Swiss cheese and mushroom instead of beef.
  • Add-ons: extra patty, extra cheese, candied bacon, jalapeños.
  • Sides and drinks: chilli fries, house-made cherry cola and ginger ale, tinned beers.
  • Cooking: patties are cooked medium.

What The Weekend Edition’s directory lists

The Weekend Edition’s directory describes the Classic on a soft milk bun with beef, cheese, pickles, lettuce, tomato, onion, ketchup, mustard and mayo. Its Cheesy has beef, cheese, pickles, onion and special sauce. It also lists a crispy chicken burger (southern-style fried chicken, American cheese, cos, pickles, peri peri mayo) and a crispy cheese and mushroom burger. I could not confirm whether the chicken and crispy mushroom burgers are still offered.

Best burger to order at Ben’s Burgers

Order the Cheesy if you came for the cheeseburger the venue is known for. The Weekend Edition’s May 2024 round-up calls Ben’s a ‘bona fide star’ for its ‘no-frills approach to the classic cheeseburger’. It suggests adding maple bacon and getting fries with special sauce. Want salad on it? The Classic is the same burger with lettuce and tomato.

Does Ben’s Burgers have a vegetarian option?

Yes. Broadsheet lists The Vegetarian, with Swiss cheese and mushroom in place of the beef patty. The 2014 opening menu also had a vegetarian burger, the BB Veg with roasted mushroom.

How much does a burger cost at Ben’s Burgers?

I could not confirm current prices at Ben’s Burgers for November 2026. The only verified prices are historical: each burger cost A$11 at opening in 2014 (Concrete Playground), and a burger, fries and beer deal cost A$20 in 2015 (Time Out). Today’s prices will be higher. Check the board when you get there.

Finding Ben’s Burgers in Winn Lane, Fortitude Valley

Ben’s Burgers sits at 5B Winn Lane, a laneway in Fortitude Valley that some directories list as 5 Winn Street, QLD 4006. Time Out’s 2015 review placed it in Winn Lane behind The Zoo, the Valley music venue. If you’re walking in, The Zoo is the landmark to look for.

The venue took over the space that used to house Flamingo Cafe. In 2015 Time Out described AstroTurf and fairy lights in the lane. I couldn’t confirm that the setting looks the same today.

Is Ben’s Burgers still open in 2026?

Ben’s Burgers in Winn Lane was still trading in 2025, based on customer reviews dated January and September 2025 on DoorDash and TripAdvisor. The Weekend Edition listed it in its May 2024 round-up of Brisbane’s best burgers.

Listings for November 2026 don’t agree. Yelp was updated in May 2026, and one directory shows opening hours only on Wednesday, 11am to 9pm, which I’d treat with suspicion for a bar that has fed the Valley for a decade. The official website returned no readable content when I checked on 18 November 2026. Ring (07) 3195 3094 before you go.

The 2014 opening menu

The first menu had three burgers at A$11 each, served on brioche, Concrete Playground reported in September 2014. They were the BB Classic, the BB Special with jalapeños and special sauce, and the BB Veg with roasted mushroom. There were also A$7 breakfast burgers, including the BB Elvis with bacon, peanut butter, banana and maple syrup. A strict ‘no alterations’ policy applied. Today’s add-ons list suggests that rule has since relaxed.

The Bleachers in West End (closed)

The Bleachers was a second, bigger venue at 153 Boundary Street, West End, in the former Sling bar, announced in 2015. Concrete Playground reported it would serve Ben’s burgers plus beef short ribs, chilli corn, a bacon-and-cheese waffle and soft serve, with bleacher seating and a basketball hoop. Broadsheet listed Ben Devlin as head chef there. It is now permanently closed.

The Lefty’s Old Time Music Hall kitchen (ended)

Ben’s Burgers took over the kitchen at Lefty’s Old Time Music Hall, 15 Caxton Street, Petrie Terrace, from a Friday 3 August, The Weekend Edition reported. It was a collaboration between Ben Chiu and Lefty’s owner Jamie Webb, serving the Classic, Cheesy and Veg and bringing back the fried chicken burger. The article doesn’t show the year. Between 2014 and 2019, 3 August fell on a Friday only in 2018, so that’s my date, but it is an inference. Lefty’s closed in November 2019 and reopened in March 2020 under Hallmark Group Australia with its own American menu, so this outpost no longer operates.

Ben’s Burgers or a chain? Other burgers around Brisbane

For a cheeseburger in Fortitude Valley, Ben’s is the independent name the Brisbane food press keeps coming back to, and I’d pick it over a chain for that reason. FAQ: Ben’s Burgers Brisbane

Who owns Ben’s Burgers in Brisbane?

Ben’s Burgers was founded in 2014 by brothers Ben Chiu and Nick Chiu, with chef Ben Devlin as a minor partner at launch. No recent primary source confirms who owns it in 2026.

Who created the Ben’s Burgers menu?

Chef Ben Devlin, formerly head chef of Esquire in Brisbane, created the original menu. He opened Pipit in Pottsville, NSW, in May 2019.

Is there more than one Ben’s Burgers in Brisbane?

As far as current sources show, only the Winn Lane venue in Fortitude Valley still operates. The Bleachers in West End is permanently closed, and the kitchen at Lefty’s Old Time Music Hall in Petrie Terrace ended when Lefty’s reopened under new management in 2020.

Can you book a table at Ben’s Burgers?

No. Broadsheet’s May 2023 listing says Ben’s Burgers takes no bookings, but takeaway is available.

Where is Ben’s Burgers?

At 5B Winn Lane (also listed as 5 Winn Street), Fortitude Valley QLD 4006, in the laneway behind The Zoo.

How much is a burger at Ben’s Burgers?

Current prices are unconfirmed. Burgers cost A$11 each in 2014, and a burger, fries and beer deal cost A$20 in 2015.
