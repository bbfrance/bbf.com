Mision 19 Tijuana: Javier Plascencia's Baja Med Landmark in 2026

Mision 19 Tijuana in 2026: founder Javier Plascencia, chef Juan Carlos Silva Chávez, Michelin Recommended (no star), Baja Med menu and no burger.

By Carmen, Restaurants writer, MexicoPublished October 13, 2026

Mision 19 Tijuana is the Baja Med fine-dining restaurant that Javier Plascencia opened in the Zona Río in January 2011. Juan Carlos Silva Chávez runs the kitchen today. Michelin lists it as Recommended, with no star, and in July 2026 it received its plaque for the third year in a row. It serves no burger. I looked hard. But if you want to know where modern Tijuana cooking comes from, this second-floor dining room is the place to start.

How I researched this profile

I have not eaten at Mision 19 yet, so this is a profile, not a review. It is built from the restaurant’s official site, the Michelin Guide listing, the chef’s own website, Wikipedia, a May 2011 review on Gastronomy Blog, and Baja California press: Zeta Tijuana, Infobaja and Directo al Paladar. I checked every link on October 10, 2026. When sources disagree, I name each one and let you see the gap.

Who is Javier Plascencia?

Javier Plascencia is the Tijuana chef and restaurateur most closely tied to Baja Med cuisine. He grew up in the business. His father is Juan José “Don Tana” Plascencia, and Javier learned the trade in the family’s Giuseppis, Caesar’s and Saverios before opening his first Tijuana restaurant in 1989, according to his official site.

Mision 19 was the eleventh venue of Grupo Plascencia when it opened in 2011. That year he told The New York Times that the restaurant’s mission was to “revitalize the food scene in Tijuana”, and the city with it. He called his cooking “Baja Mediterranean”. In 2017 he published a book, The Soul of Baja.

Javier Plascencia’s other restaurants

Mision 19 sits at the center of a group that has crossed the border more than once. These dates come from his official site and Wikipedia:

Which Plascencia restaurant has a Michelin star?

Animalón is the Javier Plascencia restaurant with a Michelin star. It won 1 star in the first Michelin Guide Mexico in 2024 and kept it on the 2026 list (Cabo Adventures, Directo al Paladar). Mision 19, the older and more famous name, is Recommended, not starred.

The history of Mision 19, from 2011 to today

Mision 19 opened in January 2011 as Plascencia’s statement restaurant for a new Tijuana. A Gastronomy Blog review from May 2011 describes it as about 5 months old. The restaurant is on the 2nd floor of Vía Corporativo, a building described as the first LEED-certified building in northern Mexico, with Bar 20 next door.

Map of Mision 19 Tijuana: Vía Corporativo, 2nd floor, Misión de San Javier 10643, Zona Urbana Río, about 3 miles from the San Ysidro border crossing (Map: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0 (marker from Nominatim: 32.5211237, -117.0078146))

The early menus are the ones that made the press. Wikipedia lists duck with licorice and guava dust, risotto with salt-cured cactus and charred octopus, and short ribs in mission fig syrup. Most English-language coverage stops around 2015 and still treats Plascencia as the chef at the stove every night. That picture is out of date, and plenty of travel pieces still repeat it.

Who is the chef at Mision 19 now?

Juan Carlos Silva Chávez is the current chef at Mision 19. The official site names him as Juan Carlos Silva, and Infobaja named him as chef when the restaurant received its Michelin plaque in July 2026. Plascencia remains the founder. When he stepped back from the kitchen, and whether he still owns a stake, is not on record in any source I found.

What is Baja Med cuisine?

Baja Med is the cooking style of Baja California: Mexican cooking with Mediterranean influences, built on local seafood and produce. Plascencia called his own version “Baja Mediterranean” in 2011, and Mision 19 became one of the restaurants most associated with the style.

Mision 19 puts a number on “local”, but the number depends on who you ask. The chef’s site says 125 miles. The 2011 Gastronomy Blog review said 120 miles. Michelin says 200 km, about 124 miles. Close enough: the kitchen buys from the region around it, from the Pacific coast to Valle de Guadalupe.

Mision 19’s local sourcing radius by source: 125 miles (chef’s site), 120 miles (Gastronomy Blog, 2011), about 124 miles / 200 km (Michelin Guide) (Chart: Burger Breton, data from chefjavierplascencia.mx, Gastronomy Blog (May 2011) and the Michelin Guide)

What to order at Mision 19 today

Order the aguachile. Michelin names 2 signature dishes: aguachile with local rockfish, salsa verde and avocado purée, and lechón prensado with roasted tomatillo salsa and pork jus. On paper, the aguachile is the dish that makes Baja Med make sense: raw Pacific fish, chile, acid, nothing to hide behind. The pressed suckling pig is the slow, rich counterweight.

A Baja-style aguachile de callo (raw scallop) at Conchas de Piedra in Valle de Guadalupe. It shows the raw, chile-and-lime style of Mision 19’s rockfish aguachile but is not Mision 19’s dish (1024×768, April 2017) (Photo: T.Tseng, CC BY 2.0, via Flickr (https://www.flickr.com/photos/68147320@N02/33543436340/))

The official site posts separate PDF menus: general, vegan, kids, coffees and desserts, carajillos and mezcals, mixology, and wines. 7 menus for 1 dining room. Plan a long evening, not a quick lunch between border lines.

Does Mision 19 serve burgers?

No. No source I found shows a burger at Mision 19, now or in the past: not the indexed menu pages, not the Michelin listing, not the 2011 reviews. The menu PDFs themselves would not open for me, so check them before you go. I would not plan a burger night around this place.

Why cover it on a burger site, then? Because you cannot make sense of eating in Tijuana without it, and our city coverage starts with the people who built the scene. When you want a burger, go to the Tijuana city page.

Is Mision 19 in the Michelin Guide?

Yes. Mision 19 is a Michelin Recommended restaurant, with no star. The first Michelin Guide Mexico, announced on May 15, 2024, listed it as Recommended, and no Tijuana restaurant won a star that year (Zeta Tijuana). The 2026 ceremony, held on May 20, 2026 in Jalisco, listed it again among Baja California’s recommended restaurants (Directo al Paladar). In July 2026 it received its plaque for the third year in a row (Infobaja).

Michelin Guide Mexico status, 2024 to 2026: Mision 19 Recommended every year with no star; Animalón 1 Michelin star in 2024, kept in 2026 (Table: Burger Breton, from Zeta Tijuana (May 2024), Directo al Paladar (May 2026), Infobaja (July 13, 2026))

Some local outlets reported a Michelin “star” for Mision 19. They got it wrong. Michelin and Infobaja both list it as Recommended: a plaque, no star.

Is Mision 19 worth visiting in 2026?

Yes, if you want to taste Baja Med where it took shape and you are ready for a top-of-the-range dinner. That buys you 15 years of history, 3 straight Michelin plaques and a newer chef cooking from the region. My advice: go for the aguachile and the story, not for a bargain. No, if you crossed the border for a burger. Start with the Tijuana city page instead.

Check the practical details before you book, because the sources do not agree. Michelin gives Monday to Saturday, 1 pm to 10 pm, closed Sunday, while some listings mention weekend brunch. The phone numbers differ too. Call the official number, +52 664 634 7322, to confirm the hours and current prices in pesos.

Who is the chef at Mision 19 in Tijuana today? Juan Carlos Silva Chávez. The official site names him, and Infobaja named him as chef in July 2026, when the restaurant received its third straight Michelin plaque.

Does Javier Plascencia still cook at Mision 19? He founded it in January 2011, but current sources name Juan Carlos Silva Chávez as the chef. No source says when Plascencia stepped back or whether he still owns part of the restaurant.

Does Mision 19 have a Michelin star? No. It is Michelin Recommended, listed in 2024 and again in 2026, and it received its plaque for the third year in a row in July 2026. It has never had a star.

What is Baja Med cuisine? Baja Med is Baja California’s cooking style: Mexican cooking with Mediterranean influences, built on local seafood and produce. Plascencia called his version “Baja Mediterranean”.

How much does dinner at Mision 19 cost, and is there a tasting menu? Michelin places it at $$$, the third of its 4 price levels. Current prices in pesos and any tasting menu were not confirmed on the official menus, so ask when you book.

Does Mision 19 serve burgers? No burger appears in any source I checked. For burgers, see our Tijuana city page.

Which Javier Plascencia restaurant has a Michelin star? Animalón, in Valle de Guadalupe. It won 1 star in the first Michelin Guide Mexico in 2024 and kept it in 2026.
