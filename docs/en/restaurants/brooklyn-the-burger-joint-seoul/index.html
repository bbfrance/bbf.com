Brooklyn The Burger Joint Seoul: Menu and Prices

How owner-chef Park Hyun built a 14-store American burger chain from 1 diner in Seorae Village: what to order, what it costs in won and where to go.

By Hana, Restaurants writer, Japan and IndonesiaPublished November 24, 2026

Brooklyn The Burger Joint is the Seoul chain to try if you want a handmade American burger rather than a sweet Korean-style 1. Owner-chef Park Hyun opened the first diner in 2010 in Seorae Village, Seocho-gu, and by June 2026 there were 14 stores nationwide. The beef is ground in-store every day; you pick a 140 g or 200 g patty and how it is cooked. The signature Brooklyn Works costs 12,500 won in the listings I checked in November 2026.

Who founded Brooklyn The Burger Joint? Park Hyun, owner-chef and CEO

Park Hyun founded Brooklyn The Burger Joint and still runs it as CEO. The Korea Herald described him in July 2015 as the brand’s 35-year-old owner-chef, and the 2020 coverage of the 10th anniversary quoted him as CEO (Korea Herald, Wikitree).

He did the research before writing the recipes. According to the anniversary coverage, the brand studied about 100 burger restaurants across the United States and drew on them for its taste, its decor and even its menu names (Sisun News). It shows. The menu reads like a New York diner’s, not a Korean take on one.

I could not confirm anything about Park Hyun’s life before Brooklyn. No primary source gives his nationality, his birthplace or any career in the US, so you will not find them here.

From 1 Seorae Village diner to 14 stores

Brooklyn The Burger Joint grew from 1 restaurant in 2010 to 14 stores in 2026, adding large-format and mall locations along the way.

  • 2010: Park Hyun opens the first restaurant in Seorae Village, Seocho-gu.
  • June 2015: the fourth outlet opens on Garosu-gil (540-11 Sinsa-dong, Gangnam-gu) as a 24/7 diner. “I wanted to do it somewhere big and I wanted to do a 24-hour spot,” Park told The Korea Herald.
  • July 2020: 9 stores nationwide, including 1 in Busan. The chain uses US beef and bacon and runs a 10th-anniversary promotion with the U.S. Meat Export Federation until 1 August 2020.
  • June 2026: 14 stores nationwide. On 13 June the chain releases a limited Steak Burger Set (1,000 units, 20,300 won) developed with teppanyaki chef Lee Hee-jun, who appeared at the Olympic Park branch (Sports Kyunghyang).

How Brooklyn makes its burgers: fresh-ground beef, 2 sizes, your doneness

Brooklyn grinds its beef in the restaurant every day, then lets you choose a 140 g or 200 g patty and how it is cooked. Sports Kyunghyang confirmed both points in June 2026.

Time Out Seoul described the same system back in 2015: “100% freshly minced beef every day”, with the 140 g patty smashed and the 200 g patty formed by hand (Time Out Seoul). The small one gets a smash-burger crust. The big 1 stays thick enough to keep a pink center. My advice: if you plan to ask for medium, take the 200 g, because a doneness choice means little on a thin smashed patty.

Brooklyn The Burger Joint’s 2 patty options: 140 g (about 5 oz), smashed thin on the griddle for a crust; 200 g (about 7 oz), formed by hand and thick enough for a pink center. Both use beef ground in the restaurant every day. (Table: Burger Breton, data from Time Out Seoul (2015) and Sports Kyunghyang (2026), CC0)

1 branch gave more detail in 2017. Gilbert Kim, who owned a Brooklyn branch in Banpo-dong, told Groove Korea that his patties blended chuck eye roll, brisket and short rib, ground every morning, and that the bacon went between the cheese and the patty (Stars and Stripes Korea). Read it as 1 branch owner’s account from 2017, not the chain’s current recipe. Kim is not the founder, and despite the name, nothing in the sources ties him to Gilbert Burger, a separate Seoul restaurant.

The directories show no date and I found no official online menu, so expect some variation between branches. If you want dollars, convert at the day’s rate.

Prices have climbed. Time Out listed burgers “from 7,500 won” in 2015, and The Korea Herald gave 7,800–12,800 won for burgers and 5,500–9,500 won for milkshakes at Garosu-gil the same year. The entry price is up about 30% in 11 years. If an English guide quotes 7,500 won, it is quoting 2015.

What to order at Brooklyn The Burger Joint: Brooklyn Works first

Order the Brooklyn Works on a first visit: it is the signature burger, with American cheese, bacon, lettuce, onion, tomato, pickles and house sauce, for 12,500 won.

For the plainest test of the beef, The Cheeseburger at 9,800 won is the one. The Lamb Burger (14,300 won) is the outlier, and it has been on the menu since at least 2015. Fries are where I would hesitate: 8,800 won, almost the price of The Cheeseburger itself. The chili cheese fries (14,700 won) make more sense split between 2. Shakes run 7,800–8,900 won; the Garosu-gil diner launched in 2015 with 43 shake and ice-cream variations, so they are no afterthought here.

Older reviews mention items I could not find on the 2026 menu. Ask in the restaurant before you plan your order around them:

  • C.R.E.A.M. (“Cheddar Rules Everything Around Meat”), a cheddar burger.
  • Big Fish, a fish-fillet burger.
  • Cheese Skirt, with grilled cheese flaring out of the bun.
  • Napkin Please and New McCoy.
  • Fantastic 4, 4 60 g patties, a Garosu-gil exclusive in 2015.

Brooklyn Burger Seorae Village: the original in Banpo-dong

The original Brooklyn The Burger Joint is in Seorae Village, Banpo-dong, Seocho-gu, phone 02-533-7180, but sources disagree on its exact street address.

VisitKorea and Time Out (2015) give 51-13 Donggwang-ro 43-gil, Seocho-gu. Polle gives 27 Seorae-ro 2-gil, Seocho-gu. Both list the same phone number, so this may be a move, or a road-name versus building-number difference. Search “브루클린 더 버거 조인트 서래마을” on Naver Map or Kakao Map before you go.

Hours vary by source too. VisitKorea lists 11:30–22:00, closed every Monday; Time Out gave Tuesday to Sunday, 11:30–21:30, in 2015. Plan around a Monday closure and check on the day. VisitKorea notes take-out, and Polle notes Coupang Eats delivery and no parking.

Garosu-gil: the 24-hour diner of 2015

The Garosu-gil branch opened in June 2015 as a 24/7 retro diner. Whether it still runs around the clock in 2026 is not confirmed.

In 2015 and 2016 it had a 1950s American interior with mint-green booths, burgers in 5 oz or 7 oz sizes and 43 milkshake and ice-cream variations, from Nutella to Burnt Marshmallow and a Pepsi Vodka Float (Time Out Seoul, Korea Herald). It also housed a FRZ ice-cream shop-in-shop, and general director Cho Whi-sung spoke for the brand at the launch. All of this is historical: call ahead before a late-night trip.

Where Brooklyn fits among the best burgers in Seoul

Brooklyn The Burger Joint belongs to the first wave of American-style burgers in Seoul and is still a reference name for a classic cheeseburger. Gilbert Burger, in Seocho-dong since 2008, came 2 years earlier.

In 2022 the Korea JoongAng Daily grouped Brooklyn with Nostress Burger, Patty and Veggies and Fault Burger in Seoul’s “classic American cheeseburger” trend, describing it as a retro American diner born in Seorae Village (Korea JoongAng Daily). Is Brooklyn The Burger Joint worth it?

Yes, if you want a US-style burger in Seoul: beef ground daily, a choice of doneness and diner sides are hard to find together in the city’s fast-food chains. The catch is price. A Brooklyn Works with fries and a vanilla shake, ordered separately, comes to 29,100 won.

My pick would be the original in Seorae Village, any day but Monday, with a 200 g Brooklyn Works cooked medium. That is where the chain started, and the thick patty is the one that makes the doneness choice worth having.

This is a sourced profile, not a rated visit: I did not eat there for this article, so it carries no score. Every fact comes from the press and directories cited below, checked on 12 November 2026. Where sources disagree, as on the original’s address and hours, I give both.

FAQ: Brooklyn The Burger Joint Seoul

Who owns Brooklyn The Burger Joint in Seoul?

Park Hyun (박현) founded it in 2010 in Seorae Village and is its CEO. The Korea Herald called him the owner-chef in 2015.

Where is the original Brooklyn The Burger Joint?

In Seorae Village, Banpo-dong, Seocho-gu, phone 02-533-7180. Sources list both 51-13 Donggwang-ro 43-gil and 27 Seorae-ro 2-gil, so check Naver Map or Kakao Map before you go.

How much does a burger cost at Brooklyn The Burger Joint?

The Cheeseburger is 9,800 won and the Brooklyn Works 12,500 won in listings checked in November 2026. The Brooklyn Works set costs 20,300 won. Prices can vary by branch.

Can you order your burger medium-rare?

Yes. You choose the doneness and the patty size, 140 g or 200 g. The 200 g hand-formed patty is the one thick enough to stay pink in the middle.

Is the Garosu-gil branch still open 24 hours?

It opened as a 24/7 diner in June 2015, but its current hours are not confirmed. Call before a late-night visit.

How many branches are there, and is there 1 near Gangnam, Jamsil or Gwanghwamun?

There were 14 stores in June 2026. Gangnam has Garosu-gil and Samseong, Jamsil has Lotte World Mall, and there is a branch in Gwanghwamun D Tower.

Is it better than Gilbert Burger or the newer smash-burger shops?

I cannot rank them without eating at each, and I have not. What Brooklyn offers that most smash-burger shops do not is a choice: a 140 g smashed patty or a 200 g hand-formed 1 cooked to order. Gilbert Burger (2008) is 2 years older and a separate business.

Is it closed on Mondays? Does it take reservations or deliver?

VisitKorea lists the original as closed every Monday, while the Samseong branch is listed as open every day. The original offers take-out and Coupang Eats delivery. I found no information on reservations.
