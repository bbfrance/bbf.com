Hans im Glück Stuttgart: Locations, Prices, Hours

All four Hans im Glück branches in Stuttgart compared: addresses, Sunday hours, 2026 burger prices from €8.50 and review scores. Which one to pick.

By Alex, Restaurants editorPublished October 23, 2026

Hans im Glück has four burger restaurants in Stuttgart: Europaviertel at Lissabonner Straße 2, Milaneo at Mailänder Platz 7, Marienstraße at Marienstraße 3B and Caleido at Tübinger Straße 41-43. Every branch opens at 11:30 am, except Milaneo on Sundays, which opens at noon. On the February 2026 Stuttgart menu, a burger costs €11.50 to €17.90. Coming by train? Milaneo and Europaviertel are the closest, about a 10-minute walk from Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof.

On 20 October 2026 I checked every address, phone number and opening time on the chain’s official pages, then lined the four branches up side by side. My short answer: between trains, pick Europaviertel. It is as close to the station as Milaneo, keeps the kitchen open an hour longer on Sundays and does not depend on a shopping mall’s opening times. For the wider picture, see our guide on where to eat burgers in Stuttgart.

Which Hans im Glück in Stuttgart should you choose?

Near the main station, go to Europaviertel, with Milaneo as the fallback. Marienstraße is the one for a large group in the city centre, and Caleido suits an evening in Stuttgart-Süd. The menu is the same in all four branches, so the choice comes down to location, Sunday hours and the room.

Burger near Stuttgart main station: Milaneo and Europaviertel

Milaneo and Europaviertel are both about a 10-minute walk from Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof, and the Stadtbibliothek Stadtbahn stop is about 3 minutes from either. Europaviertel was the chain’s first Stuttgart restaurant. According to the official page, it stands on the former site of a freight and marshalling yard. Milaneo is inside the Milaneo shopping centre, and golocal reviewers call its wood-and-jungle decor a “stylisher Burger-Tempel”, a stylish burger temple. Both are in the same neighbourhood, but I have not checked the exact walking distance between them or how their dining rooms compare. My pick is still Europaviertel. A train schedule leaves no room for a mall that may be shut.

Marienstraße: the flagship in a historic building

Marienstraße is the biggest of the four. It opened on 1 September 2014 with 300 seats inside and 60 on the terrace. According to stuttgart-informationen.de, it moved into the former Ketterer hotel and brewery building, whose history goes back to 1929, after several months of renovation. It sits right next to the Das Gerber shopping centre. With that many seats, it is the branch I would call first for a table of ten. One catch: on Sundays it closes at 10 pm, an hour before Europaviertel and Caleido.

Caleido: Tübinger Straße in Stuttgart-Süd

Caleido, at Tübinger Straße 41-43, was the chain’s second Stuttgart restaurant. It opened shortly before 11 April 2014, according to the local magazine re.flect. The official page lists it as open every day from 11:30 am to 11 pm. Along with Europaviertel, that makes it the late option on a Sunday.

All four Stuttgart branches serve the same national menu: burgers, salads and small plates in meat, vegetarian and vegan versions, plus a cocktail bar. I found no burger that is exclusive to Stuttgart.

The Mittagsmenü costs €9.50. It includes a burger, Goldfritten (the house fries), a side salad or coleslaw, and a drink: Durstlöscher lemonade, still water, iced tea or a small beer. If your timing allows a lunch stop, this is the deal I would point a first-timer to; at dinner, the same burger with fries and a lemonade comes to €22.50 or more.

Vegan and vegetarian burgers in Stuttgart

Every Hans im Glück in Stuttgart serves its burgers, salads and small plates in meat, vegetarian and vegan versions. For a group that mixes meat eaters, vegetarians and vegans, that solves the argument before it starts. Reviewers at Europaviertel single out the vegetarian burgers, and children get a vegan option too: the Kleiner Glücksgriff at €6.90.

Gluten-free diners should be careful. The official pages publish nothing about gluten-free options, and one review mentions a risk of cross-contamination. Ask the staff before you order.

Booking, delivery and takeaway

All four Stuttgart branches offer dine-in, takeaway and delivery. To reserve a table, phone the restaurant. Online booking is listed as unavailable at Europaviertel and temporarily unavailable at Milaneo, and both ask you to call. The Caleido and Marienstraße pages do not mention reservations, so call those too. Milaneo delivers through the chain’s Glücksbringer service; I could not confirm which delivery apps the other branches use.

Getting there and parking

All four Hans im Glück branches in Stuttgart are central and easy to reach by Stadtbahn (light rail) or bus. Only Milaneo has a car park in the same building.

Milaneo and Europaviertel

  • Stadtbahn Stadtbibliothek (U5, U6, U7, U15): about 3 minutes on foot
  • Stadtbahn Budapester Platz (U12): about 4 minutes on foot
  • Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof: about 10 minutes on foot
  • Milaneo underground car park: 1,700 spaces, open Monday to Saturday 7:30 am–9:30 pm, closed on Sundays and public holidays, maximum height 1.90 m

Marienstraße and Caleido

  • Bus stop Marienstraße (lines 44 and 92)
  • Stadtbahn Rotebühlplatz (U2, U4, U11, U14, U34): about 5 minutes on foot
  • Stadtbahn Österreichischer Platz (U1, U9, U34)
  • Das Gerber, next door, has entrances on Tübinger Straße, Sophienstraße and Marienstraße

The official pages publish nothing about payment methods, wheelchair access or Wi-Fi. Check with the restaurant if they matter to you.

How Hans im Glück came to Stuttgart in 2014

Hans im Glück had four restaurants in Stuttgart by the end of 2014. Three of them opened that year, after Europaviertel, the first.

  • Europaviertel: the first Stuttgart branch
  • Caleido, Tübinger Straße: opened shortly before 11 April 2014 (re.flect)
  • Marienstraße: opened on 1 September 2014 in the former Ketterer building
  • Milaneo: announced in August 2014 by re.flect under the headline “Hans im vierfachen Glück”; the Milaneo mall opened on 9 October 2014

When Marienstraße opened, the chain had 8 franchisees and more than 20 Burgergrills in Germany, according to stuttgart-informationen.de.

Who is behind Hans im Glück?

Hans im Glück is a German burger chain founded in Munich’s Hackenviertel in 2010 by Thomas Hirschberger. According to Gastroinsider, he founded it with his wife, Gunilla. The name comes from the Brothers Grimm fairy tale “Hans in Luck”.

In January 2020, Hirschberger sold his majority stake in Hans im Glück Franchise GmbH to GAB, already a minority shareholder, and to the BackWerk founders Dirk Schneider and Hans-Christian Limmer. At that time the chain had 81 restaurants, and it had made €147 million in revenue in 2019, according to Tageskarte. Gastroinsider put the count at around 100 restaurants in April 2026, but the chain has not confirmed that figure.

Some franchisees have since gone bankrupt. Saarlouis, Trier and Kaiserslautern went under in July 2024. Mainz, Wiesbaden and Fulda followed in May 2025, and head office took those restaurants over. None of the sources I read mention Stuttgart, and I could not establish whether the Stuttgart branches are run by a franchisee or by the chain itself. The Hans im Glück fountain has nothing to do with the burgers

Stuttgart’s Hans-im-Glück-Brunnen, the fountain on Geißplatz in the old town, has no link to the burger chain. It was unveiled in May 1909, on 12 May according to museum-digital. Mayor Heinrich von Gauß wanted it as a “sign of good fortune”. The streets around it are still called the Hans-im-Glück-Viertel.

There is a burger twist all the same. According to the Stuttgarter Zeitung, a Burger King opened on Nadlerstraße, close to the fountain, and closed after barely a year. Not much luck there.

FAQ: Hans im Glück Stuttgart

How many Hans im Glück restaurants are there in Stuttgart?

There are four: Europaviertel (Lissabonner Straße 2), Milaneo (Mailänder Platz 7), Marienstraße (Marienstraße 3B) and Caleido (Tübinger Straße 41-43). All four were open on 20 October 2026.

Which Hans im Glück is closest to Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof?

Milaneo and Europaviertel are both about a 10-minute walk from the main station. Both are in the Europaviertel, next to the Stadtbibliothek Stadtbahn stop.

What are Hans im Glück Stuttgart’s Sunday opening hours?

Europaviertel and Caleido are open from 11:30 am to 11 pm. Marienstraße is open from 11:30 am to 10 pm. Milaneo is open from noon to 9 pm. In each case the kitchen closes one hour earlier.

How much does a burger cost at Hans im Glück Stuttgart?

A burger costs €11.50 (Klassik) to €17.90 (Hochstapler) on the February 2026 Stuttgart menu. Smash burgers start at €8.50.

Is there a lunch menu at Hans im Glück Stuttgart?

Yes. The Mittagsmenü costs €9.50 and includes a burger, Goldfritten, a side salad or coleslaw, and a drink.

Can you book a table at Hans im Glück Stuttgart?

Yes, by phone. Online booking is listed as unavailable at Europaviertel and Milaneo, so call the branch directly.

Does Hans im Glück Stuttgart do delivery or takeaway?

Yes. All four branches offer takeaway and delivery, and Milaneo delivers through the chain’s Glücksbringer service.

Does Hans im Glück have vegan and gluten-free burgers?

Vegan and vegetarian versions are on the menu at every Stuttgart branch. The chain publishes no gluten-free information, and one review warns of cross-contamination, so ask the staff before you order.

Where can I park for Hans im Glück at Milaneo?

Use the Milaneo underground car park: 1,700 spaces, open Monday to Saturday from 7:30 am to 9:30 pm, maximum height 1.90 m. It is closed on Sundays and public holidays.

Is the Hans im Glück fountain in Stuttgart linked to the burger chain?

No. The fountain on Geißplatz was unveiled in 1909 and has no connection with the chain, which was founded in Munich in 2010.

Where does the name Hans im Glück come from?

The name comes from “Hans in Luck”, the Brothers Grimm fairy tale, chosen by founder Thomas Hirschberger.
