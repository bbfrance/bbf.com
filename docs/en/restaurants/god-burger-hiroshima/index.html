God Burger Hiroshima: Yokogawa's Lost Burger Shop

God Burger in Yokogawa, Hiroshima (1976–2013): the Royal God with okonomiyaki-sauce mayo, 2010 prices, why it closed and where to eat nearby today.

By Hana, Restaurants writer, Japan and IndonesiaPublished November 24, 2026

God Burger (ゴッドバーガー) was a family-run burger counter at the entrance of the Yokogawa shopping arcade in Nishi-ku, Hiroshima. It opened on 1 November 1976 and served its last burger on 20 October 2013, 37 years later. Owner Kazumasa Kamikawa ran it with his family. The house burger was the Royal God, dressed with mayonnaise mixed with okonomiyaki sauce. You can’t eat there anymore. The name lives on only as an online shop that sells T-shirts, not food.

Current status (checked 7 November 2026): God Burger is permanently closed. Its last day of business was 20 October 2013. Since 2017 the brand has survived only as an online shop, G-Burger by ゴッドバーガー (g-burger.com), which sells T-shirts and goods and serves no food. Everything below about the menu, prices and opening hours is history, dated to the source it comes from.

A note on “Gold Burger”: I found no record of a “Gold Burger” restaurant in Hiroshima, in English or Japanese. Japanese searches for ゴールドバーガー 広島 lead only to ゴッドバーガー, God Burger in Yokogawa. That is the shop this profile covers.

I never ate at God Burger. It closed long before I started this profile, so I rebuilt its story from dated archives: the Hiroshima Keizai Shimbun reports of 2010 and 2014, the local food site tabetainjya.com, Get Hiroshima, Japan Travel, Tabelog and a blogger’s visit in 2004. Every fact below names its source. Where two sources disagree, I say which one I trust and why.

God Burger Hiroshima at a glance

God Burger was a counter burger shop at 3-10-16 Yokogawa-cho (横川町3-10-16), Nishi-ku, Hiroshima, about 96 m from JR Yokogawa Station. Tabelog still lists it as permanently closed, with a 3.07/5 score from 65 reviews (Tabelog, Get Hiroshima).

One small blog from 2006 gives 1 November 1975 as the opening date. The same page gets the address wrong, though, while the brand’s own site and the Hiroshima Keizai Shimbun both say 1976. I go with 1976.

Who ran God Burger: Kazumasa Kamikawa and family

God Burger was owned by Kazumasa Kamikawa (神川一正), who in 2010 ran the shop with his wife Miho (美保) and his second daughter Masako (正子) (Hiroshima Keizai Shimbun, 2010).

The name is a family pun. The first character of Kamikawa, 神 (kami), means “god”, so the family’s burger shop became God Burger (Get Hiroshima).

I found nothing on what Kazumasa Kamikawa does today, or on who runs the G-Burger shop. Both questions stay open; I won’t guess.

How it started: a franchise outlet, then its own menu

Local food media report that God Burger began as a franchise outlet and left the franchise to serve its own menu (tabetainjya.com).

A second tabetainjya.com article goes further: it names the franchise as MOS Burger and says the owner left after one year (tabetainjya.com). That comes from a local blog, not from MOS or the Kamikawa family, and I could not confirm it with a primary source. For background on the chain itself, see our MOS Burger brand profile.

Was God Burger Hiroshima’s first burger shop?

Possibly, but no official record confirms it. Local food site tabetainjya.com describes God Burger as “the shop that opened Hiroshima’s first hamburger specialty store” (広島で最初にハンバーガー専門店を出店したお店) (tabetainjya.com).

Get Hiroshima is more careful: when it opened in the 1970s, it was “one of the only western eateries around” (Get Hiroshima). My reading is that “first” is local lore and “one of the first” is what the evidence supports. Either way, God Burger belongs to the opening chapter of Hiroshima’s burger history. For how the burger spread around the world before that, read our history of the hamburger.

The Royal God burger and the God Burger menu

The Royal God (ロイヤルゴッド) was God Burger’s house specialty, with an okonomiyaki-style flavor that came from mayonnaise mixed with okonomiyaki sauce (tabetainjya.com).

What was in the Royal God burger

In August 2004 the Royal God held egg, ham, cheese, tomato, lettuce and sliced onion, according to a blogger who ate there (reirei0120 blog, 2004). The okonomiyaki-sauce mayonnaise is what I find most interesting in the whole story. In Hiroshima, the city of layered okonomiyaki, it was a local touch that a national chain menu rarely allows, and a family counter simply did it. I could not confirm which sauce brand or bakery the shop used, so I don’t name one.

A bottle of Otafuku okonomiyaki sauce, made in Hiroshima: the kind of sauce God Burger mixed into the mayonnaise on its Royal God burger. How God Burger made and served its burgers

Every burger was made to order in three sizes: R, M and J, for “jumbo” (tabetainjya.com). Each was held together with a skewer and wrapped in paper printed “Do you like delicious things?” When the day’s stock ran out, a “sold out, closed” sign went up (Hiroshima Keizai Shimbun, 2014).

The menu before the 2010 remodel

Before the remodel, the classic God Burger came “dripping in grease and salt”, with signature spicy fries and very creamy shakes (Get Hiroshima). The menu listed 18 burger and hot-dog choices:

  • Meat burgers: curry, teriyaki, Hawaiian, chicken, pork, bacon and egg
  • For non-meat eaters: egg, salad and mochi burgers
  • Extras from ¥30: mochi, egg, pineapple, bacon or an extra patty

In 2004 a blogger counted about 500 possible combinations of bun size and toppings (reirei0120 blog). If the teriyaki option makes you curious, our MOS-style teriyaki burger recipe recreates that very Japanese flavor at home.

God Burger prices in August 2010

After the August 2010 reopening, a hamburger started at ¥360 and the Royal God at ¥780 (Hiroshima Keizai Shimbun, 2010). That is more than twice the price of a plain hamburger. With egg, ham and cheese stacked on the patty, the gap looks fair to me. These prices are historical and no longer apply anywhere.

The 2010 remodel: smaller, smoke-free, no fryer

God Burger closed temporarily at the end of January 2010 because its equipment was ageing. It reopened quietly on 2 August 2010 in a smaller unit of 10 tsubo (about 33 m²) with 8 seats, roughly half its former size (Hiroshima Keizai Shimbun, 2010).

The fryer was gone after the remodel, and the shop was smoke-free (Get Hiroshima, Japan Travel). Even with 8 seats, it still sold about 120 burgers on weekdays, 150–160 on Saturdays and 180 on Sundays (Hiroshima Keizai Shimbun, 2010).

Historical opening hours

God Burger’s hours changed over time, so here they are as dated snapshots only:

  • August 2004: 9:00–22:00, 9:00–20:00 on Sundays and holidays, closed Tuesdays (open on holidays), tel. 082-232-2608 (reirei0120 blog)
  • August 2010: 11:00–20:00 or until sold out, closed Tuesdays, tel. 082-232-2608 (Hiroshima Keizai Shimbun)

Why God Burger closed in 2013

God Burger closed on 20 October 2013 because its finances worsened as the price of domestic beef rose (国産牛肉の価格が高騰したことによる経営悪化) (tabetainjya.com, 2013).

I think that reason says a lot. A family team making every burger to order in an 8-seat shop, with a hamburger from ¥360, had almost no margin left when Japanese beef got more expensive. After 37 years, the Kamikawa family’s counter in Yokogawa went dark. Tabelog confirms the closure (Tabelog).

The one-day Royal God revival in May 2014

On 5 May 2014 the Royal God came back for one day at Deli Café Kajiru in Fresta Mall Kajiru Yokogawa (3 Yokogawa-cho, Nishi-ku). Twenty burgers went on sale from 11:00 at ¥1,100 each, including one drink (Hiroshima Keizai Shimbun, 2014).

The burgers came on a “medium” bun 1.5 times the regular size. Kazumasa Kamikawa took part, and chef Koji Takeda (竹田耕治) cooked each burger to order. I found no later revival.

G-Burger: the God Burger merchandise shop today

Since 2017, G-Burger by ゴッドバーガー (g-burger.com) has sold original God Burger merchandise online, starting from the T-shirts once sold at the shop counter (g-burger.com). It sells no food.

The shop says it comes from the hamburger shop that opened on 1 November 1976 in Yokogawa-cho and closed in October 2013. When I tried to open its pages directly they returned an error, so I could not check its current items or prices. Search for 「G-Burger byゴッドバーガー」 to find it.

Where to eat a burger in Yokogawa today

The nearest burger we could verify near God Burger’s old corner is MOS Burger Fresta Yokogawa. It is at 3-2-36 Yokogawa-cho, Nishi-ku, Hiroshima 733-0011, 3 minutes’ walk from the south exit of JR Yokogawa Station, and opens daily 7:00–22:00 (per Gurunavi, checked 10 October 2026).

The south exit of JR Yokogawa Station in February 2025, under the arcade roof. God Burger stood about 96 m away until 2013, and MOS Burger Fresta Yokogawa is a 3-minute walk from here today. (Photo: Ricekun, CC BY 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yokogawa\_Station\_South\_Exit\_2025\_02\_26.jpg)

No chain burger will replace a family counter that ran for 37 years. Still, MOS cooks its burgers to order, a short walk from the old arcade entrance, and that is the closest thing to God Burger left in Yokogawa. God Burger Hiroshima FAQ

Is God Burger in Hiroshima still open?

No. God Burger closed permanently on 20 October 2013, after 37 years in Yokogawa. The brand survives only as the G-Burger merchandise shop, which sells no food.

Why was it called God Burger?

The name comes from the owner’s family name, Kamikawa. Its first character, 神 (kami), means “god”.

Who founded God Burger, and when did it open?

God Burger opened on 1 November 1976 in Yokogawa-cho, Nishi-ku. It was owned by Kazumasa Kamikawa, who in 2010 ran it with his wife Miho and second daughter Masako.

What was the Royal God burger?

The Royal God was God Burger’s house specialty. In 2004 it held egg, ham, cheese, tomato, lettuce and sliced onion, and it was known for mayonnaise mixed with okonomiyaki sauce. In August 2010 it cost from ¥780.

Why did God Burger close in 2013?

Local food site tabetainjya.com reports that the shop’s finances worsened as the price of domestic beef rose.

Was God Burger really Hiroshima’s first hamburger shop?

Tabetainjya.com describes it as the shop that opened Hiroshima’s first hamburger specialty store. No official record confirms this, so treat it as a local claim.

Did God Burger start as a MOS Burger franchise?

Local food media report that it began as a franchise outlet. One tabetainjya.com article names MOS Burger and says the owner left after one year, but no primary source confirms it.

Can you still buy God Burger T-shirts?

Yes. The online shop G-Burger by ゴッドバーガー (g-burger.com) sells God Burger merchandise, starting from the T-shirts once sold at the counter.

Where can I eat a burger in Yokogawa today?

MOS Burger Fresta Yokogawa, at 3-2-36 Yokogawa-cho, is 3 minutes’ walk from the south exit of JR Yokogawa Station and opens daily 7:00–22:00 (Gurunavi, October 2026).

Is there a “Gold Burger” in Hiroshima?

I found no record of one. Searches for “Gold Burger” in Hiroshima lead to God Burger, the closed Yokogawa shop.
