Best Burgers in Minneapolis: 10 Spots to Try

The 10 best burgers in Minneapolis, checked November 2026: the Jucy Lucy at Matt's Bar and the 5-8 Club, North Loop smash burgers, addresses and closures.

By Daniel, Restaurants writer, United StatesPublished November 27, 2026

The best burger in Minneapolis is a Juicy Lucy, and ordering one means picking a side. Matt’s Bar and the 5-8 Club, two bars 23 blocks apart on Cedar Avenue, both claim to have invented the cheese-stuffed patty. If you only have time for one, start at Matt’s: it has the best-documented origin story and a spelling nobody else uses. Want a double smash instead? Go to Parlour in the North Loop. For a chef’s burger, head to Le Burger 4304 in Linden Hills. Below are my 10 picks. On November 18, 2026, I checked every address, phone number and price against the restaurants’ own pages and the local press. The places that have closed are listed at the end, because several popular lists still send people to them.

The 10 best burgers in Minneapolis at a glance

The 10 best burger restaurants in Minneapolis are Matt’s Bar, the 5-8 Club, Parlour, Le Burger 4304, 112 Eatery, Red Cow, Francis Burger Joint, Blue Door Pub, George & the Dragon and Hamburguesas El Gordo. Here is where each one is and what to order.

How I ranked these Minneapolis burger restaurants

Every restaurant on this list is recommended by at least two of six established sources: Meet Minneapolis, the Star Tribune, The Infatuation, Minnesota Monthly, Food & Wine and Explore Minnesota. I then checked that each one was still open in November 2026. To be clear about what this is: a guide checked against those sources, not a tasting panel. The order reflects how consistently local critics back each burger, and where I give an opinion, I explain what it rests on.

The list covers the city of Minneapolis only, not St. Paul or the suburbs. Addresses and hours come from each restaurant’s own website. I give a price only when a menu confirms it, with the date I checked it. Where the sources disagree, I say so instead of guessing.

1. Matt’s Bar: the original Jucy Lucy

For the Jucy Lucy, the cheese-stuffed burger Minneapolis is known for, go to Matt’s Bar. Matt Bristol opened the bar in 1954, and it still sells the “Original Jucy Lucy”, spelled without the “i”.

  • Address: 3500 Cedar Ave S, Minneapolis, MN 55407 (Powderhorn / Corcoran)
  • Order: the Jucy Lucy, a burger with the cheese sealed inside the patty
  • Website: mattsbar.com

The origin story comes from Bristol’s daughter, quoted by City Pages in 1998. In 1954, a customer asked for cheese sealed between two patties, bit in and said, “That’s one juicy Lucy!” Bristol sold the bar in 1998 to Scott Nelson, who still owns it. Bristol died on June 26, 2014, the same day Barack Obama visited the bar (Wikipedia).

Exterior of Matt’s Bar on Cedar Avenue in Minneapolis, the disputed home of the Jucy Lucy since 1954, photographed in June 2013 (3888×2592). (Photo: Jeramey Jannene, CC BY 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Matt%27s\_Bar,\_2013.jpg)

Expect a wait. The bar’s own website warns that dine-in and takeout orders go into the same queue, which tells you how busy it gets. In 2025, Matt’s added a food truck that serves its burgers around the Twin Cities (Twin Cities Business). The one thing I couldn’t pin down is the price: published figures for the Jucy Lucy disagree, so I’m leaving it out rather than printing a wrong number. Check the board at the bar.

2. 5-8 Club: the Juicy Lucy, spelled the classic way

The 5-8 Club is the other contender for the Juicy Lucy’s birthplace, and the better choice if you want to pick your cheese. It opened in 1928 as a speakeasy called the 58th Street Club. It spells the burger “Juicy Lucy”, and that name is a registered trademark.

Juicy Lucy cheese-stuffed burger served at the 5-8 Club in Minneapolis, the Cedar Avenue bar that opened as a speakeasy in 1928 (photo July 2011; 2048×1536). (Photo: Kim (thegirlsny), CC BY-SA 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jucy\_Lucy\_burger\_-\_5-8\_Club,\_Minneapolis,\_Minnesota.jpg)

  • Address: 5800 Cedar Ave S, Minneapolis (Nokomis)
  • Phone: 612-823-5858
  • Order: the Juicy Lucy, stuffed with American, blue, pepper jack or Swiss cheese (Meet Minneapolis)
  • Website: 5-8club.com

Exterior of the original 5-8 Club at 5800 Cedar Ave S in Minneapolis’s Nokomis neighborhood, photographed in February 2008 (3072×2304). (Photo: Ed Kohler, CC BY 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:5-8\_Club\_exterior\_1,\_February\_2008.jpg)

The 5-8 Club also runs locations in Champlin, Maplewood and West St. Paul, but go to the Cedar Avenue original. Matt’s Bar is 23 blocks north on the same avenue, and the two bars have argued over who invented the burger since the 1950s (Wikipedia, Star Tribune). My plan for a first trip: Matt’s for lunch, the 5-8 Club for dinner, and a straight drive down Cedar Avenue in between. Then decide for yourself.

3. Parlour: the best smash burger in Minneapolis

Of all the smash burgers in Minneapolis, Parlour’s double is the one the lists I checked name most often. The Parlour Burger is two smashed patties of chuck, ribeye and brisket with white American cheddar and house pickles on an egg bun (Meet Minneapolis).

  • Address: 730 Washington Ave N, Ste 114, Minneapolis, MN 55401 (North Loop)
  • Order: the Parlour Burger
  • Website: parlourbar.com

That three-cut blend is why I rank Parlour above Red Cow’s Double Barrel, the other North Loop smash: no other burger here is built that deliberately. Parlour started as the waiting area for Borough, the restaurant upstairs. Both belong to Jester Concepts, the group behind Butcher & the Boar. Borough served its last dinner on June 27, 2026, and on Monday, November 9, 2026, Jester reopened the space as Revival, a fried chicken restaurant (FOX 9). Parlour also has locations in St. Paul and Excelsior, a food truck, and stands at US Bank Stadium, Target Center and Huntington Bank Stadium. To see why chuck, ribeye and brisket work together, read our guide to the best ground beef for burgers.

4. Le Burger 4304: a French-style burger in Linden Hills

Le Burger 4304 is the Minneapolis chef’s burger that has drawn national attention. Food & Wine named it one of the six best new burger restaurants in the United States, based on a poll of more than 400 chefs, journalists and experts (Patch, April 17, 2026).

  • Address: 4304 Upton Ave S, Minneapolis, MN 55410 (Linden Hills; the name comes from the address)
  • Phone: 612-328-9267
  • Hours: Sunday to Thursday 11 a.m. to 8 p.m., Friday and Saturday 11 a.m. to 9 p.m.
  • Order: La Royale, with seared foie gras, Cooper American cheese, black truffle aioli and pickles
  • Website: leburger4304.com · Instagram @leburger4304

Chef Jonathan Gans and Josh Hoyt, both from The Bachelor Farmer, opened it in November 2024 with a simple idea: the burger joint two Americans would open in Paris. The patties are smashed wagyu with griddled onions. At opening, L’Américain cost $12, La Boeuf $15 and the triple-cooked fries $5 (Racket, December 12, 2024). Those are 2024 prices, so check the current menu. Foie gras on a smash burger sounds like a gimmick. A Food & Wine nod after less than two years suggests it isn’t.

5. 112 Eatery: the chef’s cheeseburger with Brie

112 Eatery puts a chef’s cheeseburger on a dinner menu: beef chuck ground in-house, topped with Brie de Meaux, on a brioche bun.

  • Address: 112 N 3rd St, Minneapolis (Warehouse District / North Loop)
  • Phone: 612-343-7696
  • Hours: Tuesday to Saturday, from 5 p.m.
  • Order: the 112 Cheeseburger
  • Website: 112eatery.com

Isaac Becker, a James Beard Award-winning chef, opened 112 Eatery in January 2005. For its 20th anniversary in January 2025, the burger came back on an English muffin, the way it was first served (Star Tribune). Brioche is the bun you can count on, so ask whether the muffin version is still around. It opens only for dinner. Of all ten, this is the one I’d plan a whole evening around.

6. Red Cow North Loop: the Double Barrel

Red Cow’s Double Barrel is the North Loop smash burger with a trophy: it won Best in Show at the 2016 Twin Cities Burger Battle. It is two smashed patties with white American cheddar, griddled onion and house sauce.

  • Address: 208 1st Ave N, Minneapolis, MN 55401 (North Loop, open since 2015)
  • Phone: 612-238-0050
  • Closed: Mondays
  • Prices (checked November 2026): Double Barrel $16.50, 60/40 $15.75
  • Website: redcowmn.com

The Uptown location at 2626 Hennepin Ave closed on June 1, 2026. That leaves Red Cow two Minneapolis addresses, the North Loop and 50th & France. The North Loop one is a short walk from Parlour and 112 Eatery. I covered the menu, prices and the Uptown closure in my Red Cow Minneapolis menu and prices guide.

7. Francis Burger Joint: the best vegan burger in Minneapolis

For a vegan burger in Minneapolis, go to Francis Burger Joint, which even makes a vegan Jucy Lucy. Everything on the menu is plant-based, down to the seitan bacon.

  • Address: 2422 Central Ave NE, Minneapolis, MN 55418 (Northeast)
  • Phone: 612-208-6870
  • Hours: Monday to Friday 11 a.m. to 9 p.m., Saturday and Sunday 10 a.m. to 9 p.m.
  • Second location: 3900 E Lake St, Minneapolis, MN 55406 (612-704-8825)
  • Order: the vegan Jucy Lucy
  • Website: francisburgerjoint.com

Francis started as a food trailer. In early 2023 it moved into its first restaurant on Central Avenue, in the former Sen Yai Sen Lek space (Star Tribune). A meat-free take on the city’s signature burger is reason enough to include it. It’s also the easy answer when someone in your group doesn’t eat meat.

8. Blue Door Pub Longfellow: the Blucy

Blue Door Pub is the home of the Blucy, its own take on the cheese-stuffed Juicy Lucy, with a rotating list of variations. The “Blucy of the Moment” changes regularly, so you may not get the same one twice.

  • Address: 3448 42nd Ave S, Minneapolis (Longfellow)
  • Order: the Blucy of the Moment
  • Website: thebdp.com

Jeremy Woerner and Pat McDonough created the Blucy in 2008 at a small St. Paul bar with a blue door. The Longfellow pub brings it to the Minneapolis side of the river.

The original Blue Door Pub on Selby Avenue in St. Paul, where the Blucy was born in 2008, photographed in May 2021. This is the St. Paul pub, not the Longfellow location in Minneapolis (2560×1152). (Photo: Gobonobo, CC BY-SA 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Blue\_Door\_Pub.jpg)

9. George & the Dragon: a British gastropub burger

George & the Dragon is a British-style gastropub in Southwest Minneapolis with two house burgers, the George Burger and the Dragon Burger. Fred Navarro’s family opened it in 2012 (Star Tribune), and it has been featured on Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives.

  • Address: 813 W 50th St, Minneapolis (50th & Bryant, Southwest / Tangletown)
  • Phone: 612-208-1047
  • Order: the George Burger or the Dragon Burger

One warning: on November 18, 2026, the pub’s website did not load, so call before you go. If nobody answers, my backup is Hell’s Kitchen at 80 S 9th St, Minneapolis, MN 55402 (Downtown, below street level, 612-332-4700), employee-owned since 2020. Meet Minneapolis recommends its “Juicy Lucifer”, made with a sweet-and-spicy red pepper jelly. The burger isn’t on the restaurant’s homepage, so ask whether it’s still on the menu.

10. Hamburguesas El Gordo: the Mexican burger on Cedar Avenue

Hamburguesas El Gordo serves northern Mexican street burgers, and La Del Gordo is the most loaded burger on this list: two cheeses, ham, bacon, avocado, onion, lettuce, tomato and pickled jalapeños (Star Tribune).

  • Address: 4157 Cedar Ave S, Minneapolis, MN 55407 (Standish)
  • Order: La Del Gordo

It’s about six blocks south of Matt’s Bar on Cedar Avenue, so a Jucy Lucy and a Mexican burger fit in the same afternoon. The West St. Paul location has closed; the Victoria Street location in St. Paul is still open. I couldn’t find an official website or Instagram account, so check its Google Maps listing for current hours.

Which Minneapolis neighborhood is best for burgers?

The North Loop and Cedar Avenue are the two best neighborhoods for burgers in Minneapolis. In the North Loop and the Warehouse District next to it, Parlour, 112 Eatery and Red Cow are within walking distance of each other. Cedar Avenue in South Minneapolis has the Juicy Lucy rivals, Matt’s Bar and the 5-8 Club, with Hamburguesas El Gordo in between. Le Burger 4304 is worth the trip to Linden Hills, and Francis is worth the trip to Northeast.

Minneapolis burger spots that have recently closed

These burger spots still show up on some best-of lists, but they have closed. Don’t plan a trip around them:

  • Stepchld: closed in 2025
  • ALTBuRGEr (Northeast): closed in early 2025
  • TokiDoki Burger: closed in late 2024 or early 2025
  • Bebe Zito at Eat Street Crossing: closed in late 2024
  • Annie’s Parlour in Dinkytown: closed in July 2025, after 50 years
  • Red Cow Uptown (2626 Hennepin Ave): closed on June 1, 2026

Sources: Mpls.St.Paul Magazine and the Star Tribune. Revival is a different story. It closed its four Twin Cities locations in January 2025. Jester Concepts bought the brand and its recipes in July 2025 and reopened it in the North Loop in November 2026, but it serves fried chicken, not burgers.

FAQ: best burgers in Minneapolis

What is the most famous burger in Minneapolis?

The Juicy Lucy is the most famous burger in Minneapolis: a patty with the cheese sealed inside, so the cheese melts in the middle as it cooks. Matt’s Bar and the 5-8 Club, both on Cedar Avenue, are the two places to try it. Other Midwest cities have their own signature burgers. Milwaukee’s is the butter burger, covered in my guide to Solly’s Grille in Milwaukee.

Who invented the Juicy Lucy, Matt’s Bar or the 5-8 Club?

No one knows for sure. Both bars have claimed the Juicy Lucy since the 1950s. Matt’s dates its version to 1954 and has the best-known origin story, the customer who said “That’s one juicy Lucy!” The 5-8 Club is the older bar: it opened as a speakeasy in 1928. For how the Juicy Lucy compares with other burger origin stories, read our history of the hamburger.

Why does Matt’s Bar spell it “Jucy Lucy”?

Matt’s Bar spells it “Jucy Lucy”, without the “i”, and sells it as the “Original Jucy Lucy”. The 5-8 Club uses the standard spelling, “Juicy Lucy”, and has registered it as a trademark. So the spelling on a menu or in a review tells you which bar it means.

Where can I find the best smash burger in Minneapolis?

The Parlour Burger in the North Loop is the city’s best-known smash burger, a double patty of chuck, ribeye and brisket. A few blocks away, Red Cow’s Double Barrel won Best in Show at the 2016 Twin Cities Burger Battle and cost $16.50 in November 2026.

Where can I get a vegan burger in Minneapolis?

Francis Burger Joint is fully plant-based, with two locations: Central Avenue NE and East Lake Street. Its menu includes a vegan Jucy Lucy and seitan bacon.

How much does a burger cost in Minneapolis?

The burgers on this list run from about $12 to $17, before tax and tip, based on the prices I could confirm. In November 2026, Red Cow charged $16.50 for the Double Barrel and $15.75 for the 60/40. When Le Burger 4304 opened in November 2024, L’Américain cost $12 and La Boeuf $15. Expect those numbers to have moved since. I couldn’t confirm current prices for the other restaurants, so check each menu before you go.

Do I need a reservation for a burger in Minneapolis?

Not at Matt’s Bar, but you can’t skip the line either. The bar says dine-in and takeout orders join the same queue because of the crowds, so expect to wait at busy times. For the other restaurants, check their websites before you go.

What about local burger chains?

Two Midwest chains are worth knowing. Culver’s, from Wisconsin, is known for its ButterBurgers. Dairy Queen has its headquarters in the Minneapolis area. Our brand profiles cover the history of each chain.
