Boise Fry Company: Menu, Bison Burger, Locations

How an economist and a chef built a fry-first burger bar in Idaho in 2009, what to order today and where to find all 7 Treasure Valley locations.

By Daniel, Restaurants writer, United StatesPublished November 24, 2026

Boise Fry Company is a fry-first burger restaurant founded in Boise, Idaho, in spring 2009 by Blake Lingle and chef Riley Huddleston. CEO Brad Walker, who started as a fry cook at the original location, runs it today. There are 7 locations, all in the Treasure Valley: 3 in Boise, 2 in Meridian, 1 in Nampa and a drive-thru in Caldwell. My advice: order the fries first and the burger second. The potatoes are double-fried Belgian-style and come out unsalted, so you pick the salt and the sauce. The patty can be wagyu-blend beef, bison, vegan or turkey.

Who owns Boise Fry Company? The founders and the CEO

Boise Fry Company was founded by Blake Lingle, an Idaho native, with chef Riley Huddleston, and has been led by CEO Brad Walker since early 2017. Their routes into the fry business could hardly be more different.

Blake Lingle, the economist who bet everything on fries

Blake Lingle is an Idaho native and a former economist for the federal government (From Boise, July 2026). He did not ease into restaurants. In 2015 he told the Idaho Business Review that he and his wife “drained every single cent we owned”, a retirement mutual fund included, to launch the company.

Riley Huddleston, the chef who wrote the first menu

Riley Huddleston is the chef who developed Boise Fry Company’s original menu in 2009. He trained at the New England Culinary Institute’s Caribbean campus in Tortola. In 2011 he moved to the Chicago area to open Wild Monk in La Grange, and he later became executive chef at Prasino in Chicago, according to his Illinois Restaurant Association bio. I couldn’t confirm whether he still holds a stake in the company, so I’m crediting him only as co-founder and author of the first menu.

Brad Walker, from fry cook to CEO

Brad Walker joined Boise Fry Company as a fry cook at the original location, worked his way up to general manager and took over as CEO around January 1, 2017. The Idaho Business Review reported the handover in 2016: Lingle stepped back to the role of managing member, focused on fundraising and investors. As of July 2026, From Boise still names Walker as CEO. I found no source on Lingle’s role or stake in 2026.

Why Idaho Fry Company became Boise Fry Company

Boise Fry Company opened on Broadway in Boise as Idaho Fry Company and changed its name after the Idaho Potato Commission objected. On June 17, 2009, both sides agreed on Boise Fry Company. Signs and marketing had to change by August 31, 2009, and the Commission agreed to pay for the switch (PotatoPro).

Only in Idaho. In a state whose name doubles as a potato trademark, a fry shop couldn’t put “Idaho” on its sign. A few months after opening, the restaurant had a new name, and the Commission had picked up the bill.

Boise Fry Company menu: the burgers

The Boise Fry Company menu has 4 burgers: The Original, The Classic, The Heat and a rotating Feature, each with your choice of protein (official menu, checked November 20, 2026).

Burger Toppings Who it’s for
The Original Spring mix, tomato, red onion gastrique, garlic aioli The house style, sweet-sour onion and garlic
The Classic Lettuce, tomato, red onion, house-made pickles, Idaho fry sauce A local take on the classic American burger
The Heat Lettuce, savory blueberry compote, habanero, spicy ketchup Fans of sweet heat
The Feature Rotating: BBQ, Pico, Greek, Umami and Elote versions so far Regulars who want something new

On paper, my pick is The Classic. It’s the only burger dressed with Idaho fry sauce, the ketchup-and-mayo dip Idaho shares with Utah (more on it on our Boise burger page). The Heat is the oddball. You won’t find blueberry compote with habanero at a national chain, and I want to know whether the fruit tames the pepper or the other way around.

Proteins: wagyu beef, bison, vegan or turkey

Every Boise Fry Company burger comes with a wagyu-blend beef patty unless you swap it for bison, a vegan quinoa and black bean patty or turkey, which the official site also lists. The patties are hand-seasoned with a house spice blend and hand-rolled (menu; From Boise).

The bison burger in Boise

Boise Fry Company’s bison comes from Brown’s Buffalo Ranch in Nyssa, Oregon, run by Tim and Deb, which the official site puts about 37 miles from Boise. If you want a bison burger in Boise, this is the easy option: bison swaps into any of the 4 burgers at all 7 locations.

Boise Fry Company fries: the Belgian double-fry method

Boise Fry Company’s fries are cooked Belgian-style in 2 steps: a long blanch at low temperature, then a short fry at high temperature. They’re served unsalted so you choose your own salt and sauce (official site). From Boise says they’re fried in beef tallow, and the restaurants run dedicated gluten-free fryers. If you order the vegan patty, ask at the counter what fat your fries are cooked in.

Cuts, potato varieties, salts and sauces

Boise Fry Company offers 5 fry cuts and 5 or 6 potato varieties at a time. From Boise counted about 14 salts and 15 sauces in July 2026; the count shifts from 1 source to the next.

  • Cuts: regular, homestyle, curly, shoestring and po’ ball.
  • Potatoes: red, gold, russet, sweet and purple, plus seasonal varieties, 5 or 6 at a time.
  • Salts and sauces: about 14 salts and 15 sauces, according to From Boise in July 2026.
  • Specialty fries: Frychos, Bourgeois (cooked in duck fat), Truffle Parmesan and Poutine.

This is the reason to go. Most burger places treat fries as an afterthought. Here you build them like a dish, cut by potato by salt by sauce, and in Idaho, of all places, that feels right.

Where the potatoes come from

Boise Fry Company usually buys its potatoes from King’s Crown Organic Farms in Glenns Ferry, Idaho, which keeps them local and organic for about 9.5 months of the year. The buns come from Gaston’s Bakery in Boise (official site; From Boise).

The Caldwell drive-thru

Caldwell is Boise Fry Company’s seventh location and its first drive-thru. BoiseDev reported in July 2025 that it was due to open by the end of August 2025, and the official site now says it has “recently opened”. I couldn’t pin down the exact opening date.

From franchising to 7 Idaho locations

In 2015, Boise Fry Company had 3 corporate-owned locations in Boise, plus franchises in Meridian and Portland, Oregon. By then the original Broadway location had closed and the downtown Capitol Boulevard location had opened (Idaho Business Review). The Portland franchise is gone from today’s list, and so are the expansion plans reported back then. Every Boise Fry Company is now in Idaho.

How much does Boise Fry Company cost?

Boise Fry Company doesn’t publish regular burger and fry prices on its official pages. The one dated price I found is the Tuesday deal: 2 cheeseburgers, 2 small fries and 2 sodas for $24, checked November 20, 2026. That’s $12 a head for a full meal. There’s also a happy-hour buy-one-get-one on beers and small fries. I’ll add regular menu prices once I’ve checked them at a counter.

Is Boise Fry Company one of the best burger places in Boise?

On the fries, yes. On the burger, I can’t say yet: I haven’t eaten one, and I won’t rank a patty I haven’t tasted.

What sets Boise Fry Company apart on paper is the supply chain: Idaho potatoes, a Northwest wagyu blend, Oregon bison and buns from a Boise bakery. Go for the fries and that sourcing, not for a classic diner smash burger. The neighborhoods to explore and Boise’s other local signature, finger steaks, are on our page on the best burgers in Boise. Boise Fry Company FAQ

Who founded Boise Fry Company and who runs it today?

Blake Lingle, an Idaho native and former federal economist, founded Boise Fry Company in spring 2009 with chef Riley Huddleston, who created the original menu. Brad Walker, a former fry cook at the original location, has been CEO since early 2017.

Why did Idaho Fry Company change its name?

The Idaho Potato Commission objected to the name Idaho Fry Company. On June 17, 2009, both sides agreed on Boise Fry Company. The signs had to change by August 31, 2009, and the Commission paid for the change.

Can you get a bison burger at Boise Fry Company?

Yes. You can swap bison from Brown’s Buffalo Ranch in Nyssa, Oregon, into any burger. The other options are a wagyu-blend beef patty (the default), a vegan quinoa and black bean patty, and turkey.

Where does Boise Fry Company source its ingredients?

The beef is a Double R Ranch and Snake River Farms wagyu blend, and the bison comes from Brown’s Buffalo Ranch in Nyssa, Oregon. The buns are from Gaston’s Bakery in Boise. The potatoes usually come from King’s Crown Organic Farms in Glenns Ferry, Idaho, for about 9.5 months of the year.

Why do Boise Fry Company fries arrive unsalted?

They arrive unsalted so you can choose your own salt and sauce from the long list on offer. The fries are double-fried Belgian-style, with a long low-temperature blanch followed by a short high-temperature fry.

How many Boise Fry Company locations are there, and is there a drive-thru?

There are 7 locations: 3 in Boise, 2 in Meridian, 1 in Nampa and 1 in Caldwell. The Caldwell location at 2110 Blaine St is the chain’s first drive-thru.

Does Boise Fry Company have gluten-free and vegan options?

Yes. There are dedicated gluten-free fryers and a vegan quinoa and black bean patty. The fries are reported to be cooked in beef tallow, so if you avoid animal fat, ask which fat your fries are cooked in.

How much does a burger and fries cost at Boise Fry Company?

Regular prices aren’t published on the official site. The Tuesday deal of 2 cheeseburgers, 2 small fries and 2 sodas costs $24, as checked on November 20, 2026.

What is fry sauce, and is it served at Boise Fry Company?

Fry sauce is a ketchup-and-mayonnaise dip popular in Idaho and Utah. Boise Fry Company puts Idaho fry sauce on The Classic burger.
