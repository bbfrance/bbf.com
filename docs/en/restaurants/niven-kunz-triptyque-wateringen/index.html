Niven Kunz at Triptyque: Michelin Star Near The Hague

Niven Kunz's Triptyque near The Hague: Michelin star, Green Star, the 80/20 vegetable rule, menu prices from €55, booking tips and why there's no burger.

By Sofia, Restaurants writer, Italy, Switzerland and the BeneluxPublished November 17, 2026

Niven Kunz is the Dutch chef-owner of Triptyque, a vegetable-led fine-dining restaurant at Plein 13G in Wateringen, in the Westland area just outside The Hague. Triptyque holds a Michelin star and a Michelin Green Star, and it cooks to Kunz’s 80/20 rule: at least 80% vegetables and fruit on every dish, at most 20% meat or fish. If you are looking for a Michelin star restaurant near The Hague with a green focus, this is it. If you are looking for a burger, it is not: no burger appears on Triptyque’s current menus.

I built this profile from Triptyque’s own pages, the Michelin Guide, Gault&Millau coverage in the local press and the We’re Smart Green Guide, all checked on 11 November 2026. I have not eaten there yet, so there is no rating here. Why cover it on a burger site at all? Because Kunz has taken steak tartare, the most beef-identified dish on any brasserie menu, and rebuilt it from carrots. That is a question every veggie burger tries to answer, asked at Michelin level.

Who is Niven Kunz?

Niven Kunz is a Dutch chef who earned a Michelin star at 24, ran the starred restaurant Niven in Rijswijk from 2009 to 2020, and now cooks at Triptyque, which he presents as the home of his 80/20 philosophy.

He was born in Gorinchem in 1981. Masters Expo is the only source I found for that date. Triptyque’s site and the We’re Smart Green Guide both name him as a co-founder of Dutch Cuisine.

The story starts in Wateringen. Kunz was part-owner of ’t Raethuys, a Michelin-starred restaurant in the old town hall, and won his star there. Wikipedia says he left after a business conflict with his parents. He then went to train at De Librije, and FavorFlav adds a spell at De Bokkedoorns.

What happened to restaurant Niven in Rijswijk?

Niven, Kunz’s restaurant at Delftweg 58-a in Rijswijk, was declared bankrupt on 1 April 2020. It had opened on 11 May 2009 and held a Michelin star from 2011 to 2020.

Wikipedia puts the bankruptcy down to underlying financial problems, not the COVID-19 lockdown that began the same spring. The same page records an odd first: Niven was the first Michelin-starred restaurant in the Netherlands to run a Groupon deal, and it sold 3,500 covers in 10 hours.

What is the 80/20 concept at Triptyque?

The 80/20 principle is Niven Kunz’s rule that every dish holds at least 80% vegetables and fruit and at most 20% meat or fish. Triptyque and the Michelin Guide both credit Kunz as its originator.

In practice, meat and fish become seasoning rather than the centre of the plate. Michelin reported in 2021 that the tasting menus were already moving further, towards 95/5, with 3 or 4 flavours per dish. I like the rule because you can check it. Most ‘plant-forward’ menus never say what that means. This one gives you a number.

Michelin made the rule the headline of Triptyque’s Green Star in 2022, writing that ‘Niven Kunz’s 80% plant-based/menu philosophy rule, which is implemented at Triptyque in Wateringen, is a true source of inspiration.’

Want to try the idea at home? My colleague Clara’s homemade veggie burger recipe is the place to start.

Westland produce on every plate

Triptyque cooks with produce from the Westland, the glasshouse district around Wateringen that Michelin calls Europe’s largest.

In 2021, Michelin reported that the team toured Westland growers twice a week and bought from 17 local suppliers. Pike-perch and river crayfish supplied the protein. The dining room had 26 seats, 32 at a push, with 4 people in the kitchen. Michelin’s current listing adds a mostly Dutch wine list and menus printed on paper made from tomato pulp.

Triptyque’s signature dish: the carrot steak tartare

Triptyque’s best-known dish is a plant-based ‘steak tartare’ made from carrots that are worked over several days, then finished with a pickle gel and croutons, as Michelin describes it.

The Gault&Millau jury, as reported by Rodi.nl, described it as finely chopped carrot with gherkin and toasted breadcrumbs. It also singled out a peeled local tomato with rose and tomato bouillon. The tartare is the closest Triptyque gets to a beef classic: the shape and garnish of a tartare, with no beef at all. If you come from the burger world, it is the dish I would book for.

Is Triptyque in The Hague?

No: Triptyque is in Wateringen, in the Westland area just outside The Hague, at Plein 13G, 2291 CA Wateringen. It is still the obvious answer when people search for a Michelin star restaurant near The Hague.

Does Triptyque serve a burger?

No: Triptyque has no burger on its current menu, and I found no record of 1 on past menus either.

The only link to burgers I could document is an event. FavorFlav announced a ‘perfect burger’ battle between Kunz, described as ‘a big fan of everything green’, and barbecue world champion Jord Althuizen. It was set for a Tuesday 23 February at Ellis Gourmet Burger, Prinsengracht 422, Amsterdam. The piece gives no year, and neither the recipes nor the winner were ever published.

That puts Triptyque in the same category as Sergio Herman: a starred chef the burger world talks about, with no burger to show for it. My profile of Sergio Herman and Le Pristine in Antwerp covers the other case. For the opposite, a Dutch Michelin chef who did put a burger on the menu, read my review of Ron Gastrobar in Amsterdam.

Niven Kunz and Triptyque: FAQ

Who is Niven Kunz and where does he cook now? Niven Kunz is a Dutch chef, born in 1981 in Gorinchem according to Masters Expo, who earned a Michelin star at 24. He now runs Triptyque in Wateringen with Virginie van Bronckhorst-Kunz.

Who is Virginie van Bronckhorst-Kunz? She is Triptyque’s co-owner. The official site says she runs the team, service, marketing, partnerships and management, and also works as a general practitioner (huisarts).

Does Triptyque have a Michelin star? Yes. Triptyque holds 1 Michelin star and, since the 2022 Michelin Guide Netherlands, a Michelin Green Star.

Is Triptyque vegetarian or vegan? No. Triptyque is vegetable-led but not vegetarian: under the 80/20 rule, up to 20% of a dish can be meat or fish. Ask the restaurant directly about fully plant-based options.

How much does a meal at Triptyque cost? As of 11 November 2026, the official site lists a 3-course star menu at €55, Saturday lunch at €75 to €125 and a Saturday-evening Menu Triptyque at €150. The two official pages differ, so confirm the price when you book.

What is a Michelin Green Star, in Triptyque’s case? The Green Star is a separate Michelin distinction for restaurants with sustainable practices, awarded on top of any stars. When Michelin gave Triptyque its Green Star in 2022, it singled out Kunz’s 80% plant-based rule as ‘a true source of inspiration’.

How do I book a table at Triptyque? Book through the Frank app, call +31 174 725 505 or email info@triptyque.nl.
