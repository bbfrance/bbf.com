Josh Boutwood Savage, Manila: Is It Open in 2026?

Savage, Josh Boutwood's open-fire restaurant in BGC, closed in July 2024 to relocate. No new address is confirmed, and it never served a burger.

By Léo, Restaurants writer, Oceania and Southeast AsiaPublished October 18, 2026

Status, checked 18 October 2026: Savage is closed. Josh Boutwood’s open-fire restaurant in Bonifacio Global City (BGC) served its last dinner at Arya Residences on 30 June 2024. Its official page still says the team is “taking a little break to allow time to relocate.” A reopening at East Gallery Place in BGC was announced for the second or third quarter of 2025. No source confirms it happened. Boutwood also runs Helm in Makati, the only restaurant awarded 2 stars in the first Michelin Guide to the Philippines, in October 2025.

1 correction before we go further, because this is a burger site. Savage never served a burger. None of the 2018 to 2024 menus I checked lists 1, and Boutwood said so himself in 2018. His burger work happened at a separate stall, Burgers & Bits, in a Makati food hall. I cover both below and keep current facts apart from history.

Is Savage in BGC open in 2026?

No. As of 18 October 2026, Savage is not operating anywhere, and no new address, opening hours or reopening date has been published.

I checked the official page at joshboutwood.com/restaurants/savage on 18 October 2026. It reads “we are taking a little break to allow time to relocate” and sends readers to Instagram @savagemnl for updates. The same website already shows Helm’s 2 Michelin stars, awarded in October 2025. So this is not a forgotten page from 2024. The relocation notice is still the official position.

Be careful with older listings. Tatler Asia’s Savage page still shows the Arya Residences address as if the restaurant were trading, and Tripadvisor and Restaurant Guru carry stale data too. If you are planning a Manila trip, treat any address, phone number or opening hours you find for Savage as historical. My advice: don’t build a BGC evening around it until @savagemnl posts an address.

Where is Savage relocating?

Savage announced in 2024 that it would move to East Gallery Place in BGC. That move has not been confirmed as completed.

In July 2024, Spot.ph reported the relocation to East Gallery Place. In January 2025, the same outlet listed Savage among the year’s expected openings, “sometime between the second and third quarter” of 2025. That was a plan. More than a year after that window closed, I found no report of an opening and no new address on the official site. A missed window and a notice still up more than 2 years after the closure: I would not plan around a reopening in 2026. I will update this page the day Savage confirms one.

What is Savage? Manila’s pre-industrial, open-fire restaurant

Savage is a restaurant built entirely on cooking with fire: wood, charcoal, smoke and ash, with no gas and no electric stoves in the kitchen.

Boutwood called it “purely pre-industrial cuisine” (GMA News). The cooking method, not a national cuisine, defines the menu. A dish belongs at Savage because it was grilled over oak and charcoal, smoked or finished in embers, wherever the recipe comes from.

He also framed Savage as the relaxed sibling of his tasting-menu work. In 2018 he compared his casual projects to “how Savage is compared to The Test Kitchen and Helm” (Philstar). In 2024 he went further and called it “my little bastard… the Jon Snow of the restaurants” (Spot.ph). Savage was where the fine-dining chef cooked louder, smokier food.

Savage’s signature dishes and 2018 prices

Savage’s best-known dishes were the deviled eggs, the charred romaine, the dry-aged rib-eye, the barramundi and a Swedish kladdkaka for dessert. The prices below date from 2018 and are historical.

Tatler Asia describes the signatures as deviled eggs with ox heart and ash, a dry-aged rib-eye grilled over oak and charcoal with smoked butter, mustard leaf emulsion and sea salt, and barramundi with preserved lemon and Kalamata olives.

2021: “Savage had to grow up”

When Savage came back after the lockdown closures in 2021, Boutwood cut the menu down and put produce and technique at the center.

He summed up the change in one line to Tatler Asia: “Savage had to grow up.” The fire stayed. The menu got shorter, and each dish leaned harder on what the kitchen did with one ingredient. ABS-CBN reported that old favorites stayed alongside the revamped dishes.

2024: closure after 6 years

Savage closed its Arya Residences dining room on 1 July 2024, after 6 years, with a farewell dinner the night before.

Manila Bulletin covered the 30 June 2024 dinner. Rappler, GMA News, Spot.ph and Esquire reported the closure, and every one of them framed it as a relocation, not an ending. GMA called it a farewell with “a new chapter” ahead.

Does Josh Boutwood make burgers? Burgers & Bits at The Grid

Yes, but not at Savage. Boutwood’s burger project was Burgers & Bits, stall 6 at The Grid Food Market in Power Plant Mall, Rockwell, Makati, which opened in 2018.

He was clear about the split. “I don’t have burgers and chicken in any of my other restaurants,” he told Philstar in August 2018. “This is our fun concept where I get to cook food that I want to eat when I’m not working, quite like how Savage is compared to The Test Kitchen and Helm.” So the chef who cooked rib-eye over oak at Savage made his burgers in a food hall across town.

Who is Josh Boutwood? The chef behind Helm’s 2 Michelin stars

Josh Boutwood is a British-Filipino chef whose restaurant Helm, in Makati, holds 2 Michelin stars, the top distinction in the first Michelin Guide to the Philippines.

He was born in England to a Filipino father and an English mother and spent his childhood between England, Boracay and Spain (Wikipedia). Long before Helm, he ran a restaurant in Boracay called Alchemy. He closed it in 2012 to become corporate executive chef of The Bistro Group, the company behind Italianni’s, TGI Fridays and Fish & Co. in the Philippines (BusinessWorld).

Helm: from 10 seats in BGC to 2 Michelin stars

Helm opened on 21 August 2018 as a 10-seat restaurant at Arya Residences, the same BGC building as Savage. It moved to Ayala Triangle Gardens in Makati in April 2023, with 24 seats.

Josh Boutwood’s restaurants

Josh Boutwood’s official website lists 5 restaurants: Helm, Ember, Juniper, The Test Kitchen and Savage, plus a catering service.

  • Helm: tasting-menu restaurant, Ayala Triangle Gardens, Makati. 2 Michelin stars.
  • Savage: open-fire restaurant, closed for relocation since July 2024.
  • The Test Kitchen, Ember and Juniper: listed on joshboutwood.com. I did not verify their current addresses or status for this profile.

Is Savage in the Michelin Guide?

No. Savage does not appear in the Michelin Guide’s Taguig listing, and it was closed during the inspection period of the first Philippine guide.

I checked guide.michelin.com on 18 October 2026. Some articles online attach Michelin recognition to Savage, but the guide itself does not list it. Boutwood’s stars belong to Helm.

I did not eat at Savage for this piece. It was closed for the whole period I could have visited. This is a dated, sourced profile, not a review, so it carries no rating.

FAQ: Josh Boutwood and Savage

Is Savage in BGC still open? No. Savage closed its Arya Residences location on 1 July 2024. As of 18 October 2026, its official page says it is taking a break to relocate, and no reopening has been confirmed.

Where is Savage relocating, and when will it reopen? The announced new site is East Gallery Place in BGC. A reopening was planned for Q2 to Q3 2025, but it has not been confirmed, and no date has been published. Instagram @savagemnl is the official channel for updates.

Who owns Savage restaurant in Manila? Josh Boutwood is the chef and creator of Savage. The current ownership structure, including any role of The Bistro Group, is not publicly confirmed.

What does “pre-industrial” cooking mean at Savage? It means cooking only with wood, charcoal, smoke and ash, with no gas or electric stoves. Boutwood defined the restaurant by this method rather than by a single cuisine.

What were Savage’s signature dishes? Deviled eggs with ox heart and ash, charred romaine, a dry-aged rib-eye grilled over oak and charcoal, barramundi with preserved lemon and olives, oak-smoked salmon and kladdkaka. In 2018 the 16 oz rib-eye cost ₱2,600 and the deviled eggs ₱260.

Where can I eat Josh Boutwood’s burger in Manila? His burger stall was Burgers & Bits, stall 6 at The Grid Food Market, Power Plant Mall, Rockwell, Makati, opened in 2018. Manila Bulletin reported in March 2023 that he was no longer involved but that his burger was still served there. Its 2026 status is unconfirmed.

Which restaurant in the Philippines has 2 Michelin stars? Helm, Josh Boutwood’s restaurant at Ayala Triangle Gardens in Makati. It received 2 stars on 30 October 2025 in the first Michelin Guide Manila and Environs | Cebu, and it is the only 2-star restaurant in that selection.
