In-N-Out Secret Menu: 12 Orders and How to Ask

Which In-N-Out secret menu orders the chain confirms, which ones crews make on request and which viral items are fake, with official calories.

By Maria, News editorPublished November 21, 2026

The In-N-Out secret menu isn’t secret at all. The chain publishes its own “Not-So-Secret Menu” with six options: Double Meat, 3x3, 4x4, Grilled Cheese, Protein Style and Animal Style. Crews will also make a handful of off-menu orders if you ask by name, such as the Flying Dutchman, Animal Style fries and a mustard-grilled patty. And some viral “secret” items, Monkey Style and Doggy Style Fries among them, are flat-out fake.

I built this guide from In-N-Out’s official nutrition PDF (January 2026) and from press coverage that quotes the chain, its staff and its owner. Every order gets one of three labels: official (on the Not-So-Secret Menu), known order (made on request but not listed) or legend (no longer served, or never was). Calories appear only where In-N-Out publishes them. Prices don’t appear at all: In-N-Out publishes no national prices for these orders, and they vary by state and restaurant.

The 12 In-N-Out secret menu orders at a glance

Twelve orders make up the full In-N-Out hidden menu: six that In-N-Out lists itself, five that crews make on request (two of them not guaranteed), and one 2004 legend you can no longer order.

  1. Animal Style (official): mustard-cooked patty, pickles, extra spread, grilled onions
  2. Protein Style (official): lettuce wrap instead of a bun
  3. Double Meat (official): two patties, no cheese
  4. 3x3 (official): three patties, three slices of cheese
  5. 4x4 (official): four patties, four slices of cheese
  6. Grilled Cheese (official): no meat, two slices of cheese
  7. Flying Dutchman (known order): two patties and two slices of cheese, nothing else
  8. Animal Style fries (known order): fries with melted cheese, spread and grilled onions
  9. Mustard-grilled patty (known order): the mustard step on its own
  10. Wish Burger (known order, not guaranteed): a veggie burger with no patty
  11. Pup Patty (known order, not guaranteed): a plain unsalted patty for your dog
  12. 100x100 (legend): the 100-patty burger of 2004, no longer allowed

What’s on the official In-N-Out Not-So-Secret Menu

In-N-Out’s official Not-So-Secret Menu lists six options: Double Meat, 3x3, 4x4, Grilled Cheese, Protein Style and Animal Style. Fox LA (March 2023) and Chowhound (March 2025) report the list in the same terms. I could not load In-N-Out’s own page while writing, so the list rests on those two matching sources.

The chain doesn’t guard any of it. In-N-Out told Fox LA: “It’s just the way some of our customers like their burgers prepared, and we’re all about making our customers happy.” My take: if you order one thing from this whole guide, make it Animal Style. It’s the only modifier that changes how the patty itself is cooked, and In-N-Out thought enough of it to trademark the name.

1. Animal Style: what it means at In-N-Out

Animal Style means a patty cooked with mustard, plus pickles, extra spread and grilled onions, and you can put it on any burger. Wikipedia and Chowhound describe it the same way. Animal Style is a registered trademark of In-N-Out.

The origin story comes from the company itself. According to a book by In-N-Out owner Lynsi Snyder, reported by The Takeout, a burger was first cooked with mustard in 1961 for rowdy customers that employees nicknamed “animals.” No independent source confirms it, so read it as In-N-Out’s version rather than settled history.

2. Protein Style: the bunless In-N-Out burger

Protein Style swaps the bun for large lettuce leaves, and it has been around since the 1970s, according to Wikipedia. It’s another registered trademark: In-N-Out’s own nutrition PDF prints it as “Protein Style®”.

If you’re cutting carbs, the numbers make the case. A Double-Double Protein Style with onion comes to 460 calories and 12 g of carbs. With the bun, the same burger is 610 calories (In-N-Out nutrition PDF, January 2026). That’s 150 calories gone just by losing the bread.

3. Double Meat: a Double-Double without cheese

The Double Meat is two patties with lettuce, tomato and spread, onions optional, and no cheese, according to Fox LA and Chowhound. Put simply, it’s a Double-Double minus the two slices of American cheese.

4. The 3x3: three patties, three slices of cheese

The 3x3 stacks three patties and three slices of cheese with the standard toppings. It extends the Double-Double, which In-N-Out’s nutrition PDF lists as a bun, two 100% beef patties, lettuce, tomato, spread and two slices of American cheese, with or without onions. The 3x3 name is trademarked too.

5. The In-N-Out 4x4: the biggest burger you can order

The 4x4 has four patties and four slices of cheese, and in practice it’s as big as In-N-Out crews will go. The Takeout (March 18, 2025) and Wikipedia both call it the maximum. The Takeout also points out that In-N-Out publishes no official size limit, so the cap is house practice, not written policy. Like the 3x3, the 4x4 name is trademarked.

6. Grilled Cheese: the meat-free option

The In-N-Out Grilled Cheese has no meat: two slices of cheese, lettuce, tomato and spread, onions optional, per Chowhound and Fox LA. It’s the only meat-free order on the official list.

Off-menu orders In-N-Out makes on request

Beyond the official six, crews make several well-documented orders that aren’t on the Not-So-Secret Menu. Ask by name and most restaurants will know what you mean. The last two are shakier, and I explain why.

7. The In-N-Out Flying Dutchman

The Flying Dutchman is two patties and two slices of cheese and nothing else: no bun, no sauce, no vegetables. It isn’t on the official page; Wikipedia and the blog Rather-Be-Shopping both describe it this way. Honestly, I’d skip it. Protein Style also drops the bun but keeps the lettuce, tomato and spread, which is most of what makes an In-N-Out burger taste like one.

8. Animal Style fries

Animal Style fries are In-N-Out fries topped with melted cheese, spread and grilled onions. Wikipedia specifies two slices of cheese; The Daily Meal lists the same three toppings. The base fries are made from potatoes prepared in the restaurant and cooked in 100% sunflower oil, and a 125 g order has 360 calories (In-N-Out nutrition PDF, January 2026). In-N-Out publishes no figure for the Animal Style version, and I won’t guess one.

In-N-Out Animal Style fries topped with melted cheese, spread and grilled onions, next to a hamburger and a cup of ketchup on a red tray. (Photo: trumanlo, CC BY-SA 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:In-N-Out\_Burger\_hamburger\_and\_animal\_fries.jpg)

9. Mustard-grilled patty

A mustard-grilled patty is the mustard step of Animal Style on its own, without the extra spread, pickles and grilled onions. The Daily Meal (November 2022) explains that you can order it this way. It’s the order for people who want the tang without the full sauce load.

10. Wish Burger (veggie burger)

The Wish Burger is a burger with no patty, and only secondary guides such as Cozymeal and Bustle use the name. It isn’t on the official page. My advice: drop the nickname and ask for a “veggie burger” at the counter.

11. Pup Patty

The Pup Patty, a plain unsalted patty for dogs, shows up in several guides, but its status is shaky. Mental Floss lists the “Scooby Snack”, another name for it, among fake secret menu items. Treat it as something some customers report getting, with no guarantee your restaurant will make it.

The 100x100 and other fake In-N-Out secret menu items

Not every viral In-N-Out order is real. The 100x100 did happen, once, but you can’t order it today. Monkey Style and Doggy Style Fries were never on any menu.

12. The 100x100: what really happened

On October 31, 2004, in Las Vegas, blogger Will Young and seven friends ordered a 100x100: 100 patties and 100 slices of cheese. The receipt came to $97.66 including tax, according to Tasting Table, The Takeout and Wikipedia. In-N-Out capped burger sizes afterwards. The Takeout puts the 100x100 at 19,490 calories, its own estimate rather than an official figure.

True or false: the viral secret menu legends

Here’s where the most-shared claims stand, based on what In-N-Out staff and its owner said in July 2024.

  • Monkey Style (Animal Style fries inside the burger): false. Employees debunked it in July 2024 after an Instagram video by the account Hungry Hotline (Fox LA, July 23, 2024; Mental Floss, July 26, 2024).
  • Doggy Style Fries (fries, cheese and a hot dog): false. In-N-Out doesn’t even sell hot dogs. Lynsi Snyder commented: “Thanks for exposing these fake menu items.”
  • Lemon Fries, Roadkill Fries and the Scooby Snack: fake, according to Mental Floss.
  • Neapolitan Shake: disputed. Fox LA listed it among custom drinks in 2023, but Mental Floss called it fake in 2024, so I left it out of the 12.
  • A burger bigger than a 4x4: false in practice. Crews treat the 4x4 as the maximum, even though no official limit is published.

How to order from the In-N-Out hidden menu

Order secret menu items by name at the counter or drive-thru, and put the modifier after the burger. Crews know the vocabulary, so keep it short.

  • “Double-Double, Animal Style” for the full Animal Style treatment on a Double-Double.
  • “Cheeseburger, Protein Style” for the lettuce-wrapped version.
  • “A 4x4” or “a 3x3” for the big stacks.
  • “A Flying Dutchman” for patties and cheese only.
  • “Fries, Animal Style” for the loaded fries.
  • “Mustard grilled” if you want only the mustard-cooked patty.
  • “A veggie burger” rather than “Wish Burger.”

Animal Style and Protein Style are ways of preparing a burger, not burgers in their own right, so either one works on any burger on the menu.

Where to try the In-N-Out secret menu

You can only order the In-N-Out secret menu in the United States. In-N-Out has 442 restaurants there and none abroad, as of August 19, 2026 (ScrapeHero data). Southern California is the heartland: the chain opened in Baldwin Park, east of Los Angeles, in 1948, and our page on where to eat burgers in Los Angeles covers the area. Further north, my colleague Daniel’s guide to In-N-Out in San Jose lists four locations with their hours.

In-N-Out secret menu FAQ

What items are on the In-N-Out secret menu?

The official Not-So-Secret Menu lists six options: Double Meat, 3x3, 4x4, Grilled Cheese, Protein Style and Animal Style. Crews also make the Flying Dutchman, Animal Style fries and a mustard-grilled patty on request.

Is the In-N-Out secret menu really secret?

No. In-N-Out publishes the main options itself on a page called the Not-So-Secret Menu, and the chain says these are simply ways customers like their burgers prepared.

What does Animal Style mean at In-N-Out?

Animal Style means a mustard-cooked patty with pickles, extra spread and grilled onions. You can order it on any burger, and on fries as Animal Style fries.

Can you order a burger bigger than a 4x4 at In-N-Out?

In practice, no. The 4x4 is the largest burger crews will make, according to The Takeout and Wikipedia, even though In-N-Out publishes no official limit. The chain capped sizes after a 100x100 was ordered in Las Vegas in 2004.

How many calories are in an In-N-Out Protein Style burger?

According to In-N-Out’s January 2026 nutrition PDF, with onion, a Hamburger Protein Style has 210 calories, a Cheeseburger Protein Style 280 and a Double-Double Protein Style 460, with 12 g of carbs.

Which In-N-Out secret menu items are fake?

Monkey Style and Doggy Style Fries are fake: In-N-Out employees debunked them in July 2024. Mental Floss also lists Lemon Fries, Roadkill Fries and the Scooby Snack as fake.
