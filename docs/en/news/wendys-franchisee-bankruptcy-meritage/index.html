Wendy's Franchisee Bankruptcy: Meritage Explained

Meritage ran 314 Wendy's when it filed for Chapter 11. About 93 have closed, and Wendy's wants to buy up to 163. Here is what it means.

By Noah, Industry reporterPublished November 24, 2026

Meritage Hospitality Group, which ran 314 Wendy’s restaurants in 15 states, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on September 17, 2026. Sales had been falling for 2 years, food and labor costs had hit a record and beef prices had jumped. As of October 29, about 93 of its Wendy’s had closed: roughly 60 before the filing and 33 since. On November 5, Wendy’s signed a letter of intent to buy up to 163 of the remaining restaurants. That deal is not final. Meritage asked the court on day one to keep paying its roughly 9,000 employees; how many jobs went with the closed stores has not been reported.

I cover franchising and chain economics for Burger Breton, and this case is hard to follow: dozens of short updates, and outlets that rarely agree on the numbers. So I rebuilt it from court-based reporting by Restaurant Dive, Nation’s Restaurant News, CNBC and the Associated Press, plus Wendy’s own SEC filing. Every figure below is tied to a named source. Where sources disagree, I say so. Facts are current as of November 12, 2026, and I will update this page as the case moves.

What happened: the Wendy’s franchisee bankruptcy in brief

Meritage Hospitality Group filed for Chapter 11 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Western District of Michigan on Thursday, September 17, 2026, 1 day after Wendy’s sent it a notice terminating its franchise agreements (AP via ABC News, Restaurant Dive).

At the time of the filing, Meritage operated 314 Wendy’s, about 5% of the brand’s U.S. system, plus 1 Bojangles and 5 independent restaurants, across 15 states (CNBC). The company is based in Grand Rapids, Michigan. According to Newsweek, it was founded in 1986 and bought its first 28 Wendy’s, in Michigan, in 1998.

Map of Meritage Hospitality Group’s Wendy’s footprint. The pin marks the company’s headquarters in Grand Rapids, Michigan. The 42 restaurants proposed for sale to Divine Infinity Operations are in Tennessee, Arkansas, Mississippi and Missouri. Meritage ran restaurants in 15 states, but no verified list of all of them is public. Robert E. Schermer Jr. is Meritage’s CEO. Kevin Cleary serves as Chief Restructuring Officer in the case (NRN).

Meritage’s 2025 net loss was more than $26 million. Its preliminary, unaudited January 2026 release put it at $26.3 million (Meritage via StockTitan), while September press coverage cites $31.5 million. Outlets give widely different figures for total assets and liabilities, and I could not verify any of them in court documents, so I have left them out.

Why Meritage went bankrupt

Meritage went bankrupt because sales fell for 2 years while costs rose to record levels, leaving its restaurants with the weakest margins in 30 years, according to the company (Restaurant Dive).

Falling sales

Same-store sales dropped 7.2% in 2025, then 8.3% in the first half of 2026. Revenue fell 14% over that half-year, and Meritage lost $23 million (Restaurant Dive). The wider fight over cheap meal deals is part of the backdrop: I covered it in our burger value wars of 2026 report.

Meritage Hospitality revenue: $668.8 million in 2024 and $617.7 million in 2025 (down 7.6%), then $274 million in the first half of 2026, down 14% from $318 million a year earlier. Meritage posted a $23 million net loss for that half-year. (Chart: Burger Breton, CC BY 4.0; data: Meritage 2025 preliminary results and Restaurant Dive, checked November 12, 2026)

Beef prices and the cost squeeze

Beef prices rose almost 19% in the second quarter of 2026, driven by a smaller cattle herd, tariffs and import restrictions from Mexico. That hits Wendy’s operators hard: the brand’s protein mix is about 80% beef and 20% chicken (Restaurant Dive). Even before that, in 2025, food, packaging and labor ate a record 66.1% of Meritage’s sales. For context on how burger prices have moved worldwide, see our Big Mac Index 2026.

What Meritage tried before filing

Meritage cut hard before going to court. It closed about 60 money-losing restaurants, stopped or changed breakfast in about 120, cut more than $7 million in overhead, did 23 sale-leaseback deals worth $52 million and tested catering through ezCater in 150 restaurants (Restaurant Dive). It was not enough. Its forbearance agreements with lenders expired on August 18, 2026.

What Chapter 11 bankruptcy means

Chapter 11 is the U.S. bankruptcy procedure that lets a company keep operating under court supervision while it restructures its debts, sells assets or closes units. Chapter 7 is different: the business stops and its assets are liquidated to pay creditors.

For Wendy’s customers, that difference matters. Meritage said it would keep its restaurants open during the case, and it asked the court for permission to keep paying wages and benefits without interruption (AP). Chapter 11 also lets a debtor reject unprofitable leases and contracts with court approval. That is the tool behind the October 23 order, which authorizes 20 to 60 more closures.

Wendy’s vs. Meritage: the franchise dispute

Wendy’s and Meritage disagree on whether the franchise agreements were validly terminated, and the court has not ruled on it. Wendy’s says its September 16 termination took effect immediately. Meritage calls it a “non-termination termination”, arguing the notice came through a defective email (NRN).

Wendy’s position, in its own words: “We worked with this franchise organization and its lenders for more than a year to find a sustainable path forward. Ultimately, based on these circumstances, we determined that termination was the appropriate course of action” (AP).

What is the Continuous Operations Fee?

The Continuous Operations Fee is a penalty Wendy’s charges when a franchisee closes restaurants, and it makes up $119.5 million of the $146.9 million Wendy’s is claiming from Meritage. The other $27.4 million is unpaid royalties and fees (NRN). Meritage’s own filing puts that debt lower: its largest unsecured creditor is Quality Is Our Recipe LLC, Wendy’s franchising entity, listed for $24.9 million in deferred franchise fees (CNBC).

Breakdown of the $146.9 million Wendy’s claims from Meritage: $119.5 million in Continuous Operations Fees, the penalty for closing restaurants, and $27.4 million in unpaid royalties and fees. (Chart: Burger Breton, CC BY 4.0; data: AP and Nation’s Restaurant News, checked November 12, 2026)

The fight over which stores to close

Wendy’s has challenged which restaurants Meritage chose to close. The stores Meritage wanted to shut averaged $1.15 million in annual sales and lost $127,000 a year each, against about $2 million in sales for the average Wendy’s (NRN).

Annual sales of the Wendy’s that Meritage wanted to close averaged $1.15 million, against about $2 million for an average Wendy’s. Each of those stores lost about $127,000 a year. (Chart: Burger Breton, CC BY 4.0; data: Nation’s Restaurant News, checked November 12, 2026)

To me, this is the most revealing part of the case. A franchisee closes stores to stop the bleeding. The brand, which collects royalties on every sale and wants its map intact, pushes back. Both are acting rationally. That tension is built into franchising; this case just drags it into a courtroom.

Wendy’s closing locations: how many and where

About 93 Meritage-run Wendy’s had closed as of October 29, 2026, and up to 60 more can be closed under the October 23 court order. On that date, Judge James W. Boyd allowed Meritage to reject leases and franchise agreements for 20 to 60 restaurants with negative EBITDA, with closure lists due by October 30, November 6 or November 13 (TheStreet via Yahoo Finance).

Meritage Wendy’s closures timeline, 2026: about 60 restaurants closed before the Chapter 11 filing on September 17, then waves of 5 on September 25, 5 on October 2 and 10 on October 9, plus 13 closures announced on October 18. That makes about 93 by October 29. A court order of October 23 allows 20 to 60 more. (Chart: Burger Breton, CC BY 4.0; data: TheStreet via Yahoo Finance, checked November 12, 2026)

The final number of closures will not be known until after the November 13 deadline. Some projections go above 150. No court document I could check supports that figure, so treat it as an estimate.

None of the sources I reviewed has published a verified state-by-state closing list. The official case docket, with the filed closure lists, is hosted by the claims agent Kroll. If you want to know whether your local Wendy’s is affected, start there.

Wendy’s buying Meritage restaurants: the 163-store deal

On November 5, 2026, Wendy’s announced a letter of intent with Meritage and its lenders to buy up to 163 restaurants. It is not a done deal: it still needs a definitive agreement, can be topped by a higher bid and requires court approval. No price has been disclosed (Wendy’s 8-K, QSRweb).

Wendy’s CEO Bob Wright put it bluntly: “Closing restaurants is no way to make a system healthier” (QSRweb).

My read: the buyback is as much about control as about system health. If Wendy’s owns those restaurants, it decides which ones stay open, instead of contesting closure lists in court one by one.

The proposed sale of 42 restaurants

Separately, Meritage proposed selling 42 Wendy’s in Tennessee, Arkansas, Mississippi and Missouri to Divine Infinity Operations for $18.6 million. The buyer is linked to Hiren Patel, a Dunkin’ and KFC franchisee (Restaurant Dive). A hearing on the sale was set for November 10, 2026. The result had not been published as of November 12, so the sale remains proposed, not approved.

What happens to the 9,000 Meritage employees

Staff at Meritage’s open restaurants are still being paid: on day one, the company asked the court for permission to keep paying wages and benefits without interruption, and it kept those restaurants running (Restaurant Dive). The picture is less clear for the roughly 93 stores that have closed, and no public figure exists for jobs lost there. If the Wendy’s buyback goes through, staff at up to 163 restaurants would work for Wendy’s corporate instead of a franchisee.

Is Wendy’s in trouble in 2026?

Wendy’s is under pressure but not in bankruptcy: the brand itself is not part of this case. Its third-quarter 2026 results show U.S. same-restaurant sales down 5.7% and global system sales down 5.9%. Wendy’s had 7,194 restaurants (5,711 in the U.S. and 1,483 abroad). It has 203 net closures so far this year: 258 in the U.S., partly offset by net openings abroad (Wendy’s 8-K). Under CEO Bob Wright, the company calls its turnaround plan “Dave’s Way”.

Wendy’s restaurant count at the end of Q3 2026: 5,711 in the U.S. and 1,483 internationally, 7,194 in total. The chain has 203 net closures so far in 2026, including 258 in the U.S., and Meritage ran 314 of its U.S. restaurants at the filing. FAQ: Wendy’s franchisee bankruptcy

What is the difference between Chapter 11 and Chapter 7? Chapter 11 lets a company keep operating while it restructures under court supervision. Chapter 7 is a liquidation: the business stops and its assets are sold to pay creditors. Meritage filed under Chapter 11 and has kept most of its restaurants open.

How many Wendy’s are closing because of the Meritage bankruptcy? About 93 Meritage Wendy’s had closed by October 29, 2026: 60 before the filing and 33 after. A court order of October 23 allows 20 to 60 more closures. The final total will only be known after the November 13 deadline.

Is my Wendy’s closing? The bankruptcy only affects Meritage-run Wendy’s, about 5% of U.S. locations at the filing. Wendy’s is also closing underperforming restaurants across its own system, with 258 net U.S. closures by the end of Q3 2026. For Meritage stores, the closure lists are filed on the official case docket hosted by Kroll; no verified state-by-state list has been published in the press.

Why can a franchisee go bankrupt when the brand survives? The brand earns royalties on sales, while the franchisee pays for food, labor and rent. When sales drop and costs rise, the franchisee’s margin disappears first. Meritage’s food, packaging and labor costs hit 66.1% of sales in 2025.

What is the Continuous Operations Fee? It is a penalty Wendy’s charges when a franchisee closes restaurants. Wendy’s claims $119.5 million in these fees from Meritage, on top of $27.4 million in unpaid royalties and fees.

Is Wendy’s buying the Meritage restaurants? Wendy’s signed a letter of intent on November 5, 2026, to buy up to 163 of them. The deal still needs a final agreement and court approval, and other bidders can top it. No price has been disclosed.

Do rising beef prices threaten other burger chains? Beef rose almost 19% in the second quarter of 2026. Chains whose menus lean heavily on beef feel it most; at Wendy’s, beef is about 80% of the protein mix. That is my reading of the numbers, not a finding in the case.
