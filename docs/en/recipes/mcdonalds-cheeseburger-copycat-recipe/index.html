McDonald's Cheeseburger Copycat Recipe (1.6 oz)

Make a McDonald's cheeseburger at home: 1.6 oz patty salted on the griddle, room-temp American cheese, rehydrated onions. Two burgers in 20 minutes.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 27, 2026

This McDonald’s cheeseburger copycat recipe makes 2 burgers in about 20 minutes, using the quick warm-water soak for the onions. Each one is a thin 1.6 oz (45 g) beef patty salted and peppered on the griddle, a slice of room-temperature American cheese, rehydrated minced onions, a pickle slice, ketchup and mustard, on a soft bun steamed for 10 to 15 seconds. It is my homemade reproduction, not McDonald’s recipe: anything the chain does not publish is marked as my estimate.

My opinion, for what it’s worth: of McDonald’s two single-patty burgers, the cheeseburger is the one worth copying. Without the cheese, the plain Hamburger is mostly bun. The melted slice is what holds the whole burger together.

What’s in a McDonald’s cheeseburger: the official ingredients

McDonald’s US lists a 100% beef patty seasoned with a pinch of salt and pepper, a tangy pickle, chopped onions, ketchup, mustard and a slice of melty American cheese, all on a regular bun (McDonald’s US). The UK list is the same: regular bun, mustard, ketchup, onions, a pickle slice, a cheese slice and a beef patty (McDonald’s UK FAQ).

McDonald’s US says the burger has no artificial flavors, no artificial preservatives and no added colors from artificial sources. The one exception is the pickle, which contains an artificial preservative. In 2026 the Cheeseburger is still on the US burger menu alongside the Big Mac, the Quarter Pounders, the McDouble, the Daily Double, the Double Cheeseburger and the Hamburger. The full lineup is in our roundup of new McDonald’s burgers in 2026.

What cheese does McDonald’s use on the cheeseburger?

In the US, McDonald’s uses pasteurized process American cheese. It is a processed cheese made with emulsifying salts such as sodium citrate, which let it melt into a smooth sheet instead of splitting into oil and curd (Tasting Table, January 18, 2026; Mashed, March 18, 2025).

Mike Haracz, a former corporate chef at McDonald’s, names Walmart’s Great Value Deluxe Pasteurized Process Sliced American as the closest store-bought match (Mashed). He also says to skip individually wrapped slices and to let the cheese come to room temperature before it goes on the patty. I do both.

How much real cheese is in the slice? Figures online disagree, so I’m not quoting one.

McDonald’s cheeseburger copycat ingredients (2 burgers)

You need 90 g (3.2 oz) of ground beef, two buns, two cheese slices and small amounts of onion, pickle, ketchup and mustard. Only the patty weight comes from published reports. The rest are my quantities.

  • 90 g (3.2 oz) ground beef, 80/20 (my choice; McDonald’s does not publish its fat ratio)
  • 2 plain, soft hamburger buns
  • 2 slices deli-style American cheese, such as Great Value Deluxe, not individually wrapped
  • 1 tbsp dehydrated minced onion (estimate)
  • 2 dill pickle slices, 1 per burger (estimate)
  • 2 tsp ketchup, 1 per burger (estimate)
  • 1 tsp yellow mustard, ½ per burger (estimate)
  • Fine salt and black pepper, a pinch each per patty

How to make a McDonald’s cheeseburger at home: step by step

Rehydrate the onions, sear a thin patty, season it on the griddle, add the cheese right after the flip and steam the bun. The onions take longest, so start with them.

1. Rehydrate the onions

Cover the dehydrated minced onion with water. Mike Haracz recommends about 2 hours in cold water in the fridge (Food Republic). Short on time? Chowhound gives about 15 minutes in warm water (Chowhound). Either way, drain them well.

2. Bring the cheese to room temperature

Take the slices out of the fridge now. A room-temperature slice melts over a hot patty in seconds. A cold slice just sits there, stiff at the corners.

3. Shape thin 45 g patties

Split the beef into two 45 g (1.6 oz) portions. Press each one between two sheets of parchment into a very thin round, a little wider than the bun, since it shrinks as it cooks. I press until the meat is almost see-through at the edges. Don’t salt the meat: McDonald’s seasons on the grill, not in the mix.

4. Sear and season the patties

Heat a dry griddle or cast-iron skillet over high heat; McDonald’s does not publish its grill temperature, so this is my estimate. Lay the patties down and season the tops with a pinch of salt and pepper (The Takeout). Leave them alone for 1 to 2 minutes, until the edges brown. Don’t press them: they are already thin, and pressing only pushes the juice out.

5. Flip and add the cheese

Flip each patty and lay a cheese slice on it immediately, as the reference copycats do (The Daily Meal; Food.com). Cook until the center reaches 160°F (71°C), the USDA safe temperature for ground beef (USDA FSIS). A patty this thin gets there fast, so keep an instant-read thermometer handy rather than guessing.

6. Warm the buns

Wrap each bun in a paper towel and microwave it for 10 to 15 seconds, as The Daily Meal and Food.com recommend. That gives you the soft, slightly steamed bun of the original. McDonald’s does not say whether it toasts its buns, so this step is my choice.

7. Assemble the cheeseburger

Dress the top bun with 1 tsp ketchup, ½ tsp mustard, half the drained onions and one pickle slice. Set the cheese-topped patty on the bottom bun and close. The order and amounts are my estimates. Then wrap each burger in paper for a minute. Don’t skip this: it is when the cheese settles into the bun and the burger starts to taste like the one from the restaurant.

Why this recipe tastes like the original

The recipe gets the four things you notice in a McDonald’s cheeseburger: a thin patty, salt and pepper on the surface, fully melted processed cheese and small, mild onions. The 45 g patty matches the regular patty, so the meat-to-bun ratio is right. Several popular copycats, including Top Secret Recipes and Mashed, use a 57 g (⅛ lb) patty, which makes the burger heavier than the original.

Salting on the griddle puts the seasoning in the crust, not inside the meat. Room-temperature American cheese melts the way the original does. Cheddar won’t: it turns greasy before it goes smooth, and in my opinion that alone gives most copycats away. Rehydrated onions are soft with a mild bite. Fresh chopped onion is crunchy and sharp, which is not what you get at the restaurant.

McDonald’s cheeseburger calories and nutrition

A US McDonald’s Cheeseburger has 300 calories, 13 g fat, 31 g carbohydrates and 15 g protein in a 120 g serving (FatSecret, checked November 24, 2026). That is about 50 calories more than the 250-calorie Hamburger, which is roughly what the cheese slice adds. These are the restaurant’s figures. Nobody has lab-tested this homemade version. Is the McDonald’s cheeseburger the same in other countries?

Not quite. The build is the same everywhere, but the cheese and the calories change by market. Product pages for McDonald’s Belgium, Switzerland and France appear to list a processed cheddar slice rather than American cheese. We couldn’t open those pages to check, so treat that as unconfirmed.

Top view of a McDonald’s cheeseburger bought in Greece in 2025: the same build as in the US, though European product pages describe a processed cheddar slice rather than American cheese. (Photo: Filippos Fragkogiannis, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=158210064)

Calories vary too, according to FatSecret listings: 300 kcal in France (a provisional figure), 306 kcal in Spain, and 323 kcal per 115 g in Mexico, where a second listing says 409 kcal. If you want the US taste, buy American cheese.

McDonald’s cheeseburger history: 19 cents in 1948

The cheeseburger was on McDonald’s first menu. In 1948 the McDonald brothers cut back the menu of their San Bernardino, California, restaurant, which sold a hamburger for 15 cents and a cheeseburger for 19 cents. The slice of American cheese was a 4-cent upgrade (EatHealthy365; McDonald’s corporate history). I couldn’t find a reliable 2026 price: it varies by restaurant, and the figures online don’t agree. The rest of the chain’s story is on our McDonald’s brand profile.

FAQ

What’s in a McDonald’s cheeseburger? A 100% beef patty seasoned with salt and pepper, one slice of American cheese, a pickle, chopped onions, ketchup and mustard on a regular bun, according to McDonald’s US.

What cheese does McDonald’s use? In the US, it uses pasteurized process American cheese, a processed cheese that melts smoothly thanks to emulsifying salts. Former McDonald’s chef Mike Haracz names Great Value Deluxe American slices as the closest store-bought match.

How much does a McDonald’s cheeseburger patty weigh? The regular patty weighs 1.6 oz (about 45 g) raw. The Hamburger, the Cheeseburger and the McDouble all use it.

How do I make McDonald’s onions at home? Soak dehydrated minced onions in cold water in the fridge for about 2 hours, or in warm water for about 15 minutes, then drain them well. I prefer the cold soak when I can plan ahead.

Is the McDonald’s cheeseburger bun toasted? McDonald’s does not say. For a soft bun like the restaurant’s, wrap it in a paper towel and microwave it for 10 to 15 seconds.

How many calories are in a McDonald’s cheeseburger? In the US, 300 calories per 120 g burger, about 50 more than the Hamburger.

Is this the official McDonald’s recipe? No. It is a homemade copycat built from the published ingredients and the reported patty weight. The sauce amounts, griddle heat and beef fat ratio are my estimates.
