Lotteria Shrimp Burger Recipe (Korean Copycat)

Minced shrimp and Alaska pollock in a crisp panko crust, tartar sauce and lettuce: Lotteria Korea's Ria Shrimp burger, rebuilt at home for two.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 27, 2026

To make a Lotteria shrimp burger at home, blend 120 g of raw peeled shrimp with 70 g of Alaska pollock (or surimi) into a paste, shape two patties, coat them in flour, egg and panko, and fry them at 170–175 °C (338–347 °F) for 3 to 4 minutes, until the center reaches 63 °C (145 °F). Serve each patty on a toasted 9–10 cm bun with 2 tablespoons of tartar sauce and shredded iceberg lettuce. The pollock is the part most copycats miss. It gives the Korean original its texture, halfway between a fried fish fillet and shrimp, with no whole shrimp to bite into.

This is my reconstruction, not Lotteria’s recipe: the chain has never published one. I modeled it on the Ria Shrimp (리아 새우) that Lotteria sells in South Korea as of November 2026, and every proportion is an estimate built from the burger’s third-party weight and the composition described by the Korean food press.

What is in the Lotteria shrimp burger?

The Lotteria shrimp burger is a bun, a breaded and fried patty of minced Alaska pollock and shrimp, lettuce and a mayonnaise-style tartar sauce. Korean food site Drinkeat described the patty in 2021 as minced pollock flesh (명태 연육) mixed with shrimp, both chopped before frying, so you never feel a whole piece of shrimp (Drinkeat via Daum, November 2, 2021). A July 2026 Korean press piece also describes a pollock-based patty served with a tartar-style mayonnaise (Daum, July 26, 2026).

So yes, there is real shrimp in it. It is just blended into a fish paste, which is why the burger eats like a crisp fish fillet with a sweet shrimp finish rather than like a fried prawn.

Why it tastes like a breaded fish fillet

The Lotteria shrimp burger tastes like breaded fish because its patty is partly Alaska pollock, the same white fish used in surimi and many fish sandwiches. The English-language “Korean shrimp burger” recipes I found while working on this one skip it entirely and use whole shrimp, gochujang or kimchi. Good burgers. Not this one.

Blending the two into a smooth paste also changes the bite. The patty is springy and uniform, close to a fish cake, and the panko crust carries all the crunch.

Lotteria shrimp burger copycat recipe for 2

This copycat Lotteria shrimp burger makes 2 burgers. Once the egg white and starch are in, the paste weighs about 230 g, so each patty is about 115 g raw. That is a little more generous than the original: Lotteria’s whole burger weighs 179 g according to third-party nutrition databases, on a bun 9.5 cm across. For an exact match, shape three smaller patties and freeze the third.

  • Prep time: about 25 minutes
  • Cook time: about 10 minutes
  • Yield: 2 burgers
  • Frying temperature: 170–175 °C (338–347 °F)
  • Safe internal temperature: 63 °C (145 °F)

Ingredients

Shrimp and pollock patties

  • 120 g (4.2 oz) raw peeled shrimp, deveined
  • 70 g (2.5 oz) Alaska pollock fillet, or surimi
  • 1 egg white
  • 1 tbsp potato starch or cornstarch
  • Salt and ground white pepper

Breading and frying

  • 3 tbsp plain flour
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • About 40 g (¾ cup) panko breadcrumbs
  • Neutral oil for frying, about 3 cm (1¼ in) deep in a small pan

Assembly

  • 2 soft brioche-style buns, 9–10 cm (3.5–4 in) across
  • 4 tbsp tartar sauce (2 per burger)
  • Iceberg lettuce, finely shredded
  • Optional: 2 tbsp pink Thousand Island-style sauce (1 per burger)

How to make a Lotteria shrimp burger, step by step

1. Blend the shrimp and pollock paste

Pulse the shrimp, pollock, egg white, starch, a pinch of salt and a pinch of white pepper in a food processor until you get a smooth, sticky paste with no visible shrimp pieces. Do not stop early. The original has no whole shrimp under the tooth, and a lumpy paste gives you a different burger.

2. Shape the patties

Divide the paste into 2 equal portions of about 115 g and shape each into a flat round slightly wider than your bun. Wet your hands with cold water first, or the paste will stick to everything.

3. Bread the patties

Dredge each patty in flour, dip it in beaten egg, then press it into panko on both sides and around the edge. Handle gently, since the paste is soft until it is fried.

4. Fry at 170–175 °C

Heat the oil to 170–175 °C (338–347 °F) and fry the patties for 3 to 4 minutes, turning once, until golden and 63 °C (145 °F) in the center. I fry one patty at a time in a small pan so the oil does not drop below 170 °C. Drain on a rack, not on paper, so the crust stays crisp.

5. Toast the buns

Toast the cut sides of the buns in a dry pan until lightly golden. A toasted face keeps the tartar sauce from soaking into the crumb.

6. Assemble the burger

Spread 1 tablespoon of tartar sauce on the bottom bun, add the hot patty, then 1 more tablespoon of tartar and a handful of shredded lettuce. Add the optional pink sauce on the lettuce if you use it, close with the top bun and eat right away.

Which sauce goes in the Lotteria shrimp burger?

Tartar sauce is the safe answer: Korean sources describe the Lotteria shrimp burger’s sauce as a mayonnaise-style tartar. Drinkeat calls it mayonnaise in one line and tartar in the next, and Lotteria does not publish the formula.

Some recipe summaries add a second, pink Thousand Island-style sauce on top of the lettuce. I could not confirm it from a reliable source, so it stays optional here. My advice: make the burger once with tartar alone, then decide. Japan offers a related data point, not proof for Korea: Lotteria Japan’s Kappa Ebisen Burger, a 2015 limited edition sold for ¥380, paired a breaded shrimp patty with a sakura-shrimp tartar sauce and lettuce (SoraNews24).

The patty is where this recipe earns the Lotteria name. The bun is where it can drift. Lotteria does not describe its bun officially, and a third-party database lists a butter-bun variant without a date, so I use a soft enriched bun that toasts well. A dense or seeded bun would bury the shrimp.

A short history of Lotteria’s shrimp burger

Lotteria launched its shrimp burger in Japan in 1977 and brought it to South Korea after the chain arrived there in 1979, according to Tastecooking. Korean press calls it a classic that has been on the menu since 1979, “47 years” in 2026 (Daum).

Rivals followed. MOS Burger launched a shrimp burger in 2000, and McDonald’s Japan launched the Ebi Filet-O in 2005; Tastecooking reports that the Ebi Filet-O made $10 million in sales in three months.

In July 2024, with its first brand identity change in 12 years, Lotteria Korea renamed the 새우버거 (shrimp burger) to 리아 새우, Ria Shrimp, with the new identity rolling out from September 2024 (Womennews, Hankyung). Japan no longer has any Lotteria: the last restaurant became a Zetteria on March 31, 2026, which is why this recipe follows the Korean version. Ria Shrimp vs. Ria Thick Shrimp

The Ria Thick Shrimp is Lotteria Korea’s thicker 2026 spin-off: its patty mixes whole shrimp meat with minced shrimp, where the classic Ria Shrimp uses a smooth pollock-and-shrimp paste. It launched on July 16, 2026, in a horseradish sauce version and a spicy tomato version made with ttaksaeu shrimp extract (Dailian).

It sold more than 1 million units in two weeks, 242% of Lotteria’s target, according to Dailian. It costs ₩7,900 alone or ₩9,900 as a combo, and comes with lettuce and tomato (Daum).

To lean toward the Thick Shrimp at home, chop part of the shrimp into small chunks and fold them into the paste instead of blending everything, then shape a thicker patty and add a slice of tomato. Fry it to the same 63 °C (145 °F) in the center. If you can only make one, I would still start with the classic: the smooth patty is the version no other chain copies.

FAQ

Is there real shrimp in the Lotteria shrimp burger? Yes. The patty contains shrimp blended with minced Alaska pollock, so the shrimp is real but not in whole pieces.

Can I make the shrimp patties in an air fryer or the oven? Yes, but the crust will be less crisp than deep-fried. Spray the breaded patties with oil on both sides and cook them until the panko is golden and the center reads 63 °C (145 °F) on a thermometer.

Can I make the patties ahead and freeze them? Yes. Bread the raw patties, freeze them flat on a tray, then bag them. Fry them straight from the freezer at the low end of the 170–175 °C range and check that the center reaches 63 °C (145 °F).

What is the difference between the Lotteria shrimp burger and McDonald’s Japan’s Ebi Filet-O? The Lotteria shrimp burger came first, in 1977, and the Ebi Filet-O followed in 2005, inspired by its success. The Lotteria patty’s signature is the pollock blended with minced shrimp.

How many calories are in a Lotteria shrimp burger? A Lotteria shrimp burger has 492 kcal for 179 g, with 26 g of fat and 810 mg of sodium, according to third-party nutrition databases.

Can I use surimi instead of pollock? Yes. Surimi is made from white fish such as pollock, so it gives the patty the same springy, fish-cake texture.
