Lentil Burger Recipe: Patties That Don't Crumble

A gram-by-gram ratio for firm, crisp lentil patties, cooked in a skillet or baked, with vegan, red lentil and mushroom variations.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 28, 2026

A lentil burger that holds together comes down to one ratio: 400 g (14 oz) of well-drained cooked lentils, 150 g (5.3 oz) of sautéed mushrooms and carrot, 40 g (1.4 oz) of a dry binder such as blitzed oats or breadcrumbs, and 1 egg or a flax egg. Shape 4 patties of about 140 g (5 oz) each, chill them for 15 to 30 minutes, then sear them in a hot skillet for 3 to 4 minutes per side or bake them at 375 °F (190 °C) for 20 to 25 minutes. Get the water out and the patties stop crumbling. Every step below comes with the reason it’s there.

What is a lentil burger?

A lentil burger is a meatless burger whose patty is made of cooked lentils mashed with vegetables, spices and a binder, then pan-fried or baked until it forms a crust. Brown or green lentils give it a firm, slightly coarse bite, plus fiber and plant protein. It belongs to the same family as the black bean patties in my veggie burger recipe that doesn’t fall apart. Lentils are less work, though: no soaking, and they cook in about 20 minutes.

The lentil burger ratio: ingredients for 4 patties

For 4 lentil patties of about 140 g (5 oz) each, use 400 g of cooked lentils for every 150 g of vegetables and 40 g of dry binder. Keep those three numbers and you can swap the vegetables and spices freely. I weigh everything here. A cup of cooked lentils packs differently every time, and an extra spoonful of wet lentils is enough to tip a patty from firm to mushy.

  • 400 g (14 oz) cooked brown or green lentils, drained and patted dry
  • 150 g (5.3 oz) finely chopped mushrooms and carrot, about half and half
  • 40 g (1.4 oz) rolled oats blitzed to a coarse flour, or dry breadcrumbs
  • 1 large egg, or a flax egg for a vegan burger: 1 tbsp (about 7 g) ground flaxseed and 3 tbsp water
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Neutral oil for the pan
  • 4 burger buns and your usual toppings

Short on time? Canned lentils work. A standard US can is 15 oz (425 g), but that weight includes the liquid, so one drained can falls well short of 400 g. Open two, drain, weigh, and keep the leftovers for a salad. Directo al Paladar, the Spanish recipe site, starts its version from a 500 g jar of cooked lentils for the same reason.

Which lentils work best in a burger?

Brown and green lentils are the best choice for a burger because they keep their shape after cooking. My default is plain brown lentils. French Le Puy green lentils are the premium option: they cook in about 20 to 25 minutes without soaking, according to the French producer Sabarot. They were the first dried pulse to receive an AOC in France, by decree on August 7, 1996, and have carried the European PDO label since 2008 (INAO specification). Use them if you have them, but don’t make a special trip. Red lentils cook in 10 to 15 minutes and break down into a purée, so they need the adjustments in the red lentil section below.

How to make lentil burgers, step by step

Making lentil burgers takes six steps: cook and dry the lentils, sauté the vegetables, pulse the mix, add the binder, chill the patties and cook them over high enough heat. Allow about 1 hour 10 minutes from dry lentils, closer to 50 minutes with canned ones.

1. Cook and dry the lentils

Simmer dry brown or green lentils in unsalted water until tender, about 20 to 25 minutes, or 10 to 12 minutes in a pressure cooker. Drain them in a colander for a few minutes, then spread them on a clean towel and pat them dry. Water is the first reason lentil patties fall apart. Don’t skip the towel.

2. Sauté the mushrooms and carrot

Cook the chopped mushrooms and carrot in a little oil over low heat until they are soft and the pan is dry. Directo al Paladar gives its vegetables 20 minutes on low heat, and that’s not too long: mushrooms release water for a long time, and you want all of it gone before it reaches the patty. Season with the garlic powder, cumin, salt and pepper, then let the mix cool.

3. Pulse the lentils, keeping some texture

Pulse the lentils with the vegetables in a food processor in short bursts, or mash them with a fork. Stop when about half the lentils are broken and the rest are still whole. A smooth purée turns pasty and cracks in the pan. The whole lentils are what hold the patty together.

4. Add the binder and shape the patties

Mix in the blitzed oats or breadcrumbs and the egg or flax egg, then divide into 4 portions of about 140 g (5 oz). Shape them into patties roughly 2 cm (¾ inch) thick and press the edges smooth so they don’t crack. If the mix sticks to your hands, add binder a tablespoon at a time.

5. Chill the patties for 15 to 30 minutes

Rest the shaped patties in the fridge for 15 to 30 minutes on a plate lined with parchment paper. The binder absorbs moisture and the patties firm up. Directo al Paladar chills its patties for 15 minutes before cooking. If you can, give them the full 30.

6. Cook in a hot skillet or in the oven

Heat a thin film of oil in a skillet over medium-high heat and cook the patties for 3 to 4 minutes per side. Leave them alone until a crust forms. In the oven, bake them on a parchment-lined sheet at 375 °F (190 °C) for 20 to 25 minutes, flipping them halfway through. Serve on toasted buns.

How to keep lentil burgers from falling apart

Lentil burgers fall apart for five reasons: wet lentils or vegetables, a mix blended too smooth, too little dry binder, no rest in the fridge and a pan that isn’t hot enough. Picky Eater and One Green Planet in English and Directo al Paladar in Spanish all point to the same causes. If you only fix one, fix the water.

Problem What happens Fix
Wet lentils or vegetables The patty is soft and sticks to the pan Drain the lentils, pat them dry, sauté the vegetables until the pan is dry
Mix blended too smooth The patty turns pasty and cracks Pulse only until half the lentils are broken
Too little dry binder Nothing holds the pieces together Add oats, breadcrumbs or chickpea flour, 1 tbsp at a time
No rest in the fridge The patty breaks when you flip it Chill 15 to 30 minutes before cooking
Pan not hot enough No crust forms, so the patty steams and slumps Medium-high heat, oil shimmering, don’t move the patty for 3 minutes

Skillet or oven: the best way to cook lentil patties

The skillet gives lentil patties the crispest crust in 6 to 8 minutes. The oven cooks a full batch hands-off at 375 °F (190 °C) in 20 to 25 minutes. Down Madrid’s baked lentil burgers go in at 180 °C (350 °F) for 20 to 25 minutes, depending on thickness. My rule: skillet for 4 burgers eaten right away, because the crust helps hold the patty together, and oven when I’m cooking for a crowd or filling the freezer.

Vegan lentil burger: binders without egg

To make a vegan lentil burger, replace the egg with a flax egg: stir 1 tbsp (about 7 g) of ground flaxseed into 3 tbsp of water and leave it 5 to 10 minutes, until it turns gel-like. Some recipes use more flax. A Spanish recipe published by Pulzo uses 10 g of ground flaxseed for 6 tablespoons of water, and Cookie and Kate’s lentil burgers list the eggs as optional, with a flax egg as the swap.

America’s Test Kitchen goes another way in its Vegan Lentil and Mushroom Burgers. It binds lentils, mushrooms, bulgur and cashews with ⅓ cup of aquafaba, the liquid from a can of chickpeas, and 2 cups of panko, for 12 burgers.

Lentil and mushroom burger: the umami variation

Mushrooms give a lentil burger a deeper, more savory flavor, and my base recipe already uses them. You can push them further. Directo al Paladar adds 100 g of fresh shiitake for 500 g of lentils, and America’s Test Kitchen uses 1 lb of cremini mushrooms in its 12-burger batch. More mushrooms means more water to cook off, so give them the time.

Red lentil burgers: how to adjust the recipe

Red lentil burgers work if you treat the red lentils as a purée rather than a base. They cook in 10 to 15 minutes and break down, so they bring far more moisture than brown or green lentils. Cook them only until just tender, drain them in a fine sieve, and add extra dry binder a tablespoon at a time until the mix holds its shape. Curry spices and sweet potato suit them well. For a first batch, though, I’d stick with brown lentils.

Lentil burger nutrition: protein and calories

Cooked lentils provide 9.0 g of protein, 116 calories and 7.9 g of fiber per 100 g, according to USDA FoodData Central (Lentils, mature seeds, cooked, boiled, without salt). Each patty in this recipe contains 100 g of cooked lentils, so the lentils alone give it about 9 g of protein and 116 calories, before the binder, egg and oil.

Lentil burger FAQ

How do you keep lentil burgers from falling apart?

Dry the cooked lentils well, pulse them only until half are broken, add a dry binder such as oats or breadcrumbs, chill the patties for 15 to 30 minutes and cook them over medium-high heat. Excess moisture and a pan that isn’t hot enough are the most common reasons they crumble.

Can you freeze lentil burgers, and how do you reheat them?

Yes. Freeze the shaped patties raw, separated by parchment paper, and use them within 2 to 3 months. Bake them straight from frozen at 375 °F (190 °C), adding a few minutes to the usual 20 to 25, and check that the center is hot. Cooked patties keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge in an airtight container.

Can you make lentil burgers with canned lentils?

Yes. Canned lentils skip the cooking step and work well as long as you rinse, drain and pat them dry. A 15 oz (425 g) US can includes its liquid, so one drained can won’t reach 400 g. Open two cans and weigh out what the recipe needs.

What can I use instead of egg in lentil burgers?

A flax egg is the most common replacement: 1 tbsp (about 7 g) of ground flaxseed stirred into 3 tbsp of water and left until it thickens. Oats, breadcrumbs, chickpea flour and aquafaba also bind lentil patties without egg.

Is it better to bake or pan-fry lentil burgers?

Pan-frying gives a crisper crust in 3 to 4 minutes per side, which also helps the patty hold together. Baking at 375 °F (190 °C) for 20 to 25 minutes, flipping halfway, is easier for large batches and uses less oil.

How much protein is in a lentil burger?

A 140 g patty from this recipe contains 100 g of cooked lentils, which provide about 9 g of protein according to USDA data. The egg and oats add a little more.
