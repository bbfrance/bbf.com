Whataburger Copycat Recipe: Classic No. 1 at Home

Make two classic Whataburgers at home: thin salted 4 oz patties, yellow mustard, pickles, diced onion and a toasted 5-inch bun, with what the chain confirms.

By Julia, Recipes writerPublished November 26, 2026

This Whataburger copycat recipe makes two homemade Whataburgers built like the Texas chain’s classic No. 1. Each has a thin, wide beef patty salted on the griddle, with yellow mustard, lettuce, tomato, dill pickles and diced onion on a toasted 5-inch bun. No cheese, no ketchup, no mayo. You need 8 oz (226 g) of 80/20 ground beef, two soft 5-inch buns and a hot griddle, and the patties take 6 to 8 minutes.

It is a home reproduction, not Whataburger’s official recipe. The chain publishes its toppings and its bun size. It does not publish its mustard formula, its bun recipe or its seasoning. So below I keep three things apart: what Whataburger publishes, what others report, and the choices I made to fill the gaps. Of all the Whataburger builds I write up, this is the one I’d cook first. With no cheese to hide behind, every shortcut shows.

What comes on a Whataburger?

A classic Whataburger comes with mustard, lettuce, tomato, pickles and diced onions on a toasted 5-inch bun, around a 100% American beef patty. That is the build listed in Whataburger’s press kit and confirmed by Mashed. The chain says its patties are “100% fresh, never-frozen American beef” that “don’t hit the grill until you order it”, and that its vegetables are chopped in-house daily (Whataburger, August 23, 2023).

Mustard is the only sauce, and that is the whole point: this is a Texas mustard burger, not a ketchup-and-mayo one. Want a slice of American cheese on it? That is a different build, covered in my Whataburger with cheese copycat recipe.

Whataburger copycat ingredients for 2 burgers

To make two homemade Whataburgers you need 8 oz of 80/20 ground beef, two 5-inch buns, yellow mustard, iceberg lettuce, tomato, dill pickles, white onion and salt. The quantities are my choice, not an official spec. They match our other Whataburger recipes, so you can switch between builds without re-weighing anything.

  • 8 oz (226 g) ground beef, 80/20, split into 2 patties of 4 oz (113 g)
  • 2 soft white burger buns, 5 inches (13 cm) across
  • Softened butter, for the cut sides of the buns
  • 2 tbsp yellow mustard (Whataburger Original Mustard if you can find it)
  • 8 dill pickle chips
  • About 1.4 oz (40 g) white onion, finely diced
  • 4 to 6 tomato slices
  • Iceberg lettuce
  • Salt

How to make a Whataburger at home, step by step

To make a Whataburger at home, press 4 oz patties thin and 5 inches wide, toast buttered buns, sear the patties 3 to 4 minutes per side on a hot griddle with salt, and stack them with mustard and fresh toppings. A flat-top, a plancha or a cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat all work.

1. Prep the toppings

Finely dice about 1.4 oz (40 g) of white onion, slice the tomato and pull off a few iceberg leaves. Count out 8 pickle chips, 4 per burger. Don’t skip the onion. I think it does more work than the tomato: raw, finely diced white onion gives the sharp bite that stands up to the mustard. Whataburger chops its vegetables daily, so cut yours just before cooking.

2. Shape thin, wide patties

Divide 8 oz (226 g) of 80/20 beef into two 4 oz (113 g) portions and press each one into a thin patty 5 inches (13 cm) across, the width of the bun. I press each ball between two sheets of parchment with the flat bottom of a skillet, then trace around a 5-inch lid to check the size. The patty shrinks a little on the heat. That’s fine: a thin, wide patty cooks fast and still covers most of the bun.

3. Toast the buns

Butter the cut sides of the buns and toast them on the griddle over medium-high heat for 60 to 90 seconds, until golden. Set them aside cut side up.

4. Sear and salt the patties

Lay the patties on the hot griddle and salt them as they cook. Sear 3 to 4 minutes per side. Once they’re on the heat, leave the spatula alone: pressing down only squeezes out the juice.

5. Check the internal temperature

Cook the patties to 160°F (71°C) at the center, the USDA minimum for ground beef. With patties this thin, 3 to 4 minutes per side usually gets you there, but check with an instant-read thermometer rather than trusting the clock. My colleague Clara’s burger patty internal temp guide covers the other doneness levels.

6. Assemble the burger

Spread 1 tbsp of mustard on each bun, then add the patty, 4 pickle chips, the diced onion, 2 or 3 tomato slices and the lettuce, and close it. Whataburger lists the toppings but not a stacking order. This order is mine: the lettuce on top keeps the toasted crown from going soggy.

Why this homemade Whataburger tastes like the original

This recipe tastes like a Whataburger because it copies the four things the chain actually documents: a wide toasted 5-inch bun, mustard as the only sauce, a fresh patty cooked to order, and salt on the griddle. Add anything else and you drift toward a generic diner burger.

The 5-inch bun sets the proportions

The 5-inch bun has been the chain’s signature since 1950. Founder Harmon Dobson worked with the Rainbo bakery to produce it. The standard bun was an inch smaller, so the bakery needed special pans with 8 cavities per sheet (Mashed; St. Mary’s University Research Scholars). In my view the bun is the part most copycats get wrong. Put the same patty on a 4-inch bun and you have a different burger.

Whataburger bun size compared: the 5-inch toasted bun Harmon Dobson had the Rainbo bakery make in 1950 is one inch wider than the standard 4-inch bun of the time, which is why the patty is pressed to 5 inches in this recipe. Mustard, not ketchup

Yellow mustard gives the sharp, vinegary note in every bite. Whataburger does not publish its formula. Mashed relays the claim that it has not changed since the 1950s, but that is marketing, not a verified fact. If you can’t find the branded bottle, plain yellow mustard gets you close. I wouldn’t swap in Dijon: its heat takes over the whole burger.

Fresh, salted and cooked to order

Whataburger says its patties are never frozen and only go on the grill when you order. According to Reddit accounts relayed by Tasting Table (May 22, 2026), the patties are salted on the griddle. Those are employee and customer accounts, not an official statement, and no reliable source confirms any other seasoning blend. So I use salt and nothing else.

Where to buy Whataburger mustard and 5-inch buns

Whataburger Original Mustard is sold in 16 oz bottles in grocery stores. Where you can’t find it, plain American yellow mustard is the closest substitute. The chain’s condiments first reached shelves in 2013 at H-E-B in Texas and Mexico, alongside its Fancy Ketchup and Spicy Ketchup, and are now sold in more than 5,000 stores (Whataburger; Progressive Grocer).

For the bun, look for soft white buns about 5 inches (13 cm) across. If your store only stocks smaller ones, bake your own: my colleague Clara’s homemade burger buns recipe works if you shape each bun to 5 inches. Her buns are brioche, richer than a plain white bun. I’d still take them over a 4-inch supermarket bun, because here the size matters more than the crumb.

What is a Whataburger? A short history of the Texas mustard burger

A Whataburger is the signature burger of the Texas chain of the same name. It was first sold on August 8, 1950, at a stand on Ayers Street in Corpus Christi, Texas. Harmon Dobson and Paul Burton opened it with the goal of a burger “so big it took two hands to hold”, on “a big toasted five-inch bun” (Whataburger, Our History).

Map of Ayers Street in Corpus Christi, Texas, where Harmon Dobson and Paul Burton opened the first Whataburger stand on August 8, 1950, selling a quarter-pound burger on a 5-inch bun for 25 cents. (Map: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL)

The 1950 burger had a quarter-pound patty with fresh lettuce, 3 tomato slices, 4 dill pickles and chopped onions, and it sold for 25 cents (Wikipedia; National Day Calendar). Today the chain has more than 1,200 restaurants, all in the US, as of October 2026 (PR Newswire). It has been headquartered in San Antonio since 2009. Since June 2019 it has been majority-owned by BDT Capital Partners, now BDT & MSD Partners. In 2001 the Texas Legislature designated it a “Texas Treasure”. Whataburger nutrition and price in 2026

A classic Whataburger from the restaurant, on its 5-inch bun, has about 590 calories and 1,220 mg of sodium in the US, according to third-party nutrition databases. These figures describe the chain’s burger, not this homemade version with a 4 oz raw patty. They come from CalorieKing, cross-checked with FatSecret and Mashed (checked November 16, 2026).

Prices vary by restaurant and state, and Whataburger does not publish a national price. A NetCredit value study reported by Fox News on March 24, 2026, priced the classic Whataburger at $5.92 for 3.56 oz of meat, or $1.66 per ounce. That made it the best value of the 14 US chains in the study. Two homemade burgers cost you one pound of beef’s worth of patties and a pack of buns, and you control the salt.

Whataburger copycat recipe FAQ

What is on a classic Whataburger?

A classic Whataburger has a 100% American beef patty with mustard, lettuce, tomato, pickles and diced onions on a toasted 5-inch bun. Cheese is not included by default.

Does a Whataburger have ketchup or mayo?

No. The classic Whataburger uses mustard as its only sauce. You can add ketchup or mayo when you order, since every part of the burger can be customized.

How big is a Whataburger bun?

The Whataburger bun is 5 inches (13 cm) across and toasted. In the restaurant you can also swap it for a small 4-inch bun, a 4-inch wheat bun, Texas toast or no bun at all (Tasting Table).

How much does a Whataburger patty weigh?

Whataburger does not publish its current patty weight. The 1950 original was a quarter pound, and a 2026 NetCredit study measured 3.56 oz (about 101 g) of meat, probably cooked. This recipe uses an estimated 4 oz (113 g) raw.

What mustard does Whataburger use?

Whataburger uses its own mustard, sold in grocery stores as Whataburger Original Mustard in 16 oz bottles. Its formula is not published. Plain American yellow mustard is the closest substitute.

How is a Whataburger patty seasoned?

According to employee accounts relayed by Tasting Table, Whataburger patties are salted on the griddle. The chain has not published its seasoning, and no reliable source confirms anything beyond salt.

Is Whataburger beef fresh or frozen?

Whataburger says its patties are 100% fresh, never-frozen American beef that only goes on the grill once you order.

How many calories are in a Whataburger?

A classic restaurant Whataburger on a 5-inch bun has about 590 calories, 29 g of protein and 1,220 mg of sodium in the US, according to CalorieKing, cross-checked with FatSecret and Mashed.

How much does a Whataburger cost in 2026?

A NetCredit study reported by Fox News in March 2026 priced the classic Whataburger at $5.92. Prices vary by location and state.

Are there Whataburger restaurants outside the US?

No. Whataburger’s more than 1,200 restaurants are all in the US (October 2026). It has no restaurants in Mexico, though its condiments are sold at H-E-B stores there.

What is the difference between a Whataburger, a Whataburger with cheese and a Triple Meat Whataburger?

The classic Whataburger has one patty and no cheese. The Whataburger with cheese adds cheese to the same build, and the Triple Meat Whataburger stacks three patties. Each has its own recipe: the Whataburger with cheese copycat and the Triple Meat Whataburger copycat.
