Double Quarter Pounder Copycat Recipe at Home

Double Quarter Pounder copycat recipe: 2 4.25 oz beef patties seared hard, 2 slices of American cheese, slivered onion and pickles. Ready in 25 minutes.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 20, 2026

This Double Quarter Pounder copycat recipe makes 2 burgers in about 25 minutes. You need 4 120 g (4.25 oz) patties of fresh 80/20 ground beef, salt and pepper, 4 slices of American cheese, slivered yellow onion, dill pickles, ketchup, mustard and 2 sesame seed buns. Sear the patties hard in cast iron until the center hits 160°F (71°C), melt the cheese on top, stack and eat. This is my home reproduction, built from what McDonald’s publishes about the burger. The chain does not publish its own recipe.

What’s on a Double Quarter Pounder with Cheese?

A Double Quarter Pounder with Cheese is 2 quarter-pound patties of 100% beef on a sesame seed bun, with 2 slices of American cheese, ketchup, pickle slices, onions and mustard. That is how the McDonald’s US product page describes it in November 2026. McDonald’s says it seasons the patties with a pinch of salt and pepper, sears them on a flat iron grill, then tops them with slivered onions, pickles and the two cheese slices.

Canada sells the same burger. The McDonald’s Canada page lists 2 quarter-pound patties of 100% Canadian beef, 2 slices of melty cheese, slivered onions and pickles on a sesame seed bun. In Canadian French it is the Double Quart de livre avec fromage.

Nothing on that list is exotic. What makes the burger recognizable is the ratio: a lot of beef, a soft bun, and just enough sharp toppings to cut through the fat.

How much does a Quarter Pounder patty weigh?

A Quarter Pounder patty weighs about 113 to 120 g (4 to 4.25 oz) raw, depending on the source. The original patty was 4 oz (113.4 g) before cooking. McDonald’s raised it to 4.25 oz (120.5 g) raw in 2015, according to Wikipedia and Restaurant Business. Some official US mentions still say a quarter pound, so there is no single certified figure for 2026.

I use 120 g per patty. It matches the 2015 weight, and it is an easy number to hit on a kitchen scale.

Cooked weight is only an estimate. Open Food Facts, a community database, puts the beef at about 57% of the 278 g burger. That works out to roughly 159 g of cooked beef for both patties. Treat it as a ballpark, not an official number.

Why the double needs its own copycat recipe

Most copycat Quarter Pounder with Cheese recipes dismiss the double in 1 line: 2 patties, 1 slice of cheese on each. That skips everything that actually changes. You cook twice the beef, so pan space and timing matter. Two cheese slices need to melt on hot meat. And the burger is tall, so the bun has to hold up.

I learned the cheese part the hard way. On my first try I stacked a cold slice between the two patties, and it was still stiff when I bit in. Now both slices go on in the pan, under a lid.

I also disagree with a common shortcut. Mashed’s copycat uses rehydrated dried onion. That is the right call for a McDouble copycat, because the McDouble uses rehydrated onions. The Quarter Pounder uses fresh slivered onions, so mine does too.

Ingredients for 2 Double Quarter Pounders

These amounts make 2 burgers. The patty weight follows the published 4.25 oz. McDonald’s does not publish its amounts of onion, pickles or sauce, so those are my estimates.

  • 480 g (1 lb 1 oz) fresh ground beef, 80/20, divided into 4 patties of 120 g (4.25 oz)
  • Fine salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 4 slices American cheese
  • 2 sesame seed hamburger buns
  • About 30 g (1 oz) yellow onion, a quarter of a small onion, cut into thin slivers
  • 6 to 8 dill pickle slices
  • 2 tablespoons ketchup
  • 2 teaspoons yellow mustard

Why 80/20 beef? The fat keeps a thick patty juicy through a long sear. My burger patty guide explains how the fat ratio changes texture and shrinkage.

How to make a Double Quarter Pounder at home, step by step

Shape 4 thick patties, toast the buns, sear the beef with salt and pepper, melt the cheese on the patties, then stack and eat right away. Total time is about 25 minutes: 15 minutes of prep and 10 minutes of cooking.

1. Weigh and shape the patties

Divide the beef into 4 portions of 120 g (4.25 oz) on a scale. Press each one into a flat round slightly wider than the bun, because the patty shrinks as it cooks. I press mine to about 12 cm (4.75 in) across. Keep them in the fridge until the pan is hot.

2. Sliver the onion and prep the toppings

Cut the onion into very thin slivers, not dice. Lay out the pickles, ketchup, mustard and cheese next to the stove. Once the patties hit the pan, things move fast.

Onion cut into thin slivers, not diced, on a cutting board: the fresh slivered onion a Double Quarter Pounder copycat needs. Illustrative photo showing the cut: it shows red onion and minced garlic, while the recipe uses about 1 oz of yellow onion and no garlic (1024×768). (Photo: Joy Holland (jchristin), CC BY 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/43205168@N06/19004061322)

3. Toast the buns

Toast the cut sides of the buns in a dry pan until they turn light golden. Set them aside cut side up. A toasted face keeps the bun from soaking up the meat juices.

4. Sear the patties with salt and pepper

Heat a cast iron skillet or griddle over medium-high heat. Lay the patties down and season the top with a pinch of salt and pepper, the way McDonald’s says it seasons on the grill. Cook 3 to 4 minutes per side, and season the second side after flipping. Check with a thermometer: the center must reach 160°F (71°C). My 26 cm (10-inch) skillet only takes 2 patties at a time without crowding, so I cook in 2 batches and keep the first pair warm on a rack in a low oven.

5. Melt the cheese

In the last minute of cooking, put 1 slice of cheese on each patty. Cover the pan for 30 to 60 seconds so the steam melts the slices over the edges.

6. Assemble the burger

Spread 1 tablespoon of ketchup and 1 teaspoon of mustard on each top bun, then add the onion slivers and 3 to 4 pickle slices. Stack two cheese-topped patties on each bottom bun and close the burger.

7. Steam the bun (optional)

Wrap each burger in paper and microwave it for 10 to 15 seconds. This is a copycat trick suggested by Mashed, not a McDonald’s practice. It softens the bun and gives it that slightly steamed, fast-food feel. I skip it with fresh buns and use it when they are a day old.

Why this recipe tastes like the original

This copycat gets close because it copies the 4 things McDonald’s states publicly: fresh beef, a simple salt-and-pepper seasoning, a hard sear on a flat surface and fresh slivered onions. Everything else is a classic cheeseburger build.

Fresh beef matters most. After about 2 years of testing in Dallas and Tulsa, McDonald’s started cooking fresh beef to order on Quarter Pounders in March 2018. By May 2018 it covered most of its roughly 14,000 US restaurants, as AP reported. The fresh beef applies only in the contiguous United States. Elsewhere, the patty may be frozen.

The Quarter Pounder also sits apart from the rest of the menu. McDonald’s ‘Best Burger’ changes of 2023 and 2024 brought softer buns, better-melted cheese, 6 patties per grill load instead of 8, and onions added to patties on the grill. They applied to every burger except the Quarter Pounder, which had already been reworked in 2018, according to CNBC.

The proof that the onions are fresh came from an unhappy source. In the October 2024 E. coli O157:H7 outbreak linked to Quarter Pounders, the CDC pointed to slivered onions supplied by Taylor Farms. The final count was 104 people sick, and the outbreak was declared over on December 3, 2024.

What I can’t copy exactly: McDonald’s does not publish its bun recipe, its cheese specification, its sauce amounts or any grill seasoning beyond salt and pepper. My recipe fills those gaps with ordinary supermarket ingredients. Of all of them, the bun matters most. A brioche bun makes a fine burger, but not this one: it’s too sweet and too rich. Buy the plainest soft sesame bun you can find.

A US Double Quarter Pounder with Cheese has 740 calories and 48 g of protein in a 278 g serving, according to McDonald’s. The figures below come from the McDonald’s US product page, cross-checked against Open Food Facts. Quarter Pounder names around the world

The Quarter Pounder changes names from country to country, so a copycat search abroad may need a different name. The names below come from Wikipedia and McDonald’s own country sites. Most refer to the single Quarter Pounder line. Whether a double version is on the menu, and what goes on it, varies by market.

Market Local name
United States, Canada (English), UK, Ireland Quarter Pounder with Cheese / Double Quarter Pounder with Cheese
Canada (French) Quart de livre avec fromage / Double Quart de livre avec fromage
France, Belgium Royal Cheese
Germany Hamburger Royal Käse
Spain, Spanish-speaking Latin America Cuarto de Libra con Queso
Hungary, Latvia, Poland McRoyal
Switzerland (closest double) Double Cheeseburger Royal

In Germany, don’t confuse the Hamburger Royal Käse with the Hamburger Royal TS: TS stands for Tomate Salat, and that version adds tomato and lettuce. Switzerland’s Double Cheeseburger Royal is the closest European cousin I found: a double portion of beef, 2 slices of melted cheese, thinly sliced pickles, chopped onions, ketchup and mustard.

A short history of the Quarter Pounder

The Quarter Pounder was created in 1971 in Fremont, California, by franchisee Al Bernardin, and joined the national US menu in 1973. Its patty grew from 4 oz to 4.25 oz in 2015, and fresh beef cooked to order arrived in 2018. For the longer story of the burger itself, read our history of the hamburger.

Double Quarter Pounder FAQ

What’s on a Double Quarter Pounder with Cheese?

2 quarter-pound patties of 100% beef, 2 slices of American cheese, slivered onions, pickles, ketchup and mustard on a sesame seed bun, according to McDonald’s US. The patties are seasoned with salt and pepper on the grill.

Is the Quarter Pounder made with fresh or frozen beef?

In the contiguous United States, Quarter Pounders have used fresh beef cooked to order since 2018. Outside that area, the patty may be frozen.

Does McDonald’s season its patties?

Yes. McDonald’s says it seasons Quarter Pounder patties with a pinch of salt and pepper as they cook. It does not publish anything more about its grill seasoning.

What cheese should I use for a Quarter Pounder copycat?

Use American cheese slices, the cheese McDonald’s names for the US burger. They melt into a smooth layer over hot beef in under a minute, where cheddar slices tend to split and release oil.

Fresh or dried onions on a Quarter Pounder?

Fresh. The Quarter Pounder uses slivered fresh onions, as the CDC’s 2024 outbreak investigation confirmed. Rehydrated dried onions belong on the McDouble, not the Quarter Pounder.

What’s the difference between a Double Quarter Pounder and a McDouble?

The Double Quarter Pounder uses 2 fresh patties of about 4.25 oz each, 2 slices of cheese and fresh onions. The McDouble uses 2 frozen 1.6 oz (45 g) patties, 1 slice of cheese and rehydrated onions.

What temperature should a burger patty reach?

Ground beef must reach 160°F (71°C) in the center, per the USDA and Health Canada. Color is not a reliable test, so use a thermometer.

Which bun should I buy for a McDonald’s copycat?

Any plain, soft sesame seed hamburger bun works, toasted on the cut side. Avoid brioche, which is too sweet for this burger. If you’d rather bake your own, follow our homemade burger buns recipe and sprinkle the tops with sesame seeds.

How do I get the soft, steamed McDonald’s bun?

Wrap the assembled burger in paper and microwave it for 10 to 15 seconds. It’s a copycat trick, not something McDonald’s says it does, but it brings a day-old bun back to life.

What is the Double Quarter Pounder called in France?

In France and Belgium, the Quarter Pounder with Cheese is sold as the Royal Cheese. I could not confirm on McDonald’s France whether a Double Royal Cheese is on the menu in November 2026, or whether it matches the US burger.
