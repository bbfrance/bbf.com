MOS Burger Copycat Recipe (Meat Sauce Burger)

MOS Burger copycat recipe for 2: a simmered beef-and-pork meat sauce, raw onion, a thick tomato and a bun toasted inside only, built from MOS's own data.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 28, 2026

This MOS Burger copycat recipe makes 2 burgers in about 60 minutes: a 100% beef patty, mustard, a mayonnaise-style sauce, plenty of raw chopped onion, a hot beef-and-pork meat sauce and one thick slice of tomato, on a bun toasted on the inside only. The classic MOS Burger has no cheese, no lettuce and no pickles. It is a home reproduction built from MOS’s published data, not the chain’s official recipe, which MOS has never released.

I write the Japanese copycats at Burger Breton, and most English-language MOS Burger recipes I found copy each other without naming a single source. So I started from three official MOS documents: the ingredient-origin sheet and the nutrition table dated 11 November 2026, and the company interview ‘モスバーガーのひみつ’ (The secrets of the MOS Burger). Where MOS publishes no quantity, the number below is my estimate, and I flag it. My opinion after cooking it? The meat sauce is the burger. Get it thick and hot, and even a supermarket bun will do.

What is in a real MOS Burger?

A real MOS Burger, as sold in Japan in November 2026, is a 100% beef patty topped with mustard, a mayonnaise-type sauce, a generous pile of chopped raw onion, hot meat sauce and a thick tomato slice, in a bun toasted on the cut side only. MOS describes it as ‘our special meat sauce with fresh tangy tomatoes and meaty rich flavor of beef and pork, topped with chilled tomato slices’ (MOS Burger).

MOS assembles the burger in 13 steps, and the company says developing it took about 180 days (MOS interview). Every item is cooked only after you order. One caveat: this is the Japanese build. MOS Burger outlets in Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Thailand, South Korea and the Philippines may differ, and I have not checked them.

MOS Burger meat sauce: chili, bolognese or something else?

The MOS Burger meat sauce is a tomato-based beef-and-pork sauce closer to a sweet, smooth bolognese than to a spicy chili, though it descends from a chili burger. MOS says it starts from a chicken and pork-bone stock, adds sautéed onions, butter, dairy and red wine, then adjusts it with an in-house spice blend. The recipe has changed several times since 1972, and the exact proportions are not public (MOS interview). If you expect chili heat, you will be disappointed. This is a round, slightly sweet comfort sauce, and the raw onion supplies the bite.

The chili link is historical. Founder Atsushi Sakurada took his inspiration from the chili burger at Original Tommy’s in Los Angeles, a place he visited in the early 1960s (Wikipedia, SoraNews24). My sauce below follows the simmered method of the Japanese site DELISH KITCHEN, which is also an unofficial reproduction, and I swap its water and bouillon for chicken stock to match the official base (DELISH KITCHEN). I kept its celery and carrot even though MOS does not list them: without them the sauce tastes of tomato paste and little else.

MOS Burger ingredients for 2 burgers

You need a small batch of meat sauce, two 3-ounce beef patties, two soft buns and four simple toppings. The sauce batch makes enough for 3 burgers: a smaller amount scorches before it thickens, and the leftover keeps well.

Meat sauce (makes enough for 3 burgers)

  • 3½ oz (100 g) ground meat, half beef and half pork (my choice: MOS does not publish its ratio)
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • ½ celery stalk, finely chopped
  • ½ carrot, finely chopped
  • 1½ tbsp (20 g) salted butter
  • 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • Scant ½ cup (100 ml) red wine
  • ¾ cup (200 g) tomato purée or passata
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar
  • Scant ½ cup (100 ml) chicken stock
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Black pepper and a pinch of nutmeg

Patties and assembly

  • 6 oz (170 g) ground beef, for two 3-oz (85 g) patties
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 2 soft round burger buns
  • 1 tsp yellow mustard
  • 2 tsp mayonnaise
  • ½ small onion, very finely chopped
  • 2 thick tomato slices, about ½ inch (1.2 cm), chilled

Why 3 ounces of beef? MOS does not publish its patty weight. The Double MOS Burger weighs 65.3 g more than the MOS Burger, which points to a cooked patty of about 65 g, so roughly 80 to 90 g (about 3 oz) raw. Treat that as an estimate. For the bun, a soft milk or brioche-style bun works best; I use my homemade burger buns recipe.

How to make a MOS Burger at home, step by step

Make the meat sauce first, keep it hot, then cook the patties and build each burger in under five minutes. The order matters: hot sauce against a chilled tomato is the contrast MOS describes.

1. Sweat the vegetables

Melt the butter in a small saucepan over medium heat. Add the onion, celery and carrot and cook for about 5 minutes, stirring, until soft and translucent but not browned.

Diced onion, celery and carrot on a cutting board, the base you sweat in butter for step 1 of the MOS Burger meat sauce. The leeks in this photo are not part of the recipe (3237 × 2207 px). (Photo: Pigup, CC BY-SA 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mirepoix\_on\_cutting\_board.jpg)

2. Brown the meat

Add the ground beef and pork. Break it up finely with a spoon and cook for about 3 minutes, until no pink remains.

3. Simmer the meat sauce

Stir in the flour for 1 minute, then pour in the red wine and let it bubble for 2 minutes. Add the tomato purée, tomato paste, vinegar, chicken stock, sugar, salt, pepper and nutmeg. Simmer on low heat for 30 minutes, stirring now and then, until thick enough to sit on a spoon. Don’t cut this short: a thin sauce runs straight into the bun. Keep it hot.

4. Shape the patties

Divide the beef into two 3-oz (85 g) balls. Press each into a round about ⅜ inch (1 cm) thick and slightly wider than the bun, since it shrinks as it cooks. Season both sides with salt and pepper just before cooking.

5. Toast the buns on the inside only

Split the buns and place them cut side down in a dry skillet over medium heat for about 1 minute, until golden. Leave the outside soft: MOS toasts only the inner face so the bun does not absorb the sauce.

Soft MOS Burger buns in bags with the MOS logo, stacked in Yamazaki Baking crates in Japan in February 2023. These are the round, pale buns MOS toasts on the cut side only. For the home version, use a soft milk or brioche-style bun (612 × 612 px). (Photo: monoprixgourmet, CC BY 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MOS\_Burger%27s\_Yamazaki\_buns\_in\_2013.jpg)

6. Sear the patties

Cook the patties in a lightly oiled skillet over medium-high heat for about 3 minutes per side, until the center reaches 160°F (71°C), the USDA safe temperature for ground beef.

7. Assemble the MOS Burger

Spread mustard on the bottom bun, add the patty, then the mayonnaise. Pile on the chopped onion, spoon over 2 to 3 tablespoons of hot meat sauce, top with the cold tomato slice and close the bun. This is the order MOS describes in its interview, not an official step-by-step procedure.

Why this copycat recipe tastes like the original

This recipe tastes like a MOS Burger because it keeps the four features MOS describes in its own interview: a hot beef-and-pork meat sauce, a cold thick tomato, lots of raw onion and a bun toasted on one side only. Everything else is my interpretation.

MOS Burger vs MOS Cheeseburger and Teriyaki Burger

The MOS Burger is the meat sauce burger; the MOS Cheeseburger adds a slice of cheese, and the Teriyaki Burger swaps the meat sauce for a teriyaki sauce. MOS’s own nutrition table puts numbers on the difference.

Burger Weight Calories
Hamburger 128.7 g 314 kcal
Teriyaki Burger 170.2 g 385 kcal
MOS Burger 211.2 g 372 kcal
MOS Cheeseburger 226.2 g 425 kcal
Double MOS Burger 276.5 g 532 kcal

For a MOS Cheeseburger-style variation, lay a slice of your usual melting cheese on the patty in the last minute of cooking. MOS does not say which cheese it uses. Where the MOS Burger comes from

The MOS Burger is the chain’s first product, sold from the day the first MOS counter opened in March 1972 near Narimasu Station in Itabashi, Tokyo. MOS Food Services was founded on 21 July 1972 (Wikipedia). Until 23 April 2013 the patty mixed beef and pork; MOS then switched to 100% beef and reworked the sauce (SoraNews24). Today the pork is in the sauce only.

Map of Narimasu Station in Itabashi, Tokyo, the area where the first MOS Burger counter opened in March 1972 and where the MOS Burger was first sold (800 × 450 px). (Map: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL, https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)

In Japan, a MOS Burger costs ¥500 including tax since 15 July 2026, up from ¥470 (Impress Watch). My colleague Hana visited the original district in her guide to MOS Burger in Tokyo, from the first Narimasu store to current prices, and covered MOS Burger in Osaka, where she describes the same chili-style meat sauce. For the chain’s full history and figures, see the MOS Burger brand profile.

MOS Burger recipe FAQ

What is in a MOS Burger?

A MOS Burger contains a 100% beef patty, mustard, a mayonnaise-type sauce, chopped raw onion, a hot beef-and-pork meat sauce and a thick tomato slice, in a bun toasted on the inside. There is no lettuce and no pickle.

Is there cheese in a MOS Burger?

No. The classic MOS Burger has no cheese. The MOS Cheeseburger is the version with cheese, at 226.2 g and 425 kcal, 15 g heavier than the original.

Is the MOS Burger sauce a bolognese or a chili?

It is a tomato meat sauce made with beef and pork, a chicken and pork-bone stock, onions, butter, dairy and red wine. It tastes closer to a sweet bolognese than to a spicy chili, but it was inspired by the chili burger of Original Tommy’s in Los Angeles.

Is the MOS Burger patty beef or pork?

The patty is 100% beef since 23 April 2013, from Australia, the US and Canada. Before that, it was a beef and pork blend. The pork now sits in the meat sauce.

How many calories are in a MOS Burger?

A MOS Burger has 372 kcal for 211.2 g, according to MOS’s nutrition table of 11 November 2026.

How much does a MOS Burger cost in Japan?

A MOS Burger costs ¥500 including tax in Japan since 15 July 2026. It was ¥470 before that price rise.

What is the difference between the MOS Burger and the Teriyaki Burger?

The MOS Burger is topped with meat sauce, onion and tomato; the Teriyaki Burger uses a teriyaki sauce instead. The Teriyaki Burger is lighter at 170.2 g but slightly richer at 385 kcal.

How do I stop the bun from getting soggy?

Toast the cut side of each bun until golden, and leave the outside soft. MOS toasts only the inner face for this exact reason. Spread the mustard and mayonnaise first, and add the hot sauce at the last moment.

Can I make the meat sauce ahead or freeze it?

Yes. Cook the sauce the day before, cool it, keep it covered in the fridge and reheat it until steaming before assembly. Leftovers freeze well in small portions; thaw them in the fridge and reheat until hot.

Sources and method

I built this recipe from MOS Burger’s official English page, its ingredient-origin sheet and nutrition table dated 11 November 2026, and its interview ‘モスバーガーのひみつ’, all checked on 24 November 2026. I used SoraNews24 and Wikipedia for history, Impress Watch for the price, and DELISH KITCHEN’s unofficial reproduction as the base for the sauce. MOS publishes no recipe, so the quantities, the patty weight and the sauce proportions are my own interpretation. Find more copycats and techniques in our burger recipes hub.
