Cast Iron Burgers: How to Sear a Real Crust

Preheat time, oil, minutes per side by thickness and how to keep the smoke down, with 160°F as the only finish line.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 27, 2026

To make cast iron burgers, preheat the empty skillet for at least 5 minutes over medium-high heat, add a thin film of high-smoke-point oil, and sear 1/2-inch patties for 3 to 4 minutes per side. They are done at 160°F (71°C) in the center, the USDA minimum for ground beef (USDA FSIS). Below: patty size, timings by thickness, which oil to use, how to keep the smoke down, and why I reach for cast iron over stainless steel or nonstick every time.

Why cast iron skillet burgers get a better crust

A cast iron skillet builds a better crust because it stays hot when cold meat lands in it. Browning, the Maillard reaction, gets going at around 285°F (140°C) on the surface of the meat. A heavy, properly preheated pan stores enough heat to stay above that line when a fridge-cold patty goes in. A thin pan doesn’t. The temperature drops and the patty steams in its own juice.

So the method below spends more time on preheating than on flipping. Plenty of recipes say “cook over medium-high” and leave it there. To me the crust is decided before the meat ever touches the pan, by the heat you have built up in the iron.

Cast iron burger recipe

This recipe makes four 5-ounce (140 g) burgers on a 12-inch cast iron skillet, cooked two at a time and taken to 160°F (71°C).

Ingredients

  • 1 1/4 lb (560 g) ground chuck, 80/20 (80% lean, 20% fat)
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus black pepper to taste
  • 1 tbsp refined canola or peanut oil
  • 1 tbsp butter (optional)
  • 4 slices American or cheddar cheese (optional)
  • 4 burger buns
  • Toppings of your choice

Don’t go leaner than 80/20. A burger cooked to 160°F needs that fat to stay juicy, and leaner blends dry out. The reasons are in our guide to the best ground beef for burgers.

Equipment

  • 12-inch cast iron skillet
  • Thin, stiff metal spatula
  • Instant-read thermometer
  • Paper towels

Times at a glance

  • Prep time: 10 minutes
  • Cook time: about 20 minutes (5 minutes of preheating, two batches of 6 to 8 minutes, then the buns)
  • Yield: 4 burgers

How to cook burgers in cast iron, step by step

Shape cold patties slightly wider than the bun, preheat the dry skillet, oil it, season at the last second, then sear two at a time without moving them until they release.

1. Shape the patties

Divide the beef into four 5-ounce (140 g) portions. Press each one into a patty about 20% wider than the bun and about 1/2 inch (1.3 cm) thick. Press a shallow dimple into the center so the patty does not dome as it shrinks. Our guide on how to make burger patties shows the shaping in detail. Keep the patties in the fridge until the skillet is ready.

2. Preheat the skillet

Set the empty skillet over medium-high heat for at least 5 minutes. Cast iron maker Field Company gives 5 minutes at medium or medium-high as the minimum (Field Company). I trust the water test more than the clock. Flick in a few drops: when they bead up and skate across the surface instead of hissing away, the pan is ready. Expect 5 to 7 minutes.

3. Add the oil

Add 1 tablespoon of refined canola or peanut oil and spread it into a thin film. Lodge’s advice is to preheat first, then add the oil (Lodge). If you want butter for flavor, Lodge says to start with oil and add the butter just before the meat.

4. Season and sear the first side

Season both sides of two patties with salt and pepper right before they go in. I salt at the last second because salt starts pulling moisture to the surface, and a wet surface browns slowly. Lay the patties in the skillet dimple side up and leave them alone for 3 to 4 minutes, until the edges are deep brown and the meat lets go of the pan without tearing. If it sticks, it isn’t ready. Give it another 30 seconds rather than forcing the spatula.

5. Flip and cook to 160°F

Flip once and sear the second side for 3 to 4 minutes. Add the cheese in the last minute if you use it. Check the center with an instant-read thermometer and take the burgers off at 160°F (71°C). If they are short, keep going and check again every minute. Move them to a plate, pour off or blot the fat in the pan, and cook the second batch the same way.

6. Assemble

Toast the buns cut side down in the fat left from the second batch, about 30 seconds, while the burgers rest. Build the burgers and serve right away.

How long to cook burgers in cast iron by thickness

A 1/2-inch burger needs about 3 to 4 minutes per side in a well-preheated cast iron skillet over medium-high heat, 6 to 8 minutes in total. Thicker patties need longer, and smash patties need far less. Treat these times as starting points from recipe sites and cast iron makers, not rules. Your burner, the weight of your skillet and how cold the meat is all shift them. The thermometer is the only thing I trust to tell me a burger is done.

Patty Weight Time per side Total Done when
Smash patty, well under 1/2 in (1.3 cm) once smashed 2–3 oz (55–85 g) 60–90 seconds 2–3 min 160°F (71°C)
Standard, 1/2 in (1.3 cm) about 4–5 oz (115–140 g) 3–4 min 6–8 min 160°F (71°C)
Thick, 1 in (2.5 cm) 6–8 oz (170–225 g) 5–6 min 10–12 min 160°F (71°C)

Sources: smash times from Field Company; 1/2-inch and 1-inch totals from Taste of Home and other stovetop guides.

Every bar ends at the same finish line: 160°F (71°C) in the center. Many guides also give 3 to 4 minutes per side for a rare 4-ounce burger at 130 to 135°F. I don’t recommend it for ground beef. Grinding spreads surface bacteria through the meat, which is why the USDA sets 160°F (71°C) for ground beef and why France’s Santé.fr recommends cooking ground meat through to 70°C for children and older people (Santé.fr). Our internal temp for burgers guide explains each threshold and how to take a reading.

Cast iron smash burgers

Cast iron also works for smash burgers: use 2 to 3 ounce balls of cold beef, smash them once as soon as they hit the pan, and cook 60 to 90 seconds per side. Field Company recommends 80/20 beef at minimum, 70/30 if you can find it, and a thin layer of neutral oil or beef tallow (Field Company).

J. Kenji López-Alt’s method, as reported by Food52, rubs a heavy 12-inch stainless or cast iron skillet with oil on a paper towel, heats it over medium-high until it just starts to smoke, then smashes each patty once, right away, while the meat is still cold (Food52). Timing is the whole trick. Smash later and you squeeze out juice instead of building crust.

Thick cast iron burgers: the oven preheat

For 1-inch patties, America’s Test Kitchen preheats the cast iron skillet in a 500°F (260°C) oven, then moves it to medium-high heat with 1 tablespoon of vegetable oil and sears 6-ounce patties about 3 minutes per side (America’s Test Kitchen). The oven heats the whole pan evenly, so the center of the skillet is no hotter than the edges.

One caution: ATK takes those burgers off at 120 to 125°F for rare. That is well under the USDA’s 160°F for ground beef. If you use the oven preheat, keep the patties on the heat until your thermometer reads 160°F (71°C).

Which oil to use and how to keep the smoke down

Use a refined oil with a high smoke point, and use very little of it. That alone solves most of the smoke problem. Smoke points vary with refining and freshness, so treat these figures from ThermoWorks as rough guides (ThermoWorks):

Oil Approximate smoke point
Refined avocado 520°F (271°C)
Refined peanut 450°F (232°C)
Refined canola 400°F (204°C)
Unrefined peanut 320°F (160°C)

Pan surface temperatures for cast iron burgers on one scale: browning starts near 285°F (140°C), then come the smoke points of unrefined peanut oil at 320°F (160°C), refined canola at 400°F (204°C), refined peanut at 450°F (232°C) and refined avocado at 520°F (271°C). Nonstick makers cap their pans at about 500°F (260°C), and Germany’s BfR puts PTFE breakdown at about 680°F (360°C). (Chart: Burger Breton, data from ThermoWorks (oil smoke points), BfR (https://www.bfr.bund.de/en/service/frequently-asked-questions/topic/selected-questions-and-answers-on-cookware-ovenware-and-frying-pans-with-a-non-stick-coating-made-of-ptfe/) and cookware makers’ guidance)

  • Use a thin film. One tablespoon for a 12-inch skillet is enough. A pool of oil smokes before the pan is fully hot.
  • Wipe off excess fat. An 80/20 patty releases a lot of fat. Pour it off or blot it with folded paper towels held in tongs between batches.
  • Add butter last. Butter burns at searing heat, so add it just before the meat, as Lodge advises, or skip it.
  • Ventilate before you start. Turn the range hood on high and open a window before the skillet goes on the stove, not when the smoke alarm goes off.

How to cook burgers on the stove: cast iron vs stainless steel vs nonstick

For pan-fried burgers, cast iron is my first choice because it holds heat. A heavy stainless steel skillet comes close. Nonstick is the wrong tool: a real sear needs heat its coating is not built to take.

Cast iron Stainless steel Nonstick (PTFE)
Crust Excellent Very good Weak
Holds heat when the meat goes in Best Good if heavy Poor
High-heat preheat Yes, 5 minutes or more Yes No, never heat empty
Sticking Low once seasoned and hot Releases once the crust forms Lowest
Smash burgers Yes Yes (Kenji López-Alt’s method) Not recommended

The nonstick limit is a safety point, not a matter of taste. Germany’s Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) puts the decomposition of PTFE coatings at around 360°C (680°F) and above, and says coated pans must never be heated empty (BfR). Manufacturers generally give a maximum of about 500°F (260°C). A dry preheat over medium-high is exactly what you should not do with them.

Cast iron burgers FAQ

How long do you cook burgers in a cast iron skillet on each side?

Cook a 1/2-inch patty for about 3 to 4 minutes per side over medium-high heat in a preheated cast iron skillet. A 1-inch patty needs about 5 to 6 minutes per side, and a thin smash patty 60 to 90 seconds. Check with a thermometer: ground beef is done at 160°F (71°C).

Should you oil a cast iron skillet before cooking burgers?

Yes, but only after preheating, and only a thin film. Add about 1 tablespoon of refined canola, peanut or avocado oil to a hot 12-inch skillet. Even fatty 80/20 beef can stick in the first seconds without it.

What heat should cast iron be for burgers?

Use medium-high heat and preheat the empty skillet for at least 5 minutes. The pan is ready when drops of water bead up and skate across the surface. For thick patties, you can preheat the skillet in a 500°F (260°C) oven first.

How do you keep a cast iron skillet from smoking when cooking burgers?

Use a refined oil with a high smoke point, keep it to a thin film, and pour off or blot the fat the burgers release between batches. Add butter only at the end, and turn the range hood on before you start.

Is cast iron better than stainless steel or nonstick for burgers?

Cast iron is best because it stays hot when cold meat goes in, which builds the crust. Heavy stainless steel works well too. Nonstick should not be preheated empty or run at high heat, so it cannot give the same sear.

Should you press burgers in the pan?

Only smash burgers, and only once, right when the cold patty hits the pan. Pressing a burger later in the cook pushes out juice and fat, which dries the meat and makes the skillet smoke.
