Black Bean Burger Recipe: Crispy, Never Mushy

Crispy Tex-Mex black bean burgers that hold together: oven-dried beans, a tortilla-chip binder, a 1-hour chill and a 5-minute sear per side.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 27, 2026

A black bean burger is a patty of mashed black beans bound with egg and crumbs, spiced with cumin and seared in a skillet until it forms a crust. To get one that is crispy outside and not mushy inside, dry the beans before you mash them, chill the mixture for an hour, and sear the patties for 5 minutes per side over medium heat. What follows is my Tex-Mex take on America’s Test Kitchen’s ratios: oven-dried beans, crushed tortilla chips, cilantro and, if you like heat, chipotle.

This is not my veggie burger recipe with sweet potato and brown rice, which leans on cooked starches for body. No sweet potato here, no rice. Just beans, egg, a little flour and crushed tortilla chips, and every step serves the crust. You’ll find more patties, buns and techniques in the Burger Breton recipes section.

Black bean burger recipe: ingredients for 6 patties

This black bean burger recipe makes 6 patties from two 15-ounce cans of black beans, held together by 2 eggs, 2 tablespoons of flour and 1 ounce of crushed tortilla chips. The quantities follow America’s Test Kitchen, whose recipe uses the same beans, eggs, flour, chips and spices for 6 burgers. The optional chipotle comes from Love and Lemons.

  • 2 cans (15 oz / 425 g each) black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tablespoons (30 ml) all-purpose flour
  • 1 oz (28 g, about ½ cup) tortilla chips, finely crushed
  • 4 green onions, thinly sliced
  • 3 tablespoons (45 ml) chopped fresh cilantro
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon (5 ml) ground cumin
  • ½ teaspoon (2.5 ml) ground coriander
  • Optional: chipotle chiles in adobo, up to 2 chiles plus 2 tablespoons (30 ml) of the sauce, as Love and Lemons uses for 6 burgers
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 8 teaspoons (40 ml) vegetable oil, divided between two batches

Drained weights vary from brand to brand, so check the label on your cans rather than trusting a fixed gram figure. And if you add the chipotle, remember that adobo is wet. Love and Lemons’ fix for a mixture that is too loose is simple: 20 more minutes in the fridge.

Timing: 20 minutes of oven drying, 1 hour of chilling and about 20 minutes of searing in two batches, so plan on 1 hour 40 minutes plus mixing and shaping. Most of it is hands-off. Oven: 350 °F (177 °C) for drying, then 200 °F (93 °C) to keep cooked patties warm.

How to keep black bean burgers from falling apart: dry the beans first

Black bean burgers fall apart and turn mushy because canned beans carry a lot of water, so the fix is to remove that moisture before mashing and let the starches absorb what is left. Recipe developers use three drying methods. None of the sources I found has tested them side by side.

Method Source Temperature Time What the source says it does
Paper towels America’s Test Kitchen Room temperature 15 min on a triple layer Removes surface moisture
Low oven Sally’s Baking Addiction 325 °F (163 °C) 15 min Beans slightly dried out, so the patty is not a purée
Hot oven J. Kenji López-Alt, via Tasting Table 350 °F (177 °C) About 20 min A crust forms on the beans, for a crispier patty

My pick is the hot oven: 350 °F (177 °C) for about 20 minutes. This recipe lives or dies by its crust, and that is the one method its source ties to a crispier patty. It’s also how I dry the beans for my veggie burger, so if you’ve made that one, you already know the drill. Short on time? America’s Test Kitchen’s paper towels will do, but they only take water off the surface.

Drying is half the job. America’s Test Kitchen also refrigerates the mixed patty mixture for 1 hour so the starch absorbs the remaining moisture. Tasting Table adds two rules I wouldn’t skip: press the mixture firmly, and keep patties no larger than 4 to 5 inches (10 to 13 cm) across. Bigger bean patties can’t support their own weight.

Homemade black bean burgers, step by step

Homemade black bean burgers take seven steps: dry, mash, mix, chill, shape, sear and build. Don’t skip the chilling hour. It is what lets the patties hold together in the pan.

1. Dry the beans in the oven

Heat the oven to 350 °F (177 °C). Spread the drained, rinsed beans on a rimmed baking sheet in a single layer and bake for about 20 minutes, until the skins look dry and start to split.

2. Mash the beans, leaving chunks

Tip the warm beans into a large bowl and crush them with a potato masher. Stop while some beans are still half whole. Love and Lemons mashes to a coarse texture, and those chunks are what keep the inside from turning into paste.

Three raw black bean patties shaped on a cutting board, with whole beans and diced onion still visible: the coarse mash keeps the inside from turning to paste (2048 × 1365 px). Illustrative photo, made from a Veganomicon recipe, not this one. (Photo: J Doll, CC BY 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Black\_Bean\_Burgers\_(140363379).jpeg)

3. Mix in the binder and spices

Whisk the eggs and flour together, then add them to the beans with the crushed tortilla chips, green onions, cilantro, garlic, cumin, coriander and the chipotle if using. Season with salt and pepper and stir until evenly combined.

4. Chill the mixture for 1 hour

Cover the bowl and refrigerate for 1 hour. America’s Test Kitchen relies on this step to let the starch soak up the last of the moisture.

5. Shape 6 firm patties

Divide the mixture into 6 equal portions. Press each one firmly into a patty ½ to ¾ inch (1.3 to 1.9 cm) thick and 4 to 5 inches (10 to 13 cm) across. Soft patties? Chill them for another 15 to 20 minutes before cooking.

6. Sear 5 minutes per side

Heat 4 teaspoons (20 ml) of oil in a 10-inch (25 cm) nonstick skillet over medium heat. Cook 3 patties for about 5 minutes per side, flipping once, until both faces are deep brown and crisp. Keep them warm in a 200 °F (93 °C) oven while you cook the second batch with the remaining oil.

7. Build the burger

Toast the buns and stack the patties with your toppings. A crisp bean patty needs a soft, rich bun around it, and my homemade burger buns recipe makes the brioche I use for these.

Crispy black bean burgers: troubleshooting

Most black bean burger problems come down to too much moisture or too little pressure, and each one has a specific fix.

Problem Likely cause Fix
Mushy inside Wet beans, or beans mashed to a purée Dry the beans in the oven and leave chunks when mashing
Falls apart when flipped Patty too big, too loose or too warm Keep it 4 to 5 inches (10 to 13 cm) wide, press firmly, chill 15 to 20 minutes
Mixture too wet to shape Adobo sauce or undrained beans Refrigerate 20 more minutes before shaping
No crust Pan not hot enough, or flipping too early Medium heat, enough oil, 5 full minutes before the first flip

Baked or grilled black bean burgers

You can bake or grill black bean burgers, but my advice is to stay with the skillet: the timings in this recipe come from it, and hot oil in direct contact with the patty is what builds the crust. If you grill, Love and Lemons cooks its patties at 400 °F (204 °C) for 8 minutes, then 4 minutes on the second side. For baked black bean burgers, a Spanish-language recipe from Iowa State University Extension uses 375 °F (190 °C). I can’t give you a sourced bake time for this recipe, so flip once and pull the patties when both sides are firm and browned.

Vegan black bean burgers: replacing the egg

To make a vegan black bean burger, replace each egg with a flax egg. Love and Lemons uses 1 tablespoon (15 ml) of ground flaxseed mixed with 2½ tablespoons (37 ml) of warm water for the one egg in its recipe. This recipe has 2 eggs, so make two flax eggs. If you’d rather skip the binder question altogether, my veggie burger recipe with sweet potato and brown rice holds together with starches and no egg.

Storing and freezing black bean burgers

Black bean burgers freeze well once cooked: Love and Lemons keeps cooked patties for up to 2 months in the freezer. That figure comes from a recipe blog, not a food-safety agency, so treat it as an upper limit.

Black bean burger nutrition

Black beans bring protein and iron to the patty. According to USDA FoodData Central, 100 g of canned black beans, drained and rinsed, contain about 120 kcal, 6.91 g of protein, 1.5 g of fat, 218 mg of sodium and 1.69 mg of iron. The eggs, flour, chips and oil add to those numbers, and I haven’t calculated a per-patty total.

Black bean burger FAQ

How do you keep a black bean burger from falling apart? Dry the beans before mashing, chill the mixture for 1 hour, press the patties firmly and keep them 4 to 5 inches (10 to 13 cm) wide. Bigger patties can’t hold their own weight.

Why is my black bean burger mushy inside? The beans held too much water, or they were mashed too smooth. Dry them on paper towels or in the oven, then mash coarsely so whole pieces remain.

Should I use canned or dried black beans? Both work. This recipe uses canned beans, drained and rinsed. Love and Lemons measures cooked beans (3 cups for 6 burgers), so home-cooked dried beans work too, as long as they are well drained and dried before mashing.

Can you make black bean burgers without egg? Yes. Replace each egg with a flax egg: 1 tablespoon (15 ml) of ground flaxseed and 2½ tablespoons (37 ml) of warm water.

Can you cook black bean burgers on the grill? Yes, but chill them well first so they hold together. Love and Lemons grills them at 400 °F (204 °C) for 8 minutes, then 4 minutes after flipping.

Can you freeze black bean burgers? Yes. Cooked patties keep up to 2 months in the freezer, according to Love and Lemons.
