Andalouse Sauce Recipe: Belgium's Fry Sauce

Make Belgian andalouse sauce at home in 10 minutes: mayo, tomato paste and red pepper, in grams and US cups, plus how it differs from Algerian and samurai.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 24, 2026

Andalouse sauce is a cold Belgian sauce made from mayonnaise, tomato paste and finely chopped red bell pepper, sharpened with lemon and a pinch of chili. To make it at home, stir 200 g (about ¾ cup + 2 tbsp) of mayonnaise with 30 g (2 tbsp) of tomato paste and 60 g of finely chopped red pepper, about half a pepper. Ten minutes, no cooking. Belgians squeeze it over their fries; I put it on burgers too.

What is andalouse sauce?

Andalouse sauce is a Belgian friterie sauce: mayonnaise colored and flavored with tomato and sweet peppers, mildly spicy rather than hot. It sits next to ketchup and plain mayo at Belgian fry stands, and it is popular in France and Luxembourg too, according to Wikipedia.

The core is always mayonnaise, tomato paste and peppers, which can be fresh, roasted or jarred pimientos. Some cooks add lemon juice, onion and a mix of red and green peppers, as Chowhound describes. Expect something tangy and faintly sweet, with heat that arrives late and stays gentle.

Is andalouse sauce the same as American fry sauce?

No. In the United States, fry sauce usually means the Utah mix of mayonnaise and ketchup. Andalouse uses tomato paste instead of ketchup, so it is less sugary and more concentrated. The chopped pepper gives it texture, the lemon gives it acidity, and paprika and chili add warmth.

Where does andalouse sauce come from?

Despite its Spanish name, andalouse is Belgian. Bruxelles Secrète calls it a 100% Belgian invention and credits La William, a Belgian family company founded in 1963, as the first to bottle it (source). La William claims the invention itself and says the recipe was inspired by gazpacho, the cold tomato soup of Andalusia (La William).

That story is contested. Chowhound reports a rival claim: Lea De Boeck-Vandenwyngaert, founder of Anda Premium Sauces, is said to have created the sauce in the late 1960s, though “few other sources back up this story” (Tim Forster, Chowhound, November 23, 2024). Don’t confuse either with the much older French sauce andalouse of classical cuisine, served hot or cold, which has nothing to do with the friterie squeeze bottle (Wikipédia FR).

Andalouse sauce recipe: ingredients

This homemade andalouse sauce serves 4 as a dip. It uses fresh pepper and shallot, so it tastes brighter than the bottled kind. The ratio is my own synthesis of the published recipes from Bruxelles Secrète and ohmydish, tuned for fries: enough tomato for color, not so much that it turns pasty. The US measures are estimates based on density, so use a kitchen scale if you have one.

Ingredient Metric US measure
Mayonnaise 200 g about ¾ cup + 2 tbsp
Tomato paste 30 g about 2 tbsp
Red bell pepper, very finely chopped 60 g about ½ pepper
Shallot, finely chopped 20 g about 1 small shallot
Lemon juice 5 ml 1 tsp
Paprika, sweet or smoked 2 g about 1 tsp
Cayenne pepper (or a dash of harissa) 1 pinch 1 pinch
Salt and black pepper to taste to taste
  • Prep time: 10 minutes
  • Cook time: none
  • Resting time: 30 minutes in the fridge, optional
  • Yield: about 300 g (1¼ cups), 4 servings

Which mayonnaise should you use?

Use a Belgian or French-style mayonnaise, which is savory and has a clear mustard edge. If yours is on the sweet side, like Dutch-style mayo, ohmydish suggests adding 1 teaspoon of mustard to balance it. In the US, a plain, unsweetened mayonnaise is the closest match; skip anything labeled as a sandwich spread.

How to make andalouse sauce, step by step

Andalouse is a mix-and-chill recipe: chop the aromatics very fine, stir everything into the mayonnaise, season, then let it rest.

1. Chop the pepper and shallot

Seed the red bell pepper and cut it into the smallest dice you can manage, about 2 mm. Chop the shallot just as fine. Bigger pieces make a chunky relish, not a sauce you can dip a fry into.

2. Mix the base

In a bowl, whisk the mayonnaise with the tomato paste until the color is even: a soft orange-pink, no red streaks.

3. Add the aromatics and spices

Fold in the pepper, shallot, lemon juice, paprika and cayenne. Taste. Then season with salt and black pepper.

4. Rest and adjust

Cover and chill for 30 minutes if you can, so the pepper and shallot flavor the mayonnaise. Taste again before serving. Want more heat? Add another pinch of cayenne.

Andalouse sauce variations

Andalouse is forgiving: you can change the pepper, the acid or the heat without losing what makes it andalouse.

  • Roasted pepper: swap the fresh pepper for jarred roasted red peppers or pimientos, patted dry and chopped. Wikipedia lists roasted peppers as a standard option. The sauce comes out sweeter and smokier, though for fries I still prefer the crunch of fresh pepper.
  • No fresh pepper: ohmydish makes a quick version without pepper: 250 g mayonnaise, 3 tsp tomato purée (tomato paste in the US), 3 tsp lemon juice, 2 tsp vinegar, 1 tsp chili powder (plain ground chili, not the US spice blend) and 1 tsp fine salt.
  • Spicier: replace the cayenne with a dash of harissa, as Bruxelles Secrète suggests.
  • Lighter: replace part of the mayonnaise with Greek yogurt or sour cream. It turns tangier, so cut back on the lemon.

Andalouse vs Algerian vs samurai sauce

Andalouse is the mildest of Belgium’s three big orange fry sauces. It is built on tomato and pepper, Algerian sauce on onion and mustard, and samurai sauce on ketchup and harissa or sambal oelek. All three start from mayonnaise, which is why they look so alike on a friterie counter.

Sauce Base Heat Typical use
Andalouse Mayonnaise, tomato paste, bell pepper Mild to moderate Fries, burgers, grilled meat, raw veggie dip
Algerian (algérienne) Mayonnaise, onion or shallot, harissa or chili, mustard Medium, sweet-spicy Kebab, French tacos, fries
Samurai (samouraï) Mayonnaise, ketchup, harissa or sambal oelek Hottest of the three Fries, mitraillette, kebab

The heat levels are my reading of the recipes, not Scoville measurements. Sources: Wikipedia on samurai sauce and Algerian sauce, and Chowhound.

Algerian sauce vs andalouse

Algerian sauce is sweet, spicy and yellow-orange. Its bite comes from onion or shallot, black pepper, vinegar and chili or harissa, sometimes with tomato. Andalouse leads with tomato and sweet pepper instead. On paper, Algerian sauce is the older of the two: Wikipedia cites a mention in Le livre des menus as early as 1876.

Samurai sauce vs andalouse

Samurai sauce is the hot one. It mixes mayonnaise with ketchup and harissa or sambal oelek, and it is a staple of mobile friteries and kebab shops in Belgium and France. On a burger I pick andalouse every time: samurai’s heat flattens the taste of the beef, while andalouse lets it come through. It is also the one to serve kids.

What to eat with andalouse sauce

Fries first, then anything grilled or fried. In Belgium it is a standard fry sauce. On a burger, one generous spoonful replaces both the ketchup and the mayo. I also use it with grilled chicken and as a dip for raw carrots and cucumber.

What to eat with andalouse sauce: thick-cut Belgian frites in a paper tray with a cup of andalouse for dipping. (Photo: anders pearson, CC BY-SA 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Belgian\_frites\_with\_andalouse\_sauce.jpg)

To taste it at the source, go to Maison Antoine on Place Jourdan in Etterbeek, Brussels. It has been a friterie since 1948 and serves andalouse alongside samurai and américaine. How to store homemade andalouse sauce

Keep andalouse sauce in an airtight container in the fridge. Made with store-bought mayonnaise, it keeps 3 to 5 days, according to dubocalalassiette.fr. Homemade mayonnaise made with raw egg is another story: it does not keep like a commercial one, so with homemade mayo I make only what we will eat that day.

Can you buy andalouse sauce?

Yes. In France, the common bottled brands are La William and Samia, which sells a halal andalouse made with mustard and tomatoes, and the supermarket chain Auchan has its own 340 g squeeze bottle. In the US, look for imported Belgian brands such as La William at import grocers or online. Honestly, though, the homemade version takes 10 minutes and tastes fresher than any bottle I have tried.

Andalouse sauce FAQ

What does andalouse sauce taste like? Like a tomato-and-pepper mayonnaise with a mild chili finish: tangy, faintly sweet, with bits of pepper. It is less sugary than ketchup-based sauces and much less hot than samurai sauce.

What is the difference between andalouse sauce and Algerian sauce? Andalouse is built on tomato paste and sweet pepper, while Algerian sauce is built on onion or shallot, mustard and chili or harissa. Algerian sauce is spicier and sweeter.

Which is hotter, andalouse or samurai sauce? Samurai sauce is hotter. It uses harissa or sambal oelek with ketchup, while andalouse gets only a pinch of cayenne and some paprika.

Does andalouse sauce contain egg or mustard? It contains egg through the mayonnaise, and usually mustard too, which many mayonnaises and La William’s own recipe include. If you have an allergy, check the label, or make it at home with an egg-free, mustard-free mayonnaise.

How long does homemade andalouse sauce keep? Made with store-bought mayonnaise, 3 to 5 days in an airtight container in the fridge. Made with raw-egg homemade mayonnaise, eat it the same day.

Is andalouse sauce Spanish? No, it is Belgian. The name points to Andalusia: La William, which claims the invention, says its recipe was inspired by gazpacho, the region’s cold tomato soup.
