Double Jack Copycat Recipe (Jack in the Box)

My Double Jack copycat: two thin beef patties, two slices of American cheese, mayo, ketchup and mustard on a buttered bun. Makes 2 in 25 minutes.

By Alice, Recipes writerPublished November 24, 2026

To make a Double Jack at home, sear two thin 3.5 oz (100 g) beef patties per burger, stack them with two slices of American cheese, and build it on a buttered, toasted bun with mayonnaise, ketchup, yellow mustard, pickles, onion, tomato and lettuce. My copycat makes 2 burgers in about 25 minutes. It’s my own reproduction, not Jack in the Box’s recipe. The chain published the Double Jack’s ingredients when it launched in 2016, but never the patty weight, the bun recipe or the build order.

Every composition fact below comes from the US market and from two official Jack in the Box documents: the February 8, 2016 launch press release and the chain’s Ingredient & Allergen Statements dated July 27, 2016. Where I had to fill a gap, I say so. If you want the single-patty version first, start with my Jumbo Jack copycat recipe, or browse all our copycat burger recipes.

What’s in a Jack in the Box Double Jack?

A Double Jack is two 100% beef patties, two slices of American cheese, lettuce, tomato, mayonnaise, ketchup, mustard, pickles and onions on a buttery bakery bun. That’s the official description from Jack in the Box’s launch release of February 8, 2016, word for word: “a new buttery bakery bun, two juicy 100% beef patties, two slices of American cheese, crisp hand-leafed lettuce, freshly sliced tomatoes, real mayonnaise, ketchup, mustard, pickles and onions” (Jack in the Box press release, February 8, 2016, checked November 17, 2026).

Two independent launch reviews list the same ingredients (The Impulsive Buy, January 29, 2016 and Brand Eating, February 2016). Third-party menu sites in 2026 describe variants, such as “seasoned patties”, a “split-top bun” or no mustard at all. None of them cites a source, so I built this recipe on the dated 2016 description.

The Double Jack launch in 2016

Jack in the Box announced the “NEW Double Jack” on February 8, 2016, in a release called “Declaration of Delicious” that changed ingredients across 29 menu items. To launch it, the chain pledged to give away one million burgers: one coupon per person for a free Double Jack or Jumbo Jack, no purchase needed, valid for a week or while supplies lasted. Brand Eating reports it hit restaurants on February 17, 2016.

The patties: beef only, weight unknown

The 2016 statement lists the “Beef Hamburger Patty, Jumbo and Regular” with one ingredient: beef. The same document also lists a seasoned “¼ lb. Signature” patty, with salt, yeast extract, onion powder, spices, garlic powder and beef tallow, but it doesn’t say which patty goes in the Double Jack. So I mix nothing into the meat and season it in the pan.

No source publishes the patty weight. The launch nutrition gives 46 g of protein for a 319 g burger, which points to two Jumbo-style patties. Todd Wilbur’s Jumbo Jack copycat uses 1/5 lb (about 3.2 oz, 91 g) per patty (Top Secret Recipes). I go a little heavier, 3.5 oz (100 g), because thin patties lose weight fast on a hot pan and I want two of them to hold their own against the bun. That’s an estimate, not a spec.

The sauces: mayo, ketchup and yellow mustard

The Double Jack gets three plain sauces, not a special sauce. The 2016 statement describes a classic mayonnaise (soybean oil, egg yolks, vinegar, lemon juice), a regular ketchup and a mustard of water, vinegar, mustard seed, salt, turmeric and spices. In other words, standard American yellow mustard. Buy those three and you’re close.

Today’s Jumbo Jack uses a mayo-onion sauce instead. Nothing confirms the Double Jack switched to it. If you like that sauce, the one from my Jumbo Jack Cheeseburger copycat works as a variation, but it’s not the 2016 build.

Double Jack copycat recipe ingredients (2 burgers)

These quantities make 2 Double Jacks with four patties in total, 14 oz (400 g) of beef.

Patties

  • 14 oz (400 g) 80/20 ground beef, cold
  • Salt and black pepper

Buns and cheese

  • 2 brioche-style or soft bakery burger buns
  • 1 tbsp (15 g) softened butter
  • 4 slices American cheese

Sauces and toppings

  • 2 tbsp (30 g) mayonnaise
  • 2 tsp (10 g) ketchup
  • 2 tsp (10 g) yellow mustard
  • 6 dill pickle chips
  • 2 tbsp (20 g) chopped white onion
  • 4 thin tomato slices
  • 2 large iceberg lettuce leaves

Times: 15 minutes prep, 10 minutes cooking, 25 minutes total.

How to make a Double Jack at home, step by step

Cook four thin patties hard and fast, melt cheese on two of them, and stack everything on a buttered, toasted bun while the beef is still hot.

1. Prep the toppings and the cheese

Slice the tomato thin, about ¼ inch (6 mm), tear the lettuce to the size of the bun, chop the onion finely and pat the pickle chips dry. Take the cheese out of the fridge now. At room temperature it melts in the 30 seconds it gets on the patty.

2. Shape four thin patties

Split the cold beef into 4 portions of 3.5 oz (100 g). Press each into a patty about ⅜ inch (1 cm) thick and ½ inch (1.3 cm) wider than the bun. Don’t mix anything into the meat, and handle it as little as you can. The patties shrink as they cook and should end up the width of the bun.

3. Butter and toast the buns

Spread the cut sides of the buns with the softened butter and toast them cut side down in a skillet over medium heat for 45 to 60 seconds, until golden. The butter is my nod to the “buttery bakery bun”. Spread the mayonnaise on the bottom buns and lay 1 slice of cheese on each.

4. Sear the patties and melt the cheese

Cook the patties on a skillet or griddle over medium-high heat, around 400 °F (200 °C), 2 to 3 minutes per side, seasoning with salt and pepper, until well browned and 160 °F (71 °C) in the center, the USDA safe temperature for ground beef (USDA FSIS, checked November 17, 2026). If your skillet can’t hold all four without crowding, cook them in two batches. Crowded patties steam instead of browning. For the last 30 seconds, put 1 slice of cheese on 2 of the patties and cover the pan so the steam melts it.

5. Build the Double Jack

Set a melted-cheese patty on each bottom bun, cheese up, then a plain patty on top. Add the ketchup and mustard, then the pickles, onion, 2 tomato slices and the lettuce, close with the top bun and eat it right away.

Bottom to top, my build is: bun, mayo, cheese, patty, melted cheese, patty, ketchup and mustard, pickles, onion, tomato, lettuce, bun. Jack in the Box never published its order, so this is a reconstruction.

Why this copycat stays close to the original

This copycat sticks to the only ingredient list Jack in the Box ever published for the Double Jack, and it gets right the three things that define it: two thin beef-only patties, two slices of real American cheese, and the plain mayo-ketchup-mustard trio.

The American cheese matters most. The 2016 statement lists sodium citrate and sodium phosphate in the chain’s sliced cheese. Those emulsifying salts make it melt into a smooth sheet instead of turning greasy. The slice under the bottom patty softens against the hot beef and shields the bun from the juices, so it doesn’t go soggy before the last bite. If you only follow one rule here, make it this one: a fancier bun changes the burger far less than swapping in a slice of cheddar.

The yellow mustard is the other tell. On the sauce side, it’s what separates the Double Jack from the Jumbo Jack. Its vinegar cuts through two patties’ worth of fat and cheese. Leave it out and you’ve made a different burger.

Double Jack vs Jumbo Jack: what’s the difference?

The Double Jack has two beef patties, two slices of American cheese and mustard on a buttery bun; the Jumbo Jack has one patty, no cheese and a mayo-onion sauce on a sesame seed bun. The comparison uses the Double Jack’s 2016 launch description and the Jumbo Jack’s current US menu listing (Allmenus, checked November 17, 2026).

Double Jack calories and nutrition

A Jack in the Box Double Jack has 830 calories and 46 g of protein, according to the nutrition published at its 2016 launch. Brand Eating reported the full panel for a 319 g burger: 58 g of fat including 22 g saturated, 34 g of carbohydrates including 7 g of sugars, and 1,130 mg of sodium. The Impulsive Buy confirms 830 calories, 46 g of protein and 1,130 mg of sodium.

Current third-party databases such as FatSecret agree on 830 calories, 58 g of fat, 46 g of protein and 33 to 34 g of carbs, for a 313 g serving. Sodium is where they split: some recent sites list 1,430 mg. I couldn’t find a current official figure, so treat sodium as uncertain. All of these numbers describe the restaurant burger, not my homemade version.

Is the Double Jack still on the menu?

I can’t confirm the Double Jack is sold nationwide in November 2026. The jackinthebox.com menu wouldn’t load for me on November 17, 2026, and the Allmenus listing I checked doesn’t include it.

What’s certain is that the name was still in use in 2025. From July 24 to August 7, 2025, Jack in the Box ran a “Texas Double Jack” in about 600 Texas restaurants, donating $1 per burger or combo sold to the American Red Cross for flood relief (Jack in the Box press release, July 23, 2025). The release doesn’t say how the Texas version differs from the standard one. Check your local restaurant’s menu in the app before you drive over.

Double Jack FAQ

What’s in a Double Jack from Jack in the Box?

Two 100% beef patties, two slices of American cheese, lettuce, tomato, mayonnaise, ketchup, mustard, pickles and onions on a buttery bakery bun, according to the chain’s February 8, 2016 launch release (US market).

What’s the difference between the Double Jack and the Jumbo Jack?

The Double Jack has two patties, two slices of American cheese and mustard on a buttery bun. The Jumbo Jack has one patty, no cheese and a mayo-onion sauce on a sesame seed bun.

How many calories are in a Double Jack?

830 calories, with 46 g of protein, according to the 2016 launch nutrition. Current third-party databases give the same calorie count.

What patty weight should I use for a homemade Double Jack?

Jack in the Box doesn’t publish it. I use 3.5 oz (100 g) of 80/20 beef per patty, two per burger, based on the 1/5 lb Jumbo Jack patty in Top Secret Recipes’ copycat.

What sauce goes on a Double Jack?

Three plain sauces: mayonnaise, ketchup and mustard, per the 2016 description. There’s no special sauce. The mayo-onion sauce belongs to today’s Jumbo Jack and isn’t confirmed on the Double Jack.

How much does a Double Jack cost?

There’s no national price. Brand Eating paid $5.29 at one local restaurant in February 2016. Unofficial menu sites quote widely different 2026 prices, so check your restaurant’s menu in the Jack in the Box app.

Can I get a Double Jack outside the US?

Not in Europe: Jack in the Box has no restaurants there. The chain operates in Mexico and Guam, but I couldn’t confirm the Double Jack is sold in those markets.

What allergens are in a Double Jack?

The 2016 ingredient statement shows milk and soy in the American cheese and egg in the mayonnaise, and the bun contains wheat. Sesame isn’t confirmed by an official source for this bun. At home, check the labels of the products you buy.

Should I cook the patties in a skillet, on a griddle or on the grill?

A skillet or flat griddle, around 400 °F (200 °C). Thin patties brown fast on a flat surface and cook in their own fat, and you can cover the pan to melt the cheese. A grill grate works, but thin patties can stick and the cheese is harder to melt.

What cheese can I use instead of American cheese?

Individually wrapped processed cheese slices with emulsifying salts on the label melt the same way. A thin slice of young, mild cheddar is the fallback, though it melts less evenly.
