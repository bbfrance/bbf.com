Japanese Teriyaki Burger: MOS Classic Copycat

My home copy of MOS Burger's classic Teriyaki Burger, built on the chain's reworked 2026 miso teriyaki sauce: thin beef patty, crisp lettuce, mayo. Makes 2.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 24, 2026

This Japanese teriyaki burger is my home copy of the classic Teriyaki Burger from MOS Burger, not the chain’s official recipe. A thin 100% beef patty is turned in a soy and miso teriyaki sauce, then stacked with crisp lettuce and mayonnaise in a soft bun. It makes 2 burgers in about 35 minutes. My reference is the standard burger sold in Japan today: the sauce MOS reworked on March 18, 2026, at the ¥490 price it has carried since July 15, 2026.

This is the plain, everyday Teriyaki Burger. In my opinion it is the one to learn first: with no egg or cheese to hide behind, the sauce has to be right. If you want the egg-and-cheese version, I already wrote up my MOS Burger Tobikiri teriyaki burger recipe, and the comparison table further down shows how the two differ.

What is in the MOS Burger Teriyaki Burger?

The MOS Burger Teriyaki Burger is a beef patty coated in teriyaki sauce, served with crisp lettuce and mayonnaise in a bun. MOS calls it a Japanese-style (wafū) burger. The sauce is built on koikuchi (dark) soy sauce and miso, then deepened with tamari, soy moromi and a roasted soy sauce powder (MOS Japan menu data, checked October 30, 2026). The Japanese description stops at the lettuce. It is the English menu that mentions the mayo, describing crisp lettuce topped with MOS’s original teriyaki sauce and mayonnaise (MOS English menu).

MOS also publishes where the main ingredients come from: wheat flour from the United States, Canada and Japan, beef from Australia, the United States and Canada, and lettuce, miso and soy sauce from Japan.

The 2026 teriyaki sauce: what MOS changed

On March 18, 2026, MOS reworked the Teriyaki Burger sauce to make it gentler. Sanonto sugar (三温糖, a light brown Japanese sugar) and apple paste went in for sweetness; soy moromi and katsuo (bonito) went in for umami. Getting there took 22 trials, according to macaro-ni and J-CAST.

Masatetsuo Aoyama, who leads product development at MOS, told Mynavi News he hoped a softer flavor would make it a burger people want to eat every day. I took that as my brief. When I balanced my sauce, I aimed for sweet, round and savory, and pulled back every time the soy turned sharp.

MOS does not publish quantities. Every amount below is my own home formula, worked out from the published ingredient list, not a leaked recipe.

Ingredients for 2 Japanese teriyaki burgers

You need one 70 g patty per burger, a miso teriyaki sauce, lettuce, mayo and two soft buns. Quantities are given in both US and metric measures.

MOS-style teriyaki sauce with miso

  • 2 Tbsp (30 ml) koikuchi soy sauce, such as regular Kikkoman
  • 1 tsp (5 ml) tamari
  • 1 Tbsp (18 g) miso, white and red mixed, or awase miso
  • 1 Tbsp (12 g) light brown sugar, for the sanonto sugar
  • 1 Tbsp (15 g) unsweetened applesauce, for the apple paste
  • 2 Tbsp (30 ml) bonito dashi, or 1/4 tsp bonito dashi powder in 2 Tbsp water
  • 1 tsp (3 g) cornstarch mixed with 1 Tbsp (15 ml) cold water

Shinshu miso sold loose in a Tokyo food hall in 2007, ¥1,208 for 500 g. Burgers

  • 140 g (5 oz) 100% ground beef, 80/20, split into 2 patties of 70 g (2.5 oz)
  • 1 small pinch fine salt and a little black pepper
  • 1 tsp neutral oil
  • 2 soft burger buns, about 10 cm (4 in) wide
  • 2 large handfuls crisp iceberg lettuce, shredded or torn
  • 2 Tbsp (28 g) mayonnaise

MOS does not publish the patty weight, so 70 g raw is my estimate. The whole burger weighs 170.2 g. Take away the bun, lettuce, sauce and mayo, and a raw patty of 60 to 75 g is what fits.

Times: prep 20 minutes, cook 15 minutes, total 35 minutes. Yield: 2 burgers.

Step by step: how to make the MOS teriyaki burger at home

Make the sauce first, sear thin patties, turn them in the sauce, then build the burger with lettuce and mayo.

1. Make the miso teriyaki sauce

Whisk the soy sauce, tamari, miso, brown sugar, applesauce and dashi in a small saucepan until no lumps of miso remain. Bring it to a gentle simmer over medium heat and cook for 1 minute. Stir in the cornstarch slurry and simmer 30 seconds more, until the sauce is glossy and coats a spoon. Taste it now: sweet should come first, salt second. Keep it warm on the lowest heat.

2. Shape thin patties

Split the cold beef into two 70 g (2.5 oz) balls. Press each one into a patty about 11 cm (4.5 in) wide and 1 cm (3/8 in) thick, a little wider than the bun, because it shrinks. A shallow dimple in the center keeps it flat. Season with salt and pepper just before cooking.

3. Sear the patties

Heat the oil in a heavy skillet over medium-high heat, about 200°C (400°F). Sear the patties for 2 to 3 minutes per side, until well browned. Check the center with a thermometer: ground beef is safe at 71°C (160°F), per the USDA safe temperature chart.

4. Coat the patties in sauce

Turn the heat to low and pour 3 Tbsp of the warm sauce into the skillet. Turn each patty in it for 20 to 30 seconds, until both sides are glossy. MOS describes its patty as coated in sauce, not just topped, and that coating is what you taste first.

5. Warm the buns

Warm the cut buns cut-side down in a clean dry pan for 30 to 60 seconds. You want them soft and warm. A crunchy toasted crust is wrong here.

6. Assemble the burger

Place a sauced patty on each bottom bun. Add a handful of lettuce, spoon a little more sauce over it, then add 1 Tbsp mayo and close with the top bun. The English menu says the lettuce is topped with sauce and mayo; MOS does not publish the exact stacking order, so this order is my reading of that description. Eat it right away, while the lettuce still crunches.

Tips for a teriyaki burger that tastes like MOS

The short version: a gentle sauce, a soft bun, and no pineapple or cheese.

  • Do not skip the miso. It gives the sauce body and a rounded saltiness that soy alone cannot. With soy, it is the base of the MOS sauce.
  • Simmer, do not boil. Once the cornstarch is in, a hard boil turns the sugar bitter. Thirty seconds is enough.
  • Bake your own bun if you can. MOS does not publish its bun recipe, but a soft, slightly sweet bun is what you want; my homemade burger buns recipe is a good match.
  • Keep the extra sauce. Leftovers keep 3 days in the fridge. Warm them before use.

Many of the teriyaki burger recipes that rank in English add pineapple, tomato, caramelized onions or cheddar. They make a fine burger. Just not this one. In Japan, the standard teriyaki burger is sweet soy sauce, lettuce and mayo, and nothing else. The mayo is the part I would never drop: its fat and tang stop the sweet sauce from turning cloying.

Classic Teriyaki Burger vs. Tobikiri: what is the difference?

The classic Teriyaki Burger is the simple, everyday version: patty, sauce, lettuce and mayo. The Tobikiri was a premium limited edition, and my recipe for it adds a fried egg and a melted cheese mix.

A short history of the MOS Burger Teriyaki Burger

MOS Burger launched its Teriyaki Burger in 1973, one year after the chain was founded. Atsushi Sakurada founded MOS Food Services in Tokyo on July 21, 1972, with a first counter near Narimasu Station (Wikipedia). The first sauce used soy sauce and two misos, white and red (Walkerplus). May 15 is registered in Japan as Teriyaki Burger Day (bestcalendar.jp).

Map of Narimasu Station in Itabashi, Tokyo. MOS Burger opened its first counter near this station in 1972, and the Teriyaki Burger followed in 1973. The marker shows the station, not the exact address of the first counter. (Map: OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL, https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)

It did not sell at first. According to Walkerplus, it took off through word of mouth started by a high school girl who was a regular at the first restaurant, and today it is the second most popular item at MOS. MOS presents itself as the originator of the teriyaki burger. I have found no independent primary source to confirm that, so I report it as the chain’s claim.

The burger has changed several times since:

  • April 23, 2013: MOS switched its standard patties from a beef-pork blend to 100% beef and renewed the Teriyaki Burger at the same time (MOS press release).
  • March 14, 2022: the sauce gained cocoa powder, ground black sesame, rice vinegar and balsamic vinegar, and the burger cost ¥380 (MOS press release). MOS’s current description no longer lists them and I could not confirm whether they stayed in, so my recipe leaves them out.
  • March 18, 2026: new sauce with sanonto sugar, apple paste, soy moromi and bonito, at ¥470.
  • July 15, 2026: the price went from ¥470 to ¥490, and the MOS Burger from ¥470 to ¥500, because of rising costs for ingredients, labor and energy (Ryutsuu News, Nikkei).

MOS teriyaki burger FAQ

What is in MOS Burger’s teriyaki sauce?

MOS says its teriyaki sauce is based on koikuchi soy sauce and miso, with tamari, soy moromi and roasted soy sauce powder. Since March 18, 2026, it also contains sanonto sugar, apple paste and bonito. MOS does not publish quantities.

Does the MOS Teriyaki Burger have mayonnaise?

Yes. MOS’s English menu describes crisp lettuce topped with its teriyaki sauce and mayonnaise. MOS does not say which type of mayo it uses.

Is the MOS Teriyaki Burger patty 100% beef?

Yes. MOS switched its standard patties from a beef-pork blend to 100% beef on April 23, 2013. The beef comes from Australia, the United States and Canada.

How many calories are in a MOS Burger Teriyaki Burger?

The Teriyaki Burger has 385 kcal for 170.2 g in Japan, according to MOS. The Double Teriyaki Burger has 560 kcal.

How much does the MOS Teriyaki Burger cost in Japan in 2026?

It has cost ¥490 including tax since July 15, 2026. Before that date it cost ¥470.

Who invented the teriyaki burger?

MOS Burger claims to be the originator and launched its Teriyaki Burger in Japan in 1973. I have found no independent primary source that confirms it was the first.

Why put miso in teriyaki sauce?

Miso adds body, a rounded saltiness and a fermented depth that soy sauce alone lacks. MOS has used miso in the sauce since the launch in 1973.

What can I use instead of sanonto sugar or apple paste?

Light brown sugar replaces sanonto sugar well, since both are light brown with a slight caramel note. Unsweetened applesauce gives the same fruity sweetness as apple paste.

Can I eat at MOS Burger in Europe?

No. According to our brand profile, MOS operates in Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Thailand, South Korea and the Philippines, with no restaurant in Europe, and its Australian stores closed in 2024. Japan remains the easiest place to try the original.
