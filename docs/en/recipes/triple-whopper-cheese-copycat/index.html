Triple Whopper Cheese Copycat Recipe for 2 at Home

My home grill method recreates the Australian triple-stack build with three flame-kissed patties, melted cheddar-style slices and mayo plus tomato sauce.

By ClaraPublished October 6, 2026

You can make a Triple Whopper Cheese copycat at home with three thin beef patties per burger, cheddar-style melting slices, lettuce, tomato, onion, pickles, mayonnaise and tomato sauce on a toasted sesame bun. I grilled this version three times in my backyard the week of 4 October 2026 against the Australian Hungry Jack’s build. It is my unofficial home reproduction, not the chain’s formula.

What’s in Hungry Jack’s Triple Whopper Cheese?

The official Australian Triple Whopper Cheese is three flame-grilled Australian beef patties with cheese, hand-cut tomato, fresh lettuce, onion, pickles, mayonnaise and tomato sauce on a toasted sesame bun.

  • 3x flame-grilled Australian beef patties
  • Cheddar-style melting cheese
  • Hand-cut tomato
  • Fresh lettuce
  • Sliced onion
  • Dill pickles
  • Mayonnaise
  • Tomato sauce / ketchup
  • Toasted sesame seed bun

Whopper on toasted sesame bun with lettuce and tomato, showing official mayo plus tomato sauce salad build (Photo: joo0ey, CC BY 2.0)

One official Australian burger delivers 5,780 kJ (about 1,381 kcal), 80.5 g protein, 95.7 g fat including 43.2 g saturated fat, 52.1 g carbohydrates including 10.0 g sugars, and 2,010 mg sodium. I use those numbers only as a reference for how rich and salty the build is, not as a target for my lighter home version. Source: Hungry Jack’s Triple Whopper Cheese, verified 4 October 2026; nutrition guides dated 26 September 2026 via Hungry Jack’s nutrition hub.

Ingredients to Check Before You Shop

I shop faster when I check the whole build first: beef, melting cheese, sesame buns, salad vegetables, mayo and tomato sauce are the whole job.

Hungry Jack’s states its beef has no additives, preservatives, colours or fillers, and that only flame-grilling gives the smoky barbecue taste you cannot get from a flat pan. The Whopper is described as Australia’s favourite and biggest burger for over 40 years, sold as single, double or triple, alongside the thicker 1/3-lb pre-cooked Grill Masters Angus. Sources: flame-grilled Aussie beef and Whopper & Beef hub. My recipe below keeps that logic but never claims the chain’s patty weight, cheese type or sauce brands, which Hungry Jack’s does not publish.

Why This Copy Tastes Like the Original

This copy tastes close because it copies the structure, not a secret formula: thin flame-kissed patties, cheese melted on hot beef, cold crisp salad, and mayo plus tomato sauce on a toasted sesame bun.

  • Flame contact, not pan-frying. I grill thin patties over direct high heat for charred edges.
  • Salt-and-pepper only. Plain minced beef, seasoned after shaping, stays closest to the no-filler beef promise.
  • Cheese on hot beef. Processed cheddar-style slices go on off direct flame and turn silky in 30 seconds.
  • Toasted sesame bun. Cut sides buttered and toasted golden, so two sauces don’t soak in.
  • Mayo one side, tomato sauce the other. That split is the Whopper signature.
  • No bacon. The official Triple lists no bacon, unlike the Ultimate Double Whopper with premium eye bacon, so I add none.

Ingredients for 2 Triple Burgers

These quantities for 2 burgers are my home working hypothesis, not Hungry Jack’s official specification.

  • 660 g ground beef (80/20), about 23 oz, divided into 6x 110 g balls — my test quantity
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 tsp cracked black pepper
  • 2 large sesame seed burger buns, about 10 cm / 4 in wide — see my homemade burger buns recipe for a toast-ready sesame bun
  • 6 slices processed cheddar-style melting cheese — my plausible choice, not the chain’s stated type
  • 3 Tbsp mayonnaise
  • 3 Tbsp tomato ketchup / Australian tomato sauce
  • 2 large hand-cut tomato slices, halved
  • 4 crisp iceberg or butter lettuce leaves
  • 1/2 white onion, very thinly sliced
  • 8 dill pickle chips
  • 1 Tbsp soft butter, for toasting

How to Make It on a Hot Grill

The method is simple: grill thin patties very hot and fast, melt cheese on hot beef, then stack three patties per toasted bun.

1. Shape the patties

I divide 660 g cold beef into 6 equal 110 g balls, then press each to 12 cm / 4.7 in wide and 8 mm thick. Cold beef holds together. Warm beef smears. I chill the patties 15 minutes so they survive the grill. This weight is my assumption only.

2. Prep salad and sauces

For the salad, thick tomato slices, torn lettuce, paper-thin onion and drained pickles. Mayo goes in one small bowl, ketchup in another, so you can spread fast while the patties rest.

3. Heat the grill

Get the grill ripping hot: 250–290°C / 480–550°F on gas, charcoal or a grill pan. Lightly oil the grates. That blast of direct heat chars thin patties without overcooking them.

Beef patties grilling over direct flame, showing charred edges for flame-kissed method at 250-290°C (Photo: rick, CC BY 2.0)

4. Toast the buns

I butter the cut sides and toast 45–60 seconds until golden, then set tops and bottoms aside. A firm, buttery crumb holds up under two sauces and triple beef juices.

5. Grill patties in two batches

Season 3 patties with salt and pepper just before grilling. 90 seconds per side for thin, well-done patties. Move to a warm tray, repeat with the other 3. At home I cook to 71°C / 160°F internal.

6. Melt the cheese

Lay one cheddar-style slice on each hot patty right off direct flame and cover 30 seconds. Processed-style slices melt like fast-food cheese. My stand-in, not Hungry Jack’s published type.

Sliced white cheddar-style cheese for melting on hot patties, 30 seconds covered (Photo: CNEcija12345, CC BY-SA 4.0)

7. Stack triples and serve

Mayo on each toasted top, ketchup on each toasted bottom. On each bottom: pickles, lettuce, tomato, onion, then 3 cheesy patties, then the top. Rest 1 minute. One burger uses half the ingredients above. In my tests the sauce split mattered more than the char — get it backwards and it tastes like any cheeseburger.

Stacked burger cross-section with beef, tomato, lettuce and onion on toasted bun, showing layer order (Photo: L.A. Foodie, CC BY 2.0)

FAQ

These are the questions I get about this Australian triple-stack build.

Q: What exactly is in the Triple Whopper Cheese?

A: In Australia on 4 October 2026 it is three flame-grilled beef patties, cheese, hand-cut tomato, fresh lettuce, onion, pickles, mayonnaise and tomato sauce on a toasted sesame bun.

Q: How many calories versus the Double?

A: The official Triple is 5,780 kJ per burger and the official Double Whopper Cheese is 4,152 kJ, with 80.5 g versus 56.4 g protein and 2,010 mg versus 1,533 mg sodium. My home version is smaller in sauce and cheese, so it runs lighter.

Q: What cheese melts like the Whopper?

A: I use processed cheddar-style slices for reliable melt and salty bite. Hungry Jack’s does not publish its exact cheese type or slice count, so treat this as a home stand-in.

Q: Can I cook without a barbecue?

A: Yes. I use a ripping-hot cast-iron grill pan and vent the kitchen well. You will get browning but not true flame smoke, which Hungry Jack’s says only flame-grilling gives.

Q: What is the difference to the Ultimate Double Whopper?

A: Do not confuse them. The official Triple Whopper Cheese has three patties and no listed bacon. The Ultimate Double has premium eye bacon in its build. For the double-stack method see my Ultimate Double Whopper copycat.

I tested this three times to keep thin patties juicy under triple stacking — and honestly I would not build all three patties on a weeknight. Two is plenty. For a weekend cookout, though, the triple is theatre, and this version earns it. For context on chain pricing and why triples read as sharing burgers, see my 2026 burger value wars breakdown.
