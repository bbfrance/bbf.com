In-N-Out Burger Recipe: Copycat Hamburger

In-N-Out burger recipe: rebuild the plain Hamburger at home with a 2 oz chuck patty, copycat spread, the reported stacking order and official facts.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 24, 2026

To make an In-N-Out burger at home, sear 2 thin 2 oz (57 g) patties of fresh ground chuck for about 2 minutes per side. Serve each one on a dry-toasted plain bun with spread (a Thousand Island-style sauce), tomato, hand-torn iceberg lettuce and onion. This is my homemade reproduction, not the chain’s recipe. In-N-Out lists what goes into the burger but has never published its spread formula.

What is in an In-N-Out Hamburger?

The official In-N-Out Hamburger is a bun, a 100% beef patty, lettuce, tomato and spread, with or without onions. That is the full ingredient list in the chain’s nutrition sheet, dated January 2026 (In-N-Out nutrition information). No cheese, no pickles. The Cheeseburger adds 1 slice of American cheese, and the Double-Double has 2 patties and 2 slices of cheese.

Most copycats online jump straight to the Double-Double or Animal Style. I started with the plain Hamburger on purpose: 1 2 oz patty, no cheese, nothing to hide a dry patty or a flat sauce. Get this one right and the Double-Double is just arithmetic.

How big is an In-N-Out patty?

An In-N-Out patty weighs 2 oz, about 57 g, raw. It is cooked medium-well and served on a toasted bun (Wikipedia, In-N-Out Burger products). 2 homemade Hamburgers take about 4 oz (115 g) of ground beef.

What beef does In-N-Out use?

In-N-Out says it grinds its own fresh 100% USDA ground chuck, with no additives, fillers or preservatives, and that the beef is delivered fresh, never frozen (In-N-Out food quality). Its restaurants have no microwaves and no freezers, and every burger is cooked to order (Tasting Table). The chain does not publish the fat ratio of its chuck.

Official vs. homemade: what we know and what we estimate

Only the ingredient list, the patty weight, the beef cut and the nutrition figures are official. The spread formula, the seasoning and the griddle temperature are reconstructions. The table keeps the two apart.

In-N-Out spread recipe

In-N-Out spread is a Thousand Island-style sauce. Chowhound describes the closest reproductions as mostly mayonnaise, with about a quarter as much ketchup and a little less sweet pickle relish (Chowhound). I use the copycat formula Todd Wilbur published on Top Secret Recipes in March 2018 (Top Secret Recipes). The real recipe has never been published.

Wilbur’s version uses more ketchup than Chowhound’s ratio: 3 tbsp to ½ cup of mayo is closer to a third. I keep his amounts because the extra ketchup balances the relish. If you want a paler, creamier sauce, drop the ketchup to 2 tbsp.

  • ½ cup (120 ml) mayonnaise
  • 3 tbsp (45 ml) ketchup
  • 2 tbsp finely minced white onion
  • 1½ tbsp sweet pickle relish
  • ¾ tsp sugar
  • ½ tsp white vinegar
  • ¼ tsp yellow mustard
  • ¼ tsp paprika
  • ⅛ tsp salt

Stir everything together in a small bowl. The batch is more than 2 burgers need. Keep the rest covered in the fridge for the next round, or for fries.

Copycat In-N-Out Hamburger ingredients (makes 2)

2 Hamburgers take 4 oz of fresh ground chuck, 2 plain buns, the spread and 4 fresh toppings. The topping quantities are my own, since In-N-Out does not publish them.

  • 4 oz (115 g) fresh ground chuck, 75/25 to 70/30 if ground at home, or 80/20 store-bought
  • 2 plain hamburger buns, soft and not brioche
  • 2 tbsp In-N-Out-style spread (recipe above)
  • 2 to 4 tomato slices
  • 2 to 4 iceberg lettuce leaves, torn by hand to fit the bun
  • 2 thin slices of white onion, raw (or chopped and grilled)
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper

How to make an In-N-Out burger at home, step by step

There are 5 steps: make the spread, shape thin patties, toast the buns dry, sear the patties for about 2 minutes per side, then stack from the bottom up. The searing and toasting follow Todd Wilbur’s copycat (Top Secret Recipes).

1. Make the spread

Mix the spread ingredients and let the bowl sit in the fridge while you cook. That gives the onion and relish time to flavor the mayo.

2. Shape 2 thin 2 oz patties

Divide the beef into 2 2 oz (57 g) portions. Press each into a thin round about 4 inches (10 cm) wide, slightly wider than the bun, because it shrinks in the pan.

3. Toast the buns dry

Place the buns cut side down in a dry, preheated pan, with no butter or oil, until crisp. A crisp face stops the spread and tomato from soaking the bun.

4. Sear the patties

Season the patties with salt and pepper and lay them in the preheated pan over medium-low to medium heat. Cook about 2 minutes per side for a medium-well finish, like the restaurant’s. On cast iron I aim for 390–450 °F (200–230 °C), my starting point to test, not an In-N-Out figure. Don’t press them once they’re in: they’re already thin, and pressing squeezes out the fat that browns them.

5. Stack the burger from the bottom up

Build each burger in this order: toasted bottom bun, spread, tomato, lettuce, patty, onion, top bun. Cold vegetables go under the hot patty, so the lettuce stays crisp and the onion warms slightly on top.

Why this recipe tastes like the original

4 choices do most of the work: a thin 2 oz chuck patty, a dry-toasted plain bun, a mayo-heavy spread and the cold-under-hot stacking. Change one and it drifts away from In-N-Out. The bun is where most home versions go wrong, in my view: brioche is too sweet and too rich for a burger this simple.

  • The patty. The chain uses fresh ground chuck at 2 oz raw. A thin patty seared fast gets a browned crust with no thick, rare center.
  • The bun. In-N-Out promotes buns made from an “old-fashioned, slow-rising sponge dough”. Its long-time West Coast supplier, Puritan Bakery (founded in 1938 by Dwight Grimes), runs a process of about 7 hours (Tasting Table). A soft, plain bun comes closer than an enriched brioche.
  • The spread. Mostly mayonnaise with ketchup and relish, it brings the creamy, sweet-sharp note.
  • The stacking. Cold tomato and iceberg under a hot patty give the contrast you get at the counter.

The assembly order: reported, not official

In-N-Out does not publish its stacking order. The order here is the one reported by copycat writers (Top Secret Recipes). Sources disagree on 1 point: Wikipedia and Todd Wilbur put the raw onion in different places, either on the patty or on the spread. I put it on the patty. Try both and keep the one you prefer.

By subtraction, the spread adds roughly 60 calories, and a slice of cheese roughly 70 calories and 410 mg of sodium. Those are my estimates, not published figures. They do not describe the homemade version, whose values depend on your beef, bun and sauce.

Variations: Cheeseburger, Protein Style and Animal Style

The same base gives you 3 In-N-Out variations: add cheese, swap the bun for lettuce, or go Animal Style. Only the first 2 are in the chain’s nutrition sheet. Animal Style belongs to the unofficial “secret” menu.

Cheeseburger

Lay 1 slice of American cheese on each patty for the last moments of cooking. That is the only difference between the official Hamburger and Cheeseburger.

In-N-Out Protein Style

Protein Style replaces the bun with large iceberg lettuce leaves. Wikipedia dates the option to the 1970s (Wikipedia). The official Protein Style Hamburger has 210 calories and 9 g of carbohydrates.

Animal Style

Animal Style means a patty cooked with mustard, plus pickles, grilled chopped onions and extra spread (Wikipedia). At home, brush the raw top of each patty with yellow mustard before you flip it, then add pickles and grilled onions.

Where to taste the original In-N-Out

In-N-Out only operates in the United States. Harry and Esther Snyder founded the chain on October 22, 1948, in Baldwin Park, California; it is now headquartered in Irvine and owned by Lynsi Snyder. It had 442 US restaurants as of August 19, 2026, according to ScrapeHero, across 10 states: California, Arizona, Nevada, Utah, Texas, Oregon, Colorado, Idaho, Washington and Tennessee. Tennessee, the 10th, opened with Lebanon, Antioch and Murfreesboro near Nashville, with about 35 restaurants planned in the long run (KTLA).

In-N-Out burger recipe FAQ

What sauce is on an In-N-Out burger? It is called spread, a Thousand Island-style sauce. In-N-Out has never published the formula. Copycats mix mayonnaise with roughly a quarter to a third as much ketchup, sweet pickle relish and a few seasonings, as in the recipe above.

How big is an In-N-Out patty? Each patty weighs 2 oz (57 g) raw. A Hamburger has 1 patty, a Double-Double has 2.

What is the difference between the Hamburger, the Cheeseburger and the Double-Double? The Hamburger has 1 patty and no cheese (360 calories). The Cheeseburger adds 1 slice of American cheese (430 calories). The Double-Double has 2 patties and 2 slices of cheese (610 calories), per the January 2026 nutrition sheet.

Does the In-N-Out Hamburger come with pickles? No. Pickles are not in the official January 2026 ingredient list. Add them as an option, or as part of Animal Style.

Why are the tomato and lettuce under the patty? In the reported assembly order, the spread, tomato and lettuce sit on the bottom bun under the hot patty. In-N-Out does not publish its order, so treat it as observed, not official.

What bun should I use for a homemade In-N-Out burger? A soft, plain hamburger bun, not brioche, toasted dry, cut side down. The original is a slow-rising sponge-dough bun, which a plain bun imitates better than a rich, buttery one.

What can I use instead of sweet pickle relish? Finely chop sweet-and-sour pickles and add a pinch of sugar. That is my own substitute to test, not an official equivalent.

Can you eat at In-N-Out outside the United States? Not on a regular basis: In-N-Out has no permanent restaurant outside the United States, where it operates in 10 states. Elsewhere, this copycat recipe is the closest you will get.
