Quarter Pounder with Cheese Copycat Recipe

Quarter Pounder with Cheese copycat recipe for 2: a thick 120 g fresh-beef patty, two slices of American cheese, fresh onion, exact timings and temps.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 19, 2026

To make a homemade Quarter Pounder with Cheese, sear a thick 4.25 oz (120 g) patty of fresh 80/20 ground chuck on a very hot, dry griddle. Season it with salt and pepper once it is cooking, and flip it only once: 3 to 4 minutes per side, until the center reads 160°F (71°C). Stack it on a toasted sesame bun with two slices of American cheese, fresh slivered onion, pickles, ketchup and yellow mustard. That’s it. This is my copycat for 2 burgers, not the official recipe. McDonald’s does not publish one, so I built this version from the chain’s own product description and ingredient list.

What’s on a Quarter Pounder with Cheese?

A Quarter Pounder with Cheese is a fresh 100% beef patty, two slices of American cheese, slivered onions, pickles, ketchup and mustard on a toasted sesame seed bun. That is how McDonald’s describes it on its US product page, which also says the patty is “cooked when you order” (McDonald’s US, checked November 13, 2026).

The cheese is pasteurized process American cheese. The US ingredient statement lists milk, cream, water, sodium citrate, salt, cultures, citric acid, enzymes, soy lecithin and a color. The bun, ketchup, mustard and cheese formulas are proprietary. So every brand choice below is mine, not McDonald’s.

How much does the Quarter Pounder patty weigh?

The Quarter Pounder patty weighs about 4.25 oz (120 g) raw and about 3 oz (85 g) cooked, according to Wikipedia, which notes that McDonald’s raised it from 4 oz to 4.25 oz in 2015 (Wikipedia, checked November 13, 2026). The official US page does not give a weight, so treat these figures as close estimates, not a spec.

Most published copycats, including Mashed and CopyKat, stick to the old 113 g (4 oz). I weigh out 120 g. Seven grams sounds trivial, but on a thick patty it is the difference between a burger that fills the bun and one that shrinks back from the edge.

Ingredients for 2 homemade Quarter Pounders

You need fresh 80/20 ground chuck, sesame buns, American cheese and four condiments. This makes 2 burgers.

  • 240 g (8.5 oz) fresh 80/20 ground chuck, split into two 120 g (4.25 oz) portions
  • 2 sesame seed hamburger buns
  • 4 slices American cheese
  • 2 tbsp fresh white onion, slivered very thin
  • 6 to 8 dill pickle slices
  • 2 tbsp ketchup
  • 2 tbsp yellow mustard
  • Kosher salt and finely ground black pepper

How to make a Quarter Pounder at home, step by step

Shape thick patties, toast the buns, sear hard, season on the griddle, melt the cheese, then build the burger. Count about 25 minutes in total, 10 of which is the patties chilling in the fridge.

1. Shape thick patties

Form each 120 g (4.25 oz) portion into a patty about 1/2 inch (1.2 cm) thick and a little wider than the bun, because it shrinks as it cooks. Do not press it on the griddle later: this is a thick-patty burger, not a smash burger. Chill the patties for 10 minutes so they hold their shape when they hit the pan.

2. Slice the onion and toast the buns

Cut the white onion into paper-thin slivers and keep them in the fridge until assembly. Toast the cut sides of the buns in a dry pan over medium heat for about 1 minute, until golden, then set them aside.

Fresh sliced onions. The Quarter Pounder gets fresh slivered onion, not the rehydrated kind used on regular McDonald’s hamburgers, so slice yours thin just before cooking. (Photo: Janet Guynn / Alabama Cooperative Extension System, CC0 1.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/184594136@N08/50409845357)

3. Heat the griddle very hot

Preheat a cast-iron skillet or flat griddle over medium-high heat for a good 5 minutes, until a drop of water skitters and vanishes. I aim for around 425°F (220°C) on an infrared thermometer. Any cooler and the patty steams in its own fat instead of browning.

4. Sear and season the patties

Lay the patties in the dry pan and leave them alone for 3 to 4 minutes. Once they are sizzling, sprinkle the tops with salt and a little pepper. That mirrors how McDonald’s seasons patties on the grill, mid-cook, from a dedicated shaker that The Takeout reports is mostly salt, about 86% salt to 14% pepper (The Takeout). Go heavier on salt than feels natural.

5. Flip and melt the cheese

Flip each patty once, season the seared side, and cook 3 to 4 minutes more, until the center reads 160°F (71°C). For the last minute, lay two slices of American cheese on each patty and cover the pan so the cheese slumps over the edges.

6. Steam the buns

Put the toasted buns in a covered pan off the heat, or in the microwave, for 10 to 15 seconds. It sounds fussy. It is also what gives you that soft, slightly squashy drive-thru crumb, and in my opinion it does more for the illusion than any brand of ketchup.

7. Assemble the burger

Put the cheese-topped patty on the bottom bun. On the inside of the top bun, spread 1 tbsp ketchup and 1 tbsp mustard, then add the pickles and slivered onion, and close the burger. Wrap it in parchment or deli paper for 1 minute so the steam softens the bun and the cheese settles into the meat.

Why this copycat tastes like the real thing

It works because it keeps the four things that make a Quarter Pounder different from the rest of the McDonald’s menu: thick fresh beef, seasoning on the griddle, a double layer of cheese and raw onion.

  • Fresh, not frozen beef. Since May 2018, McDonald’s restaurants in the contiguous US have cooked the Quarter Pounder from fresh beef, while the other burgers stay frozen (Chowhound). Fresh chuck gives you that same loose, juicy bite.
  • A thick patty. Smashing it, as some copycats do, turns it into a McDouble-style thin patty and loses the pink-to-gray gradient in the middle.
  • Fresh slivered onion. Mashed uses rehydrated dried onion. McDonald’s keeps the rehydrated kind for regular hamburgers and puts fresh slivers on the Quarter Pounder, and the sharp crunch is half the point.
  • Two slices of cheese. With one slice, you have a big plain cheeseburger. The second slice is what gives the QP its salty, almost saucy layer.

Where does my version fall short? The bun. A supermarket sesame bun is denser and sweeter than the chain’s, and no amount of steaming fully fixes that. Everything else gets close enough that I stopped making the drive-thru run.

Fresh onion is also why the Quarter Pounder made headlines in October 2024. Federal investigators linked an E. coli O157:H7 outbreak to its slivered onions, and Taylor Farms recalled yellow onions on October 22, 2024 (FDA). At home, buy whole onions and slice them yourself just before serving rather than using pre-cut bags.

The Quarter Pounder outside the US: France, Spain and Singapore

The same burger goes by other names abroad: Royal Cheese in France and several other European countries, Cuarto de Libra con queso in Spanish-speaking ones. Pulp Fiction made the French name famous with its “Royale with Cheese” scene (Wikipedia).

A McDonald’s Royal Cheese box from Paris, 2008. Royal Cheese is the French name of the Quarter Pounder with Cheese, the one Pulp Fiction made famous as the “Royale with Cheese”. (Photo: Steven Coutts, CC BY 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/11679961@N05/2477708220)

  • France. The Royal Cheese lists a special bun, a beef patty, melted cheese, pickles, onions, mustard and ketchup, at about 206 g and 527 kcal per burger (Open Food Facts, checked November 13, 2026). Neither the patty weight nor whether the beef is fresh or frozen is published, so don’t assume the US fresh-beef switch applies in France.
  • Spain. McDonald’s Spain holds an AENOR certification for 100% Spanish beef with no added ingredients, made from whole-muscle cuts of forequarter and flank (Agronews Castilla y León).
  • Singapore. McDonald’s Singapore lists its Quarter Pounder with Cheese at 507 kcal, 32 g protein, 25 g fat and 1,265 mg sodium (McDonald’s Singapore).

A short history of the Quarter Pounder

The Quarter Pounder was created in 1971 in Fremont, California, by McDonald’s franchisee Al Bernardin, and went on the national US menu in 1973. His slogan: “Today Fremont, tomorrow the world.” In 1991 he told the Oakland Tribune he had wanted to serve “the adult who wanted a higher ratio of meat to bun” (Wikipedia). Fifty-odd years later, that ratio is still the whole idea. It is also why I will never smash this patty.

Quarter Pounder copycat FAQ

Is the French Royal Cheese the same burger as the Quarter Pounder with Cheese? Yes. Royal Cheese is the French name of the Quarter Pounder with Cheese, with the same toppings: cheese, pickles, onions, mustard and ketchup. McDonald’s France does not publish the patty weight or the beef sourcing, so the details may differ.

What ground beef should I use for a Quarter Pounder copycat? Fresh 80/20 ground chuck. Most published copycats use it, and the 20% fat keeps a thick patty juicy all the way to 160°F (71°C).

Should I smash the patty like a smash burger? No. The Quarter Pounder is a thick patty of about 4.25 oz (120 g) raw. Smash it and you have made a different burger.

Does McDonald’s use fresh or dried onion on the Quarter Pounder? Fresh slivered onion. Regular McDonald’s hamburgers get rehydrated onion; the Quarter Pounder gets fresh slivers.

How does McDonald’s season its patties? With a salt-heavy salt and pepper blend shaken onto the patties on the grill once they are cooking, from a dedicated dispenser, according to The Takeout citing McDonald’s and former McDonald’s chef Mike Haracz.

What is the difference between a Quarter Pounder, a McDouble and a Double Cheeseburger? The Quarter Pounder has one thick patty, cooked from fresh beef in the contiguous US, with fresh slivered onion and two slices of cheese. The McDouble and the Double Cheeseburger stack two thinner patties cooked from frozen beef, with rehydrated onion. My McDouble copycat covers that build.

How many calories are in a Quarter Pounder with Cheese? McDonald’s lists 520 kcal in the US. The French Royal Cheese shows 527 kcal for about 206 g, and Singapore lists 507 kcal. Our McDonald’s calories by country ranking compares the rest of the menu.
