Culver's Wisconsin Swiss Melt Copycat Recipe

A smashed salt-and-pepper patty, natural Swiss and griddled red onions on buttered caraway rye, rebuilt for 2 from what Culver's actually publishes.

By Julia, Recipes writerPublished November 24, 2026

To make a Culver’s Wisconsin Swiss Melt copycat at home, smash a 3.5 to 4 oz (100 to 115 g) ball of 80/20 beef thin on a griddle at 450 to 480°F (230 to 250°C), season the raw side with salt and pepper only, flip and melt a slice of Swiss on top. Add red onions grilled in butter until soft and golden, then close it between 2 slices of caraway rye, buttered and toasted at the last minute. No sauce. This is my homemade reproduction, not Culver’s official recipe: the chain publishes the components but not the weights or timings, so every quantity below comes from my own test batches. Of the 3 Culver’s melts I’ve rebuilt, this is the one I make again. The caraway does the job a sauce would.

What is in Culver’s Wisconsin Swiss Melt?

Culver’s Wisconsin Swiss Melt is a patty melt: a fresh beef patty with Wisconsin Swiss and red onions between 2 slices of grilled rye, with no sauce. Culver’s describes it as “featuring tangy red onions and real Wisconsin Swiss in between 2 slices of grilled rye bread,” made with fresh, never frozen beef and Wisconsin butter (Culver’s, Melts: A Melty Classic, checked November 17, 2026).

Third-party nutrition data fills in the rest. FastFoodNutrition lists lightly buttered and toasted rye, 1 slice of Swiss, a 100% ground beef patty seasoned with salt and pepper, and grilled red onions. Allergens are gluten, milk and wheat.

It sits in Culver’s current US ButterBurgers menu next to the ButterBurger, ButterBurger Cheese, Culver’s Deluxe, Bacon Deluxe, Mushroom & Swiss and Sourdough Melt, all “always made to order with fresh, never frozen beef” (Culver’s ButterBurgers menu). You can order it single, double or triple, with 1 slice of Swiss per patty, and the patties are pressed like a smashburger (Mashed, October 10, 2026). Ingredients for 2 Wisconsin Swiss Melts

This Wisconsin Swiss Melt recipe makes 2 melts in about 25 minutes: 10 minutes of prep and 15 minutes of cooking, with a griddle or cast-iron skillet plus a second pan for the bread.

  • 7 to 8 oz (200 to 230 g) 80/20 ground beef, ideally chuck and sirloin, split into 2 balls
  • 4 slices light rye bread with caraway seeds, about 1/2 inch (1.2 cm) thick
  • 1.5 tbsp (20 g) salted butter, softened, for the bread, plus a small knob for the onions
  • 2 slices natural Swiss cheese, about 0.7 oz (20 g) each
  • 1/2 red onion, sliced into rings
  • Fine salt and freshly ground black pepper

How much beef goes in a Culver’s patty?

Culver’s doesn’t publish a patty weight, so my 3.5 to 4 oz (100 to 115 g) raw is an informed estimate. FastFoodNutrition lists the single at 172 g and the double at 259 g. 1 extra patty plus 1 slice of Swiss therefore adds about 87 g, which points to a cooked patty of roughly 65 to 70 g, or about 3.2 to 4 oz (90 to 115 g) raw. That’s my own math, not an official figure, and it’s the same patty I use in my Culver’s Sourdough Melt copycat.

Which rye and which Swiss to buy

Buy a light seeded rye with caraway, not marble rye. The only official ingredient declaration I could read lists “Rye Bread” with caraway seeds and wheat flour as its first ingredient, with no cocoa or coloring. For the cheese, pick a natural sliced Swiss rather than a processed one, since Culver’s specifies natural Swiss. Outside the US, Emmental is the closest substitute, though American Swiss is milder and less aged.

Culver’s Wisconsin Swiss Melt copycat, step by step

Grill and drain the onions first, then toast the rye and smash the beef at the same time, so the patty lands on bread that is still crisp.

1. Prep the onion, beef and bread

Slice half a red onion into rings about 1/4 inch (6 mm) thick. Roll the beef into 2 loose balls of 3.5 to 4 oz (100 to 115 g) without packing them, and keep them in the fridge. Spread the 20 g of salted butter over 1 side of each rye slice.

Raw red onion rings sliced about 1/4 inch (6 mm) thick, ready to grill in butter for the Wisconsin Swiss Melt. (Photo: Wilhelm Gunkel, Unsplash License, https://unsplash.com/photos/sliced-onion-y4Yf84W-DKc)

2. Grill and drain the red onions

Melt the small knob of butter in the skillet over medium heat and cook the onion rings 5 to 7 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and golden at the edges but not jammy. Drain them on a paper towel.

3. Start toasting the rye

Set the rye slices butter side down in a second skillet over medium heat and toast 2 to 3 minutes, until golden and crisp. Move straight on to the beef so both finish together.

4. Smash and season the patties

Heat the griddle or cast iron to 450 to 480°F (230 to 250°C). Press each beef ball flat and thin with a sturdy spatula, then sprinkle the raw top with salt and pepper. Sear 1 to 1.5 minutes, until the edges are deeply browned.

5. Flip and melt the Swiss

Scrape under the crust, flip, and lay a slice of Swiss on each patty right away. Cook another 1 to 1.5 minutes, until the cheese has slumped over the edges.

6. Assemble the melt

Set each patty on a toasted slice, cheese up, top with the drained red onions and close with the second slice, toasted side out. Wait 30 seconds, cut on the diagonal and eat hot.

Why this recipe tastes like the original

This copycat follows only what Culver’s states plainly: fresh beef pressed on the grill, salt and pepper, natural Swiss, tangy red onions and buttered, toasted caraway rye with no sauce.

  • The thin, smashed patty. Culver’s says pressure “seals the beef,” and Mashed describes the melt’s patties as pressed like a smashburger. Hard pressure on very hot iron is the closest home version of that browned, lacy edge.
  • Salted butter on the rye. Culver’s butter is cream and salt. Salted butter on caraway rye gives the firm, crisp, nutty-buttery slice that Mashed (June 15, 2026) singles out.
  • Red onions grilled, not caramelized. Culver’s calls them “tangy”; Chowhound says “griddled”; Mashed calls them “close to caramelized.” My first batch went 25 minutes, jammy and brown, and the melt turned into a sweet, generic diner sandwich. Now I stop at 5 to 7 minutes: soft, still sharp.
  • No sauce, no extras. Nothing in Culver’s description or the ingredient lists calls for sauce, so the tang comes from the onions and the rye.

How my version differs from other copycats

The best-known online copycat, from Mashed (September 5, 2026), is clear and quick, but it uses thicker patties and adds Worcestershire and avocado oil, none of which Culver’s lists.

Mashed copycat (serves 4) My version (serves 2)
Beef 1¼ lb lean, 4 1/4-inch patties, not smashed 80/20, 3.5 to 4 oz per patty, smashed thin
Cooking 3 minutes per side 1 to 1.5 minutes per side at 450 to 480°F
Extras 2 tbsp Worcestershire, avocado oil with the butter Salt and pepper only
Onions 1 large red onion, sautéed 7 minutes Half a red onion, grilled 5 to 7 minutes, drained
Butter on the bread Added after toasting Spread before toasting

Worcestershire and avocado oil appear in no composition Culver’s has published, so I leave them out. Lean beef is the bigger problem, in my view: with no sauce, the fat of an 80/20 patty is what keeps the melt juicy.

How to keep a patty melt from getting soggy

A patty melt gets soggy when wet onions or steam soften the bread, so drain the onions and toast the rye just before assembly. Chowhound ranked the Wisconsin Swiss Melt 6th of Culver’s 7 burgers in April 2025 and found it “more soggy than is typical” (Chowhound). I learned that the hard way: my first test, buttered after toasting, went limp before I’d finished cutting it. 3 fixes: butter the bread before it hits the pan, drain the onions on paper towel, and never let toasted rye sit under a cover or in foil.

Patty melt vs. burger: what’s the difference?

A patty melt is a beef patty with melted cheese and onions served on griddled sliced bread, while a burger goes on a soft bun. Mashed calls the Wisconsin Swiss Melt a patty melt rather than a burger for exactly that reason. The classic American diner patty melt is a rye patty melt with Swiss and caramelized onions; Culver’s version keeps the rye and Swiss and swaps in tangy red onions.

FAQ: Culver’s Wisconsin Swiss Melt

What is in a Culver’s Wisconsin Swiss Melt?

A fresh beef patty seasoned with salt and pepper, a slice of Wisconsin Swiss and red onions between 2 slices of buttered, grilled caraway rye. There’s no sauce.

Does the Wisconsin Swiss Melt have a sauce?

No. Neither Culver’s description nor third-party ingredient listings include a sauce. The flavor comes from the beef, Swiss, red onions and buttered rye.

Should the red onions be caramelized or just grilled?

Grilled. Culver’s calls them “tangy” and doesn’t publish a cooking time. I cook them 5 to 7 minutes in butter so they turn soft and golden but keep some bite.

What patty weight and thickness should I use for a patty melt?

I use 3.5 to 4 oz (100 to 115 g) of raw beef per patty, smashed thin. That’s an estimate based on third-party weights; Culver’s doesn’t publish its patty size.

What cheese can replace American Swiss?

Any natural sliced Swiss works best. Outside the US, Emmental is the closest match, though it’s more aged and stronger than American Swiss.

Can I order the Wisconsin Swiss Melt another way?

Yes. According to Mashed, you can swap the rye for sourdough, a kaiser roll or brioche, the Swiss for cheddar or American, and add mushrooms, bacon or jalapeños. Ask your location, since options can vary.

Can you eat at Culver’s outside the United States?

No. All of Culver’s 1,041 restaurants at the end of 2025 were in the United States, so outside the US this copycat is the way to taste it.
