Carl's Jr. California Classic Copycat Recipe

Carl's Jr. California Classic copycat: 2 charbroiled patties, American cheese, grilled onions and homemade Thousand Island sauce. Makes 2 in 50 minutes.

By Alice, Recipes writerPublished November 14, 2026

To make a Carl’s Jr. California Classic copycat at home, grill 2 thin 80/20 beef patties of about 60–70 g (2.1–2.5 oz) each over very high heat. Melt American cheese on them and stack them on a toasted bun with soft grilled onions, Thousand Island sauce, crisp lettuce and tomato. This California Classic Double Cheeseburger recipe makes 2 burgers in about 50 minutes, including 20 minutes of chilling. It works on a ridged cast-iron grill pan or a barbecue, so you get the chain’s charbroiled taste without a restaurant grill.

A homemade reproduction, not the official recipe. Carl’s Jr. publishes the build of the California Classic, but not its patty weight, its seasoning, its onion method or its sauce formula. I rebuilt it from the 2016 launch announcement, the current US menu and 2 independent tastings, and I flag every estimate below. Reference market: United States, sources checked on November 11, 2026.

What’s on a Carl’s Jr. California Classic?

A Carl’s Jr. California Classic is a double cheeseburger: 2 charbroiled beef patties, American cheese, grilled onions, Thousand Island sauce, lettuce and tomato on a bun. That is the official build Carl’s Jr. announced when it launched the burger in May 2016 (La Opinion / LatinoWire; The Impulsive Buy). It is still on the official US menu under its full name, California Classic Double Cheeseburger (Carl’s Jr.).

Some details have moved since 2016, and the official menu page doesn’t spell them out. Here is what each source says, and what I chose.

How much does a California Classic patty weigh?

Carl’s Jr. doesn’t publish the weight of the California Classic patties. The only figure the chain gives is for the bigger Cali XL: 2 3.5 oz (about 99 g) raw patties (The National Provisioner). Brand Eating says the California Classic patties are smaller than that (Brand Eating). So I use 60–70 g (2.1–2.5 oz) of raw beef per patty. Treat it as a homemade estimate, not a spec.

Thousand Island or Classic Sauce?

The 2016 launch announcement says Thousand Island; the Cali XL and some unofficial menu sites say “Classic Sauce”. Carl’s Jr. hasn’t published the Classic Sauce recipe, and I couldn’t confirm whether it’s the same sauce under a new name. I stick with Thousand Island, because that’s the sauce the brand built the burger’s story on.

Ingredients for 2 California Classic copycats

For 2 California Classic copycats you need about 260 g (9 oz) of 80/20 ground beef, American cheese, 1 onion, 2 plain buns, a quick Thousand Island sauce, lettuce and tomato. The weights are my estimates, not Carl’s Jr.’s.

Burgers

  • 240–280 g (8.5–10 oz) 80/20 ground beef, split into 4 portions of 60–70 g (2.1–2.5 oz)
  • Fine salt and ground black pepper
  • 2 to 4 slices American cheese (1 per burger for the 2025 build, 2 for the 2016 build)
  • 2 plain hamburger buns
  • 1 medium yellow onion (about 150 g / 5 oz), thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • 2 to 4 iceberg lettuce leaves
  • 4 thin tomato slices

Thousand Island sauce

  • 3 tbsp mayonnaise
  • 1 tbsp ketchup
  • 1 tbsp sweet pickle relish
  • ½ tsp white vinegar

Equipment: a ridged cast-iron grill pan or a barbecue, a skillet for the onions, parchment paper, a thin spatula and an instant-read thermometer.

Step by step: the California Classic Double Cheeseburger copycat

Make the sauce, chill the thin patties, grill the onions slowly, then grill the patties hard and fast and build the burger. Count about 50 minutes in total: 15 minutes of prep, 20 minutes of chilling and 15 minutes of cooking.

1. Mix the Thousand Island sauce

Stir 3 tbsp mayonnaise, 1 tbsp ketchup, 1 tbsp sweet pickle relish and ½ tsp white vinegar until smooth. Cover and keep it in the fridge while you prepare the rest. Half a teaspoon of vinegar sounds like nothing. It isn’t: it cuts through 2 patties and 2 slices of cheese.

2. Shape and chill the patties

Divide the beef into 4 portions of 60–70 g (2.1–2.5 oz). Press each between parchment into a thin round about 11.5 cm (4½ in) wide, a little wider than the bun. Freeze them for 20 minutes. Brand Eating found the original patties narrower than the bun (Brand Eating), so I size mine up to fill every bite.

3. Grill the onions

Melt 1 tbsp butter in a skillet over medium heat, add the sliced onion with a pinch of salt and cook for 10–12 minutes, stirring often, until soft and golden. Keep them warm on low. Brand Eating found the chain’s onions barely caramelized in 2016. I push mine further, because the grilled onions are what the brand itself says makes this burger special.

Step 3: thinly sliced yellow onions at the start of cooking in a stainless skillet, before they soften and turn golden over 10–12 minutes. Illustrative photo, not taken during this recipe. Alt: sliced onions starting to cook in a skillet for California Classic copycat grilled onions (Photo: ccbarr, CC BY-SA 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/30458688@N00/34528394490 (1024×576; loading=lazy, decoding=async))

4. Toast the buns

Toast the cut sides of the buns on the grill pan over medium heat for about 1 minute, until lightly golden, then set them aside. Do it before the pan gets very hot, or the bread scorches. Toasting also fixes the dry crumb Brand Eating noted on the original bun.

5. Grill the patties

Heat the ridged cast-iron grill pan over high heat for at least 5 minutes, until it smokes lightly, or set the barbecue up for direct high heat. Season the cold patties with salt and pepper. Grill 2–3 minutes without moving them, flip once and grill 1½–2 minutes more, until the center reaches 160 °F (71 °C), the safe minimum for ground beef (FoodSafety.gov). Don’t cut the preheat short. On my first test, after 2 minutes on the heat, the patties stuck and steamed grey instead of marking.

Step 5, barbecue option: 4 raw beef patties placed over direct heat on a charcoal kettle grill. These patties are thicker than the copycat’s 60–70 g (2.1–2.5 oz) ones, so grill yours only a few minutes and check for 160 °F (71 °C) in the center. Illustrative photo. Alt: raw beef patties on a charcoal barbecue grate over direct heat for charbroiled burgers (Photo: Stevie Rocco, CC BY 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/98336893@N00/3583276044 (1024×768; loading=lazy, decoding=async))

6. Melt the cheese

Lay a slice of American cheese on each patty right after the flip so it melts during the last minute or 2. For the 2025 build, cheese only 1 patty per burger; for the 2016 build, cheese both. On a grill pan, cover with a lid or a metal bowl for 20 seconds if the corners stay stiff.

7. Assemble the burger

Spread 1 tbsp sauce on the bottom bun, add a cheesy patty, half the grilled onions and the second patty. Top with 2 tomato slices and the lettuce, spread a little sauce on the top bun and close. Carl’s Jr. doesn’t publish its build order, so this one is mine: the onions sit between the hot patties so they stay warm, and the lettuce stays high and crisp.

Homemade Thousand Island sauce for burgers

A homemade Thousand Island sauce for burgers is mostly mayonnaise, loosened with ketchup and sweetened with pickle relish, with a splash of vinegar for bite. My ratio is 3 parts mayo to 1 part ketchup and 1 part sweet relish. That’s my approximation, not Carl’s Jr.’s formula, which has never been published.

It belongs to the same family of mayo-and-relish sauces as the special sauce in our Double Big Jack recipe, so you can swap one for the other in a pinch. It’s a different sauce from the Famous Star’s, though. There, Carl’s Jr. lists a red Special Sauce and mayonnaise as 2 separate layers, and my Famous Star copycat recipe keeps them apart for that reason.

How to get the charbroiled taste without a barbecue

The charbroiled taste comes from direct, intense heat that chars the beef in lines, and a smoking-hot ridged cast-iron grill pan is the closest indoor match. Carl’s Jr. cooks its patties over an open flame on a chain-driven broiler, and since September 9, 2026, it grills each burger to order instead of holding it warm (Restaurant Dive; Wikipedia). “Carl’s Jr. has never been in the business of serving burgers that are just good enough,” brand president Iwona Alter said of that change.

A Carl’s Jr. at Canyon Plaza in Los Angeles in May 2026, with “Charbroiled Burgers” on the façade: the chain grills its patties over an open flame, and has cooked every burger to order since September 9, 2026. Alt: Carl’s Jr. restaurant in Los Angeles with its Charbroiled Burgers sign (Photo: TaurusEmerald, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carl%27s\_Jr.\_Canyon\_Plaza\_Los\_Angeles,\_California.jpg (4032×2310; loading=lazy, decoding=async))

  • Use a ridged pan, not a flat skillet. The ridges mark the beef and let fat drip into the grooves and sizzle.
  • Preheat for 5 minutes on high. If the patty doesn’t hiss loudly, the pan isn’t ready.
  • Start cold. Thin patties overcook fast; 20 minutes in the freezer buys you time to char the outside.
  • Leave them alone. Don’t move or press the patties during the first 2–3 minutes.
  • On a barbecue, go direct and high. Thin patties need only a few minutes, so check the 160 °F (71 °C) core early.

Why this copycat tastes like the original

This copycat stays close to the original because it keeps the 4 things Carl’s Jr. built the burger around: grilled onions, Thousand Island, 2 thin charbroiled patties and American cheese. Everything else is my estimate, and labeled as 1.

Carl’s Jr. said so itself. Brad Haley, then the chain’s marketing chief, put it plainly at launch: “What really made them special was the addition of grilled onions and Thousand Island dressing.” Brand Eating’s 2016 review added 2 things the chain didn’t mention, crisp lettuce and a tomato that actually tasted of something, which is why I slice the tomato thin and keep the lettuce on top, away from the heat.

My pick is the 2016 build, with a slice of cheese on each patty. With 1 slice, the top patty and the onions sit a little dry against the sauce. 2 slices hold the whole stack together, and that’s the burger Carl’s Jr. launched.

Cali XL copycat

To make a Cali XL copycat, keep this recipe and change 3 things: 2 3.5 oz (about 99 g) patties per burger, 2 slices of American cheese, and a toasted sesame seed bun. Carl’s Jr. sold it at $5.99 under the slogan “Stacked Tall. Priced Small.” The offer ran until January 21, 2026, and I couldn’t confirm whether the Cali XL is still on the menu today. Its launch was an open swipe at McDonald’s: from November 12 to 21, 2025, the “Sad Mac Buy Back” traded a free Cali XL for a McDonald’s burger receipt, with Carl’s Jr. claiming “double the beef of a Big Mac”. For the bigger picture, read our coverage of the 2026 burger value wars.

Is the California Classic a Double-Double copy?

The press read the California Classic as Carl’s Jr.’s answer to the In-N-Out Double-Double, and the build makes the comparison hard to avoid. Foodbeast tasted it in May 2016 at about $2.70 and found less cheese, less melt and milder onions than the Double-Double, but a sauce very close to In-N-Out’s spread. My take: if you want a homemade In-N-Out-style burger, this recipe gets you most of the way, with grill marks instead of a griddle sear.

California Classic history: a 2016 tribute to Southern California

Carl’s Jr. announced the California Classic on May 25, 2016, for its 75th anniversary; the chain was founded in Los Angeles in 1941 by Carl and Margaret Karcher. The brand presented it as a tribute to California’s first iconic double cheeseburgers, made with fresh produce, American cheese, grilled onions and Thousand Island. The idea came from John Nelson, president of the Carl’s Jr. franchisee association (La Opinion / LatinoWire).

It launched at $2.49 on its own and $4.99 as a combo with fries and a drink, backed by a “Welcome to California” campaign from the agency 72andSunny, on air from May 30, 2016 (The Impulsive Buy; Brand Eating). You’ll also see a 1997 first-release date online. I found no primary source for it, so I go with May 2016.

California Classic calories and price

The official US menu lists the California Classic Double Cheeseburger at 570 calories, checked on November 11, 2026 (Carl’s Jr.). In 2016, the 283 g (10 oz) burger had 620 calories, 39 g of fat (15 g saturated), 1,130 mg of sodium, 41 g of carbohydrates (12 g sugars) and 29 g of protein (The Impulsive Buy). The drop likely reflects recipe changes such as the cheese count, but Carl’s Jr. hasn’t said. I give no nutrition for the homemade version: it depends too much on your bun, beef and cheese.

Where to eat a California Classic

The California Classic is a US menu item, and no official source confirms it outside the United States. In Mexico, Carl’s Jr. sells a different burger called the Classic Burger: grilled beef, yellow cheese, Classic Sauce and lettuce, listed at 115 MXN on an unofficial menu site (fastfoodsmenu.com). Don’t mistake it for this one. For the chain’s full story and figures, see our Carl’s Jr. brand profile.

California Classic FAQ

What’s on a Carl’s Jr. California Classic? 2 charbroiled beef patties, American cheese, grilled onions, Thousand Island sauce, lettuce and tomato on a bun, per the 2016 launch announcement. It’s sold in the US as the California Classic Double Cheeseburger.

Is the sauce Thousand Island or Classic Sauce? Carl’s Jr. announced Thousand Island in 2016. The Cali XL and some unofficial menus say Classic Sauce, whose recipe isn’t published, so I can’t confirm whether they’re the same sauce.

What’s the difference between the California Classic and the Cali XL? The Cali XL, launched in October 2025, has bigger 3.5 oz (99 g) patties, 2 slices of cheese and a toasted sesame bun, at $5.99. The California Classic Brand Eating tried that month had smaller patties, 1 slice of cheese and a plain bun, at $4.99 in Los Angeles.

Is the California Classic a copy of the In-N-Out Double-Double? It was compared to the Double-Double from launch. Foodbeast found less cheese and milder onions, but a sauce very close to In-N-Out’s spread.

How many calories are in a California Classic Double Cheeseburger? 570 calories on the official US menu, checked November 11, 2026. The 2016 version was listed at 620 calories for 283 g.

How much does the California Classic cost in the US? It launched at $2.49 in 2016. The latest price I could source is $4.99 at a Los Angeles restaurant in October 2025; prices vary by location.

Can I get a California Classic outside the US? No official source confirms it outside the United States. In Mexico, Carl’s Jr. sells a different burger called the Classic Burger.

How do I get the flame-grilled taste without a barbecue? Preheat a ridged cast-iron grill pan on high for 5 minutes, grill frozen-firm thin patties and don’t move them for the first 2–3 minutes.

What ground beef and patty weight should I use? 80/20 ground beef, 60–70 g (2.1–2.5 oz) per patty, 2 per burger. Carl’s Jr. doesn’t publish the weight; this is my estimate, below the Cali XL’s official 3.5 oz.

Is this the official Carl’s Jr. recipe? No. The build is official; the patty weight, seasoning, onion method, sauce ratio, cooking times and assembly order are my homemade choices.
