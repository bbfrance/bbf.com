Chicken Burger Recipe: Crispy or Grilled, Juicy

A crispy buttermilk chicken burger for four, a full grilled version, and the internal temperatures that keep the chicken juicy and safe to eat.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 26, 2026

The best homemade chicken burger starts with a breast sliced into thin, even cutlets, soaked for 12 hours in buttermilk, then fried or grilled until the thickest part reaches 74 °C (165 °F) on an instant-read thermometer. Buttermilk and an even thickness keep the meat tender. The thermometer keeps it safe, and it stops you from cooking the chicken into cardboard. The main recipe is a crispy buttermilk chicken burger for four, followed by a grilled version, an oven-baked one and a house sauce.

Grilled, baked or fried: which chicken burger method to choose

Choose deep-fried buttermilk chicken for the crunchiest chicken sandwich, oven-baked breaded chicken for the least fuss and less fat, and grilled chicken for the lightest burger with a smoky edge. All three are safe and juicy if the chicken is cut thin and taken off the heat at 74 °C (165 °F) in the center.

Method Texture Fat Effort Reference timing
Grilled cutlet (buttermilk brine) Tender, smoky, no crust Lowest Easy, needs a hot grill Cook to 74 °C (165 °F) in the center; brine 8 to 24 h
Breaded, oven-baked Crisp outside, drier crust than fried Low Easiest, shares a tray with the fries Jow bakes a frozen breaded cutlet at 220 °C (425 °F) for 20 to 25 min, flipped halfway
Deep-fried (buttermilk soak) Craggy, crunchy crust, very juicy Highest Most work: oil bath, thermometer Baby Love Burger fries at 180 °C (356 °F) for 7 min after a 12 h soak

Sources: Le Fooding, Baby Love Burger recipe, Jow, Weber, all checked on November 18, 2026.

My pick is the fried version, so that’s the main recipe below. It comes closest to the crispy chicken sandwich you get at a fast-food counter, and twelve hours in buttermilk forgives a lot of small mistakes at the fryer. Grilled is lighter. But with no crust, nothing protects the meat from one minute too long on the heat.

Crispy buttermilk chicken burger recipe for 4

This crispy chicken burger is built on the fried chicken burger of chef Jérémie Kanza at Baby Love Burger, in Paris’s 11th arrondissement, as published by Le Fooding. The original makes one burger; I scaled it to four. Each burger gets half a chicken breast, soaked overnight in buttermilk with paprika, coated in crushed cornflakes and flour, and fried at 180 °C (356 °F).

  • Time: 12 hours marinating, then about 30 minutes of active work
  • Serves: 4 burgers
  • Equipment: instant-read thermometer, deep pot or fryer, frying thermometer, two separate cutting boards

Ingredients

Chicken

  • 2 boneless, skinless chicken breasts (half a breast per burger)
  • 1 liter (about 4¼ cups) buttermilk
  • Paprika, to taste
  • 80 g (2.8 oz) cornflakes
  • 40 g (1.4 oz) all-purpose flour
  • Neutral oil for deep-frying

Burger

  • 4 brioche buns
  • 8 slices cheddar
  • Lettuce leaves
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 16 pickle slices

Deluxe sauce (Jow’s proportions, multiplied by four)

  • 4 tbsp crème fraîche
  • 4 tsp mayonnaise
  • 2 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 shallot, finely chopped
  • 4 tbsp chopped chives

Chicken burger step by step

Slice, soak, coat, fry to temperature, then build the burger on a toasted bun. At the stove, one number matters: 74 °C (165 °F) in the thickest part of the chicken.

1. Slice the chicken breasts into even cutlets

Lay each breast flat and cut it in half through its thickness, starting from the thick side, to get two thin, even cutlets. That gives you four cutlets, one per burger. Even thickness is the whole point: the center reaches a safe temperature before the edges dry out. Use a dedicated board and knife for the raw chicken.

Two raw, skinless chicken breasts in a bowl, ready to be cut in half through their thickness into four even cutlets, one per burger. Keep a separate board and knife for raw chicken. Alt: Two raw chicken breasts for a homemade chicken burger, before slicing into cutlets (Photo: Philippe Zuber, Unsplash License, https://unsplash.com/photos/two-raw-chicken-breasts-in-a-black-bowl-9ZrWR8R961U)

2. Soak the chicken in buttermilk for 12 hours

Whisk the paprika into the buttermilk, add the four cutlets so they are fully covered, and refrigerate for 12 hours. Baby Love Burger uses 250 ml (about 1 cup) of buttermilk per half breast, hence the liter for four. Don’t cut the soak short.

3. Make the cornflake coating

Crush the 80 g (2.8 oz) of cornflakes into coarse crumbs and mix them with the 40 g (1.4 oz) of flour in a wide dish. Lift each cutlet out of the buttermilk and let the excess drip off. Then press it firmly into the coating, both sides.

4. Stir together the deluxe sauce

Mix the crème fraîche, mayonnaise, garlic powder, chopped shallot and chives in a bowl, then keep it in the fridge until you assemble.

5. Fry at 180 °C until the center reads 74 °C

Heat the oil to 180 °C (356 °F) and fry the cutlets in batches until deep golden; Baby Love Burger gives about 7 minutes. After that, trust the probe, not the clock. The thickest part must read 74 °C (165 °F). Drain on a rack.

Buttermilk fried chicken with its craggy, deep-golden crust, the texture you’re aiming for after frying at 356 °F (180 °C). Check that the thickest part reads 165 °F (74 °C). Restaurant dish served with sides, illustrative photo, not our cutlets. Alt: Buttermilk fried chicken with a crunchy golden crust (Photo: Arnold Gatilao, CC BY 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/58819758@N00/3999409677)

6. Toast the buns

Toast each half of the buns, cut side down, for 1 minute, as Baby Love Burger does. A toasted face keeps the sauce from soaking into the bread. If you bake your own, my soft homemade brioche buns stand up to a crispy cutlet and a generous spoonful of sauce.

7. Assemble the chicken burger

Spread the sauce on the bottom bun, add lettuce, the hot chicken, 2 slices of cheddar, sliced onion and 4 pickle slices, then close the burger. Eat it straight away, while the coating still crunches.

Grilled chicken burger variation

For a grilled chicken burger, swap the cornflake coating for a salted buttermilk brine and cook the cutlets on a hot grill or griddle until they reach 74 °C (165 °F) in the center. With no crust to protect the meat, the salt in the brine is what keeps it juicy.

Weber’s buttermilk brine for roast chicken works for burger cutlets too: about 1½ pints of buttermilk, ¼ cup of kosher salt, 6 minced garlic cloves and 2 tablespoons of sugar, with the chicken left in it for 8 to 24 hours. Pat the cutlets dry before grilling, or they steam instead of browning. Cook them to temperature, then build the burger exactly as above, same sauce, same toppings.

On the grill, I use boneless thighs rather than breasts. Thigh meat stays moist at temperatures where breast is already dry, and over live fire that margin is worth having. The French food safety agency ANSES lists undercooked barbecued food as a risk, so probe every piece, not just the biggest.

Oven-baked breaded chicken burger

An oven-baked chicken burger trades a little crunch for much less oil and mess. Coat the cutlets as in step 3, bake them on a tray, flip them halfway, and pull them when the center reads 74 °C (165 °F).

For reference, Jow bakes a store-bought frozen breaded cutlet at 220 °C (425 °F) for 20 to 25 minutes, on the same tray as the fries. A homemade cutlet is thinner and goes in fresh, not frozen, so start checking well before that.

Safe internal temperature for chicken burgers

Cook chicken for burgers to an internal temperature of 74 °C (165 °F). That is the safe temperature Health Canada sets for poultry pieces, breast or thigh, and for ground poultry.

Poultry Safe internal temperature (Health Canada)
Pieces (breast, thigh) 74 °C (165 °F)
Ground poultry 74 °C (165 °F)
Whole bird 82 °C (180 °F)

France’s food safety agency ANSES states that heat treatments above 65 °C destroy Campylobacter, the main bacteria linked to poultry. I still aim for 74 °C: it’s the figure food safety agencies publish for home cooks, and at home I’d rather have the margin. ANSES also advises never eating raw poultry and keeping one cutting board for raw meat and fish and another for everything else, so bacteria don’t travel to the lettuce. Sources: Health Canada and ANSES, checked on November 18, 2026.

Chicken breast or thigh for a chicken burger

Pick breast for a classic, lean crispy chicken sandwich and thigh for a juicier, more forgiving burger, especially on the grill. The difference comes down to collagen.

According to ThermoWorks, breast meat has very little collagen and turns dry and tough above about 68 °C (155 °F). You still need 74 °C, so breast leaves you almost no margin: pull it the moment the probe hits 74 °C. Thigh is the opposite. It improves at 77 °C (170 °F) and above, often 85 °C (185 °F), and stays good up to about 93 °C (200 °F), because its collagen melts into gelatin.

Cut Best internal temperature Margin for error
Breast 74 °C (165 °F), not more Very small: dry above about 68 °C (155 °F)
Thigh 77 to 85 °C (170 to 185 °F) Wide: good up to about 93 °C (200 °F)

How to keep chicken from drying out in a burger

Juicy chicken in a burger comes from four things: a thin, even cutlet, a buttermilk soak or salted brine, thighs when you want a safety net, and a thermometer so you stop cooking at 74 °C (165 °F).

  1. Even thickness. Slice the breast in half through its thickness so the edges don’t overcook while the center catches up.
  2. Buttermilk or brine. Soak 12 hours in buttermilk for fried chicken, or 8 to 24 hours in a salted buttermilk brine for grilled chicken.
  3. The right cut. Thighs tolerate 85 °C (185 °F); breasts start drying out above about 68 °C (155 °F).
  4. A thermometer. Cooking times are guides. Only the probe tells you when the center hits 74 °C (165 °F).

Chicken burger FAQ

What internal temperature should a chicken burger reach?

A chicken burger should reach 74 °C (165 °F) in its thickest part, the safe temperature Health Canada sets for poultry pieces and ground poultry. Check it with an instant-read thermometer rather than relying on color or time.

Is breast or thigh better for a chicken burger?

Breast gives the classic lean crispy chicken sandwich but dries out above about 68 °C (155 °F), so pull it right at 74 °C (165 °F). Thigh stays juicy up to about 93 °C (200 °F), which makes it the safer choice for grilling.

How do I stop the chicken from being dry?

Slice the breast into even cutlets, soak them in buttermilk or a salted brine, and stop cooking the moment the center reads 74 °C (165 °F). Thighs give you extra margin.

Do I have to marinate the chicken in buttermilk?

No, but it helps. The fried version soaks for 12 hours in buttermilk with paprika, and the grilled version uses Weber’s salted buttermilk brine for 8 to 24 hours. Skip the soak and you have less room for error.

Can I make a crispy chicken burger in the oven or air fryer?

Yes. Coat the cutlets as for frying, cook them until the center reads 74 °C (165 °F), and flip them halfway. The crust will be less craggy than deep-fried, but you’ll use far less oil. I don’t give a fixed air fryer time: use the thermometer.

What sauce goes in a chicken burger?

A cool, creamy sauce works best against hot, crispy chicken. The deluxe sauce here mixes crème fraîche, mayonnaise, garlic powder, shallot and chives. Baby Love Burger makes its own sauce from egg yolks, vinegar, mustard, oil, ketchup and paprika.

What coating makes the crispiest chicken?

Of the methods in this recipe, crushed cornflakes mixed with flour and deep-fried give the crunchiest crust. Baby Love Burger uses 2 parts cornflakes to 1 part flour by weight, 80 g to 40 g for four burgers. A breaded cutlet baked in the oven comes out thinner and less craggy.
