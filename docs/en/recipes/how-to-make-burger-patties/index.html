How to Make Burger Patties: Size, Weight, Dimple

How to make burger patties that fit the bun and stay flat: weight per style, size, thickness, the thumb dimple and when to salt, in oz and grams.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 25, 2026

To make burger patties, weigh ¼ to ⅓ lb (113–151 g) of cold ground beef per patty and shape it about 20% wider than the bun. For a thick burger, go to about ¾ in (1.9 cm). Grilling? Press a thumb dimple into the center. Salt only the outside, right before cooking. The rest of this guide explains those numbers: why a patty shrinks, why it domes, and how the weight changes between a smash burger, a classic and a pub burger.

This guide is about shaping. Doneness, thermometers and cooking times are in my guide to burger patty internal temperature. For copycats and other techniques, browse the Burger Breton recipes section.

How to make burger patties: the 7 rules

Seven rules cover it: weigh, size to the bun, set the thickness, dimple for the grill, salt late, plan for shrinkage, and match the patty to the style.

  1. Weigh every portion: ¼ lb (113 g) or ⅓ lb (151 g) for a classic burger.
  2. Size to the bun: shape the raw patty about 20% wider than the bun (Chowhound).
  3. Set the thickness: about ¾ in (1.9 cm) for a thick grilled or pub burger (Food Network, America’s Test Kitchen).
  4. Dimple for the grill: press a thumb dent into the center. On the grill it’s needed; in a skillet, skip it (America’s Test Kitchen).
  5. Salt at the last minute: salt the outside just before cooking, never in the mix (America’s Test Kitchen).
  6. Plan for shrinkage: water evaporates and fat melts, and fattier beef loses more weight in the pan (JAOCS, 2025).
  7. Match the patty to the style: two 2 oz (57 g) balls for a smash burger, 6–8 oz (170–227 g) for a thick grilled or pub burger.

Burger patty size by style: weight, diameter and thickness

Burger patty size depends on the style: two 2 oz (57 g) balls for a smash burger, ¼ to ⅓ lb (113–151 g) for a classic, 6 oz (170 g) for a thick grilled burger and about 8 oz (227 g) for a pub burger, the last two at 4½ in (11.4 cm) across and ¾ in (1.9 cm) thick.

How much meat per burger

Plan on ¼ lb (113 g) or ⅓ lb (151 g) of ground beef per classic burger. Chowhound’s rule of thumb: 3 to 4 burgers per pound (454 g).

Going bigger? Food Network Kitchen uses 1½ lb (680 g) of chuck for four 6 oz (170 g) patties. America’s Test Kitchen grinds 2 lb (907 g) of sirloin steak tips for 4 pub-style burgers, about 8 oz (227 g) each. Take those numbers to the butcher counter.

I weigh every portion, every time. Eyeballed patties come out uneven, and uneven patties don’t finish together: one reaches 160°F (71°C) while its neighbor is still short of it. With equal weights, they go on together and come off together. If you buy one tool for burgers, make it a kitchen scale, not a patty press.

Burger patty size vs. the bun: 20% wider

Shape a burger patty about 20% wider than the bun, because it shrinks as it cooks. Chowhound’s example: for a bun about 4 in (10 cm) across, form a raw patty just under 5 in (about 12.5 cm).

Food Network and America’s Test Kitchen both shape their thick patties to 4½ in (11.4 cm). Still, measure your own bun. Buns vary, and a patty sized for someone else’s bun is a guess. If you bake my homemade burger buns, measure them once they’ve cooled and add a fifth to the diameter.

How thick a burger patty should be

A thick burger patty should be about ¾ in (1.9 cm) thick. Food Network Kitchen uses that for its 6 oz (170 g) grilled patties, and America’s Test Kitchen for its 8 oz (227 g) pub burgers.

Smash burgers are the exception. You start with a ball and flatten it on hot metal, so the pan decides the thickness, not your hands.

Why dimple burgers: the thumb trick explained

You dimple a burger patty so it stays flat instead of puffing into a dome. According to America’s Test Kitchen, the collagen in ground beef shrinks once it goes past 140°F (60°C), and that pulls the edges in and pushes the center up.

On the grill: dimple every patty

On a grill or under a broiler, dimple every patty. Heat reaches the edges as well as the bottom, so the collagen around the rim tightens and squeezes the center up (America’s Test Kitchen). How deep? Food Network Kitchen goes ½ in (1.3 cm) into a ¾ in (1.9 cm) patty. That’s their number, not a standard: America’s Test Kitchen gives no depth at all.

In a skillet: the dimple is optional

In a skillet, you can skip the dimple. America’s Test Kitchen explains that only the side touching the pan gets hot enough to tighten the collagen, so the edges never squeeze the patty into a dome. Most burger pages never mention this. Cast iron is my weeknight default, so it’s the step I skip most often.

How to keep burgers from shrinking

You can’t stop a burger patty from shrinking, but you can plan for it. Water evaporates and fat melts as it cooks (Chowhound), so shape it 20% wider than the bun and the cooked patty will fit.

Fat matters too. A 2025 study in the Journal of the American Oil Chemists’ Society (Acevedo-Estupinan et al., vol. 102, no. 9) found that the fattier the ground meat, the lower its cooking yield. In leaner meats, more of the weight lost is water than fat. I take that trade-off every time: a fattier patty loses more weight in the pan, but the fat is what keeps it juicy. It’s why I cook 80/20 chuck patties to 160°F (71°C) and not leaner blends.

Be wary of exact shrinkage percentages. The 20–25% figure repeated across the web doesn’t trace back to a primary study, and the JAOCS abstract gives none. The 20%-wider rule is a shaping guide, not a lab result.

When to salt burger patties

Salt a burger patty on the outside, just before it goes on the heat. America’s Test Kitchen found that salt mixed in early dissolves a protein called myosin, which forms a sticky gel and gives the patty a firm, springy, sausage-like texture. Fine in a sausage. Wrong in a burger.

The one exception America’s Test Kitchen makes is beef you grind yourself for the grill. For store-bought ground beef in a skillet or on a griddle, keep the salt out of the mix.

Smash burger patty size

For a smash burger, use 2 oz (57 g) balls, two per burger, instead of one 4 oz (113 g) patty. Kenji López-Alt does it this way because two thin patties give you two crusts instead of one (via Good Food).

My colleague Julia goes up to a 115 g (about 4 oz) ball for her Shake Shack-style smash burger in cast iron, and that’s the portion I use for a single smash too. A smash ball isn’t shaped to the bun at all: roll it loosely, keep it cold and let the pan flatten it.

How to make burger patties, step by step

This method makes 4 classic ⅓ lb (151 g) patties. For a thick grilled burger, scale to 6 oz (170 g) per patty, as in the table above.

What you need: 1⅓ lb (605 g) cold 80/20 ground chuck, salt, a kitchen scale and a ruler.

1. Weigh the portions

Divide 1⅓ lb (605 g) of cold ground chuck into 4 portions of ⅓ lb (151 g) on a kitchen scale. Keep the meat in the fridge until this moment: cold fat holds the patty together with less handling.

2. Measure the bun

Measure your bun across and add 20%. For a 4 in (10 cm) bun, aim for a raw patty just under 5 in (about 12.5 cm).

3. Shape the patty

Pat each portion into a round of that width without kneading it. For a thick burger, aim for about ¾ in (1.9 cm).

(Photo: Los Muertos Crew (Cristian Rojas), Pexels License, https://www.pexels.com/photo/burger-patties-on-steel-tray-8477222/)

4. Press the dimple

For the grill or broiler, press your thumb into the center of each patty to make a shallow dent. Food Network goes ½ in (1.3 cm) deep. For a skillet, skip this step.

5. Salt and cook

Salt both sides right before cooking, then cook to 160°F (71°C) in the center, the USDA minimum for ground beef, checked with a thermometer.

Burger patty FAQ

How much meat do you need per burger patty?

Use ¼ lb (113 g) or ⅓ lb (151 g) for a classic burger, 6 to 8 oz (170–227 g) for a thick grilled or pub-style burger, and two 2 oz (57 g) balls for a smash burger. A pound (454 g) of ground beef makes 3 to 4 burgers.

How big should a burger patty be compared to the bun?

Shape it about 20% wider than the bun. For a 4 in (10 cm) bun, that’s a raw patty just under 5 in (about 12.5 cm), which shrinks to the width of the bun as it cooks.

How thick should a burger patty be?

About ¾ in (1.9 cm) for a thick grilled or pub-style burger, as in the Food Network and America’s Test Kitchen recipes. A smash burger starts as a ball and is flattened in the pan.

Why do you put a dimple in a burger patty, and is it needed in a pan?

The dimple stops the patty from doming, which happens when its collagen shrinks above 140°F (60°C). It matters on a grill or under a broiler, where heat hits the edges too. In a skillet it’s optional, because only the side touching the pan gets hot enough (America’s Test Kitchen).

How do I keep burgers from shrinking?

You can’t stop it completely: water evaporates and fat melts. Shape the patty 20% wider than the bun to make up for it. Fattier beef loses more weight but stays juicier.

Should you salt burger meat before forming patties?

No. Salt the outside just before cooking. Salt mixed in early dissolves myosin and gives the patty a firm, sausage-like texture. America’s Test Kitchen’s only exception is beef you grind yourself for the grill.
