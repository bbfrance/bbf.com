Lamb Burger Recipe: Feta and Yogurt-Mint Sauce

Lamb shoulder at 20% fat, feta folded into the patty, a cold yogurt-mint sauce and the 160°F (71°C) rule, plus Greek, Moroccan and Australian takes.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 27, 2026

A juicy lamb burger starts with ground lamb shoulder at about 20% fat, seasoned with cumin, oregano, garlic and lemon zest, with crumbled feta folded into the meat. Shape 150 g (5.3 oz) patties 1.5 cm (⅝ in) thick, sear them over medium-high heat and cook until a thermometer reads 160°F (71°C) in the center. Serve them in a toasted bun with a cold yogurt, mint and feta sauce. Every quantity below is in grams and US units. After the method come three variations: Greek, Moroccan harissa and Australian beetroot.

Lamb burger recipe at a glance

This lamb burger recipe makes 4 burgers in about 65 minutes, including 30 minutes of chilling for the patties.
Yield 4 burgers
Prep time 20 minutes
Chilling time 30 minutes
Cook time 12 to 14 minutes
Total time About 65 minutes
Patty 150 g (5.3 oz), 1.5 cm (⅝ in) thick
Safe internal temperature 160°F (71°C)
Equipment Skillet, griddle or grill, and an instant-read thermometer

Choosing the meat: which cut of lamb and how much fat

Ground lamb shoulder with roughly 20% fat makes the juiciest lamb burger, because the fat bastes the patty while it cooks to a safe temperature.

Shoulder is a working muscle, marbled all the way through. Jamie Oliver uses it for his Moroccan-style lamb burgers: 600 g of minced shoulder for 6 burgers. Ask your butcher to grind it once, coarsely. Or grind it yourself.

Many burger recipes call for 20 to 30% fat, meaning meat that is 70 to 80% lean, and The Takeout gives the same range for ground lamb. I aim for 20%. Leaner lamb, like the lean mince in the Australian Lamb recipe, is fine for a quick, thin patty. Take a thick one all the way to 160°F, though, and lean meat dries out first. Buying pre-packed ground lamb? Check the lean-to-fat ratio on the label.

Lamb burger ingredients

Each lamb burger needs 150 g of ground lamb, and the sauce uses Greek yogurt, fresh mint and feta.

For the patties

  • 600 g (1⅓ lb) ground lamb shoulder, about 20% fat, kept cold
  • 60 g (2 oz, about ⅓ cup) feta, crumbled
  • 1 garlic clove (5 g), finely grated
  • 2 g (1 tsp) ground cumin
  • 1 g (1 tsp) dried oregano
  • Finely grated zest of 1 lemon
  • 6 g (1 tsp) fine salt
  • 1 g (½ tsp) freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp (15 ml) olive oil, for the pan

For the yogurt-mint-feta sauce

  • 200 g (a scant 1 cup) plain Greek yogurt
  • 40 g (1.4 oz, about ¼ cup) feta, crumbled
  • 15 g (½ oz, a large handful) fresh mint leaves, finely chopped
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) lemon juice
  • A pinch of salt and black pepper

To build the burgers

  • 4 burger buns
  • 4 cos (romaine) lettuce leaves
  • 1 large tomato, sliced
  • ½ red onion, thinly sliced

How to make a lamb burger, step by step

A lamb burger takes five steps: make the sauce, season the meat, shape and chill the patties, cook them to 160°F (71°C), then build the burger.

1. Make the yogurt-mint sauce

Stir the yogurt, crumbled feta, chopped mint and lemon juice together, then season with a pinch of salt and pepper. Refrigerate until serving so the sauce stays cold against the hot patty. I make it first on purpose: ten minutes in the fridge lets the mint flavor the yogurt.

Illustrative photo, not made from this recipe. 1024×678, lazy-loaded. (Photo: jeffreyw, CC BY 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/7927684@N03/10257686745)

2. Season the lamb

Put the cold ground lamb in a bowl with the garlic, cumin, oregano, lemon zest, salt and pepper. Add the crumbled feta and mix with your fingertips for 30 seconds, just until the spices are evenly spread. Stop there. Overworked lamb turns dense and springy.

3. Shape and chill the patties

Divide the meat into four 150 g (5.3 oz) balls. Flatten each one into a patty 1.5 cm (⅝ in) thick and slightly wider than the bun, and press a shallow dimple in the center with your thumb. The dimple keeps the patty flat instead of doming as it shrinks. Chill the patties for 30 minutes so they hold together in the pan.

4. Cook the patties to 160°F (71°C)

Heat the oil in a skillet or griddle over medium-high heat, or prepare a grill for direct medium-high heat. Cook the patties for 6 to 7 minutes per side, flipping once and never pressing them. Insert an instant-read thermometer through the side into the center: the burger is done at 160°F (71°C). I start checking just before the 12-minute mark, because a patty this thin climbs fast at the end.

5. Build the lamb burgers

Toast the cut sides of the buns for 1 minute. Spread sauce on the bottom bun, then add lettuce, the patty, tomato, red onion and another spoonful of sauce. Close the burger and serve right away.

For the buns, my homemade brioche burger buns stand up well to the yogurt sauce.

Lamb burger internal temperature: why 160°F (71°C)

A lamb burger is safe to eat when its center reaches 160°F (71°C), the minimum temperature the USDA sets for ground lamb.

Grinding spreads bacteria from the surface of the meat all the way through the patty. That is why a lamb chop can be served pink but ground lamb cannot, as Clemson University’s guide to safe handling of lamb explains, citing the USDA.

Color tells you nothing. According to the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, a burger cooked to 160°F, measured with a thermometer inserted from the side, is safe whatever its color. A brown center proves nothing. A pinkish one isn’t necessarily undercooked.

In the United Kingdom, the Food Standards Agency asks that burger meat reach 70°C (158°F) for 2 minutes throughout, or an equivalent: 75°C (167°F) for 30 seconds or 80°C (176°F) for 6 seconds. Two agencies, one conclusion.

Some American recipes stop lamb burgers at 125°F (52°C) or 135°F (57°C) for a pink center, often without a word of warning. I won’t. None of the food safety agencies above accept those temperatures for ground meat, and this burger doesn’t need them: the 20% fat and the feta keep it juicy at 160°F. For the beef side of the grill, see my burger doneness and internal temperature guide.

Why feta goes inside the patty

Crumbled feta folded into the meat softens as it cooks and leaves salty, creamy pockets in the lamb burger.

Feta doesn’t melt and run like mozzarella, as Well Seasoned Studio also points out. It stays where you put it and won’t drip into the pan. Feta in the sauce alone gives you a salty top and a plain middle. Put it in the meat as well and you get a salty, creamy bite all the way through. The 40 g in the sauce then adds a second hit of tang.

Greek, Moroccan or Australian: lamb burger variations

The same patty takes three classic styles: a Greek lamb burger with feta and tzatziki, a Moroccan lamb burger with harissa, and an Australian lamb burger with beetroot.

Style Seasoning Sauce Signature toppings Reference recipe
Greek lamb burger Cumin, oregano, garlic, lemon zest, feta in the meat Yogurt, mint and feta, or tzatziki Cos lettuce, tomato, red onion This recipe; American Lamb Board (whipped feta and tzatziki)
Moroccan harissa lamb burger Cumin seeds, ground coriander, ras el hanout, smoked paprika Yogurt with harissa, lemon and mint Fresh mint Jamie Oliver
Australian lamb burger Lean lamb mince, cheese melted on top Tzatziki Canned beetroot, cos lettuce, tomato, pickled red onion, mint Australian Lamb (MLA)

Greek lamb burger with tzatziki

For a Greek lamb burger with tzatziki instead of the feta sauce, grate half a cucumber, squeeze out the water and stir it into the yogurt with the mint. Leave out the feta in the sauce and keep it in the patty. Squeeze hard: wet cucumber thins the tzatziki and soaks the bottom bun.

Moroccan lamb burger with harissa

For a Moroccan-style lamb burger, swap the oregano for Jamie Oliver’s rub: ½ tsp cumin seeds, 1 tsp ground coriander, 1 tsp ras el hanout and 1 to 2 tsp sweet smoked paprika. Stir 1 heaped tablespoon of harissa into 6 heaped tablespoons of plain yogurt for the sauce. Harissa heat varies a lot between brands, so taste the sauce before you add the whole spoonful. Beetroot is the signature topping of the Australian burger. Meat & Livestock Australia puts 8 slices of canned beetroot in its official “Ultimate Lamb Burger” for 4. Add tzatziki, cos lettuce, tomato, red onion pickled in wine vinegar and mint. What to serve with lamb burgers

Lamb burgers go best with sides that echo their Mediterranean flavors: a Greek salad, oven fries with oregano, or grilled vegetables.

Cucumber, tomato, olives and red onion cut through the richness of the lamb. A lemon wedge on the side does the rest. My pick is a plain horiatiki with no lettuce, because the burger already has its own. If someone at the table doesn’t eat meat, my homemade veggie burger recipe uses the same buns and toppings and takes the yogurt-mint sauce well. Lamb burger FAQ

What temperature should a lamb burger be cooked to?

Cook a lamb burger to an internal temperature of 160°F (71°C), measured with a thermometer inserted from the side into the center. That is the USDA minimum for ground lamb. The UK Food Standards Agency asks for 70°C (158°F) held for 2 minutes.

Can lamb burgers be pink in the middle?

Ground lamb should not be served undercooked, whatever its color. Grinding spreads surface bacteria through the whole patty, so the center must reach 160°F (71°C). According to the USDA, color is not a reliable sign of doneness: only a thermometer tells you.

What cut of lamb is best for burgers?

Shoulder is the best cut of lamb for burgers because its marbling keeps the patty juicy. I aim for about 20% fat. Many burger recipes recommend between 20 and 30%.

What cheese goes with lamb burgers?

Feta is the classic cheese for a lamb burger: folded into the meat, it softens into salty, creamy pockets without running. Fresh goat cheese and grilled halloumi also stand up to lamb’s strong flavor.

How do I stop lamb burgers from falling apart?

Keep the meat cold, mix it only briefly and chill the shaped patties for 30 minutes before cooking. With 20% fat, the patty holds without egg or breadcrumbs. Flip it once only, and never press it in the pan.

How do you get the gamey taste out of lamb burgers?

You can’t remove it, but you can balance it. Cumin, oregano, garlic and lemon zest in the meat, plus a cold yogurt-mint sauce, soften lamb’s strong flavor without hiding it.
