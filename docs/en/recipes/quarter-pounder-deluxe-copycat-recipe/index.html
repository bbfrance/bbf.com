Quarter Pounder Deluxe Copycat Recipe at Home

Make McDonald's Quarter Pounder with Cheese Deluxe at home: fresh 4.25 oz patties, 2 slices of American cheese, lettuce, Roma tomato and mayo. Serves 2.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 20, 2026

To make a Quarter Pounder with Cheese Deluxe copycat at home, shape 4.25 oz (120 g) of fresh 80/20 ground beef into a patty, sear it on a very hot griddle and salt and pepper it only once it is in the pan. Melt 2 slices of American cheese on top after the flip. Build it on a soft sesame seed bun with ketchup, mustard, slivered onion, pickles, shredded lettuce, 3 slices of Roma tomato and mayonnaise. That is the whole trick.

This is my home reproduction, not McDonald’s recipe. The build follows what McDonald’s US lists, which I checked on November 5, 2026. The quantities, griddle heat and timings are my own, worked out in my kitchen. It makes 2 burgers: about 10 minutes of prep and 4 to 6 minutes on the heat.

What’s on a Quarter Pounder Deluxe

A Quarter Pounder with Cheese Deluxe is a quarter-pound 100% beef patty on a sesame seed bun with American cheese, ketchup, mustard, onions, pickle slices, shredded lettuce, 3 slices of Roma tomato and mayonnaise. That is how McDonald’s US lists it, and both Chowhound and Tasting Table describe the same build.

  • Patty: a quarter-pound of 100% beef, 4.25 oz (120.5 g) raw since 2015 (Fortune)
  • Cheese: 2 slices of American cheese
  • Bun: sesame seed bun
  • Classic toppings: ketchup, mustard, onions, pickle slices
  • Deluxe toppings: shredded lettuce, 3 Roma tomato slices, mayonnaise

Quarter Pounder vs Quarter Pounder Deluxe: the difference

The Deluxe is a Quarter Pounder with Cheese plus lettuce, tomato and mayonnaise. The patty, bun, cheese, onions, pickles, ketchup and mustard are identical on both, according to Tasting Table.

Tasting Table gives no store or city for those prices, and they vary from 1 restaurant to the next, so read them as a rough gap of about a dollar. My take: at the counter, the extra is debatable. At home, lettuce, a tomato and a spoon of mayo cost next to nothing, and the cold, crunchy layer is what keeps a double-cheese patty from feeling heavy. I make the Deluxe every time.

Want the plain Quarter Pounder with Cheese? Follow the recipe below and skip the lettuce, tomato and mayo. If you prefer lettuce, tomato and mayo on 2 thinner patties, my McDonald’s Daily Double copycat recipe is the closer match.

Ingredients for 2 Quarter Pounder Deluxe copycats

You need 2 fresh 4.25 oz (120 g) beef patties, 4 slices of American cheese, 2 sesame buns and the Deluxe toppings. McDonald’s does not publish its sauce doses or how much lettuce goes on each burger, so the amounts below are mine.

  • 8.5 oz (240 g) fresh ground beef, about 20% fat, split into 2 4.25 oz (120 g) portions
  • 2 soft, lightly enriched sesame seed burger buns
  • 4 slices American cheese (2 per burger, like the original)
  • 6 thin slices of Roma tomato (3 per burger, like the original)
  • About 1.4 oz (40 g) shredded iceberg lettuce
  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise (1 tbsp per burger)
  • Ketchup and yellow mustard
  • 4 to 6 dill pickle slices
  • 1/4 white onion, slivered very thin
  • Salt and black pepper

McDonald’s grinds its beef from chuck, round and sirloin trimmings, according to The Takeout. Supermarket 80/20 is the easiest match. Leaner beef dries out on a griddle this hot. The US Quarter Pound Bun lists enriched flour, water, sugar, yeast and soybean oil, with 2% or less of sesame seeds, salt, wheat gluten and dextrose. So skip the crusty brioche-style bakery buns: you want soft and slightly sweet.

Step by step: how to make a Quarter Pounder with Cheese Deluxe at home

Shape the beef, prep the toppings, sear the patties on a very hot flat surface and season them in the pan, then melt the cheese and build. McDonald’s cooks the Quarter Pounder on a flat iron grill. A griddle, plancha or cast-iron skillet is the closest thing in a home kitchen. A ridged grill pan is not.

1. Portion and shape the beef

Divide the beef into 2 4.25 oz (120 g) portions and press each into a round patty a little wider than the bun, since it shrinks. Do not salt the raw meat: McDonald’s does not season the beef before it hits the grill.

2. Prep the toppings

Sliver the onion very thin, cut the Roma tomato into 6 thin slices and shred the lettuce. Line up the cheese, pickles and sauces next to the stove. Once the beef is down, there is no time to slice anything.

3. Heat the griddle

Heat a griddle or cast-iron skillet to about 400 to 450°F (200 to 230°C). McDonald’s does not publish its grill temperature. This range is the one that gave me a dark crust before the center overcooked.

4. Sear and season the patties

Lay the patties on the hot surface and season the tops with salt and black pepper right away, the method McDonald’s describes. Leave them alone for about 2 to 3 minutes, until the underside is deeply browned. Pressing them only pushes the juice out.

5. Flip and melt the cheese

Flip the patties and lay 2 slices of American cheese on each. Cook 2 to 3 minutes more, until an instant-read thermometer reads 160°F (71°C) in the center. The USDA recommends a thermometer for ground beef because color is not a reliable sign of doneness. My burger patty internal temp guide explains why.

6. Build the Deluxe

Spread ketchup and mustard on the bottom bun. Add the patty with its cheese, then the onion and pickles, then the 3 tomato slices and the lettuce. Spread 1 tbsp of mayonnaise on the top bun and close. McDonald’s does not publish its layer order, so this one is mine: it keeps the hot cheese away from the lettuce, which wilts in seconds otherwise.

Why this copycat tastes like the original

4 things make a Quarter Pounder taste like a Quarter Pounder: a fresh 4.25 oz patty, salt and pepper added on the grill, 2 slices of American cheese and a flat-griddle sear. Lettuce, tomato and mayo make it a Deluxe. In my tests, the sear and the fresh onion mattered more than any topping.

  • Fresh beef cooked to order. McDonald’s US has used fresh, never frozen beef in Quarter Pounders since 2018, cooked when you order. It is the only McDonald’s US burger not cooked from frozen (Chowhound). Frozen patties will not give you the same crust.
  • The weight. The raw patty has weighed 4.25 oz since 2015, up from 4 oz (Fortune, Wikipedia).
  • The seasoning. Salt and pepper only, added once the patty is on the grill (The Takeout). It is the same method as my McDouble copycat recipe.
  • Double cheese. 2 slices, not one. American cheese melts flat into the hot patty and binds the onions, which is why the Quarter Pounder doesn’t fall apart.
  • Fresh onion. The Quarter Pounder with Cheese gets slivered fresh onions, not the rehydrated onions of the McDouble. The Deluxe page only says onions, so fresh slivered onion is the safest bet.

Fresh onion has a footnote. In fall 2024, an E. coli outbreak across 14 US states was traced to the slivered onions on Quarter Pounders, supplied by Taylor Farms in Colorado Springs. The beef patties tested negative. Buy whole onions and slice them yourself.

What McDonald’s does not publish

Several details of the recipe are not public, and this copycat fills them with my own estimates. Here they are, so you know which parts are fact and which are my judgment.

  • The cooked weight of today’s 4.25 oz patty. Only the old 4 oz patty’s cooked weight, about 2.8 oz, has been reported.
  • The grill temperature and cooking time.
  • The amounts of ketchup, mustard, mayonnaise and lettuce.
  • The exact recipes for McDonald’s American cheese and mayonnaise.
  • The salt-to-pepper ratio of the grill seasoning. Figures circulate online, but I found no primary source for any of them.

Royal Deluxe and Cuarto de Libra: the Quarter Pounder abroad

Outside the US, the Quarter Pounder changes its name, and its Deluxe versions change their build. In France, Belgium, Croatia and Cyprus it is the Royal Cheese. In Spain and Spanish-speaking America it is the Cuarto de Libra (con Queso) (Wikipedia).

Quarter Pounder Deluxe around the world: the US Quarter Pounder with Cheese Deluxe (American cheese, 3 Roma tomato slices, ketchup, mustard, mayo, 630 kcal) vs France’s Royal Deluxe (melted cheddar, lettuce, fresh onions, whole-grain mustard sauce, no tomato listed, 547 kcal for 227 g). Spain’s McRoyal Deluxe build is not confirmed. (Table: Burger Breton, data from McDonald’s US, Open Food Facts (ODbL), McDonald’s France 2020 via Purebreak, and Alimarket (2022))

The French Royal Deluxe is its own burger, not a renamed Deluxe. Open Food Facts lists no tomato, and its sauce contains 14% mustard. When the Royal range returned for a limited run from September 29 to November 2, 2020, McDonald’s France described it as Charolais beef, melted cheddar, crisp lettuce, fresh onions and a whole-grain mustard sauce (Purebreak). The range only comes back in limited runs, and I could not confirm it is on the French menu today. For a French-style version, drop the tomato and swap the ketchup, mustard and mayo for a whole-grain mustard sauce.

In Spain, McDonald’s changed how it prepares the Cuarto de Libra and McRoyal Deluxe on April 26, 2022: a new bun, beef cooked in its own juices, grilled onions, warmed cheese and preparation to order (Alimarket). I could not confirm the current McRoyal Deluxe toppings, so the table leaves them open.

A short history of the Quarter Pounder

The Quarter Pounder was created in 1971 in Fremont, California, by Al Bernardin, a McDonald’s franchisee and former vice president of product development at the company (Wikipedia). Deluxe versions have come and gone since. I could not confirm when the current US Deluxe launched.

FAQ: Quarter Pounder Deluxe

What is on a Quarter Pounder with Cheese Deluxe?

A quarter-pound beef patty, 2 slices of American cheese and a sesame seed bun, with ketchup, mustard, onions, pickles, shredded lettuce, 3 Roma tomato slices and mayonnaise.

What is the difference between a Quarter Pounder and a Quarter Pounder Deluxe?

The Deluxe adds shredded lettuce, Roma tomato and mayonnaise to the Quarter Pounder with Cheese. Everything else is the same.

Is the French Royal Deluxe the same burger as the US Quarter Pounder Deluxe?

No. The Royal Deluxe uses melted cheddar, lettuce, fresh onions and a whole-grain mustard sauce, with no tomato listed. The US Deluxe has American cheese, Roma tomato, ketchup, mustard and mayonnaise.

How much does a Quarter Pounder patty weigh?

4.25 oz (120.5 g) raw, the weight McDonald’s has used since 2015. The cooked weight of the current patty is not published. The old 4 oz patty weighed about 2.8 oz cooked.

How does McDonald’s season Quarter Pounder patties?

With salt and pepper only, added once the patty is on the grill. The raw beef is not seasoned.

Is Quarter Pounder beef fresh or frozen?

Fresh. McDonald’s US has used fresh, never frozen beef in Quarter Pounders since 2018, cooked to order.

Which cheese should I use for a homemade Quarter Pounder?

American cheese, 2 slices per burger, like the US original. Cheddar slices get you closer to the French Royal Deluxe.

What sauce goes on a Quarter Pounder Deluxe?

Ketchup, mustard and mayonnaise. There is no special sauce on the US Deluxe.

How many calories are in a Quarter Pounder with Cheese Deluxe?

630 calories for the McDonald’s US version.

What temperature should a burger patty reach to be safe?

The USDA recommends at least 160°F (71°C) in the center for ground beef. Check it with a thermometer, not by color.
