Double ShackBurger Recipe: Shake Shack Copycat

Double ShackBurger copycat recipe: 2 smashed 4 oz patties, American cheese, ShackSauce and a toasted potato bun, from Shake Shack's own published method.

By Julia, Recipes writerPublished November 24, 2026

To make a Double ShackBurger at home, smash 4 4 oz (113 g) pucks of cold ground beef in a hot skillet, sear them for 2 minutes 30 seconds, flip, top each with a slice of American cheese and cook for 1 more minute. Stack 2 patties on the bottom half of a buttered, toasted potato bun, then close it with a top bun spread with ShackSauce and topped with lettuce and tomato. This recipe makes 2 burgers from 1 lb (454 g) of beef. It follows the method Shake Shack published in its 2017 cookbook and in a 2020 recipe from culinary director Mark Rosati, scaled for a double. It is a homemade copycat, not the chain’s official in-store recipe.

Most copycats online cover the single ShackBurger and stop at “just cook 2 patties.” That isn’t enough. A double puts twice the beef in a bun built for one, sheds more juice, and won’t fit 4 patties in a home skillet without crowding. So this version is written for the double from the start: quantities, pan batches and the stacking order that keeps it together. If you want the one-patty version, start with my single ShackBurger recipe.

What is a Double ShackBurger?

A Double ShackBurger is Shake Shack’s signature ShackBurger with 2 beef patties and 2 slices of American cheese instead of one, served with lettuce, tomato and ShackSauce on a toasted potato bun. Shake Shack’s Canadian menu describes the ShackBurger as an “Angus beef cheeseburger with lettuce, tomato, and ShackSauce on a toasted potato bun” (Shake Shack Canada menu, checked November 6, 2026). That page does not list the double separately. The two-patty, 2-cheese build described here is the one listed on Shake Shack’s US nutrition sheet, as reported by FatSecret (see the FAQ).

Don’t confuse it with the Double Cheeseburger. That one comes with cheese and the toppings you choose, but no sauce, lettuce or tomato by default. I wrote a separate Shake Shack Double Cheeseburger recipe for it. It isn’t the cookbook’s “Roadside Double” either. That burger uses Swiss cheese, onions cooked with beer and bacon, and Dijon mustard on the bun (Leite’s Culinaria).

Ingredients for 2 Double ShackBurgers

You need 1 lb of cold ground beef, 4 slices of American cheese, 2 potato buns, butter, lettuce, Roma tomato and a half batch of ShackSauce.

For the burgers

  • 1 lb (454 g) cold ground beef, shaped into 4 pucks of 4 oz (113 g), 1 inch (2.5 cm) thick
  • 4 slices American cheese
  • 2 potato buns (Martin’s Potato Rolls are the original)
  • 2 tbsp (28 g) unsalted butter, melted
  • 2 leaves green leaf lettuce
  • 4 slices Roma tomato, 1/4 inch (6 mm) thick
  • Kosher salt and black pepper

For the ShackSauce (half batch)

  • 1/4 cup (55 to 60 g) Hellmann’s mayonnaise
  • 1/2 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 3/8 to 1/2 tsp Heinz ketchup
  • 1/8 tsp kosher dill pickle brine
  • 1 small pinch cayenne pepper

ShackSauce recipe: what the cookbook says

The cookbook ShackSauce is mayonnaise, Dijon mustard, ketchup, kosher dill pickle brine and a pinch of cayenne. For 4 burgers, the book uses 1/2 cup mayo, 1 tbsp Dijon, 1 pinch cayenne and 1/4 tsp pickle brine (Tasting Table, Time Out New York).

The sources disagree on the ketchup. Tasting Table and Mark Rosati’s 2020 Time Out recipe use 1 tsp for 4 burgers. The Takeout and the Atlanta Journal-Constitution give 3/4 tsp. Hence the range in my half batch: 3/8 to 1/2 tsp. With 2 patties I go to the top of it, because the extra beef can take a little more sweetness.

1 thing is clear: the formula served in the restaurants has never been published. Versions with paprika or garlic come from copycat writers, not from Shake Shack. Treat the cookbook sauce as the closest public reference, not as the real thing.

Step-by-step Double ShackBurger

Make the sauce and prep the toppings first. Once the beef hits the pan, everything moves fast.

1. Make the ShackSauce

Whisk the mayonnaise, Dijon, ketchup, pickle brine and cayenne in a small bowl. Refrigerate it while you cook.

2. Shape and chill the beef

Divide 1 lb (454 g) of ground beef into 4 pucks of 4 oz (113 g), each about 1 inch (2.5 cm) thick. Keep them in the fridge until the pan is hot: the cookbook insists on cold meat.

3. Prep the lettuce and tomato

Wash and dry 2 lettuce leaves. Slice the Roma tomato into 4 rounds, 1/4 inch (6 mm) thick.

4. Toast the buns

Brush the cut sides of the buns with melted butter. Toast them cut side down for 2 to 3 minutes over medium-low heat until golden, then set them aside.

5. Preheat the pan and smash

Heat the skillet over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes. Season the pucks with salt and pepper, then press each one to about 1/3 inch (8 mm) with a sturdy spatula. Sear for 2 minutes 30 seconds without moving them.

6. Flip and add the cheese

Flip each patty, lay a slice of American cheese on top and cook for 1 more minute. The cheese should melt into the crust.

7. Cook in batches

Cook all 4 patties at once only if they fit with room to spare. Otherwise, do 2 batches of 2. Build the first burger while the second batch cooks, so it stays hot.

Total: about 12 minutes of cooking. (Chart: Julia (Burger Breton), original work, CC BY 4.0; timings from the Shake Shack cookbook recipe (Tasting Table))

8. Build the Double ShackBurger

Put 1 cheese-topped patty on the bottom bun and stack the second on top. Spread ShackSauce on the top bun, add a lettuce leaf and 2 tomato slices, then close the burger. Chowhound documents this order: sauce, lettuce and tomato go on the top bun, and the patties sit directly on the bottom bun (Chowhound, March 7, 2026).

Cooking time: 2 min 30 s plus 1 minute

Cook each patty 2 minutes 30 seconds before the flip and 1 minute after it with the cheese, as in the Tasting Table version of the cookbook recipe. Summaries of the cookbook in Time Out (2020) and the AJC (2017) say “2 1/2 minutes per side.” I don’t follow them: with patties this thin, a full second side overcooks the beef and the cheese slides off. My cue before flipping is the edge, deep brown and lacy, more than the timer. For doneness targets, see my colleague Clara’s burger patty internal temperature guide.

How to keep the bun from falling apart with 2 patties

A buttered, well-toasted bun and the right stacking order keep a Double ShackBurger together. Tasting Table ranked the Double ShackBurger the best fast-food double cheeseburger on June 14, 2026. Its reviewer praised “the best texture and a mouthwatering flavor” but admitted the potato bun “didn’t quite house her double cheeseburger with the utmost structural integrity” (Tasting Table). I agree: the bun is the weak point of the double, at home as much as in the restaurant.

  • Toast every cut side to golden. The butter and crust slow down how fast the juices soak into the crumb.
  • Keep the sauce on the top bun. The bottom bun only meets the patty and its cheese.
  • Let the cheese glue the stack. The melted slice on the lower patty holds the upper 1 in place.
  • Serve at once. A double sheds more juice than a single, and the bun softens by the minute.

Why this recipe tastes like the original

This copycat comes close because it uses the chain’s own published method: same patty weight, smash thickness, timing, cookbook sauce and assembly. Mark Rosati repeated those quantities in Time Out New York on April 15, 2020. That year, Shake Shack also sold DIY ShackBurger kits for 8 burgers in the US and Mexico. The Mexican kits held Angus patties, Martin’s Potato Rolls, American cheese, lettuce, tomato, condiments and ShackSauce (Chilango): the same parts as this recipe.

The gap is the beef. Without the Pat LaFrieda blend, supermarket 80/20 chuck gives a slightly leaner, less beefy bite. Top Secret Recipes builds its copycat from a blend of 4 cuts if you want to grind your own. I compare more versions in my ShackBurger copycat recipe.

My take: at home, the double is the better build of the two. A single 4 oz patty smashed to 1/3 inch cools fast and gets lost in the bun. 2 of them give you the beef-to-bread ratio the potato roll seems made for.

Potato bun substitutes

Martin’s Potato Rolls are the original bun, made in Chambersburg, Pennsylvania, according to the 2017 cookbook. US supermarkets carry them widely. Outside the US, a soft potato roll is the closest match, and a soft brioche bun is the next best option. You can also bake your own with my colleague Clara’s homemade burger buns recipe. Shake Shack has not confirmed that its locations outside the US use the same bun.

Where to taste the original Double ShackBurger

Shake Shack’s US menu carries the Double ShackBurger, and it shows up in Mexico too. On Rappi, 1 Mexico City Shake Shack listed the ShackBurger at MX$140 and a “ShackBurger Doble + Papas + Bebida” combo at MX$360 on November 6, 2026. Those are delivery prices and may differ from in-store prices (Rappi). Elsewhere, check the local menu: the Canadian site, for one, does not list the double separately.

Double ShackBurger FAQ

How many calories are in a Double ShackBurger?

FatSecret lists the restaurant Double ShackBurger at 760 calories, 48 g fat (20 g saturated), 27 g carbohydrates, 6 g sugars, 51 g protein and 2,280 mg sodium (FatSecret). It attributes these figures to Shake Shack’s official US nutrition sheet, which we could not open to verify. Your homemade version will vary with your beef, bun and cheese.

Double ShackBurger nutrition facts as a bar chart: 760 calories, 51 g protein, 48 g fat (20 g saturated), 27 g carbs (6 g sugars) and 2,280 mg sodium per restaurant burger. Third-party figures (FatSecret), not checked against Shake Shack’s official sheet. (Chart: Julia (Burger Breton), original work, CC BY 4.0; data from FatSecret)

How much does a Double ShackBurger patty weigh?

Each patty is 4 oz (113 g) of raw beef, according to the published recipe, so a Double ShackBurger has 8 oz (226 g) of beef. 2 burgers take 1 lb (454 g).

Should both patties get cheese?

Yes. The Double ShackBurger comes with 2 slices of American cheese, 1 melted on each patty during the last minute of cooking.

Is the cookbook ShackSauce the real recipe?

No, not exactly. It is the version Shake Shack published for home cooks. The formula used in the restaurants has never been released.

What fat ratio should I use for a Shake Shack-style smash burger?

Shake Shack does not publish a fat ratio. I use 80/20 ground beef because it browns well when smashed thin and stays juicy.

Can I cook a Double ShackBurger on a griddle or grill?

A flat-top griddle or plancha works well, because you need a solid surface to smash the beef. Grill grates don’t work for smashing. Put a cast-iron pan or griddle plate on the grill instead.

Can I use a different cheese than American?

Sliced American cheese is what Shake Shack uses, and it melts smoothly over a thin patty. Any processed cheese slice that melts the same way is the closest substitute. Aged cheeses don’t melt as evenly.

Is there a Shake Shack in France or Spain?

No. As of November 6, 2026, we know of no Shake Shack location or announced opening in France or Spain.
