Dairy Queen Backyard Bacon Ranch Copycat Recipe

Two thin 75 g patties, white cheddar, thick-cut applewood bacon, onion rings, ranch and honey BBQ: DQ's Signature Stackburger at home, 2 doubles in 45 minutes.

By Julia, Recipes writerPublished November 27, 2026

To make a Dairy Queen Backyard Bacon Ranch burger at home, stack 2 thin 2.6 oz (75 g) seasoned beef patties with 2 slices of melted white cheddar, thick-cut applewood smoked bacon, crispy onion rings, ranch and honey BBQ sauce on a soft toasted bun. That is the official build of the Backyard Bacon Ranch Signature Stackburger, which Dairy Queen launched in the US on April 5, 2023. This copycat recipe makes 2 double burgers in about 45 minutes, including a quick homemade ranch. It is my reproduction, not Dairy Queen’s recipe: DQ publishes the toppings, never the proportions or the sauce formulas.

What is in the Dairy Queen Backyard Bacon Ranch burger?

The Dairy Queen Backyard Bacon Ranch burger is a double or triple stack of seasoned beef with white cheddar, applewood smoked bacon, onion rings, ranch and Honey BBQ sauce on a soft toasted bun. Dairy Queen’s launch announcement of April 5, 2023 describes “house-made Hidden Valley® Ranch and DQ signature Honey BBQ […] crispy, golden onion rings, thick-cut Applewood smoked bacon and White Cheddar melted to perfection.”

DQ’s National Hamburger Day 2026 announcement and Brand Eating add the rest of the spec: 2 slices of white cheddar, “100% seasoned real beef” and a bun that is “soft and toasted.” Everything else in this recipe, from the bacon brand to the bun type, is my own choice.

Is the Backyard Bacon Ranch still on the Dairy Queen menu in 2026?

Yes, in the United States: Dairy Queen listed the Backyard Bacon Ranch among the 5 Signature Stackburgers of its National Hamburger Day promotion, which ran May 25 to 31, 2026. DQ Rewards members got $1 off at participating US restaurants, on the Backyard Bacon Ranch, the FlameThrower, the Bacon Cheese Deluxe, the Cheese Deluxe and the Original Cheeseburger.

It started as a limited-time offer. Brand Eating reported on April 7, 2023, that it was available “at participating locations that serve hot food.” In Canada, the burger arrived in 2023. It was not part of the 6 Stackburgers DQ Canada launched on February 28, 2022, per Daily Hive. DQ Canada has a menu page for it, but the page blocked direct access when I checked on November 20, 2026, so I can’t confirm the current Canadian menu or price.

What is a Dairy Queen Signature Stackburger?

A Signature Stackburger is Dairy Queen’s line of double and triple burgers built on thin seasoned beef patties. The US line launched on March 30, 2022, with 5 burgers, and DQ called it “the most significant product rollout for ADQ in the past 20 years” (Business Wire). The Backyard Bacon Ranch joined a year later.

I covered the line and the patty method in my Dairy Queen Original Cheeseburger copycat recipe, and the spicy member in my FlameThrower burger copycat recipe. Same 75 g patties here, same skillet timing. If you have cooked either one, only the toppings change.

How much do the patties of a Stackburger weigh?

A Backyard Bacon Ranch double carries 1/3 lb of beef in total (about 5.3 oz, 151 g) and a triple 1/2 lb (8 oz, 227 g), so each patty weighs about 2.6 oz (75 g). Brand Eating gives those totals for “two or three beef patties,” and the DQ Canada menu page lists “double (1/3lb) or triple (1/2lb).”

Dairy Queen doesn’t say whether those weights are raw or cooked, so I use 75 g raw. Some news coverage from 2023 describes two one-third-pound patties. That contradicts DQ’s own figures and would double the beef, so ignore it.

“Stackburger” does not mean smash burger. Dairy Queen has never said it smashes its patties. I smash mine because, on a home stove, it is the most reliable way to get a thin patty with a real crust.

Ingredients for 2 Backyard Bacon Ranch double burgers

You need 10.6 oz (300 g) of ground beef, 4 slices each of white cheddar and thick-cut bacon, a handful of onion rings, a quick homemade ranch and honey BBQ sauce for 2 double burgers. Weigh the beef: equal balls cook in the same time.

For the burgers:

  • 10.6 oz (300 g) ground beef, 80/20, divided into 4 balls of 2.6 oz (75 g)
  • 1/2 teaspoon (about 3 g) fine salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
  • 4 slices sharp white cheddar
  • 4 slices thick-cut applewood smoked bacon
  • 6 frozen onion rings (about 3 per burger), plus a few extra in case some break
  • 2 soft burger buns, about 3.5 in (9 cm) wide, store-bought or homemade brioche burger buns
  • 1 tablespoon (15 g) unsalted butter, softened, for the buns
  • 1 teaspoon neutral oil (canola or sunflower)

For the homemade ranch:

  • 1/4 cup (60 g) mayonnaise
  • 1/4 cup (60 ml) 2% milk
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons ranch seasoning mix (Hidden Valley, from a packet)

For the honey BBQ:

  • 1/4 cup (60 ml) store-bought honey barbecue sauce, or 3 tablespoons regular barbecue sauce stirred with 1 tablespoon honey

Homemade ranch sauce: how Dairy Queen makes its ranch

Dairy Queen employees say the chain’s “house-made” ranch is mayonnaise and milk mixed in equal parts with a packet of Hidden Valley seasoning. That account comes from DQ workers on Reddit, reported by Dennis Lee in The Takeout on October 19, 2026. One restaurant used 2% milk. The employees say the result is less tangy than a buttermilk ranch.

Treat it as testimony, not a recipe Dairy Queen has confirmed; practices may vary from one restaurant or country to the next. I scale it down to a small batch: 60 g of mayo, 60 ml of milk and 1 1/2 teaspoons of seasoning cover 2 burgers, with some left for dipping the onion rings. Don’t skip the chill. My first batch went on straight from the bowl and ran right off the patty. After 30 minutes in the fridge, it clings.

Which BBQ sauce replaces DQ’s Honey BBQ?

Use any store-bought honey barbecue sauce, or stir 1 tablespoon of honey into 3 tablespoons of your usual barbecue sauce. Dairy Queen has never published its Honey BBQ formula, so no recipe can claim to be the real one.

Sweetness matters here. The Honey BBQ is the sweet half of the burger, the ranch the tangy half. Smooch Food’s review of May 28, 2023, from the Vancouver area, found the two balanced each other without the burger tasting too sugary (Smooch Food). Taste your sauce first, and skip the extra honey if it is already very sweet.

In my view, the ranch is the reason to build this burger at all. Leave it off and you have bacon, rings and BBQ on thin patties, a trio Carl’s Jr. has sold for decades. Put it on and the cool, mild sauce cuts through the bacon fat and the sugar.

How to make the Backyard Bacon Ranch copycat, step by step

Make the ranch first so it chills, cook the bacon and onion rings, then smash the patties last: the beef takes about 5 minutes and should go straight into the buns.

1. Mix the ranch sauce

Whisk 1/4 cup (60 g) of mayonnaise, 1/4 cup (60 ml) of 2% milk and 1 1/2 teaspoons of ranch seasoning mix until smooth. Cover and chill for 30 minutes so it thickens and the herbs hydrate.

2. Cook the thick-cut bacon

Lay 4 slices of thick-cut applewood smoked bacon in a cold cast-iron skillet and set it over medium heat. Cook 8 to 10 minutes, turning a few times, until crisp at the edges, then drain on paper towels. Pour off all but a thin film of fat.

3. Bake the onion rings extra crisp

Bake 6 to 8 frozen onion rings following the package directions, then leave them in a few minutes past the minimum time, until deep golden. Cool them on a wire rack, not a plate, so the bottoms stay crisp.

4. Portion the beef

Divide 300 g of cold ground beef into 4 loose balls of 75 g each. Keep them in the fridge until the skillet is hot.

5. Toast the buns

Spread the cut sides of the buns with the softened butter. Toast them cut side down in a second pan over medium heat for 60 to 90 seconds, until golden, and set aside.

6. Heat the skillet

Add the oil to the cast-iron skillet and heat it over high heat for about 4 minutes, until it reaches around 450 °F (230 °C) and the oil just starts to smoke.

7. Smash, season and sear the patties

Place 2 beef balls in the skillet with room between them. Within 10 seconds, press each one into a thin patty about 4 in (10 cm) wide, season the top with salt and pepper, and sear 90 seconds without moving them, until the edges are deep brown.

8. Flip and melt the white cheddar

Scrape the patties up, flip them and lay a slice of white cheddar on each. Cook 30 to 45 seconds, until the cheese melts, move them to a warm plate and repeat with the last 2 balls.

9. Assemble the Backyard Bacon Ranch

Spread about 1 tablespoon of ranch on the bottom bun and stack 2 cheese-covered patties. Add 2 slices of bacon, then 3 onion rings, drizzle about 1 tablespoon of honey BBQ over the rings, close with the top bun and serve right away.

Dairy Queen doesn’t publish the stacking order. On my first try I laid the rings straight on the melted cheese, and they went soft before I was halfway through. Now they go on top, under the BBQ sauce, away from the steam of the patties, and they hold their crunch to the last bite. Smooch Food warned that the original is hard to eat without a mess. Mine is too. Serve it with napkins.

How close this copycat gets to the original Backyard Bacon Ranch

On everything Dairy Queen publishes, this copycat follows the original: the patty weight (75 g each, 1/3 lb per double) and the topping list with nothing added, meaning white cheddar, applewood smoked bacon, onion rings, ranch and honey BBQ.

The ranch follows the method DQ employees describe, which gives a thinner, milder sauce than bottled dressing. Smashing and a 90-second sear give the thin, browned patties of a Stackburger. Two gaps remain, and I won’t pretend otherwise: DQ’s beef seasoning and its Honey BBQ formula are unpublished, so those parts are educated guesses. If you upgrade only one ingredient, make it the bacon. Frozen onion rings do the job; flimsy bacon disappears under everything else.

Backyard Bacon Ranch copycat FAQ

What is in the Backyard Bacon Ranch Signature Stackburger?

Two or three seasoned beef patties, 2 slices of white cheddar, thick-cut applewood smoked bacon, onion rings, Hidden Valley ranch made in the restaurant and DQ’s Honey BBQ sauce, on a soft toasted bun. That is Dairy Queen’s official description from its April 5, 2023, launch.

What ranch does Dairy Queen use?

Dairy Queen uses Hidden Valley ranch seasoning, mixed in the restaurant. According to DQ employees quoted by The Takeout in October 2026, the sauce is equal parts mayonnaise and milk with a Hidden Valley packet. DQ has not confirmed that method.

Can I use frozen onion rings?

Yes, frozen onion rings work well and are what I use. Bake them a few minutes past the package time, cool them on a rack and put them on top of the bacon, under the BBQ sauce, so they don’t sit against the hot, juicy patties.

What can I use instead of white cheddar or applewood smoked bacon?

Any sharp white cheddar sliced from the deli counter works; a mild white cheddar melts faster but tastes flatter. For the bacon, any thick-cut smoked bacon will do if you can’t find applewood smoked. In the UK, thick-cut smoked streaky bacon is the closest match.

How is it different from the Carl’s Jr. Western Bacon Cheeseburger?

Both burgers rely on the same trio of bacon, onion rings and barbecue sauce. The Dairy Queen version adds a ranch layer next to the Honey BBQ, uses white cheddar and stacks thin 75 g patties. My colleague Alice rebuilt the Carl’s Jr. classic in her Western Bacon Cheeseburger copycat recipe.

Can I make a triple?

Yes: add a third 75 g ball of beef per burger, for 450 g in total for 2 triples, and a third slice of cheese only if you like it extra cheesy, since DQ lists 2 slices. That gives you DQ’s 1/2 lb triple.
