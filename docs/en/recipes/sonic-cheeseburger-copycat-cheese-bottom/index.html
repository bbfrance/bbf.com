Sonic Cheeseburger Copycat: Cheese on the Bottom

How to make a Sonic Cheeseburger at home for 2, rebuilt from Sonic's 2021 ingredient order, with every detail marked as official, reported or my own guess.

By Alice, Recipes writerPublished November 14, 2026

This Sonic Cheeseburger copycat recipe makes 2 burgers in about 20 minutes of hands-on work, plus an hour for the patties to firm up in the freezer. Each one gets a seasoned 4 oz (113 g) beef patty, a slice of American cheese melted under the patty on the bottom bun, then tomatoes, ketchup, diced onions, shredded lettuce, crinkle-cut pickles and mayo on top. That stacking order is what most copycat recipes miss. It comes from the burger lineup Sonic relaunched in June 2021, and it’s why this version tastes like today’s Sonic Cheeseburger, not the old cheeseless Sonic Burger most copycats still rebuild. To be clear: this is my home version. Sonic has never published its recipe.

What is the Sonic Cheeseburger?

The Sonic Cheeseburger is the single-patty cheeseburger in the National Burgers section of Sonic Drive-In’s US menu. Sonic describes it as a “100% pure seasoned beef patty layered with melty American cheese, crinkle cut pickles, ketchup, onions, creamy mayo, lettuce, and tomatoes on a golden, toasted bun” (official product page, checked November 12, 2026).

The rest of the National Burgers lineup is the SONIC Smasher variants, the Jr. Double Cheeseburger, the SuperSONIC burgers and the Hatch Green Chile Cheeseburger. The chain’s history and figures are on our Sonic Drive-In brand profile.

What changed in 2021: ketchup, mayo and a “precise order”

In June 2021, Sonic added ketchup and mayo to the Sonic Cheeseburger and announced that every ingredient would be stacked in a fixed order. On June 28, 2021, the chain said it had made “incremental improvements to every single ingredient” across its burgers. It listed the build in a “precise order”: “golden toasted bun, creamy mayo, crisp crinkle-cut pickles, crunchy fresh lettuce, fresh diced onions, tangy ketchup, hand-sliced USA-grown tomatoes, juicy 100% pure beef patty and melty American cheese” (Inspire Brands, Franchising.com).

According to Sonic, the update brought lighter buns, a thicker patty, more melted cheese and crunchier pickles. Scott Uehlein, Sonic’s vice president of product innovation, called them “incremental yet meaningful ingredient improvements” (Mashed, July 28, 2021). On the Sonic Cheeseburger and the SuperSONIC Double Cheeseburger, ketchup and mayo were the only new ingredients (The Fast Food Post, July 16, 2021). The Takeout’s Dennis Lee reported that they replaced the mustard Sonic used to serve (The Takeout, July 28, 2021).

Before that, the sauce was your call. Sonic’s 2016 printed menu described the burger as “¼ lb. pure beef with mayo, mustard, or ketchup,” so customers picked one. When the relaunched Sonic Cheeseburger arrived in July 2021, it cost $4.59, with prices varying by location (The Fast Food Post).

Why is the cheese on the bottom at Sonic?

At Sonic, the American cheese goes on the bottom bun under the patty, not on top of it. 2 separate taste tests confirm it. Carmen Varner at Tasting Table pointed out the “cheese placement on the bottom” (Tasting Table, October 27, 2026), and Wilder Shaw at Cheapism described “American cheese positioned beneath the patty on the bottom bun” (Cheapism).

Sonic’s 2021 list runs from the bun to the cheese. I read it from the top down: top bun, mayo, pickles, lettuce, onions, ketchup, tomatoes, patty, cheese, bottom bun. That reading is my own reconstruction, because Sonic doesn’t say which way the list runs. It does match what both tasters found on their burgers. I couldn’t find an official explanation for the cheese on the bottom.

Does it matter? I built one burger each way on the same evening, and yes. With the slice on top, I had a generic diner cheeseburger. Underneath, the cheese melts into the toasted bun and the seared beef is the first thing you taste. I wouldn’t go back.

Sonic Cheeseburger ingredients for 2 burgers

For 2 burgers you need 8 oz (227 g) of ground beef, 2 large white buns and 2 slices of American cheese. The topping amounts per burger come from Todd Wilbur’s Top Secret Recipes copycat of the old Sonic Burger (Top Secret Recipes). I added the cheese and ketchup to match the current build.

  • 8 oz (227 g) ground beef, shaped into 2 patties of 4 oz (113 g)
  • 2 large white hamburger buns
  • 1 tbsp butter, softened, for the buns
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 2 slices American cheese
  • 4 tsp mayonnaise (2 tsp per burger)
  • 6 crinkle-cut dill pickle slices (3 per burger)
  • 2 tbsp diced white onion (1 tbsp per burger)
  • 2/3 cup shredded iceberg lettuce (1/3 cup per burger)
  • 4 tomato slices (2 per burger)
  • 4 tsp ketchup (2 tsp per burger)

How to make a Sonic Cheeseburger at home, step by step

To make a Sonic Cheeseburger at home, freeze thin 4 oz patties, sear them in a skillet over medium heat, and set them on a slice of cheese with the toppings stacked above. 5 steps, in that order.

1. Shape and freeze the patties

Shape each 4 oz (113 g) portion of beef into a thin patty a little wider than the bun, then freeze it for about 1 hour, until firm. The Top Secret Recipes method also starts from frozen patties. A frozen patty holds its shape in the pan and doesn’t overcook while the outside browns.

2. Prep the toppings

Dice the onion small, shred the iceberg, slice the tomato thin and set out the pickle chips. Once the patties hit the pan, the build takes less than a minute. Have everything ready first.

3. Toast the buns

Spread the butter on the cut sides of the buns and toast them face down in a skillet over medium heat until golden, about 1 minute. A toasted bun is part of Sonic’s official description, and the crisp surface keeps ketchup and tomato juice from soaking through.

4. Cook the patties to 160°F

Season the frozen patties with salt and pepper and cook them in a skillet over medium heat for 3 to 4 minutes per side. They’re done when the center reaches 160°F (71°C), the USDA safe temperature for ground beef (USDA FSIS). If you’re not sure how to read a thermometer in a thin patty, see our burger patty internal temperature guide.

5. Stack in Sonic’s 2021 order

Put a slice of American cheese on each bottom bun and set the hot patty right on it so its heat melts the cheese. Then add 2 tomato slices, 2 tsp of ketchup, 1 tbsp of onion, 1/3 cup of lettuce and 3 pickle chips. Spread 2 tsp of mayo on the inside of the top bun, close the burger and serve right away.

Why this recipe tastes like the original

This copycat comes close because it follows the post-2021 build: cheese under the patty, ketchup and mayo together, no mustard. Most copycats that rank today, including Top Secret Recipes and the Food.com “House Burger,” still rebuild the older Sonic Burger, with mayo on the bottom and no cheese or ketchup.

Tasting Table found the current burger “slightly more comparable to McDonald’s cheeseburgers” but “noticeably larger.” Fair enough. My view: without mustard, the pickles and the raw onion do all the heavy lifting against the beef and cheese. Cut back on either and the burger goes flat. So I keep 3 pickle chips per burger and never swap the raw onion for grilled. For another drive-in classic to compare, try Julia’s Dairy Queen Original Cheeseburger copycat.

Variations: from Jr. Double to SuperSONIC

The Sonic Cheeseburger is the base for the chain’s bigger and smaller burgers, and each has its own copycat on Burger Breton:

  • Sonic Jr. Double Cheeseburger copycat: 2 small patties with ketchup and mustard.
  • SuperSONIC Bacon Double Cheeseburger copycat: 2 patties with bacon on the same base.
  • SuperSONIC Jalapeño Double Cheeseburger copycat: the spicy double.
  • All-American Bacon Smasher copycat: a patty smashed thin and crusty on the griddle, a different technique from the formed patty used here.

Sonic Cheeseburger FAQ

What comes on a Sonic Cheeseburger? A Sonic Cheeseburger comes with a seasoned beef patty, American cheese, crinkle-cut pickles, ketchup, onions, mayo, lettuce and tomatoes on a toasted bun, according to Sonic’s US menu.

Does the Sonic Cheeseburger have mustard? No. Sonic’s official description doesn’t list mustard. The Takeout reported in 2021 that ketchup and mayo replaced it, and Tasting Table noted it was missing in its October 2026 test.

How big is the Sonic Cheeseburger patty? Sonic doesn’t publish the current patty weight. The 2016 menu said ¼ lb, and in 2021 Sonic said the patty had become thicker. This recipe uses 4 oz (113 g) raw as a best estimate.

How much does a Sonic Cheeseburger cost? I couldn’t confirm a 2026 price. Wilder Shaw recorded $5.29 for a plain Sonic Cheeseburger in a Cheapism article published in January 2024 and updated in October 2025. The 2021 relaunch price was $4.59, and prices vary by location.

How many calories are in a Sonic Cheeseburger? Sonic’s nutrition pages were not accessible when I checked. Third-party nutrition sites (FatSecret, CalorieKing, Fitbit) list 700 to 720 calories and 1,380 mg of sodium. Treat those numbers as unofficial.

What’s the difference between the Sonic Cheeseburger, the Jr. Double and the SuperSONIC Double? The Sonic Cheeseburger has 1 patty. The SuperSONIC Double Cheeseburger is the double, and in 2021 it got the same ketchup-and-mayo update. The Jr. Double Cheeseburger is a smaller burger with ketchup and mustard.

In what order should I stack a Sonic Cheeseburger? From the bottom up: bottom bun, American cheese, patty, tomatoes, ketchup, onions, lettuce, pickles, mayo, top bun. This is my reading of the order Sonic announced in 2021.

Are there Sonic restaurants outside the United States? No. At the end of 2025, Sonic had 3,412 restaurants, all in the US: 3,120 franchised and 292 company-operated, with $5.242 billion in sales (QSR 50, via Yahoo Finance). The chain began in 1953 as Troy N. Smith Sr.’s Top Hat stand in Shawnee, Oklahoma, took the Sonic name in 1959, and was bought by Inspire Brands for $2.3 billion in a deal that closed on December 7, 2018 (Wikipedia).
