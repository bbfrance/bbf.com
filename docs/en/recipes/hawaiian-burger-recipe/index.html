Hawaiian Burger Recipe with Grilled Pineapple

A tested Hawaiian burger for four: 6 oz beef patties, grilled pineapple, bacon, Swiss cheese and homemade teriyaki sauce in 45 minutes.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 26, 2026

A Hawaiian burger is a beef burger topped with a ring of grilled pineapple, melted cheese and a salty meat such as bacon or ham, usually glazed with teriyaki sauce. To make four, shape 680 g (1.5 lb) of ground beef into four patties, grill 1.2 cm (1/2 in) pineapple rings for 2 to 3 minutes per side, cook the patties to 71 °C (160 °F) and stack everything on a toasted bun with teriyaki mayo. The whole thing takes about 45 minutes.

I made this burger at home several times before writing it down, and two choices settled the final version. Fresh pineapple, because canned rings in syrup scorched in my pan before they took any color. And bacon rather than ham, because the crunch stands up to soft, juicy fruit where ham just goes limp. So the recipe below is the American-style Hawaiian teriyaki burger, with bacon and Swiss cheese. Further down: the ham-and-cheese version Latin American chains sell, and Australia’s everything-on-it Tropical Whopper.

What is on a Hawaiian burger?

A Hawaiian burger always has two things: a grilled beef patty and a slice of pineapple. Most versions also have cheese, a salty meat and a sweet-salty sauce. The usual combinations look like this:

  • Pineapple: fresh rings grilled until caramelized, or canned rings in syrup.
  • Meat on top: bacon in most US recipes, ham in Peru and Colombia.
  • Cheese: Swiss or mozzarella in the US, provolone or plain sliced cheese in Latin America.
  • Sauce: teriyaki, teriyaki mayo, or plain mayonnaise.
  • Greens: lettuce and onion, sometimes tomato.

My cheese is Swiss. Its nutty edge cuts through the sweet glaze; mozzarella melts nicely but tastes of almost nothing next to teriyaki.

You need about 680 g (1.5 lb) of ground beef, one fresh pineapple, four buns and a short list of pantry staples for the teriyaki sauce. Every quantity is given in grams and in US cups and spoons.

How to make a Hawaiian burger, step by step

Make the sauce first, then grill the pineapple, cook the bacon and patties in the same pan, and assemble on toasted buns. One pan does all the cooking, and the bacon fat it collects on the way seasons the patties.

1. Make the teriyaki sauce

Put the soy sauce, water, mirin, brown sugar, honey, ginger and garlic in a small saucepan and bring to a simmer. Stir in the cornstarch slurry and simmer for 3 to 4 minutes, until the sauce coats the back of a spoon. Take it off the heat and mix 20 g (1 tbsp) of it into the mayonnaise for the teriyaki mayo.

2. Shape the patties

Divide the beef into four 170 g (6 oz) portions. Shape each into a patty about 2 cm (3/4 in) thick and 1 cm wider than the bun, because it shrinks as it cooks. Press a shallow dimple into the center with your thumb so the patty stays flat, then season both sides with salt and pepper just before cooking.

3. Grill the pineapple

Heat a grill pan or plancha over medium-high heat and brush it lightly with oil. Lay the pineapple rings down and leave them alone for 2 to 3 minutes, until they have dark grill marks. Flip, brush the top with a little teriyaki sauce and cook 2 to 3 minutes more.

Thick slices of fresh pineapple with dark grill marks and lightly charred edges. This is how the rings should look after 2 to 3 minutes per side over medium-high heat. Alt: Grilled pineapple slices with grill marks for a Hawaiian burger (Photo: ozmafan, CC BY 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/35735307@N00/6018974391)

4. Cook the bacon

Cook the bacon in the same pan over medium heat until crisp, then drain it on paper towels. Leave a thin film of bacon fat in the pan for the patties.

5. Cook the patties

Turn the heat back to medium-high and cook the patties for 4 to 5 minutes per side. After the flip, brush the tops with teriyaki sauce and lay a slice of cheese on each for the last minute, covering the pan so it melts. Check with an instant-read thermometer: the center must reach 71 °C (160 °F).

6. Toast the buns and assemble

Toast the cut sides of the buns in the pan for about 1 minute. Spread teriyaki mayo on both halves, then stack from the bottom: lettuce, patty with cheese, bacon, grilled pineapple, red onion and the top bun. Serve right away, while the pineapple is still warm.

How to grill pineapple for burgers

Cut fresh pineapple into rings 1 to 1.5 cm (about 1/2 in) thick and grill them on a lightly oiled pan over medium-high heat for 2 to 3 minutes per side, until marked and caramelized. These details decide whether you get caramel or a soggy, grey ring:

  • Cut the core out if it is hard and woody. An apple corer or a small round cutter does it cleanly.
  • Pat the rings dry before grilling. A wet ring steams instead of browning.
  • Do not move them for the first 2 minutes. The grill marks need direct, uninterrupted contact.
  • On a barbecue, grill over medium heat for about 3 minutes per side (Mayo Clinic).
  • Thin rings are another option. Food Network’s Hawaiian burger cuts 1/4 in (about 6 mm) rings and grills them about 5 minutes per side until golden and tender (Food Network). I stick with 1.2 cm: the ring stays juicy and you still get a bite of fruit through the bun.

Fresh or canned pineapple?

Fresh pineapple gives the best grilled pineapple burger, because it holds its shape and browns deeply. Canned rings work too, and they are the norm in many Latin American recipes. Drain them well and pat them very dry, or the syrup will burn before the ring takes on color.

Homemade teriyaki sauce for a Hawaiian teriyaki burger

Teriyaki is a Japanese technique: food grilled and glazed with a reduced sauce of soy sauce, mirin or sake, and sugar. The name says it: “teri” means shine and “yaki” means grilled (Wikipedia). A teriyaki burger is simply a burger glazed with that sauce.

My version adds honey for gloss and ginger for a bit of heat. I keep it thick on purpose, with the cornstarch: a thin teriyaki runs off the patty and soaks the bottom bun. If you like teriyaki burgers, try my MOS Burger-style teriyaki burger recipe, the Japanese chain’s classic, which has no pineapple at all.

Hawaiian burger variations: ham, bacon or teriyaki

The main versions differ in the meat on top and the sauce: ham and mayo in Latin America, bacon and teriyaki in the United States, and an everything-on-it version in Australia. All of them keep the beef and the pineapple.

My pick is the bacon-teriyaki version, the one in this recipe. The ham version is milder and closer to a ham-and-cheese sandwich with fruit, which is not a criticism: it is the one I would make for anyone who finds teriyaki too sweet.

The ham and pineapple version from Peru

In Peru, the burger chain Bembos sells a Hamburguesa Hawaiana: a grilled burger with cheese, ham, pineapple in syrup and mayonnaise. It cost S/ 22.90 (Peruvian soles) on the chain’s online menu when I checked it on November 17, 2026; prices can vary by restaurant and ordering channel (Bembos). To make it at home, swap the bacon in this recipe for one slice of ham per burger, warmed in the pan for 30 seconds, and use plain mayo.

A similar home recipe from Colombia uses 450 g (1 lb) of beef seasoned with garlic, scallion, onion and cumin, topped with ham, pineapple and provolone (My Colombian Recipes).

Australia’s Tropical Whopper

Hungry Jack’s, Burger King’s name in Australia, lists a Tropical Whopper with grilled beef, bacon, cheese, egg, BBQ sauce, ketchup, onion, two slices of pineapple, tomato, lettuce, beetroot and mayonnaise (Hungry Jack’s). I could not confirm it is on the menu right now. For another Hungry Jack’s build at home, see my Hungry Jack’s Bacon Deluxe recipe.

Why is it called a Hawaiian burger?

The Hawaiian burger is named after the ham-and-pineapple pairing of Hawaiian pizza, which was created in Canada, not Hawaii. Sam Panopoulos put ham and pineapple on a pizza at the Satellite Restaurant in Chatham, Ontario, in 1962 (Wikipedia). Who first put pineapple on a burger is not documented by any source I could verify.

Alt: Hawaiian pizza with ham and pineapple on a wooden board (Photo: PattayaPatrol, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=189487855)

The teriyaki side has a real Hawaiian link. Teriyaki arrived in Hawaii with Japanese immigrants, and teriyaki dishes are now a staple of the Hawaiian plate lunch (Wikipedia). Our page on burgers in Honolulu, from teriyaki burgers to the loco moco, covers the local styles. Some American recipes call the same burger an “aloha burger”.

Pineapple also had a brief moment at McDonald’s. In the early 1960s, Ray Kroc pitched the Hula Burger, a breaded and fried pineapple slice with cheese on a toasted bun, against the Filet-O-Fish created by Cincinnati franchisee Lou Groen. The Filet-O-Fish won (Wikipedia; Smithsonian Magazine).

Hawaiian burger FAQ

Should I use fresh or canned pineapple for burgers?

Fresh pineapple browns better and keeps its shape on the grill. Canned rings work if you drain them and pat them very dry, so the syrup does not burn.

How long do you grill pineapple for burgers?

Grill 1 to 1.5 cm (1/2 in) rings for 2 to 3 minutes per side over medium-high heat on a grill pan, or about 3 minutes per side over medium heat on a barbecue, until they show dark grill marks.

What internal temperature should a Hawaiian burger reach?

A beef patty is safe at 71 °C (160 °F) in the center, according to the USDA. Check it with a thermometer, because the color of the meat is not a reliable sign of doneness (USDA FSIS).

Is the Hawaiian burger from Hawaii?

Not in any documented way. Its ham-and-pineapple pairing comes from Hawaiian pizza, created in Ontario, Canada, in 1962. The teriyaki glaze is the part with a real Hawaiian connection, through the islands’ Japanese community.

How many calories does the pineapple add?

Raw pineapple has about 50 calories per 100 g, according to USDA FoodData Central figures (getfoodfacts.com). One ring adds little: the beef, bun, cheese and sauce make up most of the burger’s total.
