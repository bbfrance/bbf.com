A&W Cheeseburger Copycat Recipe (US Double)

2 thin beef patties, American cheese, ketchup, mustard and pickles on a lightly toasted bun: the A&W Restaurants cheeseburger rebuilt at home in 20 minutes.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 20, 2026

This A&W cheeseburger copycat recipe rebuilds the US Cheeseburger from A&W Restaurants with exactly what the chain lists: 2 beef patties, American cheese, ketchup, mustard and pickles on a lightly toasted bun. For 2 burgers you need 300 g (10.6 oz) of 80/20 ground chuck, 4 slices of American cheese and 2 plain buns. Count about 25 minutes, 10 of them spent chilling the patties. This is my homemade reproduction, not A&W’s recipe. The chain does not publish patty weights, seasoning or cooking method, so those numbers are mine, settled after 4 rounds at the griddle.

Which A&W Cheeseburger is this recipe based on?

This recipe copies the Cheeseburger on the US menu of A&W Restaurants, as described on awrestaurants.com and checked on November 11, 2026. A&W Restaurants is headquartered in Lexington, Kentucky, and has been owned since December 19, 2011 by A Great American Brand, LLC, a group of franchisees (Wikipedia).

It is not the same company as A&W Canada. The Canadian division was sold to Unilever in 1972 and has run as a separate, unaffiliated chain ever since. If you are after the Canadian classic with its own sauce, see my A&W Teen Burger copycat recipe and our A&W Canada brand profile.

Menus can vary by franchisee, so treat this as the US reference as of November 2026.

What’s on an A&W Cheeseburger? The official ingredients

The A&W Cheeseburger has 2 beef patties, American cheese, ketchup, mustard and pickles on a lightly toasted bun. The official description reads: “2 juicy beef patties and melty American cheese topped with ketchup, mustard, and pickles on a lightly toasted bun,” under the line “Why mess with perfection?” A single-patty version is also offered.

No lettuce, no tomato, no onion, no Papa Sauce. Those belong to the Papa Burger, which stacks 2 patties, American cheese, lettuce, tomato, onion, pickles and Papa Sauce on the same lightly toasted bun.

The chain promotes “100% U.S. Beef.” In August 2018 it upgraded its patty and started reviewing its supply to cut antibiotics and hormones (QSR Web, August 20, 2018).

How I matched the original

The trick is restraint: thin patties, real American cheese, a ketchup-mustard hit and sour pickles, nothing else. Everything A&W does not publish, I had to guess, and I say where.

  • The lineage. In 1963 A&W launched its “Burger Family”: Papa, Mama, Teen and Baby Burger (Wikipedia). The Cheeseburger reads like a Mama-style burger with cheese and a second patty, so I borrowed from that family.
  • The patty weight. A&W does not publish it. An old, undated menu line for the Bacon Double Cheeseburger mentioned “a full 1/3 pound of 100% U.S. beef,” which works out to about 75 g (2.6 oz) per patty. I also tried 90 g patties: they ate like a pub burger and buried the pickles. 75 g stays.
  • The condiments. The amateur copycats on CDKitchen mix ketchup and mustard for the Mama Burger and use a plain 4-inch (10 cm) bun. I do the same. 1 swipe of the mix spreads evenly; 2 separate squirts never do.
  • The bun. Tasting Table (July 31, 2026) describes A&W buns as “puffed up and fluffy.” A soft plain bun, barely toasted, then rested in paper gets you there.

Ingredients for 2 A&W-style double cheeseburgers

You need 6 ingredients plus salt and pepper for 2 double cheeseburgers.

  • 300 g (10.6 oz) 80/20 ground chuck, divided into 4 patties of 75 g (2.6 oz)
  • 2 soft plain hamburger buns, about 4 in (10 cm) across
  • 4 slices American cheese
  • 2 tbsp ketchup
  • 2 tsp yellow mustard
  • 8 dill pickle chips
  • Salt and black pepper

For a single cheeseburger, use 1 75 g patty and 1 slice of cheese per burger. On cheese, I tested both deli-counter American and individually wrapped singles. The singles draped over the thin patties faster and looked more like the burger in A&W’s photos, so they won. A&W does not name its mustard or pickle variety. Plain yellow mustard and dill chips give the fast-food taste you expect.

Step by step: how to make an A&W Cheeseburger at home

The method is a hot griddle, thin patties, cheese melted on the flip and a quick rest in paper. Timings below are for a cast-iron skillet or griddle.

1. Shape the patties

Divide the beef into 4 75 g (2.6 oz) balls and press each into a thin disc about 4.5 in (11 cm) wide, slightly wider than the bun because it shrinks. Chill for 10 minutes so the patties hold their shape.

2. Mix the sauce and heat the griddle

Stir the ketchup and mustard together in a small bowl. Heat a cast-iron skillet or griddle over medium-high heat to about 400°F (204°C), the temperature CDKitchen uses for its A&W copycats.

3. Lightly toast the buns

Place the buns cut side down on the dry griddle for 30 to 45 seconds, until just golden. Pull them early. You want warmth and a thin crisp edge; a crunchy bun is the fastest way to lose the A&W feel.

4. Sear the patties

Season the patties with salt and pepper and lay them on the griddle. Cook about 2 minutes, until the edges brown, then flip.

5. Melt the cheese

Lay 1 slice of American cheese on each patty right after the flip and cook 1 to 2 minutes more, until the cheese drapes over the edges and the center of the beef reaches 160°F (71°C).

6. Stack and rest

Stack 2 cheesy patties on each bottom bun, spread half the ketchup-mustard mix on top, add 4 pickle chips and close with the crown. Wrap each burger in parchment or deli paper for 1 minute. The trapped steam softens the bun the way a fast-food wrapper does. Skip it and you get a nice homemade burger, not a copycat.

Single vs. double A&W Cheeseburger: calories and build

On fatsecret, the double has roughly 170 more calories than the single (550 vs. 380 kcal). These figures are not official: A&W’s own nutrition page could not be read, and the databases disagree. My verdict is simple: make the double. With 1 thin patty the bun does most of the talking; with 2, the beef and cheese finally lead.

A quick history of A&W, from root beer stand to burger chain

A&W began on June 20, 1919, when Roy W. Allen opened a root beer stand in Lodi, California. Frank Wright joined him in 1922, the first A&W restaurant opened in Sacramento in 1923, and the name comes from their initials, Allen & Wright (A&W Restaurants). Burgers came later: the Burger Family arrived in 1963, 44 years after the first root beer.

A&W Cheeseburger FAQ

What are the ingredients in an A&W Cheeseburger?

According to A&W Restaurants, it is 2 beef patties, American cheese, ketchup, mustard and pickles on a lightly toasted bun. The chain does not publish its seasoning, patty weight or bun recipe.

Does the A&W Cheeseburger have one or 2 patties?

The official US description lists 2 patties, and a single version is also offered. This recipe follows the two-patty build checked on November 11, 2026, with the single as an option.

What’s the difference between the Cheeseburger, the Papa Burger and the Teen Burger?

The Cheeseburger keeps it to ketchup, mustard and pickles. The Papa Burger adds lettuce, tomato, onion and Papa Sauce. The Teen Burger is an A&W Canada burger, covered in my Teen Burger copycat.

Are A&W Restaurants and A&W Canada the same chain?

No. A&W Canada was sold to Unilever in 1972 and has been a separate, unaffiliated company since. A&W Restaurants is the US chain based in Lexington, Kentucky.

How many calories are in an A&W Cheeseburger?

There is no official figure we could read. Public databases list 380 to 398 calories for the single (fatsecret, CalorieKing) and 550 calories for the double (fatsecret).

What ground beef should I use?

Use 80/20 ground chuck. The 20% fat keeps thin patties juicy on a hot griddle. CDKitchen’s A&W copycats also use ground chuck.

Should I toast the bun?

Yes, but lightly: 30 to 45 seconds cut side down. A&W says “lightly toasted,” and a minute wrapped in paper keeps the bun soft and fluffy.

Can I cook it on a grill or flat-top instead of a skillet?

Yes. A flat-top or griddle works best because it sears the whole patty surface. On a grill, use a cast-iron plate or griddle insert so thin patties don’t fall through and the cheese melts evenly.
