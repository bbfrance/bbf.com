Cowboy Whopper Recipe: Hungry Jack's Copycat

Cowboy Whopper recipe for 2: Hungry Jack's bacon, onion ring and smoky BBQ Whopper rebuilt at home from the chain's 3 November 2026 ingredient guide.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 25, 2026

To make a Cowboy Whopper at home, grill a 120 g beef patty very hot and melt a processed cheese slice on it. Then stack it on a toasted sesame bun with crisp smoked bacon, battered onion rings, smoky BBQ sauce, yellow mustard, mayo, pickles, iceberg lettuce and tomato. That is every component Hungry Jack’s lists for its Cowboy Whopper. The recipe below makes 2 burgers. It is my homemade rebuild, not the chain’s official recipe, and the one layer I would never drop is the mustard.

I worked from Hungry Jack’s Australia’s Whopper & Beef Burgers nutrition guide, dated 3 November 2026. It names every component and its ingredients. It gives no quantities, no rasher count and no assembly order, so every amount and every layer below is my call, and I say so each time. Hungry Jack’s is Burger King’s name in Australia: Jack Cowin opened the first restaurant in Innaloo, near Perth, in 1971. The full story is on our Hungry Jack’s brand profile.

What’s in the Hungry Jack’s Cowboy Whopper?

The Cowboy Whopper is a Whopper with cheese, bacon, a battered onion ring, smoky BBQ sauce and mustard. Hungry Jack’s sells it as “100% flame-grilled Aussie beef topped with melted cheese, double bacon, crispy onion rings, smoky BBQ sauce, mustard, pickles, lettuce, tomato and mayo on a toasted sesame seed bun”. The 3 November 2026 guide lists these components in the order below, which is not the assembly order:

  • Bun Glazed 5”: a glazed sesame bun, about 12 to 13 cm (5 inches) wide
  • Beef Patty 5”: listed as 100% beef
  • Bacon: pork, water, brine (salt, sugar, mineral salts 451 and 450, antioxidant 316, preservative 250) and wood smoke
  • Onion Ring: 50% onion in a batter of flour, rice flour, oil, thickeners, starch, sugar, onion powder, salt, raising agents, whey powder, yeast extract, corn flour, flavour and brown sugar
  • Tomato Slice
  • Cheese SOS: processed cheese made from cow’s milk, with emulsifying salts and colour 160a
  • Lettuce: iceberg
  • Mayonnaise: soybean oil, egg yolks, water, distilled vinegar, corn syrup, salt, sugar, mustard flour
  • Pickles: cucumbers, water, vinegar, salt, calcium lactate
  • Sauce BBQ Smoky: tomato paste (48%), sugar, vinegar, apple paste, thickener, caramel colour, molasses, salt, smoke flavour, dried vegetables, Worcestershire flavour, spices
  • Mustard sauce: water, mustard powder (15%), food acids, salt, sugar, thickener, starch, spices (composition given in the Double Cheeseburger entry of the same guide)

Two parts of the classic Whopper are gone: raw onion and ketchup. The onion ring replaces the raw onion. BBQ sauce and mustard replace the ketchup. The rest is the Whopper you know, which I break down in what’s in a Whopper and how to make it at home.

The marketing copy promises “double bacon”, but the guide never says how many rashers that means. “Aussie beef” comes from the brand’s Better Beef Guarantee; the guide itself only says “Beef 100%”.

Cowboy Whopper calories and kJ

A Cowboy Whopper Single has 3,481 kJ, about 832 kcal, and weighs 363 g. The Double has 4,897 kJ, about 1,170 kcal, and weighs 465 g. These are Hungry Jack’s own figures from the 3 November 2026 guide.

Hungry Jack’s uses a reference intake of 8,700 kJ a day for an adult. One Single covers about 40% of it, one Double about 56%. I converted kJ to kcal by dividing by 4.184.

How much the Cowboy toppings add

The cowboy toppings add 61 g to a Whopper Cheese. The same guide puts a Whopper at 280 g (2,437 kJ), a Whopper Cheese at 302 g (2,747 kJ) and a Double Whopper at 359 g (3,543 kJ). The Cowboy Whopper Single weighs 363 g. The 61 g gap is the bacon, the onion ring, the BBQ sauce and the mustard, minus the raw onion and ketchup.

The 79 g between a Whopper and a Double Whopper hints at the weight of one cooked 5-inch patty. That is my deduction: Hungry Jack’s does not publish the weight of its Whopper patty.

Ingredients for 2 homemade Cowboy Whoppers

You need the burger components below, plus either frozen battered onion rings or the short batter recipe that follows.

Burgers

  • 240 g (8.5 oz) beef mince, 80/20
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 2 sesame burger buns, 12 to 13 cm (5 in)
  • 4 rashers smoked bacon
  • 2 processed cheese slices
  • 30 g (1 oz) smoky BBQ sauce
  • 2 tsp mild yellow mustard
  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise
  • 6 to 8 dill pickle slices
  • 2 to 3 iceberg lettuce leaves
  • 2 slices of tomato

Homemade onion rings (my batter, for 4 to 6 rings)

  • 1 medium brown onion
  • 60 g (2.1 oz) plain flour
  • 20 g (0.7 oz) rice flour
  • 1/2 tsp onion powder
  • 1/2 tsp baking powder
  • 1 pinch salt and 1 pinch sugar
  • About 90 ml (3 fl oz) cold milk
  • Neutral oil for frying

My batter copies the logic of the official ingredient list. Wheat flour does the coating, rice flour adds crunch, baking powder lifts it, and a pinch of sugar helps it brown. Milk stands in for the whey powder.

Cowboy Whopper recipe step by step

Fry the onion rings and cook the bacon first. Then grill the patties hot and assemble fast, while the rings are still crisp. The layer order in step 6 is mine: Hungry Jack’s does not publish its assembly order.

1. Prepare the toppings

Slice the tomato and the pickles, and shred the iceberg lettuce. Divide the beef into two 120 g balls and press each into a thin patty about 1 cm wider than the bun, since it shrinks on the grill. Keep the patties in the fridge until they go on the heat.

2. Fry the onion rings

Heat the oil to 180 °C (355 °F). Cut the onion into 1 cm rings and keep the largest ones. Whisk the flours, onion powder, baking powder, salt and sugar with the cold milk until the batter coats a spoon. Dip each ring, let the excess drip off and fry for 2 to 3 minutes, until golden. Drain on a rack, not on paper, or the underside steams soft. For frozen rings, follow the packet.

Onion rings soaking in a smooth batter before frying, the step that makes the homemade onion rings for the Cowboy Whopper; fry them at 180 °C (355 °F). (Photo: Jonathunder, CC BY-SA 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:OnionRingBatter.JPG)

3. Cook the bacon

Lay the bacon in a cold frying pan and cook it over medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes, turning once, until crisp. Drain on paper towel. Two rashers per burger is my reading of “double bacon”.

4. Grill the patties and melt the cheese

Heat a grill, barbecue or cast-iron pan to about 260 °C (500 °F). Season the patties with salt and pepper and cook them about 2 minutes a side, until the centre reaches 71 °C (160 °F). Lay a cheese slice on each patty for the last 30 seconds. For thermometer technique, see our burger patty internal temperature guide.

5. Toast the buns

Toast the cut sides of the buns on the grill or in a dry pan for about a minute, until golden. Toasted crumb soaks up less of the BBQ sauce and mayo.

6. Assemble the Cowboy Whopper

Spread the mustard on the bottom bun and add the cheesy patty. Top it with two rashers of bacon, two or three onion rings and the BBQ sauce. Spread mayo on the top bun, add the lettuce, tomato and pickles, then close the burger. I keep the rings above the bacon, away from the steam of the patty, and drizzle the sauce over them rather than under them. Serve straight away: after five minutes the rings start to go soft.

Why this recipe tastes like the original

The Cowboy Whopper works because it puts smoke, acid and crunch on a flame-grilled patty. The official list gets its smoke from three places: the flame-grilling, the wood-smoked bacon and the smoke flavour in the BBQ sauce. Mustard and dill pickles bring the acid, which cuts through the fat and through a sauce made from tomato paste, sugar and molasses. The battered ring gives the crunch. Lettuce, tomato and mayo keep it a Whopper rather than a bacon burger.

That is why I keep both the mustard and the BBQ sauce, and why I think generic cowboy burger recipes miss the point. Most of them leave out the mustard, the mayo, the pickles, the tomato, the lettuce and the 5-inch sesame bun. What you get tastes of bacon and barbecue, not of a Whopper. If you have time to make one part from scratch, make the onion rings. Frozen rings are fine on a weeknight, but a homemade rice-flour batter stays crisp for longer under the sauce.

Is the Cowboy Whopper still available in Australia, and how much is it?

The Cowboy Whopper is a limited-time offer. The 3 November 2026 nutrition guide lists it as available in select Hungry Jack’s stores. It was missing from the guide printed on 14 September 2026, so it came back between those two dates. On 6 November 2026, its official product page returned an error and the official beef menu no longer listed it. Ask your local restaurant before you go.

Third-party price sites recorded A$15.55 for a Cowboy Whopper and A$21.00 for a Double Cowboy Whopper in October 2026. These are not Hungry Jack’s prices, and they vary by restaurant. One third-party site also lists a Cowboy Whopper Hunger Tamers meal: a BBQ Cheeseburger, medium thick-cut chips, 3 chicken nuggets with sauce and a medium Coke.

Cowboy Whopper FAQ

What’s in a Cowboy Whopper?

A beef patty, processed cheese, bacon, a battered onion ring, smoky BBQ sauce, mustard sauce, pickles, iceberg lettuce, tomato and mayonnaise, on a glazed 5-inch sesame bun. Unlike the classic Whopper, it has no raw onion and no ketchup.

How many calories are in a Cowboy Whopper?

The Single has 3,481 kJ, about 832 kcal. The Double Cowboy Whopper has 4,897 kJ, about 1,170 kcal. That is about 40% and 56% of Hungry Jack’s 8,700 kJ adult daily reference.

Can I buy a Cowboy Whopper at Burger King in the US?

As far as we know, it is a Hungry Jack’s product sold in Australia. We found no Cowboy Whopper at Burger King in the United States, France or Spain, though we have not checked every market.

Is the Cowboy Whopper halal?

No. Hungry Jack’s marks both the Single and the Double as not halal in its 3 November 2026 guide, and the bacon is pork.

Which allergens does the Cowboy Whopper contain?

Wheat (gluten), egg, milk, soy, sesame and mustard. It may also contain barley, oat and rye gluten, and added sulphites.

Which BBQ sauce is closest to Hungry Jack’s Smoky BBQ?

A sweet, smoky, tomato-based sauce. The official one is 48% tomato paste, with sugar, vinegar, apple, molasses, smoke flavour and Worcestershire flavour, so look for those on the label.

Should I make the onion rings or buy frozen ones?

Both work. The official ring is battered, so frozen battered rings are a quick match. Homemade rings in a flour and rice flour batter stay crisp for longer and let you pick the size.

Can I get the flame-grilled taste without a barbecue?

Mostly, yes. A cast-iron pan at about 260 °C (500 °F) gives a deep crust, and the wood-smoked bacon and smoky BBQ sauce supply much of the smoke.

Which cheese matches the Hungry Jack’s slice?

A processed cheese slice. The official Cheese SOS is a processed cow’s milk cheese with emulsifying salts, which is why it melts smooth instead of splitting into oil.

Why put both mustard and BBQ sauce on a burger?

The mustard’s acidity balances the sugar and molasses in the BBQ sauce and the fat of the bacon. Leave it out and the burger turns flat and sweet.
