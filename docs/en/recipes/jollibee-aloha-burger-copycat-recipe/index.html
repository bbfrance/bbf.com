Jollibee Aloha Burger Recipe: Double Jr. Copycat

Copycat Jollibee Aloha burger for 2 in 30 minutes: 2 thin beef patties, bacon, caramelized pineapple and a mustard-forward honey-mustard sauce.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 19, 2026

This Jollibee Aloha burger recipe makes 2 copies of the Double Amazing Aloha Champ Jr. in about 30 minutes: 2 thin beef patties, melted cheese, crisp bacon, a pan-caramelized pineapple ring, lettuce and a honey-mustard sauce on a small toasted bun. It is my copycat, not Jollibee’s recipe. The chain publishes neither its patty weight, nor its seasoning, nor its sauce formula, so below I mark which parts come from Jollibee and which are my own calls. I cooked this stack 4 times before settling on the quantities.

What is in the Jollibee Double Amazing Aloha Champ Jr.?

The Double Amazing Aloha Champ Jr. is the double-patty version of Jollibee’s smaller Aloha Champ: beef, cheese, vegetables, pineapple and bacon on a toasted bun. Jollibee Philippines lists it on its official burger menu solo, with fries and a drink, and in a “w/o Bacon” version (Jollibee burger menu, checked November 11, 2026). The chain does not publish a full ingredient list for the double Jr. format.

The full-size Amazing Aloha Champ is better documented. It pairs the 1/3 lb (about 151 g) Champ patty with 2 pineapple rings, a honey mustard dressing and bacon (SunStar, November 19, 2020). A 2022 review adds lettuce, tomato, cheese and a toasted bun (3rd World Geeks). The 2009 photo of the earlier Aloha further down this page shows a sesame bun.

The Jr. versions arrived around September 2021: the Champ Jr., the Amazing Aloha Champ Jr. and the Bacon Champ Jr., all built on “100% pure beef” with cheese, vegetables, and pineapple or bacon depending on the variant (PhilSTAR Life, September 3, 2021). Nobody has published the year the double Jr. joined the menu.

Ingredients for 2 Double Aloha Champ Jr. burgers

You need about 8 oz (220 g) of ground beef for 4 thin patties, plus canned pineapple, bacon, cheese and a 3-ingredient sauce.

Patties

  • 8 oz (220 g) ground beef, 80/20
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 1 pinch black pepper
  • 1 pinch garlic powder
  • 1 pinch sugar (my choice for the slightly sweet Jollibee profile; optional)
  • 1 tsp neutral oil

Toppings and buns

  • 2 small soft burger buns, about 3.5 in (9 cm) wide, sesame optional
  • 1 tbsp (15 g) butter, softened
  • 4 slices American cheese
  • 4 strips bacon
  • 2 canned pineapple rings, drained and patted dry
  • 2 leaves green leaf lettuce

Honey-mustard sauce

  • 2 tbsp (30 g) mayonnaise
  • 2 tsp (10 g) yellow mustard
  • 1 tsp (7 g) honey

My patty seasoning scales down the spice mix of the best-known English-language copycat, The Unlikely Baker, which seasons 2 lb of beef with 1¼ tsp salt, ½ tsp pepper, ½ tsp garlic powder, ⅛ tsp onion powder and 1 tbsp Worcestershire. That recipe makes 8 quarter-pound burgers. Mine targets the much smaller Jr. bun with 2 thin patties, so the Worcestershire goes: on a 55 g patty it is all you taste.

Honey-mustard sauce for the Aloha burger

The sauce makes the Aloha, and it should taste of mustard first, with honey in the background. A 2022 review of the Amazing Aloha Champ found the sauce generous but noted that the honey “fades into the background” (3rd World Geeks). So I use 3 parts mayo to 1 part mustard and only half as much honey as mustard.

The Unlikely Baker uses Dijon, honey, mayo and a pinch of cayenne. I prefer yellow mustard. It is milder and closer to the soft, sweet sauces of Jollibee’s Yumburger, which I took apart in my Jollibee Yumburger copycat with Yum sauce. If you want more bite, swap half the yellow mustard for Dijon.

How to make the Jollibee Aloha burger step by step

The build takes about 10 minutes of prep and 20 minutes of cooking in 1 heavy skillet: bacon first, then pineapple in the bacon fat, then the patties. 1 pan, in that order, and almost nothing to wash.

1. Make the honey-mustard sauce

Whisk 2 tbsp mayonnaise, 2 tsp yellow mustard and 1 tsp honey until smooth, then chill while you cook. Taste it. Mustard should lead; honey only rounds it off.

2. Shape 4 thin patties

Divide 8 oz (220 g) ground beef into 4 2 oz (55 g) balls and press each into a disc about 4 in (10 cm) wide and 1/4 in (6 mm) thick. Make the patties slightly wider than the bun, because thin patties shrink as they cook. Keep them cold until the pan is ready.

3. Cook the bacon until crisp

Lay 4 bacon strips in a cold cast-iron or heavy skillet, then cook over medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes, turning twice, until evenly crisp. Drain on paper towels and keep about 1 tsp of fat in the pan. Even cooking matters here: the 2022 review complained of 1 crisp strip and 1 limp one.

4. Caramelize the pineapple rings

Pat 2 canned pineapple rings dry and sear them in the bacon fat over medium-high heat for 2 minutes per side, until the edges turn amber. Don’t skip the drying. On my first try I put the rings in straight from the can, and they simmered in their own juice without ever taking color.

5. Toast the buns in butter

Butter the cut sides of 2 buns and toast them face down in the skillet over medium heat for about 1 minute, until golden. Jollibee US describes its Angus Aloha Burger bun as butter-toasted (Jollibee US).

6. Sear the patties and melt the cheese

Wipe the skillet, add 1 tsp oil and heat over high heat until it shimmers. Season the patties on both sides with salt, pepper, garlic powder and the pinch of sugar. Sear for 90 seconds, flip, lay a cheese slice on each and cook 60 seconds more. Ground beef is safe at 160°F (71°C) inside; see our burger patty internal temperature guide.

7. Assemble the burger

Build from the bottom: bun, lettuce, patty with cheese, second patty with cheese, 2 bacon strips, pineapple ring, a generous spoon of honey-mustard sauce, then the top bun. Sauce on the pineapple, not on the bun: it runs down into the bacon and beef, the way the original eats.

Why this recipe tastes like the original

This copycat gets close because it keeps the 4 contrasts that define the Aloha: thin beef, salty bacon, juicy pineapple and a mild mustard sauce, all in a small bun. 2 55 g patties give more beef than bun without the bulk of a 1/3 lb Champ patty. Searing the pineapple concentrates its juice and takes the raw sharpness off. The pinch of sugar in the seasoning nods to the sweet profile Jollibee fans know from the Yumburger.

My position: 1 pineapple ring is enough. The full-size Champ carries 2, and on a 9 cm bun a second ring buries the beef. If you remember your Jr. with 2, add one. I wouldn’t. For me, this double Jr. is the best way to make an Aloha at home, because the beef stays in charge.

Where it can differ: Jollibee’s exact cheese, bun, patty seasoning and current sauce for the Jr. format are not published, and the number of pineapple rings on the double Jr. is not confirmed. Treat my choices as a starting point and adjust them to the one you remember.

A short history of the Jollibee Amazing Aloha

The Aloha is a come-and-go item that Jollibee has brought back several times since the 1990s. According to Rappler, it first launched in 1996, then returned in 2013 as the Amazing Aloha Yumburger and in 2014 as the Amazing Aloha Champ.

The Champ was phased out of some branches in early 2020 (Interaksyon), then came back in late October 2020 with the Bacon Champ and the Amazing Aloha Champ. The Jr. formats followed around September 2021. Fans had asked for it before: a 2017 Change.org petition called it “a come-and-go item on the Jollibee menu since the ’90s”. It gathered 40 signatures (Change.org).

For the full story of the chain, from its first stores to its global expansion, see our Jollibee brand profile. Heading to the Philippines? My colleague Léo’s guide to Jollibee in Manila covers addresses, the menu and prices, and the best burgers in Manila page lists the city’s other burger spots.

Variations: no bacon, homemade buns and other doubles

The easiest variation is the one Jollibee sells itself: skip the bacon. The no-bacon double keeps the beef, cheese, pineapple and sauce, so the sweet-salty balance tips toward the pineapple. Add a little extra salt to the patties to compensate.

For a bakery-soft bun, bake a batch of our homemade burger buns and shape them at 2.5 oz (70 g) of dough for Jr.-size rolls. FAQ about the Jollibee Aloha burger

What is in the Jollibee Double Amazing Aloha Champ Jr.?

It is a double-patty beef burger with cheese, vegetables, pineapple and bacon on a toasted bun, sold in the Philippines solo, as a meal or without bacon. Jollibee does not publish its full ingredient list, so the number of pineapple rings and cheese slices and the sauce for this format are not confirmed.

What is the difference between the Amazing Aloha Champ, the Champ Jr. and the Double Champ Jr.?

The Amazing Aloha Champ uses the full-size 1/3 lb (about 151 g) Champ patty and 2 pineapple rings. The Amazing Aloha Champ Jr., launched around September 2021, is a smaller version. The Double Amazing Aloha Champ Jr. doubles the Jr. patty.

Which sauce does Jollibee use: honey mustard or Aloha dressing?

Philippine sources from 2020 to 2022 describe a honey mustard dressing on the Amazing Aloha Champ. In the US, Jollibee calls its sauce “Aloha Dressing” and does not publish the recipe. The sauce in this copycat is my own mustard-forward honey mustard, not an official formula.

Is the Aloha burger still on the Jollibee menu? Is it sold in the US or Europe?

Yes, the Double Amazing Aloha Champ Jr. was listed on the official Jollibee Philippines menu on November 11, 2026. In the US, Jollibee sells a different Angus Aloha Burger at $9.49, with grilled pineapple, bacon, cheese, lettuce and Aloha Dressing. The sources in this article do not confirm an Aloha burger in Europe.

Should I grill the pineapple, and should I use fresh or canned?

Yes: caramelizing the pineapple for 2 minutes per side adds color and concentrates its juice without making the burger too sweet. Canned rings are the practical choice because they already have the right size and shape; pat them dry first. Fresh pineapple works if you cut 1/2 in (1 cm) rings and core them.

What cheese should I use to imitate Jollibee’s?

Use American cheese slices: they melt fast on thin patties and give the mild, creamy taste of fast-food cheese. Jollibee does not publish its cheese type, so this is a reproduction choice; a mild cheddar slice also works.

Why does the Jollibee patty taste slightly sweet?

Jollibee does not publish its patty seasoning, so the sweetness is my reading of the taste, not a confirmed ingredient. The Yumburger’s mayo-based sauce leans sweet too. In this copycat, a pinch of sugar in the seasoning and the honey in the sauce recreate that impression.

Can I make it without bacon, like on the Jollibee menu?

Yes. Jollibee sells a “w/o Bacon” Double Amazing Aloha Champ Jr., so skipping step 3 gives you a variant that exists on the real menu. Sear the pineapple in a teaspoon of butter instead of bacon fat.
