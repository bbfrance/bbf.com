Tuna Burger Recipe: Fresh Ahi or Canned Tuna

Two tuna burgers on one page: hand-chopped fresh ahi seared pink, and canned tuna patties, with safe temperatures and a fix for patties that fall apart.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 26, 2026

A good tuna burger starts with fresh yellowfin tuna. Chop it by hand, bind it with egg yolk, chill it for 30 minutes and sear it over high heat for 1 to 2 minutes per side. The canned version is simpler: well-drained canned light tuna, egg, breadcrumbs and mayo, cooked through over medium heat. One rule matters more than any other. Leave the center pink only if the fish was frozen first; otherwise cook it to the FDA’s safe internal temperature of 145°F (63°C).

I make both, and they aren’t rivals. Fresh tuna gives you something close to a seared tuna steak in a bun, and it’s the one I’d serve guests. Canned tuna patties are what I reach for on a Tuesday night with nothing in the fridge. Below are both methods, with the temperatures in Fahrenheit and Celsius and the safety rules most recipes skip.

Fresh tuna burger vs. canned tuna burger: which should you make?

Make the fresh tuna burger if you want a steak-like burger with a pink center. Make the canned one for a cheap, cooked-through patty from the pantry. Here’s how the two compare.

Fresh tuna burger Canned tuna burger
Tuna Fresh yellowfin (ahi), chopped by hand Canned light tuna, drained well
Binder Egg yolk Egg, breadcrumbs, mayonnaise
Rest before cooking 30 minutes in the fridge 30 minutes in the fridge helps it hold
Heat High, 1–2 min per side Medium, until golden on both sides
Center Pink only if the tuna was frozen first, otherwise 145°F (63°C) Cooked through
Texture Meaty, like a seared tuna steak Soft, like a crab cake
FDA mercury category Yellowfin: Good Choice (1 serving a week) Canned light: Best Choice
Cost Higher Lower

Which tuna is best for a tuna burger?

Use yellowfin for a fresh tuna burger and canned light tuna for canned patties. Both picks follow the FDA/EPA Advice about Eating Fish, revised in October 2021, which ranks fish by mercury level.

  • Canned light tuna, including skipjack, is a Best Choice.
  • Albacore (white tuna) and yellowfin are a Good Choice: one serving a week. An adult serving is 4 oz.
  • Bigeye tuna is a Choice to Avoid.

Bar chart of mean mercury in tuna, in ppm, from FDA testing between 1990 and 2012: canned light 0.126 (545 samples), fresh or frozen skipjack 0.144 (3 samples), canned albacore 0.350 (451), fresh or frozen yellowfin 0.354 (231), fresh or frozen albacore 0.358 (43), bigeye 0.689 (21). Canned light tuna has about a third of yellowfin’s mercury, and bigeye has nearly twice as much as yellowfin. What is ahi tuna?

Ahi is the Hawaiian name for two species, yellowfin and bigeye, sold as fresh or frozen steaks. That’s a trap for an ahi tuna burger: bigeye is on the FDA’s avoid list. Don’t settle for a label that just says ahi. Ask for yellowfin by name.

Fresh tuna burger ingredients

This makes 4 burgers of 150 g (5.3 oz) each, the portion Jamie Oliver uses in his tuna burger (600 g of fresh tuna for 4). Directo al Paladar’s Spanish recipe binds 220 g of tuna with a single egg yolk for 2 burgers. Scaled up, that would be closer to 3 yolks for 600 g. Two yolks give a slightly leaner mix that tastes more of tuna. Add a third if the mix won’t hold together when you press a patty.

  • 600 g (21 oz, about 1⅓ lb) fresh yellowfin tuna, previously frozen if you want a pink center
  • 2 egg yolks (3 if the mix feels loose)
  • Salt
  • Neutral oil for the pan
  • 4 burger buns
  • Toppings of your choice

How to make a fresh tuna burger, step by step

A fresh tuna burger holds together and stays pink inside thanks to three things: hand-chopping, a 30-minute chill and a very hot pan.

1. Chop the tuna by hand

Cut the tuna into small cubes with a sharp knife, then chop it finely without letting it turn into a paste. Directo al Paladar says the same: chop finely, “sin hacer una pasta” (without making a paste). Jamie Oliver pulses his tuna coarsely in a food processor instead. I stick with the knife. Leave the blade spinning a second too long and fish turns into a pasty mince, and you can’t undo that.

Raw tuna chopped by hand into small, distinct pieces rather than a paste. This is the texture to aim for in step 1 of a fresh tuna burger. Illustrative photo: this tuna was served as a tartare with sesame seeds, scallions and a crisp tuile, not shaped into a burger. (Photo: stu_spivack (Stu Spivack), CC BY-SA 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/35034346243@N01/3195154409)

2. Bind and shape the patties

Mix the chopped tuna with the egg yolks and a pinch of salt. Shape 4 patties of 150 g (5.3 oz), and handle them as little as possible.

3. Chill for 30 minutes

Rest the patties in the fridge for at least 30 minutes, as Jamie Oliver’s recipe does. Cold patties are firmer and keep their shape when they hit the pan. Skip this and they spread.

4. Sear over high heat

Heat a film of oil in a heavy pan over high heat. For a pink center, sear the patties for 1 to 2 minutes per side. Jamie Oliver gives 2 minutes per side and accepts a slightly pink inside. If your tuna was never frozen, keep going until a thermometer reads 145°F (63°C).

5. Build the burger

Toast the buns, then add the patty and toppings. A soft, slightly sweet bun suits tuna better than a crusty roll. My homemade brioche burger buns recipe is the one I use.

Can a fresh tuna burger be pink inside, like seared tuna?

Yes, but only if the tuna was frozen first. Raw or undercooked fish can carry anisakis, a parasite, and only freezing or thorough cooking kills it. I never treat tuna as risk-free, and no primary source I found says it can be eaten raw without precautions.

  • Spanish food safety agency (AESAN): cook the fish so it reaches 140°F (60°C) all the way through for at least 1 minute. Otherwise, freeze it at 5°F (-15°C) or colder for at least 4 days. In a home freezer rated three stars or more, AESAN advises counting 5 days, so the core is sure to reach -4°F (-20°C).
  • EU rules and ANSES (France): fish served raw by professionals must be frozen to -4°F (-20°C) at the core for 24 hours. ANSES advises home cooks to freeze for 7 days.
  • FDA: the minimum safe internal temperature for fish is 145°F (63°C).

Cooking guides quote a core temperature of about 118–131°F (48–55°C) for rare-to-medium seared tuna, after 1 to 2 minutes per side on a 1-inch (2–3 cm) piece. That’s kitchen practice, not a safety standard, so use it only with previously frozen tuna. How to probe a patty is covered in my burger patty internal temperature guide.

Canned tuna patties: the pantry version

Canned tuna patties are a cheap, quick tuna burger made from canned tuna, egg and breadcrumbs, cooked through in a skillet. In the US they often come without a bun, as tuna cakes, but they work fine in one. The quantities below follow Budget Bytes’ tuna patties recipe.

Canned tuna burger ingredients

  • 4 cans (5 oz / about 142 g each) canned light tuna, drained
  • 2 eggs
  • ¾ cup breadcrumbs
  • ¼ cup mayonnaise
  • 1 tbsp Old Bay seasoning
  • Oil for the pan

How to make canned tuna patties

  1. Drain the tuna thoroughly and press out the liquid. Wet tuna is the main reason canned tuna patties fall apart.
  2. Mix the tuna with the eggs, breadcrumbs, mayonnaise and Old Bay.
  3. Shape even patties and chill them for 30 minutes so they firm up.
  4. Cook in an oiled skillet over medium heat until golden on both sides, flipping once.

Tuna burger FAQ

How do you keep tuna burgers from falling apart?

Bind the mix, drain canned tuna thoroughly and chill the patties for 30 minutes before cooking. Fresh tuna needs only egg yolk. Canned tuna needs egg, breadcrumbs and mayonnaise. Chop fresh tuna by hand rather than blending it, and handle the patties as little as possible.

What temperature should a tuna burger be cooked to?

The FDA’s minimum safe internal temperature for fish is 145°F (63°C). Cooks often serve seared tuna at 118–131°F (48–55°C), but only do that with tuna that was frozen first, as AESAN and ANSES advise.

Can I eat a tuna burger pink in the middle?

Yes, if the tuna was frozen first. AESAN says to freeze it at 5°F (-15°C) or colder for at least 4 days, or 5 days in a home freezer. ANSES says 7 days in a home freezer. Otherwise, cook it to 145°F (63°C).

Which tuna is best for burgers?

Yellowfin for a fresh tuna burger, canned light tuna for canned patties. The FDA rates yellowfin a Good Choice (one 4 oz serving a week) and canned light tuna a Best Choice. Avoid bigeye, which is sometimes sold as ahi.

Is a fresh or canned tuna burger better?

Fresh tuna makes a meatier burger that you can serve pink. Canned tuna is cheaper, needs no special handling and is always cooked through. I’d pick fresh for a special dinner and canned for a weeknight.

What is the difference between a tuna burger and tuna patties?

A tuna burger is a patty served in a bun, usually made from fresh tuna. Tuna patties, or tuna cakes, are made from canned tuna, egg and breadcrumbs, and in the US they’re often eaten without a bun.
