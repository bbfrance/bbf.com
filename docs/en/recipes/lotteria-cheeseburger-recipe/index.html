Lotteria Cheeseburger Recipe (Korean Copycat)

Make Lotteria's Classic Cheeseburger at home: butter-toasted bun, thin beef patty, melted cheese, 2 pickles, onion and mayo. Ready in 25 minutes.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 19, 2026

To make a Lotteria cheeseburger at home, toast a soft bun in butter and add a thin beef patty of about 75–80 g (2.6–2.8 oz) raw. Top it with a slice of melted cheese, 2 pickle slices, finely chopped onion and plenty of mayonnaise. That is how Korean sources describe Lotteria’s Classic Cheeseburger (클래식 치즈버거), which costs ₩5,700 in Seoul in October 2026. This is my copycat, not the chain’s recipe. Lotteria has never published its patty weight or its sauce, so every estimate below is marked as one.

What is the Lotteria Classic Cheeseburger?

The Lotteria Classic Cheeseburger is a made-to-order Korean cheeseburger with a beef patty and cheese on a butter bun. Lotteria Korea launched it on December 6, 2016 at ₩3,900 on its own, ₩5,800 as a meal and ₩4,700 as a lunch meal (11 a.m. to 2 p.m.). The launch pitch described a handmade whole-wheat sourdough bun, a thick patty of Australian beef and melted cheddar.

The menu now calls it the Classic Cheeseburger Butter Bun (클래식치즈버거(버터번)). The Edit, a Korean food site, lists melty cheese, mayonnaise, onion, 2 pickle slices and 1 beef patty in a butter bun. A Double Classic Cheeseburger, with 2 patties and 2 slices of cheese, has been on the menu since Lotteria launched its double burgers in 2023 (Asia Economy).

Lotte founded Lotteria in Tokyo in 1972. The Korean branch opened in October 1979 in Sogong-dong, Seoul. Our Lotteria brand page has the full history of the chain.

Classic Cheeseburger vs. Lotteria’s old budget cheeseburger

The Classic Cheeseburger is a different product from Lotteria’s old entry-level cheeseburger (치즈버거). On April 1, 2016, Lotteria cut the budget burger’s price from ₩2,500 to ₩2,300 and took out the lettuce. All that was left on top of the patty was 2 pickles and 8 pieces of onion (Bizhankook, May 19, 2016). 8 months later came the Classic: made to order, pricier and clearly meant as the upgrade.

What we know vs. what we assume

Lotteria publishes the burger’s name, price and main components, but not the weights or the sauce. Here is where the sources stop and my guesses start.

Element What the sources say What this recipe assumes
Bun Whole-wheat sourdough bun in 2016; “butter bun” today A soft brioche or potato bun, buttered and pan-toasted. The 2016 bun and today’s butter bun may not be the same bread.
Patty 1 beef patty; “thick” Australian beef in 2016 About 75–80 g (2.6–2.8 oz) raw, 80/20 ground beef, pressed thin. The weight is estimated from the burger’s total weight, and today’s beef origin is unverified.
Cheese Cheddar in 2016; “melty cheese” today 1 slice of cheddar or American cheese (about 18 g). The current cheese type is not confirmed.
Sauce Mayonnaise (The Edit); mayo and ketchup (Korean reviewers); mustard and mayo (ZenKimchi) Generous mayo, with ketchup or mustard as an option. No official formula exists.
Onion Fresh chopped onion (Korean press); “crispy” onions (ZenKimchi) Finely diced raw white onion.
Pickles 2 slices 2 dill pickle slices per burger.
Weight and calories 146 g, 490 kcal (FatSecret, secondary source) Used only to size the patty.

How I estimated the patty weight

At 146 g in total, the burger has room for a cooked patty of only about 55–60 g. I counted the bun at 55–60 g, the cheese at about 18 g and the sauce and toppings at about 15 g. Beef loses roughly a quarter of its weight in the pan, so I start from 75–80 g of raw beef. That is lighter than “thick” suggests. Either the 2016 patty has slimmed down, or the launch copy oversold it. I went with the weight, because a 146 g total is hard to argue with.

A 57 g cooked patty means starting from 75–80 g (2.75 oz) of raw beef. (Chart: Burger Breton, CC BY 4.0, estimates from the 146 g total weight listed by FatSecret Korea, rendered with QuickChart)

Ingredients for 2 Lotteria-style cheeseburgers

This copycat Lotteria cheeseburger takes 25 minutes, and everything on the list is sold in a regular supermarket.

  • 160 g (5.6 oz) ground beef, 80/20
  • 2 soft brioche or potato buns, about 55–60 g each
  • 15 g (1 tbsp) unsalted butter, softened
  • 2 slices cheddar or American cheese, about 18 g each
  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise
  • 2 tsp ketchup or yellow mustard (optional)
  • 4 dill pickle slices
  • 20 g (2 tbsp) white onion, finely diced
  • Fine salt and ground black pepper

Lotteria cheeseburger recipe step by step

The method needs a very hot pan for a thin patty and a buttered bun toasted until golden.

1. Prep the onion and pickles

Dice the onion very finely and pat the pickle slices dry. Tiny pieces spread across the whole patty, so every bite gets some onion instead of 1 sharp chunk.

2. Shape the patties

Split the beef into 2 balls of 80 g (2.8 oz). Press each into a thin disc about 11 cm (4¼ in) wide, slightly wider than the bun, and chill for 10 minutes. Don’t skip the chill. A disc this thin tears when you flip it warm.

3. Butter and toast the buns

Spread the softened butter on the cut sides of the buns. Toast them cut side down in a skillet over medium heat for 1 to 2 minutes, until golden and crisp at the edges. This step is the “butter bun”. Skip it and you have a plain cheeseburger.

4. Sear the patties and melt the cheese

Heat a cast-iron skillet over high heat to about 230 °C (450 °F). Sear the patties for 2 minutes and season the top with salt and pepper. Flip, lay a cheese slice on each and cook for 1 more minute, until the cheese melts and the center reaches 71 °C (160 °F).

5. Assemble the burger

Spread 1 tbsp of mayo on the top bun, plus ketchup or mustard if you use it. On the bottom bun, stack the patty and cheese, then 2 pickle slices and half the onion. Close it and eat it straight away. Lotteria makes it to order, and a toasted bun goes limp within minutes.

Why this copycat gets close to the original

Korean reviewers keep pointing to the same things in the original: a very soft toasted bun, a strong onion note and a lot of creamy sauce. ZenKimchi compared the pickles to Krystal and White Castle sliders and waited about 7 minutes for a burger made to order. The butter toast gives the crisp-edged bun, and raw onion on a thin patty gives the onion kick. Keeping it to 1 patty, 1 cheese slice and 2 pickles stops it turning into a generic deluxe burger.

My own choices: an American slice rather than cheddar, because it melts into the patty the way “melty cheese” suggests, and mayo with no ketchup. Ketchup is the one ingredient no source agrees on, and it covers up the onion.

If you bake your own bread, my homemade burger buns recipe makes a soft enriched bun that toasts well in butter. Shape them a little smaller than the recipe says, to land near 55–60 g.

FAQ

What is on a Lotteria cheeseburger? The Classic Cheeseburger has a butter bun, 1 beef patty, melty cheese, mayonnaise, onion and 2 pickle slices, according to the Korean food site The Edit. Some reviews also mention ketchup or mustard, and Lotteria has not confirmed either one.

How many calories are in Lotteria’s Classic Cheeseburger? FatSecret Korea lists 1 burger at 146 g and 490 kcal. That is a secondary source, not Lotteria’s official nutrition sheet.

What cheese should I use for a Lotteria copycat? Lotteria used cheddar at the 2016 launch, and the current description only says “melty cheese.” Cheddar is closest to the launch recipe. I prefer an American slice, which melts more smoothly and gives the fast-food finish.

What is Lotteria’s butter bun, and how do I copy it? The butter bun is the soft bun the Classic Cheeseburger comes on today. Lotteria does not publish its recipe. A brioche or potato bun, buttered and toasted cut side down in a pan, gives the same soft crumb and golden, crisp face.

Fresh onion or rehydrated dried onion? Korean press describes fresh chopped onion, so this recipe uses finely diced raw white onion. ZenKimchi described the onions as crispy, so the exact form is not confirmed.

Can you still eat a Lotteria cheeseburger in Japan? No. Lotteria’s Japanese restaurants were sold to Zensho in 2023, and all of them became Zetteria in March 2026. Japan’s old 定番チーズバーガー, with cheese sauce and ketchup, is gone, so the Korean Classic Cheeseburger is now the one to copy.
