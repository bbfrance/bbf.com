Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger Copycat Recipe

Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger copycat recipe for 2: grill-seasoned patties, American and Swiss, hickory-smoked bacon, plus the 910 vs 930 calorie mix-up.

By Alice, Recipes writerPublished November 26, 2026

This Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger copycat recipe makes 2 burgers in about 35 minutes. Each one gets two salt-and-pepper beef patties, two slices of American cheese and one of Swiss, three slices of oven-crisped hickory-smoked bacon, and ketchup, yellow mustard and mayo on a buttered, toasted soft bun. The Jack in the Box Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger is the chain’s Ultimate Cheeseburger plus bacon, nothing more. So I kept my Jack in the Box Ultimate Cheeseburger copycat exactly as it is and added the bacon. This is my home reproduction, not the official recipe: Jack in the Box doesn’t publish its weights or methods, and I flag every estimate below.

What’s on the Jack in the Box Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger?

The Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger has two 100% beef patties seasoned as they grill, hickory-smoked bacon, melted American and Swiss-style cheeses, ketchup, mustard and mayo on a buttery bakery bun. That’s the official US description from the Jack in the Box product page, checked on October 11, 2026. The page renders in JavaScript, so I read that wording through search results rather than on the page itself.

A third-party menu site breaks it down by component: Split Top Bun, Mustard, Ketchup, Mayo, Bacon, American Cheese, Jumbo Patty and Swiss-Style Cheese. That matches the official description. No lettuce, tomato, onion or pickles, whatever one price aggregator claims.

  • Bun: split-top bun, buttered and toasted
  • Beef: two “Jumbo” 100% beef patties, seasoned on the grill
  • Bacon: hickory-smoked
  • Cheese: American plus Swiss-style
  • Sauces: ketchup, mustard, mayonnaise
  • Declared allergens: soy, egg, milk, wheat (jackintheboxsmenuprice.com)

All figures in this article are for the US market as of October 2026. Jack in the Box only operates in the US, Mexico and Guam, and I couldn’t verify whether the Mexican restaurants sell the same build.

Bacon Ultimate vs. Ultimate Cheeseburger: what’s the difference?

The only difference is the bacon: the Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger is the Ultimate Cheeseburger with hickory-smoked bacon added. Using the 910-calorie dataset, the bacon adds 18 g and 90 calories to the 266 g, 820-calorie Ultimate (MyFoodDiary). That’s why this recipe reuses my Ultimate base word for word: same beef, same cheese split, same sauces, same griddle heat.

How many slices of bacon are on the Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger?

Jack in the Box doesn’t publish its bacon count, and secondary sources disagree. Search summaries and the one dedicated copycat I found (Recipezazz) say 3 slices. A fan wiki summary says 4. In the late 1990s it reportedly had 8, according to The Impulsive Buy.

My estimate is 3 thin slices per burger. The 18 g gap between the bacon and non-bacon sandwiches works out to 2 to 3 thin, fully cooked slices, roughly 6 to 9 g each. I round up to 3. A home burger with too little bacon is a sad burger.

Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger calories: 910 or 930?

A Jack in the Box Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger has either 910 or 930 calories, depending on which nutrition database you trust. I couldn’t read the chain’s own page directly to settle it. Both figures are for the US market and were checked on October 11, 2026. I used the 910 set in my Ultimate article; the 930 set shows up in more recent listings.

Ingredients for 2 Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburgers

For 2 burgers you need 14 oz (400 g) of 80/20 ground beef shaped into four patties, 6 slices of thin-cut hickory-smoked bacon, 6 slices of cheese, three sauces, two soft buns and a tablespoon of butter. The beef weight is my estimate from the Ultimate base recipe. Jack in the Box has never published the weight of its “Jumbo Patty.”

  • 6 slices thin-cut hickory-smoked bacon
  • 14 oz (400 g) ground beef, 80/20, divided into 4 patties of 3.4–3.7 oz (95–105 g) each
  • 4 slices American cheese
  • 2 slices Swiss cheese (deli-sliced or processed Swiss slices)
  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise
  • 2 tsp ketchup
  • 2 tsp mild yellow mustard
  • 2 soft burger buns, split-top if you can find them
  • 1 tbsp (15 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper

Choosing the bacon

Buy thin-cut, hickory-smoked supermarket bacon, not thick-cut. The chain doesn’t name its bacon or say whether it’s precooked, so I go by the official “hickory smoked” wording and the small weight gap. Thin slices crisp evenly and lie flat on the patty. Thick-cut adds a chew and a weight the original simply doesn’t have.

Why 3.5 oz (100 g) patties, not quarter-pounders

Each raw patty weighs about 3.5 oz (100 g), the same estimate I worked out for the Ultimate by subtracting bun, cheese and sauces from the sandwich weight. Two quarter-pound patties only apply to the late-1990s version described by The Impulsive Buy. The full math is in my Ultimate Cheeseburger recipe.

Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger recipe: step by step

Bake the bacon first, then sear thin seasoned patties on a very hot griddle, melt the cheese under a lid and stack everything on a buttered, toasted bun. Jack in the Box doesn’t publish its equipment or cook times, so the heat and timings are my home settings. Total time is about 35 minutes: 15 minutes of prep and 20 of cooking, with the bacon in the oven while you shape the patties and toast the buns.

1. Bake the bacon

Heat the oven to 400 °F (204 °C). Lay the 6 bacon slices on a wire rack set over a rimmed baking sheet and bake for 15 to 20 minutes, until crisp, then drain them on paper towels. I borrowed this rack method from Wisconsin Cheese: the slices come out flat and evenly crisp, with no splatter. Thin-cut bacon is usually done at the low end of that range.

2. Shape the patties

Divide the beef into four balls of 3.5 oz (100 g). Press each one into a thin patty a little wider than your bun, since it shrinks in the pan. Keep the patties cold until the griddle is ready.

3. Toast the buttered buns

Spread the butter on the cut faces of the buns. Toast them cut-side down on a griddle or cast-iron skillet over medium heat for 45 to 60 seconds, until golden, then set them aside. If you want to bake the buns yourself, my homemade burger buns recipe works here.

4. Sear and season the patties

Heat the griddle or skillet to 390–445 °F (200–230 °C). Lay the patties in and season them with salt and pepper right in the pan, which is my reading of the chain’s “seasoned as it grills.” Leave them alone for 2 to 3 minutes, until a brown crust forms.

5. Flip, add cheese and cover

Flip the patties and immediately lay a slice of American cheese on each one, then a slice of Swiss on two of them. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes on the second side, covering the pan for the last 30 seconds to melt the cheese. The center should reach 160 °F (71 °C), the USDA safe minimum for ground beef. My burger patty internal temperature guide explains how to check it on thin patties.

6. Sauce the buns

Spread 1 tbsp of mayonnaise on each top bun. Spread 1 tsp of ketchup and 1 tsp of mustard on each bottom bun.

7. Stack with the bacon and serve

On each bottom bun, place the American-only patty, then the patty with American and Swiss. Lay 3 slices of bacon on the melted Swiss, close the burger and serve right away. That order is my choice, not a published one. When I tested the bacon between the two hot patties, it had gone limp by the time the burger reached the table. On top, under the mayo, it stays crisp.

Why this recipe tastes like the original

This copycat follows the official component list exactly: two grill-seasoned beef patties, American and Swiss-style cheese, hickory-smoked bacon, ketchup, mustard and mayo on a buttered split-top-style bun. I left out lettuce, onion and any special sauce because the original has none. What you taste is salty, crusted beef, smoke from the bacon and the Swiss. Don’t skip that one slice: without it you have a generic bacon double cheeseburger, and its nutty edge is what sets this burger apart.

The quantities are built to land near the real thing. Two 3.5 oz patties match my weight math for the Ultimate, and 3 thin slices of bacon match the 18 g difference between the two sandwiches. The only other dedicated copy I found, on Recipezazz, uses 8 oz (227 g) of beef for a single burger and counts 1,209 calories. In my view that’s closer to the 1990s Colossus than to the sandwich Jack in the Box serves today.

A short history: from the Colossus to the Bacon Ultimate

The Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger may descend from the Colossus Burger, though that link rests on a single secondary source. According to The Impulsive Buy (February 22, 2015), citing Calorie Count, the Colossus launched in 1994 with two quarter-pound patties, three kinds of cheese and 8 slices of bacon, for 1,100 calories, 84 g of fat and 1,510 mg of sodium. The same post says the late-1990s Bacon Ultimate used that formula: two quarter-pound patties, 3 slices of cheese and 8 slices of bacon. Jack in the Box has never published a launch date for the Bacon Ultimate.

The modern version took shape in 2016, when the whole Jack in the Box burger lineup switched to buttered split-top buns and real mayonnaise, according to Meat+Poultry (March 21, 2016).

Jack in the Box also has a history with burger temperatures, which is why I never skip the thermometer on this one. The chain’s 1992–93 E. coli O157:H7 outbreak, linked to its discounted Monster Burger, sickened 732 confirmed people in four western states and killed four children. Washington State required 155 °F (68 °C) at the center, while the chain cooked to the federal standard of 140 °F (60 °C) (Wikipedia). For more burger backstory, read our history of the hamburger.

Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger FAQ

Is this the official Jack in the Box Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger recipe?

No. It’s a homemade copycat. The components match the official description, but the patty weight, the bacon count, the cheese split and the assembly order are my estimates, because Jack in the Box doesn’t publish its recipe.

Where should the bacon go: between the patties or on top?

On top, in my version. Bacon laid on the Swiss-topped patty stays crisp under the mayo; between two hot patties it went soft in my tests. The chain doesn’t publish its stacking order.

How many calories are in a Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger?

Either 910 calories for 284 g or 930 calories for 272–273 g, depending on the database (US, checked October 11, 2026). Check the Jack in the Box app for the current official figure.

Is the Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger the same as the old Colossus Burger?

Not today. According to The Impulsive Buy, the late-1990s Bacon Ultimate copied the Colossus formula of two quarter-pound patties and 8 slices of bacon. Today’s version is lighter, at 910 or 930 calories against the Colossus’s reported 1,100.

Can you add sauces to the Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger?

One third-party menu site lists Buttermilk Ranch and Good Good Sauce as paid add-ons. I couldn’t confirm them on the official site, so treat them as unverified.

Can you get a Bacon Ultimate Cheeseburger in Europe?

No. Jack in the Box has no restaurants in Europe; it operates only in the US, Mexico and Guam, per its FY2025 10-K.
