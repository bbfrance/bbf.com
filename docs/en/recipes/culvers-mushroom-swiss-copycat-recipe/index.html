Culver's Mushroom and Swiss Copycat Recipe

Culver's Mushroom and Swiss at home: a smashed salt-and-pepper patty, natural Swiss and seared buttery mushrooms on a toasted Kaiser crown.

By Julia, Recipes writerPublished November 19, 2026

This Culver’s Mushroom and Swiss copycat recipe makes 2 burgers in about 30 minutes. You need a smashed patty of fresh 80/20 beef seasoned only with salt and pepper, a slice of natural Swiss cheese, white button mushrooms sautéed with butter, garlic and parsley, and a 4-inch Kaiser bun with only the crown buttered and toasted. Culver’s doesn’t publish its recipe. I rebuilt this one from the chain’s own menu description and its official ingredient declaration, then cooked it in my kitchen until the mushrooms stopped letting me down.

What is in Culver’s Mushroom & Swiss ButterBurger?

Culver’s Mushroom & Swiss is a ButterBurger topped with sautéed mushrooms and Swiss cheese, with no sauce. The chain describes it as “fresh, never frozen, Midwest-raised beef, seared on a grill after you order”, “white button mushrooms sautéed in butter, salt, pepper, parsley and a touch of garlic”, “real Wisconsin Swiss cheese” and a “lightly buttered, toasted bun” (Culver’s product page, read through the fastfoodinusa mirror, checked November 16, 2026).

It sits on the current US ButterBurgers menu next to the ButterBurger, ButterBurger Cheese, Culver’s Deluxe, Bacon Deluxe, Sourdough Melt and Wisconsin Swiss Melt (Culver’s ButterBurgers menu, checked November 16, 2026). You can order it as a single, double or triple (Mashed, June 8, 2026).

What Culver’s publishes vs. my homemade version

Culver’s publishes the components and an ingredient declaration, but not the patty weight, the cooking time or the mushroom method. The table separates what the chain states from the choices I made. 1 caveat up front: the declaration is dated April 2018 and covers the US market. I could not find a readable official page confirming that the 2026 bun, cheese and mushrooms are unchanged.

Sources for the declaration: Culver’s Quality Ingredient Information (PDF), pages 1, 2, 3 and 11, which states “Ingredient information is current as of April 2018”.

Butter or oil? What Culver’s mushrooms are really cooked in

Culver’s own sources disagree: the product page says the mushrooms are sautéed in butter, but the April 2018 ingredient declaration lists sunflower and/or canola oil and no butter. I can’t tell you which 1 matches how Culver’s cooks them in 2026, and I won’t pretend to. The marketing text is current. The declaration is 8 years old.

At home I use butter, and not to settle the argument. Butter is what the menu promises, and it’s what you expect to taste when you order a “mushroom and Swiss ButterBurger”. Timing is what matters. In my first test I melted the butter in the pan before the mushrooms went in; the milk solids had browned too far by the time the mushrooms gave up their water, and the whole batch tasted faintly scorched. Now I sear them dry and add the butter in the last minute. You get all the butter flavor and none of the grease.

How much beef for a Mushroom & Swiss ButterBurger?

Use a raw patty of 3.9 to 4.4 oz (110 to 125 g). That is my estimate, not a Culver’s figure. FastFoodNutrition lists a single Mushroom & Swiss at 167 g, a double at 262 g and a triple at 357 g. Each extra patty adds about 95 g to the finished burger, which points to a raw patty of roughly 110 to 125 g once you allow for what the beef loses on the griddle (FastFoodNutrition, double and triple, checked November 16, 2026).

That’s a little more beef than in my earlier Culver’s copycats: 3.5 to 3.9 oz (100 to 110 g) for the Culver’s ButterBurger Cheese copycat recipe and 3.5 to 4 oz (100 to 115 g) for the Culver’s Sourdough Melt copycat recipe. I don’t mind the extra weight. A spoonful of wet mushrooms and a slice of Swiss would bury a thinner patty.

Ingredients for 2 Mushroom & Swiss burgers

These quantities make 2 single burgers. Weigh the beef; spoons are fine for the rest.

  • 7.75 to 8.75 oz (220 to 250 g) fresh 80/20 ground beef, kept cold
  • 2 Kaiser buns, about 4 inches (10 cm) across
  • 2 slices natural Swiss cheese
  • 8 oz (225 g) white button mushrooms
  • 2 tbsp (28 g) salted butter, divided: 1 tbsp for the mushrooms, 1 tbsp for the crowns
  • 1 small garlic clove, finely grated
  • 1 tbsp chopped fresh flat-leaf parsley
  • 1/2 tsp cornstarch mixed with 1 tbsp cold water
  • 5 parts fine salt to 1 part ground black pepper, mixed, about 1/4 tsp per patty, plus a pinch of each for the mushrooms

Prep takes about 15 minutes and cooking about 15. The patties take only a few minutes, so the mushrooms and crowns have to be done before the beef goes in the pan.

Step by step: Culver’s Mushroom and Swiss at home

Cook in this order: mushrooms first, then the crowns, then the beef, so everything is hot at the same moment.

1. Portion the beef

Scoop the beef into 2 loose balls of 3.9 to 4.4 oz (110 to 125 g) each. Don’t pack or shape them. Keep them in the fridge until the skillet is hot.

2. Sear the mushrooms

Wipe the mushrooms clean and slice them about 1/4 inch (6 mm) thick. Heat a wide skillet over medium-high heat with no fat and add the mushrooms in a single layer, in 2 batches if they crowd the pan. Leave them alone for 2 to 3 minutes until the undersides brown. Stir and cook 2 to 3 minutes more, until the liquid they release has evaporated.

3. Finish the mushrooms with butter and garlic

Lower the heat to medium, add 1 tbsp butter and the grated garlic, and stir for 30 seconds. Pour in the cornstarch slurry and stir until the juices turn glossy and cling to the mushrooms, then take the pan off the heat. Season with a pinch of salt and pepper and stir in the parsley.

Sautéed mushrooms finished with garlic and fresh parsley, the same finish as step 3 (illustrative photo: whole mushrooms cooked with white wine, not the sliced burger topping) (Photo: N1NJ4, CC BY 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/61343210@N00/7671469946 (1024×683; lazy))

4. Butter and toast the crowns

Spread the remaining butter thinly over the cut side of the 2 crowns only. Toast them butter side down in a pan over medium heat for about 1 minute, until golden. Toast the heels dry, or not at all.

A Kaiser roll, the bun Culver’s declares as a 4-inch Kaiser bun for its ButterBurgers; only the crown gets butter (Photo: Glane23, CC BY-SA 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kaiser\_roll.JPG (1743×1265; lazy))

5. Heat the skillet to 425°F

Heat a dry cast-iron skillet or griddle to about 425°F (218°C). No surface thermometer? A drop of water should skitter and vanish within a second or 2. Don’t oil the pan: the beef cooks in its own fat.

6. Smash and season the patties

Put a beef ball in the skillet and press it flat at once with a sturdy spatula, to about 1/3 inch (8 mm) thick and about 5 inches (13 cm) across. It shrinks to roughly the width of the bun as it cooks. Sprinkle the salt and pepper mix on the raw top side just before you flip, the way Culver’s employees describe it.

7. Flip, melt the Swiss and assemble

Flip when the edges are deep brown and lacy, scraping under the patty so the crust comes with it. Lay the Swiss on straight away, then spoon the hot mushrooms over the cheese so it melts under them. Set the patty on the heel, close with the buttered crown and eat right away.

How to sauté mushrooms for a burger without them going soggy

Sear mushrooms in a hot, dry pan in a single layer, and add butter only once their water has cooked off. Mushrooms are more than 90% water, so a crowded pan steams them instead of browning them.

I learned that the obvious way. On my first try I tipped the whole 8 oz into a 10-inch (25 cm) skillet. 5 minutes later they were grey, rubbery and sitting in a puddle. 2 batches in the same pan fixed it.

Sliced white button mushrooms sautéed until browned and dry, the texture to aim for on a burger so they don’t go soggy (Photo: Kara Angèle (TheFoodGroupie), CC BY-SA 2.0, https://www.flickr.com/photos/61052907@N03/5646315608 (1024×768; lazy))

Chowhound finds the same fault in the restaurant version: soft mushrooms cooked in batches in overloaded pans, and Swiss it calls “a bit bitter” and “wax-like” (Chowhound). Cooking for 2 at home is your advantage here: use the widest pan you own and don’t rush the batches.

Skip the milk and flour you’ll see in some copycats, such as the short 1 in Mashed’s June 2026 piece. Culver’s doesn’t list either. Its declaration binds the mushrooms with corn starch and gums, which is why I use half a teaspoon of cornstarch: enough to make the juices cling, never enough for a gravy.

Why this copycat stays close to the original

This version stays close to Culver’s because it copies every component the chain names and adds nothing it doesn’t. The beef is fresh, smashed and seasoned with salt and pepper only. The cheese is natural Swiss. The mushrooms are white buttons with garlic, parsley and butter. Only the crown is buttered.

The crown-only rule comes from the chain itself. Culver’s says “we lightly butter the crown of the bun”, a habit from Craig Culver’s mother, who was cooking at the first Culver’s when it opened on July 18, 1984, in Sauk City, Wisconsin (Culver’s). I buttered both halves once, just to check. The heel turned to paste under the mushroom juices before I was halfway through.

Sauk City, Wisconsin, where the first Culver’s opened on July 18, 1984, about 25 miles northwest of Madison (Map: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL (iframe 800×450, loading lazy; download a local poster image))

Everything else is an informed estimate, and I label it as one. The patty weight comes from nutrition data. The 425°F and roughly 90 seconds of total cooking come from former employees quoted by Tasting Table. About 45 seconds per side and a salt-to-pepper ratio between 3:1 and 5:1 come from employees quoted by Chowhound. None of it is official. On a home skillet I go by the brown, lacy edge, not the clock. For doneness, see my colleague Clara’s burger patty internal temperature guide.

I deliberately depart from the restaurant on the mushrooms. Mine are seared, not steamed, and I’d defend that choice over strict fidelity any day. Culver’s soft, pale mushrooms are the weak link in an otherwise good burger. Brown them properly and this goes from a menu filler to the burger I’d order first.

Culver’s Mushroom & Swiss vs. Hardee’s Mushroom Swiss

The main difference between the two is the sauce: Hardee’s tops its burger with a brown mushroom sauce, while Culver’s uses sautéed mushrooms and no sauce at all. Frequently asked questions

What’s in Culver’s Mushroom & Swiss?

A fresh beef ButterBurger patty seasoned with salt and pepper, a slice of Wisconsin Swiss cheese and white button mushrooms with butter, salt, pepper, parsley and garlic, on a lightly buttered, toasted Kaiser bun. The official description lists no sauce.

Are the mushrooms cooked in butter?

The product page says they are sautéed in butter. Culver’s ingredient declaration from April 2018 lists sunflower and/or canola oil and no butter. Both are official; neither confirms what the chain does in 2026.

Does the Mushroom & Swiss have a sauce?

No sauce appears in Culver’s description. 1 third-party mirror lists a dill pickle among the components, but the official description does not, so I leave it out. Add pickle slices if you like them.

How many calories are in a Culver’s Mushroom and Swiss?

FastFoodNutrition lists the single at 500 calories for 167 g, with 26 g fat, 40 g carbohydrates, 26 g protein and 582 mg sodium. The double is 780 calories (262 g) and the triple 1,060 calories (357 g). Other databases give 490 calories for the single. These are third-party figures, checked November 16, 2026; I did not find an official number on culvers.com.

What allergens does it contain?

FastFoodNutrition lists gluten, milk, soy and wheat. Culver’s declaration notes that the Kaiser bun contains wheat and soy.

Which cheese is closest to Culver’s Swiss?

Any natural sliced Swiss from the deli counter, since Culver’s uses natural Swiss, not processed. Outside the US, a young, mild Emmental is the closest substitute; older Emmental is nuttier and sharper than American Swiss.

A block of Swiss Emmentaler with its holes: outside the US, a mild, young Emmental is the closest substitute for Culver’s natural Swiss (Photo: Dominik Hundhammer (Zerohund), CC BY-SA 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Emmentaler.jpg (948×800; lazy))

Should I butter the whole bun or only the crown?

Only the crown. That is how Culver’s describes its ButterBurgers, and it keeps the heel from going soggy under the mushrooms.

Can I make it a double?

Yes. Smash a second ball of beef, put the Swiss on the top patty only and spoon the mushrooms over it. 2 patties of 110 to 125 g each match the size of Culver’s double.

Can I eat at Culver’s outside the US?

No. At the end of 2025 Culver’s had 1,041 restaurants, all in the United States (QSR 50, 2026). If you’re heading to Wisconsin, start with our guide to Culver’s in Milwaukee: addresses, hours and what to order.
