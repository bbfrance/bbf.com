All-American Sonic Smasher Copycat Recipe

All-American Sonic Smasher copycat: two smashed 75 g patties, American cheese, ketchup, mayo, lettuce and tomato, plus what Sonic confirms vs. my estimates.

By Alice, Recipes writerPublished November 18, 2026

To make an All-American Sonic Smasher copycat at home, smash 4 loose 75 g (2.6 oz) balls of 80/20 ground beef on a cast-iron skillet heated to 450–500 °F (230–260 °C). Season with salt and pepper, cook 2 minutes until the edges crisp, then flip, add a slice of American cheese to each patty and cook 30 to 45 seconds more. Stack 2 patties per burger on a toasted potato bun with ketchup, mayo, lettuce, tomato, crinkle-cut pickles and diced onion. This is my homemade reproduction, built from the ingredients Sonic publishes. It is not Sonic’s official recipe: the chain doesn’t release patty weights or its seasoning.

What is on the All-American Sonic Smasher?

The All-American SONIC Smasher is 2 seasoned, smashed Angus beef patties with American cheese, lettuce, tomato, crinkle-cut pickles, diced onions, ketchup and mayonnaise on a toasted potato bun. That’s the ingredient list in the Inspire Brands launch release of January 12, 2026, and on Sonic’s menu page.

Sonic’s menu page describes “two slices of melty American cheese” and a “pillowy soft potato bun.” The release just says “American cheese,” so treat 2 slices per burger as very likely, not certain. Sonic calls the patties “hand-smashed” and the burger “made-to-order.”

Sonic launched the All-American lineup nationwide on January 12, 2026, as a limited-time offer at participating drive-ins. Four burgers: the All-American SONIC Smasher, the All-American Triple SONIC Smasher, and a Bacon version of each. Ryan Dickerson, Sonic’s chief marketing officer, pitched it as “a premium, made-to-order, hand-smashed burger that’s simply better than you’ll find anywhere else in fast-food, or even fast-casual.” That’s a marketing line, of course. The ingredient list is what matters for a copycat.

All-American vs. Original vs. Bacon Sonic Smasher

The All-American Smasher swaps the Original’s creamy, tangy Smasher sauce for plain ketchup and mayo, and adds lettuce and tomato. The Bacon version is that same burger with bacon. Angus patties, American cheese, pickles, onions and potato bun stay the same across all three.

Most Sonic Smasher copycats online, CopyKat’s and Cup of Yum’s among them, rebuild the Original and chase its secret sauce. The All-American is a different burger. Want the bacon build? My All-American Bacon Smasher copycat walks through it step by step.

Ingredients for 2 All-American Smashers

This copycat makes 2 double smash cheeseburgers from 300 g (10.6 oz) of 80/20 ground beef, 4 slices of American cheese and 2 potato buns. The sauce and topping amounts are my proportions, not Sonic’s.

  • 300 g (10.6 oz) ground beef, 80/20 (20% fat), divided into 4 balls of 75 g (2.6 oz)
  • 4 slices American cheese
  • 2 potato buns
  • 2 tbsp ketchup (1 tbsp per burger)
  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise (1 tbsp per burger)
  • 2 leaves iceberg or green leaf lettuce
  • 2 tomato slices
  • 6 to 8 crinkle-cut pickle chips
  • 2 tbsp finely diced white onion
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp butter for toasting the buns (optional)

Equipment: a cast-iron skillet or flat griddle, a stiff metal spatula, parchment paper and an infrared thermometer.

Which ground beef works best

Pick 80/20 ground beef, and don’t go leaner than 85/15. CopyKat’s Sonic Double Smasher uses 85/15 in loose balls smashed very thin. I go one step fattier. With no bacon in the pan, the beef’s own fat has to fry those edges crisp. Sonic says Angus, so if your store sells ground Angus, that’s the closest match.

Step by step

The All-American Smasher copycat takes about 15 minutes of prep and about 10 minutes at the stove. Each batch of patties is done in under 3 minutes, so get every topping ready before the beef goes in.

1. Prep the toppings

Dice the onion fine, slice the tomato, dry the lettuce and drain the pickles. Set out the ketchup and mayo too. Once the beef hits the pan, there’s no time to slice anything.

2. Portion the beef

Weigh the beef into 4 loose balls of 75 g (2.6 oz). Don’t pack them, and don’t salt them yet. Keep them in the fridge until the pan is hot: cold fat holds together under the spatula.

3. Toast the buns

Split the potato buns and toast the cut sides in the skillet over medium heat, with a little butter if you like, until golden. Set them aside cut side up.

4. Heat the skillet to 450–500 °F

Turn the burner to high and heat the empty skillet until an infrared thermometer reads 450–500 °F (230–260 °C) on the surface. Any cooler and the patty steams instead of searing.

5. Smash and season the patties

Drop 2 balls in the skillet, lay parchment on top and press each one paper-thin with the spatula within the first few seconds. Peel off the parchment, season with salt and pepper, then leave the patties alone for 2 minutes. You want brown, lacy edges.

6. Flip and melt the cheese

Scrape under each patty to keep the crust, flip it and lay a slice of American cheese on top. Cook 30 to 45 seconds until the cheese melts, then stack one patty on the other. The USDA safe minimum for ground beef is 160 °F (71 °C), as Clara explains in her burger patty internal temperature guide. Repeat with the last 2 balls.

7. Build the All-American stack

Spread 1 tablespoon of ketchup on the bottom bun and 1 tablespoon of mayo on the top bun. From the bottom up: the double cheese patty, pickles, diced onion, tomato, lettuce, then the top bun. Sonic doesn’t publish its order. I keep the lettuce under the top bun so the tomato juice never reaches the mayo.

How close this All-American Smasher copycat gets to Sonic’s

On ingredients and build, this copycat matches Sonic’s All-American Smasher: every item Sonic lists, the same double patty, the same smash technique. On taste, I can’t promise identical. I haven’t eaten it side by side with the drive-in version. But nothing on Sonic’s list is missing.

My take: of all the Smashers, this is the one worth making at home. Nothing in it hangs on a sauce recipe Sonic keeps to itself.

  • No secret sauce to guess. Ketchup and mayo are just ketchup and mayo, which makes this the most honest Smasher to copy.
  • Same texture goal. Sonic promises crispy edges and a juicy center. A thin 75 g patty on a 450 °F pan does both.
  • Same bun type. A soft potato bun squashes around 2 thin patties instead of fighting them.
  • Where it can differ: Sonic’s seasoning, bun brand and pickle type aren’t public, and salt and pepper is my assumption.

Reviews of the drive-in burger lean warm. Taryn Pire at Mashed reports customers praising the crispy edges and tender center, one “surprised how good it was for $6.” Others found it too salty, or not much different from a regular burger. Sporked reports that YouTube reviewer Daym Drops gave the $6 meal 5 out of 5. The salt complaint is the one I’d take seriously at home. Pickles and American cheese already bring plenty, so I season the beef lightly.

FAQ

What’s in the Sonic All-American Smasher?

The All-American SONIC Smasher has 2 seasoned, smashed Angus beef patties, American cheese (2 slices per Sonic’s menu page), lettuce, tomato, crinkle-cut pickles, diced onions, ketchup and mayonnaise on a toasted potato bun, according to Sonic and its January 12, 2026 launch release.

How many calories are in the Sonic All-American Smasher?

The double All-American Smasher has 610 calories, 37 g of fat and 35 g of protein, according to FastFoodNutrition. Mashed gives the same figures plus 32 g of carbohydrates and 8 g of sugar. The Triple has 790 calories, 52 g of fat and 48 g of protein. These are third-party figures for Sonic’s burger, not for this copycat; I couldn’t check Sonic’s own nutrition page.

How much is the All-American Smasher, and what’s in the $6 meal?

The All-American Smasher Meal costs $6 before tax and launched on January 12, 2026. It includes 1 All-American SONIC Smasher, medium Tots or Groovy Fries and a medium soft drink or iced tea. The Triple and Bacon versions are excluded, and add-ons cost extra. Sonic publishes no single price for the burger alone, and prices vary by franchisee. I couldn’t confirm the $6 meal is still running in November 2026.

Is the All-American Smasher still available at Sonic?

It launched as a limited-time offer, and Mashed reported it was still on the menu on September 22, 2026. Sonic’s menu page returned an error when I tried to open it on November 5, 2026, so check the Sonic app or your local drive-in.

Do I need a secret sauce for this copycat?

No. The All-American Smasher uses ketchup and mayonnaise instead of the Original’s Smasher sauce. One tablespoon of each per burger is my proportion; Sonic doesn’t publish amounts.

How much does a Sonic Smasher patty weigh?

Sonic doesn’t publish its patty weight. This recipe uses 75 g (2.6 oz) balls as a home estimate. For reference, Eat This, Not That! weighed a whole Original Triple Smasher, bun included, at 10.9 oz (about 309 g).

Can I make a smash burger in a cast-iron skillet?

Yes. Heat a cast-iron skillet empty on high to 450–500 °F (230–260 °C) and cook 2 patties at a time. Smash each ball in the first few seconds, then leave it alone for 2 minutes so the crust forms.

What fat ratio is best for a smash burger?

Use 80/20 ground beef and don’t go leaner than 85/15, the ratio CopyKat uses for its Sonic Double Smasher. Leaner beef dries out when it’s smashed this thin, and the edges don’t crisp.
