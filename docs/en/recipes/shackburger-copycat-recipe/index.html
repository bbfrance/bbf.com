ShackBurger Recipe Copycat: Shake Shack at Home

I tested a home ShackBurger copycat with smashed patty, copycat Shack sauce and toasted potato bun.

By ClaraPublished October 4, 2026

Yes, you can make a convincing shackburger recipe copycat at home. The official ShackBurger is a beef patty with American cheese, lettuce, tomato and proprietary ShackSauce in a soft potato bun, sold as a single or double with crinkle-cut fries. I burned my first patty. The third try — one hard smash, then 90 seconds untouched — gave the lacy edge I wanted. My version below reproduces that build with a smashed patty, a mayo-mustard-ketchup-pickle copycat sauce presented as my hypothesis, and a toasted potato bun. My take: a single with extra sauce beats a double here.

What Is an Official ShackBurger?

The official ShackBurger is a beef patty topped with American cheese, lettuce, tomato and proprietary ShackSauce in a soft bun, available single or double and served with crinkle-cut fries.

I keep the official facts and my kitchen tests separate. Shake Shack does not publish the exact ShackSauce recipe, patty weight, bun formula or smash time and temperature, so everything grammed and timed below is my copycat hypothesis, not the chain formula. Sources: Shake Shack history, menu, IPO and international expansion and Shake Shack investor overview describing a premium roadside burger stand.

ShackBurger Copycat Ingredients: My Tested List

My shackburger recipe copycat makes 2 single burgers. I weighed everything to the gram after three tests.

  • 2 potato buns, about 85 g each, split — see my method for homemade soft potato burger buns
  • 2 x 115 g balls of 80/20 ground beef, cold, loosely packed — my copycat portion, not an official weight
  • Fine sea salt and cracked black pepper
  • 2 slices American cheese — my choice for melt; Shake Shack lists American cheese officially
  • 2 leaves green-leaf lettuce, 2 slices ripe tomato
  • 30 g unsalted butter, softened, for toasting
  • Copycat Shack sauce (makes about 120 g, enough for 4 burgers): 60 g mayonnaise, 15 g ketchup, 10 g yellow mustard, 20 g finely chopped dill pickles plus 5 g pickle brine, 1 g sweet paprika, 1 g garlic powder, cayenne on the tip of a knife, salt and pepper

How I Make Copycat Shack Sauce

Copycat Shack sauce is my hypothesis. The real ShackSauce is proprietary and unpublished.

I stir all sauce ingredients together and chill 30 minutes. Mine tastes creamy, tangy and lightly spiced from mayo, mustard, ketchup, pickles, paprika and cayenne, close to the well-known mayo-mustard-ketchup-pickle copycat family. I do not present it as the official formula. I like 1 tablespoon per bun; 2 turns sloppy. For what is officially known, see what is Shack sauce made of.

ShackBurger Copycat Steps, Smashed and Toasted

My home method uses a cast-iron skillet and a stiff spatula. No press or flat-top needed.

1. Chill and mix the sauce

I mix the copycat Shack sauce first so the pickle brine and spices meld. I chill it while I prep the toppings.

2. Prep lettuce, tomato and potato buns

I wash and dry the lettuce, slice the tomato 5 mm thick, and butter the cut sides of the buns. A dry leaf and a toasted bun keep the burger from going soggy.

3. Preheat cast iron hot

I heat a cast-iron skillet over medium-high until just smoking, about 230°C / 450°F. I vent the kitchen and keep everything within reach.

4. Smash for 90 seconds

I place one 115 g beef ball in the hot dry pan and smash hard to about 6 mm with a spatula for 10 seconds. I season with salt and pepper and sear for 90 seconds without moving it.

Smashing patty for ShackBurger recipe copycat — small patties searing on a hot griddle with cheese melting (Photo: jeffreyw, CC BY 2.0 — Source: https://www.flickr.com/photos/7927684@N03/8048385498)

5. Flip, cheese and finish

I flip once, top immediately with American cheese, and cook 60 to 90 seconds more until lacy at the edges. I rest 2 minutes on a warm plate.

Smashed thin burger patty with lacy crisp edges, result of 90-second smash at 230°C / 450°F (Photo: Michael Rivera, CC BY-SA 4.0 — Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Smash\_Burger,\_Village\_Commons,\_Tallahassee.jpg)

6. Toast buns and assemble

I toast the buttered potato buns cut-side down in the beef fat for 45 to 60 seconds until golden. I spread 1 tablespoon sauce per bun, then add lettuce, tomato, cheesy patty and top bun, and serve at once.

Shake Shack History: Premium Fast Casual From Madison Square Park

Shake Shack started as a hot-dog cart in Madison Square Park in 2001, run through the kitchen of Eleven Madison Park, and opened its first permanent kiosk in Madison Square Park, New York, in July 2004 under restaurateur Danny Meyer.

Original Shake Shack kiosk in Madison Square Park New York, birthplace of the premium roadside burger stand since 2004 (Photo: Beyond My Ken, CC BY-SA 4.0 — Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=10704133)

That origin explains its premium fast-casual positioning: New York-based, serving burgers, hot dogs, crinkle-cut fries, shakes, frozen custard, beer and wine, described as a premium roadside burger stand at good value. It priced its IPO at 21 USD on 29 January 2015, listed on the NYSE as SHAK on 30 January 2015, and jumped to 47 USD on day one. For context on accessible-premium pricing I link my analysis of 2026 burger value wars and premium pricing.

Shake Shack IPO pop chart — $21 IPO price on 29 Jan 2015 to $47 day-one close on 30 Jan 2015 (NYSE: SHAK) (Chart: Burger Breton (original, data from Wikipedia / investor.shakeshack.com), CC0)

Shake Shack counts more than 400 locations worldwide. Its first international opening was Dubai Mall of the Emirates in April 2011, followed by the Avenues in Kuwait in July 2011. Europe started in London in mid-2013, with 16 UK sites in 2024. In Asia it opened Japan in 2015, South Korea in Gangnam in June 2016, Singapore at Jewel Changi in 2019, Thailand at CentralWorld Bangkok on 30 March 2023, and Malaysia at Exchange TRX Kuala Lumpur on 10 April 2024. FAQ: ShackBurger Copycat

What is officially in a ShackBurger?

The official ShackBurger contains a beef patty, American cheese, lettuce, tomato and proprietary ShackSauce in a soft bun, single or double, served with crinkle-cut fries. Anything beyond that is copycat interpretation.

What is Shack sauce made of?

The exact ShackSauce recipe is unpublished and proprietary. Home versions with mayonnaise, mustard, ketchup, chopped pickles, paprika and cayenne are hypotheses, including mine above.

Who founded Shake Shack and when?

Restaurateur Danny Meyer founded it. It began as a 2001 hot-dog cart in Madison Square Park and opened as a permanent kiosk there in July 2004.

Single vs double: what changes?

Officially the choice is one or two patties with cheese. I do not state official weights, calories or prices because I could not source a dated official nutrition and price sheet for this article, so I make a double at home with two of my 115 g smashed patties.

How do I get a good smash without pro gear?

I use a ripping-hot cast-iron skillet at about 230°C / 450°F, a stiff metal spatula and parchment. I smash once hard for 10 seconds, then leave it alone for 90 seconds to crust.

Is Shake Shack really premium?

Premium here is positioning, not my verdict. The company describes itself as a premium roadside stand, and critics compare it to Five Guys on quality versus price. I separate that opinion from facts and I do not claim Angus, no-antibiotics or nutrition figures without an official dated source.
