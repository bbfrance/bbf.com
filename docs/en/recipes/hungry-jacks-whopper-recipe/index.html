Hungry Jack's Whopper Recipe: Australian Copycat

Make the Australian Whopper at home: a flame-grilled 5-inch beef patty, mayo, tomato sauce, no cheese. Tested for two, built from Hungry Jack's own guide.

By Clara, Recipes editorPublished November 26, 2026

To make a Hungry Jack’s Whopper at home, grill a thin 5-inch beef patty over high, direct heat and build it on a toasted sesame bun with iceberg lettuce, tomato, brown onion, pickles, mayo and tomato sauce. No cheese. That is the Australian Whopper as Hungry Jack’s lists it in its 17 February 2026 nutrition guide. My version below makes two burgers in about 25 minutes. It is an unofficial home reproduction, not the chain’s recipe: Hungry Jack’s publishes the components but not the proportions, the seasoning or the raw patty weight, so those parts are mine.

What is in a Hungry Jack’s Whopper? The official ingredients

A Hungry Jack’s Whopper is a 280 g burger with one 5-inch beef patty, a 5-inch sesame-glazed bun, iceberg lettuce, a tomato slice, fresh brown onion, pickles, mayonnaise and tomato sauce, and no cheese. Those are the components in the Hungry Jack’s nutrition guide of 17 February 2026 (Hungry Jack’s nutrition guide, PDF, checked 20 November 2026). The menu page sells it as flame-grilled Aussie beef with ‘an irresistible, smoky BBQ flavour’ on a toasted sesame seed bun (hungryjacks.com.au).

  • Bun: Sesame Glazed 5” (about 12.7 cm across).
  • Patty: Beef Patty 5”, listed as 100% beef.
  • Mayonnaise: soybean oil, egg yolks, water, spirit vinegar, corn syrup, salt, sugar, mustard flour.
  • Tomato sauce: tomato paste, sugar, concentrated white vinegar, salt, natural flavours, citric acid, spices.
  • Pickles: cucumbers, water, vinegar, salt, calcium lactate, natural flavours.
  • Vegetables: iceberg lettuce, tomato slice, fresh brown onion.

The official components of the Hungry Jack’s Whopper: a 5-inch (about 12.7 cm) sesame-glazed bun, a 5-inch 100% beef patty, iceberg lettuce, a tomato slice, fresh brown onion, pickles, mayo and tomato sauce. There is no cheese. Source: Hungry Jack’s nutrition guide, 17 February 2026. What the guide leaves out is quantities. The sauce amounts in my recipe are my own. It does list the allergens: the Whopper contains wheat (gluten), egg, sesame, soy and mustard, and may contain sulphites, fish and milk.

Hungry Jack’s Whopper kJ and nutrition

One Hungry Jack’s Whopper contains 2,437 kJ, about 582 Calories, according to the chain’s 17 February 2026 nutrition guide. Per 280 g serve it lists 28.1 g protein, 45.2 g fat (13.4 g saturated), 46.6 g carbohydrate (11.6 g sugars) and 846 mg sodium. That is roughly 870 kJ per 100 g.

Hungry Jack’s published a newer Whopper & Beef guide on 2 June 2026, and the Whopper is still on it as a permanent item (June 2026 guide, PDF). I worked from the February figures because those are the ones I checked line by line. I haven’t measured my homemade burger, so I give no nutrition values for it.

How heavy is the Whopper patty?

Hungry Jack’s does not publish the raw weight of the Whopper patty, but its own figures point to a cooked patty of about 79 g. A Whopper Double weighs 359 g, a single Whopper 280 g, and the 79 g gap is one extra cooked patty. Add back roughly 30% for cooking loss and you land near 113 g raw. So I shape 110 to 115 g of mince per patty. That raw weight is my estimate, not an official figure.

The only raw weight Hungry Jack’s publishes is the 1/3 lb Angus patty of its Grill Masters range (Hungry Jack’s, Better Food), which has nothing to do with the Whopper. The 4 oz (113 g) patty quoted all over the internet belongs to the US Burger King Whopper (Wikipedia). It happens to sit right on my estimate, which gave me some confidence in the maths.

Hungry Jack’s Whopper vs homemade: what changes

My copycat uses the official component list as it stands. What changes is the gear, because a home kitchen has no conveyor flame broiler, and the sauce amounts, because Hungry Jack’s keeps those to itself.

Hungry Jack’s Whopper recipe: ingredients for 2 burgers

For two Whoppers you need 230 g of beef mince, two sesame buns, iceberg lettuce, a tomato, a brown onion, pickles, mayonnaise and tomato sauce. I buy 80/20 mince (20% fat). Leaner mince dries out on a grill this hot. Hungry Jack’s doesn’t publish the fat content or the cut of its beef, so the 80/20 is my call.

  • 230 g beef mince, 80/20, kept in the fridge until shaping
  • 2 sesame seed burger buns, 12 to 13 cm across
  • 2 to 3 large iceberg lettuce leaves, roughly shredded
  • 1 ripe tomato, cut into 4 thin slices
  • ¼ brown onion, cut into thin rings
  • 6 to 8 dill pickle slices
  • 2 tbsp whole-egg mayonnaise
  • 2 tsp tomato sauce
  • Salt and black pepper, to season the patties (Hungry Jack’s doesn’t publish its seasoning)
  • 1 tsp neutral oil, for the grill

Time: 15 minutes prep (chilling the patties included), 5 to 6 minutes cooking, about 25 minutes in total, plus however long your barbecue takes to heat.

Step by step: the homemade Hungry Jack’s Whopper

Shape thin, wide patties, chill them, sear them hard over direct heat, then build each burger on a toasted bun while the patty is hot and the vegetables are still cold.

1. Shape and chill the patties

Divide the mince into two 115 g balls. Press each one between two sheets of baking paper into a thin disc about 14 cm across, wider than the bun. My first batch was pressed to bun size and shrank to about 10 cm on the grill, leaving a ring of bare bread. At 14 cm the cooked patty comes back to the edge of the bun. Press a shallow dimple into the centre with your thumb so it doesn’t dome, then chill for 10 minutes.

A Hungry Jack’s Whopper in its open box at the La Trobe Street restaurant in Melbourne, May 2025. The wide sesame bun covers the thin patty, with iceberg lettuce poking out at the edge. That is why the home patty is shaped wider than the bun. Alt: top view of a Hungry Jack’s Whopper sesame bun in its box in Melbourne (Photo: ウィ貴公子, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Whopper\_Hungry\_Jack%27s\_La\_Trobe\_Street\_Melbourne\_CBD\_May\_5,\_2025\_202505051301\_IMG\_5790.jpg)

2. Prepare the toppings

Shred the iceberg, slice the tomato and onion thinly and drain the pickles on paper towel. Back in the fridge they go until assembly. Cold lettuce against a hot patty is half of what makes the first bite of a Whopper feel right.

3. Heat the grill to 260 °C (500 °F)

Preheat a barbecue or gas grill to 260 °C (500 °F) for direct grilling, then oil the grates lightly. It’s the setup I use for my Ultimate Double Whopper copycat. Hungry Jack’s doesn’t publish its broiler temperature, so treat this as a home barbecue setting, not the restaurant’s.

4. Flame-grill the patties

Season both sides with salt and pepper and lay the patties over the hottest part of the grill. Give them 2 to 2.5 minutes per side and don’t press them: pressing squeezes the fat into the fire and dries the meat. They’re done at 71 °C (160 °F) in the centre, the safe temperature for minced beef in my burger patty internal temperature guide.

5. Toast the buns

Toast the buns cut side down on the cooler edge of the grill for 30 to 60 seconds, until golden. A toasted crumb stands up to the sauces and matches the ‘toasted sesame seed bun’ on the Hungry Jack’s menu.

6. Assemble the Whopper

Build from the bottom up: bottom bun, patty, pickles, tomato sauce, onion, two tomato slices, lettuce, mayo, top bun. Hungry Jack’s doesn’t publish its build order. This is the Burger King order from my Burger King Whopper copycat recipe, and it puts the mayo and lettuce against your top lip first, which is the bite I recognise. Wrap each burger in baking paper for a minute so the bun softens slightly, then serve.

Why this recipe tastes like the original

This copycat gets close to the Hungry Jack’s Whopper because it uses the official component list, a patty sized from the chain’s own weights, and real flame.

  • The component list is official. Every topping and both sauces come from the 17 February 2026 guide. No cheese, because the plain Whopper has none.
  • The patty is sized from published weights. A 110 to 115 g raw patty cooks down close to the 79 g suggested by the gap between the 280 g Whopper and the 359 g Double.
  • The heat is open flame. Hungry Jack’s says its Better Beef has no additives, preservatives, colourings or fillers, and that ‘the only thing we add is flame grilling’, which gives ‘the smoky flavour you just can’t get from pan-frying’ (Hungry Jack’s).

My take: skip anything else before you skip the barbecue. I made the same patties in a cast-iron pan for comparison. Good burger, but it tasted like a pan-fried burger. The char is the Whopper.

Hungry Jack’s Whopper vs Burger King Whopper

The Hungry Jack’s Whopper is the Burger King Whopper under another name, with the same published toppings: sesame bun, mayo, lettuce, tomato, pickles, ketchup (tomato sauce in Australia) and onion. Burger King created the Whopper in 1957, and co-founder James McLamore launched it at 37 US cents (Wikipedia). Hungry Jack’s calls its version ‘an Aussie legend for over 40 years’ (hungryjacks.com.au).

On paper, the differences are small. Hungry Jack’s specifies a 5-inch bun and patty and Aussie beef. The US Whopper is described with a 4 oz (113 g) flame-grilled patty. In February 2026 Burger King gave its US and Canadian Whopper a new bun and a creamier mayo, as my colleague Maria reports in the new Burger King Whopper of 2026. No source confirms that change for Australia, so I left it out. For the American build, see my Burger King Whopper copycat recipe and the breakdown in what’s on a Whopper.

Hungry Jack’s Whopper FAQ

Is Hungry Jack’s the same as Burger King? Yes. Hungry Jack’s is the Burger King franchisee in Australia. Jack Cowin opened the first restaurant in Innaloo, Perth, in 1971 under a different name because the Burger King trademark was already registered locally (Wikipedia). The chain lists 480 restaurants (Hungry Jack’s, About us, checked October 2026). The full story is on our Hungry Jack’s brand profile.

Innaloo, in Perth’s northern suburbs, where Jack Cowin opened the first Hungry Jack’s in 1971. Alt: map of Innaloo, Perth, birthplace of Hungry Jack’s (Map: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0, https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)

Is the Hungry Jack’s Whopper the same as the Burger King Whopper? It is the same burger, adapted to Australia. The toppings match, and the 17 February 2026 guide specifies a 5-inch bun and patty of 100% beef, which Hungry Jack’s describes as Aussie beef. Burger King’s 2026 bun and mayo changes in the US and Canada aren’t confirmed for Australia.

Does a Hungry Jack’s Whopper have cheese? No. The plain Whopper has no cheese. The Whopper Cheese adds processed cheese and weighs 302 g instead of 280 g, so the cheese adds about 22 g. Hungry Jack’s doesn’t say how many slices that is.

How much does the Whopper patty weigh? Hungry Jack’s doesn’t publish it. The 79 g gap between the Whopper (280 g) and the Whopper Double (359 g) suggests a cooked patty of about 79 g, or roughly 110 to 115 g raw. Both numbers are estimates.

What sauce goes on a Whopper? Both mayonnaise and tomato sauce. In the build order I use, the mayo goes under the top bun with the lettuce and the tomato sauce goes on the patty with the pickles. Hungry Jack’s doesn’t publish its own order.

How many kJ are in a Hungry Jack’s Whopper? 2,437 kJ (about 582 Calories) per 280 g burger, according to the Hungry Jack’s nutrition guide of 17 February 2026.

Is the Hungry Jack’s Whopper halal? Yes, according to Hungry Jack’s: its 17 February 2026 nutrition guide marks the Whopper as halal.

How do I get the flame-grilled taste without a barbecue? A very hot ridged cast-iron grill pan gives you char lines and some colour, but in my test it didn’t come near the smoke of open flame. Hungry Jack’s itself says pan-frying can’t reproduce it. If you can, use a barbecue at 260 °C (500 °F).

Which bun should I use for a homemade Whopper? A sesame seed bun about 12.7 cm (5 inches) across, the size Hungry Jack’s lists, toasted cut side down before you build the burger.

How much does a Whopper cost at Hungry Jack’s? I couldn’t confirm a price from an official source. Prices vary by restaurant and between the counter and the app, so check the Hungry Jack’s app or order.hungryjacks.com.au for your local store.
