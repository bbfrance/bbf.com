Jack in the Box: History, Key Facts and Reviews

Jack in the Box is a San Diego burger chain built on the drive-thru. Robert O. Peterson opened the first restaurant on El Cajon Boulevard in 1951, with a two-way intercom for ordering from the car. On July 5, 2026, the chain had 2,115 restaurants, about 93% franchised, almost all in the western and southern United States, plus a handful in Mexico and Guam. Its systemwide sales reached $4.21 billion in fiscal 2025.

History of Jack in the Box

1951-1967: Robert O. Peterson’s intercom drive-thru in San Diego

Jack in the Box was born as a drive-thru burger stand where customers ordered through an intercom. Robert O. Peterson, who had run Topsy’s Drive-In since 1941, opened the first restaurant at 6270 El Cajon Boulevard in San Diego on February 21, 1951 (Wikipedia). He had bought the rights to the two-way intercom in 1947 from George Manos, an Alaska drive-in owner. Peterson grouped his restaurants under a holding company, Foodmaker, in 1960.

1968-1992: Ralston Purina, a lost clown and a return to the market

Jack in the Box grew fast under Ralston Purina, then lost its way. Peterson sold Foodmaker to the food group in 1968, when it had more than 180 restaurants, mainly in California and the Southwest. The push into East Coast markets was cut back, then halted at the end of the 1970s. Around 1980, the company literally blew up its clown mascot in TV commercials, and in late 1985 it even tried the name “Monterey Jack’s” before going back to Jack in the Box. Management bought Foodmaker from Ralston Purina in 1985 and took it public in 1987, with 897 restaurants and $655 million in sales.

1993-2017: the E. coli outbreak and the comeback of Jack

The chain’s worst crisis changed food safety in the United States. Between December 1992 and February 1993, undercooked patties contaminated with E. coli O157:H7 at 73 Jack in the Box restaurants in Washington, Idaho, California and Nevada caused 732 confirmed cases and killed four children (Wikipedia). The USDA then classified that strain as an adulterant in ground beef, and 155 °F became the reference cooking temperature for hamburgers. The brand recovered in 1994 with ads starring “Jack,” a fictional chief executive. Our Jumbo Jack copycat recipe recreates its best-known burger.

2018-2026: Del Taco, closures and 2,115 restaurants

Today Jack in the Box is a smaller, almost fully franchised chain. It completed the purchase of Del Taco in March 2022, then sold it to Yadav Enterprises for $115 million on December 22, 2025. In fiscal 2025, which ended on September 28, 2025, it opened 31 restaurants, closed 86 and finished with 2,136, including three in Mexico, where it returned in February 2024 with a restaurant in Chihuahua, and two in Guam. Systemwide sales fell to $4.21 billion, from $4.40 billion a year earlier (Q4 2025 results). About 70% of the restaurants are in California and Texas. Its “JACK on Track” plan calls for 50 to 60 more closures in fiscal 2026, and the count stood at 2,115 on July 5, 2026 (Q3 2026 results). Key dates

  • 1951: Robert O. Peterson opens the first Jack in the Box in San Diego, on February 21.
  • 1968: Ralston Purina buys Foodmaker, the chain’s parent company.
  • 1987: Foodmaker goes public with 897 restaurants.
  • 1993: the E. coli outbreak in four western states kills four children.
  • 1994: the “Jack” advertising campaign relaunches the brand.
  • 2022: Jack in the Box completes the purchase of Del Taco.
  • 2025: Del Taco is sold on December 22; the chain closes fiscal 2025 with 2,136 restaurants.
  • 2026: 2,115 restaurants on July 5.

On Trustpilot, Jack in the Box customers mostly complain about the food and the service: cold burgers, soggy hash browns, chicken served undercooked and ingredients that taste stale. Missing items and mixed-up orders come up often, as do drive-thru waits of 10 to 15 minutes. Several reviewers describe staff as dismissive or confrontational, notably over drinks and restroom access. The few positive reviews praise polite, friendly employees, quick service at some restaurants and fresh, crunchy tacos.

How customer ratings are collected

Every chain is rated the same way, from two public sources shown side by side. Google: the ratings of the restaurants of the chain that Google Maps lists in the largest city of its home market with at least three of them, up to ten, averaged by number of reviews. Trustpilot: the rating of the brand itself.

The two are never averaged together. Google reviews judge the meal and the visit; Trustpilot mostly gathers complaints about service, orders and refunds, and many chains have no page there. A rating we could not read is left out rather than estimated, and each one shows the date we checked it.
