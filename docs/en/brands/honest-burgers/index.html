Honest Burgers: History, Key Facts and Reviews

Honest Burgers is a British casual-dining burger chain. Friends Tom Barton and Philip Eeles founded it in Brighton in 2009 and opened their first permanent restaurant in Brixton, south London, in 2011. In August 2024 the group ran 39 restaurants across England and Wales, most of them in London, with branches as far as Cardiff, Liverpool and Manchester. It employed around 700 people at the end of 2023.

History of Honest Burgers

2009-2011: from a Brighton marquee to Brixton Village

Honest Burgers began in 2009 in Brighton, where friends Tom Barton and Philip Eeles co-founded the business (Restaurant Online). The two recent graduates wanted no part of a nine-to-five job. They started with a marquee, a griddle and a fryer, and one aim: high-quality British burgers (Lucky Saint). Honest Burgers Ltd was registered on 12 January 2011 (Companies House). The same year they opened their first permanent restaurant, a small unit without its own toilet inside Brixton Village market, on Coldharbour Lane. That site still trades, and it tops our guide to the best burgers in London.

2012-2023: a London chain that reaches Cardiff and Manchester

Honest Burgers grew as a London restaurant group before opening further afield. Most of its sites are in the capital, with branches as far as Cardiff, Liverpool and Manchester (Wikipedia). The growth came with friction. In February 2023 the company told staff they could be fired and rehired if they refused to give up paid breaks. In December 2023 HMRC filed a winding-up petition after talks on a payment plan for a tax bill broke down; Honest paid and the petition was withdrawn. The chain employed around 700 people at the time (City AM). The same year it raised £2.8 million to launch Honest Smashed, a quick-service spin-off.

2024-2025: new management and 39 restaurants

Honest Burgers entered 2024 with growing sales and a new leadership team. Thomas Kelly became chief executive in January 2024 and moved to the role of group chair in September, when finance director Matt Brandon took over as interim CEO. At that point the group ran 39 restaurants across England and Wales. Sales rose 14% in the first half of 2024, like-for-like delivery sales were up 22%, and the chain had extended its exclusive deal with Uber Eats until 2026 (Restaurant Online). In August 2025 the head office moved to the Menier Building on Southwark Street, in London. The parent company, Honest Group Ltd, filed its group accounts for the year to 26 January 2025 in October 2025 (Companies House).

Key dates

  • 2009: Tom Barton and Philip Eeles start Honest Burgers in Brighton.
  • 2011: first permanent restaurant in Brixton Village, south London.
  • 2023: fire-and-rehire row over paid breaks; £2.8 million raised for Honest Smashed.
  • 2024: 39 restaurants across England and Wales; Matt Brandon becomes interim CEO.
  • 2025: head office moves to the Menier Building, Southwark.

Trustpilot reviewers of Honest Burgers are split. Many praise friendly, attentive staff at branches such as Tooting, Finsbury Park and St Katharine Docks, and enjoy the burgers when they are cooked right. The most frequent complaint is the chips, described as cold, soggy, overdone or frozen. Others report slow service, burgers served burnt or undercooked, missing items, delivery orders that arrive cold, and drinks, service charges and portions they find poor value.

How customer ratings are collected

Every chain is rated the same way, from two public sources shown side by side. Google: the ratings of the restaurants of the chain that Google Maps lists in the largest city of its home market with at least three of them, up to ten, averaged by number of reviews. Trustpilot: the rating of the brand itself.

The two are never averaged together. Google reviews judge the meal and the visit; Trustpilot mostly gathers complaints about service, orders and refunds, and many chains have no page there. A rating we could not read is left out rather than estimated, and each one shows the date we checked it.
